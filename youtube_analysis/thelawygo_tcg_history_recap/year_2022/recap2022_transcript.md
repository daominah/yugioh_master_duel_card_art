### Intro

For those who are used to me talking about historic Yu-Gi-Oh at this point,
the fact of the matter is I love this game in almost all iterations,
from retro formats to alternative game formats to the off-the-wall weird ones of
the old PlayStation games
(what the hell was going on there?), and that includes the modern game.
I've tried to keep my topics fairly rooted in the past to try to differentiate my channel
from some other large names in the community,
because I never really wanted to compete with a lot of the names I've grown to respect over
the years,
rather make content that can complement them.
However, this particular year in the game has been far more of a roller coaster
than I think any of us expected,
after the massive disappointments of like 85 percent of the sets released in 2021,
and I'd like to talk about that today.

In short, Yu-Gi-Oh over the past year has seen almost every major block of the year
bring some kind of meta-warping presence to the game,
and I wanted to talk about those,
because some of the strategies released within the past year
have brought me some of the most joy I personally had in the game in some time.
We've had four main sets, three structure decks, two deck building sets,
reprint sets galore, and even the supposed end of the Legendary Duelists sets,
which we'll get into later, which have all brought something to the table, no matter how small.
So let's rewind a bit to the start of the year.

### The Grand Creators

- Release date: January 28th, 2022
- Set type: deck building set
- Major strategies: Noh-P.U.N.K. (Punk), Exosister, Adventure
- Impact: "extra time" in the captions, likely "Adventure Time" (not resolved)

Talk about starting this year off with a bang.
The Grand Creators was released into a format dominated by Swordsoul, Tenyi and Bird Up (Lyrilusc),
but many other decks were popular thanks to `Predaplant Verte Anaconda`
and `Destiny HERO - Destroyer Phoenix Enforcer`, released at the tail end of last year.
Immediately the set was cracked open, due to the known factor that was set to dominate the TCG:
the Adventure engine,
a series of cards that could be splashed into any deck that didn't need its Normal Summon
to provide a one-card omni negate through `Wandering Gryphon Rider`
and a field bounce through `Dracoback, the Rideable Dragon`,
being a one-card engine that could be started off of any copy of `Rite of Aramesir`,
`Water Enchantress of the Temple` or `Foolish Burial`.
The engine was splashed into practically everything in the format,
even in decks that didn't make the most sense, due to its ease of use.

At this point in time the meta shifted, seeing a massive rise in the playability of Prank-Kids.
Adventure meant you didn't need the effect of a Prank-Kid on Normal Summon,
and they could easily take advantage of both the Adventure engine
and the widely popular DPE (`Destiny HERO - Destroyer Phoenix Enforcer`) and Scythe lock
(`Artifact Scythe`).
But what I don't think many talk about is the insane long-term value of this particular set.
Yes, in the short term Adventure was busted,
but there were two other extremely playable archetypes in here.
Punk jumped out to people immediately as a Hulk engine,
thanks to `Noh-P.U.N.K. Ze Amin` being summonable off of `Emergency Teleport`
and being able to search for `Noh-P.U.N.K. Foxy Tune`,
which got you another Ze Amin on board to make Hulk (`Crystron Halqifibrax`).
Exosister, on the other hand, was the odd one out for the time being,
as playing them relied heavily on the opponent doing heavy grave manipulation,
which at the time wasn't the most common,
but would eventually become a lot more playable as time went on.
Regardless, this one set shook up the meta significantly,
at least a lot more than what would be following directly after.

#### Ban list (February 7th)

The Forbidden and Limited list was updated soon after The Grand Creators was released,
on February 7th, 2022.
This list would move to make some very questionable changes to the metagame.
Banned: `Archnemeses Protos`, `Dark Simorgh` and `Imperial Order`.
Limited: `Astrograph Sorcerer`, `Destrudo the Lost Dragon's Frisson`, `Fairy Tail - Snow`,
`Lunalight Tiger`, `Lyrilusc - Recital Starling`, `Monster Gate` and `Pot of Desires`.
Semi-limited: `A Hero Lives`, `Fusion Destiny`, `Nadir Servant`, `Salamangreat Circle`
and `Scapegoat`.
Unlimited: `Danger! Nessie!`, `Performapal Skullcrobat Joker`,
`Darkness Metal, the Dragon of Dark Steel`, `Emergency Teleport`, `Raigeki` and `Skill Drain`.

While this list would successfully kneecap Bird Up, a popular deck in the format,
and remove the Herald variant of Drytron, a couple of changes here would be immediately suspect.
Releasing Snow and Destrudo was questionable immediately,
especially with some releases we already knew were coming,
and Emergency Teleport being released boosted the popularity of the Punk engine further.
But `Fusion Destiny` too was the oddest choice of all on this list:
`Predaplant Verte Anaconda` was still legal,
meaning that it didn't matter how many copies of Fusion Destiny you could play,
as long as you still had one Descent.
So the hit was a bit odd.
Even still, these changes, as unremarkable as most were,
would set the stage for the next set, to at least do something.

### Battle of Chaos

- Release date: February 11th, 2022
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Dinomorphia, Libromancer
- Impact: heard as "ritual best engine, let's go", most likely a pile of the best engines
  (not resolved)

You know, in a year with some of the highest impact from main sets that we've had in some time,
it's hard to remember what Battle of Chaos brought to the table,
because it didn't change much overall.
Dinomorphia, a series of dinosaurs that focused on having their own Life Points
and Fusion Summon their boss monsters with traps,
would do a whole lot of nothing on release, which was to be expected,
since their main line of playable support was to be released in the next set.
The new TCG exclusive archetype Libromancer seemed like it had good bones,
being able to start play lines with `Emergency Teleport` and Ritual Summon with their Field Spell,
but needed something more to be viable.
All in all, only a couple of cards from the set would have meta relevance at all.

`Swordsoul Sinister Sovereign - Qixing Longyuan` was the new Level 10 Synchro for Swordsoul decks,
which let you banish cards from the opponent and burn them up to twice per turn,
seemingly being a counter to the Adventure engine
by being able to banish the token and continue a spell on the same turn with its effect.
`Floowandereeze and the Advent of Adventure` was a search spell for any monster or the Field Spell
in the archetype, instantly becoming a staple to the strategy
and boosting the deck up a good bit into the meta as playable.
`Guardian Chimera` was about as close to the definition of "get it while it's cheap":
Chimera quickly fell to around twenty dollars,
but everyone in the community knew that it would be a staple in a major meta deck just around
the corner,
being a Fusion of any three monsters from the hand or field,
but you had to use at least one from each location,
gaining effects based on how many were used from where.
Lastly, and rather unexpectedly, `Illusion of Chaos` became the chase card of
the set shortly after release,
being able to reveal itself in hand to search for any monster that mentions "Dark Magician"
from Deck to hand, then places itself back in the Deck.
This was almost exclusively used to search `Magicians' Souls`,
which could then dump `Illusion of Chaos` from the Deck,
Special Summon itself and trigger its effects,
which would be one of the cornerstone interactions of a new pile deck rising into the meta.

The new pile deck took on many different names and, quite frankly,
never had a set-in-stone name over the course of its time in the meta,
with names like Synchropile, DPE Adventure Synchro and Kitchen Sink being applied from time to time.
But the name that seemed to stick finally was BASED,
short for Borreload, Artifact, Souls, Enforcer,
Dragon (the caption read "brave artifact souls enforcer dragon").
It aimed to jam literally as many good engine pieces as it could together
to make a deck that could perform the most powerful plays in the game at the time,
each dueling like omni negates in `Baronne de Fleur`, `Borreload Savage Dragon`
and `Herald of the Arc Light`,
walking through `Predaplant Verte Anaconda` and `Artifact Dagda` to get DPE in,
with `Artifact Scythe` and the Adventure engine to back all of it up,
which further provided the needed spells for the Souls engine to get a free draw too.
BASED became the deck to beat in the format
and would only be iterated on in the following months to be made stronger with new additions,
but for the time being would set the stage for the next reprint set, just a month later.

### Hidden Arsenal: Chapter 1

- Release date: March 11th, 2022
- Set type: reprint collection
- Major strategies: the Hidden Arsenal first generation
- Impact: shelf bulk

I bet you forgot this set was this year, didn't you?
Honestly, there's not much to say here.
This collection was doomed almost from the start,
only really being here to give some cards more accessible Dual Terminal rarities,
and even then fell flat,
as the only decently relevant reprint in the entire set was `Skill Drain`,
being its first reprint in three years.
The set was clearly just a reprint set meant for legacy formats,
but even that was so haphazardly done, as a majority of
the Hidden Arsenal cards are infamously terrible.
I'm sure everyone wanted their copies of `Blizzard Warrior` or `Naturia Rock`,
but this set was doomed to collect dust on store shelves before it even released.

#### YCS Charlotte

Just under a month after Hidden Arsenal: Chapter 1 we would have a momentous occasion
for the entire community:
the first in-person YCS in two years.
YCS Charlotte 2022 happened from April 9th through 10th,
and with it came the return of large in-person events for Yu-Gi-Oh,
after the last two years only saw online digital events.
Sure enough, this event did not disappoint, showcasing the new meta,
with 10 of the top 32 going to the new big deck in the format, Prank-Kids Adventure.
Swordsoul Tenyi and Floowandereeze also held significant top cut representation,
with the Adventure engine itself being present in over half of the top 32
being, by far and away, the biggest impact change of this tournament.
BASED would also see its first showings here under the name DPE Adventure Synchro,
and another up-and-coming deck, Adventure Cyberse outlets (heard as "Adventure cybers outlets"),
would also have a showing, sporting a similar game plan to BASED,
both taking advantage of `Illusion of Chaos` and `Magicians' Souls` for the free draw.

Power Elijah Green would win the event with the aforementioned Prank-Kids Adventure,
with a side deck heavily teched out for the mirror, with `Super Polymerization`,
Swordsoul, and Adventure with `Token Collector` and Floowandereeze with `Droll & Lock Bird`.
This showing from the Adventure engine would only get more powerful, it seemed,
as another Tier 1 competitor was just around the corner.

### Structure Deck: Albaz Strike

- Release date: April 15th, 2022
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Branded, Fallen of Albaz
- Impact: heard as "should all 2.0", most likely "Shaddoll 2.0" (not resolved)

The long-awaited Alba Strike structure deck finally released in April,
after being delayed from March, and it sure did shake up the meta.
The release of `Branded Fusion`, `Lubellion the Searing Dragon` and `Mirrorjade the Iceblade Dragon`
launched the previously fringe deck of Despia straight into Tier 1 with Branded Despia,
a deck that leveraged all of the new Fusion support pieces to control board states
with a heavy amount of interaction, and tax the opponent with `Masquerade the Blazing Dragon`.
This was further boosted by `Branded in Red`,
giving the deck easy and reliable lines to `Guardian Chimera` on the opponent's turn,
which made things even more strenuous,
as essentially any board state the opponent would try to build would get interrupted
by some form of interaction from the Despia player.
This could be even further boosted by the Adventure engine,
ascending `Water Enchantress of the Temple` to start plays with `Branded Opening`,
which could provide an additional layer of insulation to your plays by starting an Adventure line.

This was on full display the following weekends, with the multiple YCSs in Bogota and Guadalajara,
having both win the event, piloted by Chris Lofton and Andre Torres respectively.
The interesting part of this, though, is the split on the best way to play the new Despia menace,
whether it be pure or with the Adventure engine,
both having clear benefits: pure being able to always Normal Summon `Aluber the Jester of Despia`
for its effect, while the Adventure engine variant had the added insulation from hand traps
interrupting your `Branded Fusion`.
In both of these events Adventure DPE was still rampant in multiple variants,
most commonly in the Prank-Kids variants,
as their ability to generate materials for `Predaplant Verte Anaconda` and `Artifact Dagda`
was unmatched, with the combo continuing to dominate the meta,
even with the new top deck in Branded.
This would lead into another major reprint set the following month,
with other sweeping changes just around the corner.

### Ghosts From the Past: The 2nd Haunting

- Release date: May 6th, 2022
- Set type: reprint set
- Major strategies: Agents, Crystal Beasts, Code Talkers
- Impact: cheap Aluber

Well, that set sure was something.
Ghosts From the Past 2 all around was a fairly unimpactful reprint set,
and it's not hard to see why.
Its main draw, the Ghost Rares, were all catered towards the legacy format market,
which, while good for selling the set initially,
left it to have almost no impact at all on the metagame.
Its new cards, like `Borreload Dragon` and `Decode Talker Heatsoul`,
would have some impact in Dragon Link and the eventual Mathmech,
but its main purpose was reprinting a bunch of niche cards
that had gotten high in value due to the impact in current decks,
like "ryza" (not resolved) from Floowandereeze, a Banshee for side deck play against Floowandereeze,
a couple of HERO cards and the Ten-Yi monsters (Tenyi).
The only massive reprint that happened here was `Aluber the Jester of Despia`,
and this was notable since he was only about half a year old at this point,
but it was actually necessary due to two factors.
`Aluber the Jester of Despia`'s price had gotten absolutely out of control at this point,
hitting around 140 dollars at its peak on the secondary market.
But more likely, the actual reason for this reprint was the fact that
in the EU printing of the set he was misprinted to say "search a Despia Spell or Trap"
instead of a Branded one,
which means he needed to be reprinted for European players to have a correctly errata'd version,
especially with the popularity of Despia Branded rising.
Regardless, Ghosts 2 didn't impact the game nearly as much as people had hoped,
but the game was definitely about to get shaken up in the following weeks.

#### Ban list (May 17th)

The ban list was updated two weeks later, on May 17th, 2022, and oh boy, what an update.
`Mecha Phantom Beast Auroradon`, `Predaplant Verte Anaconda` and `Prank-Kids Meow-Meow-Mu`
were all banned in one swoop, absolutely destroying Prank-Kids in the meta
and removing the DPE engine from most decks.
`Mecha Phantom Beast Auroradon` was also targeted at decks using `Crystron Halqifibrax`
(heard as "calcified racks") as an engine to access two to three negates off of one card,
which was further backed up by the Limited section:
`Jet Synchron`, `Red Rose Dragon`, `Yata-Garasu`, `Crystron Halqifibrax`, `Performapal Monkeyboard`,
`Shooting Riser Dragon`, `Change of Heart` and `Time Seal` were all limited here.
Yata-Garasu and Time Seal were relics of old formats that should have been released a while ago at
this point.
Jet Synchron was being released as its main issue was in Auroradon combos,
but with Halqifibrax still legal was bound to do something.
Red Rose Dragon was a hit targeted at BASED to prevent serious amounts of advantage generation,
but also did very little with Halqifibrax still legal.
Halqifibrax going to one was targeted more so at decks using second copies as a backup plan,
meaning you only had one to use in total.
Monkeyboard was the test release for Pendulums with their upcoming support, which did very little.
Shooting Riser Dragon was the head-scratcher hit of the list,
as to this day no one really knows why this card got hit at all.
`Change of Heart` was the limit that got people really talking,
as it's one of the classic Power 5 spells,
but in reality would only see play in a couple of topping lists through the rest of the year
and wouldn't be nearly as staple as people expected.

Semi-limited here were `Dinomight Knight, the True Dracofighter`, `Cyber Angel Benten`,
`Fire Formation - Tenki`, `Pot of Desires`, `Trickstar Light Stage` and `Wall of Revealing Light`.
Dinomight Knight was to boost True Dracos, as they were no longer relevant.
Benten was to boost Drytron, as the previous hits to the Fairy engine were devastating.
Tenki was due to Zoodiac and Tri-Brigade no longer being relevant in the meta.
Desires was to adjust from the previous list's hit,
which seemed extremely weird to do so soon after.
Light Stage was a boost of Trickstar but also to other decks splashing Trickstar pieces,
and Wall of Revealing Light was a product of a bygone era that needed to be released sooner.
Lastly, in the unlimits we had `Night Assailant`, `Trishula, Dragon of the Ice Barrier`,
`Salamangreat Miragestallio`, `A Hero Lives`, `Salamangreat Circle` and `Scapegoat`.
Night Assailant had an errata at this point, Trishula hadn't done anything relevant in years,
Miragestallio hadn't been meta in a while at this point,
Heroes just weren't nearly meta enough to be relevant,
and Scapegoat cheating out a Link after a one-turn delay just wasn't as good in the modern metagame.
With all of these changes in effect, all eyes turned to the next core set
to see how the meta would respond, and boy did it respond.

### Dimension Force

- Release date: May 20th, 2022
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Therion, Scareclaw, Odd-Eyes, Performapal
- Impact: better generic engines

So Dimension Force is an odd set to talk about in retrospect,
because what it did was either extremely short term or isolated to a couple of select cards,
kind of like Battle of Chaos before it.
Go figure.
The big talk of the set was undoubtedly Therion, a series of monsters that equip either monsters
of the same type or other Therion monsters to themselves to gain advantage,
with their primary playmaker `Therion "King" Regulus` being a Machine
that can either provide an omni negate on board as a monster or give a monster it's equipped to
the same effect.
Regulus being a Machine, and evolving equipping other Machines,
immediately brought ABC into the picture,
as their Union based strategy clearly synergized with the Therion game plan.
There were also the Scareclaws, a series of monsters that were primarily Level 3
and could Special Summon themselves from the hand to a zone next to another Scareclaw monster,
immediately drawing comparisons to Burning Abyss in the community.
Unfortunately they wouldn't do much in the span of time,
but would be considered as a supplemental engine for another deck in a few months.

Odd-Eyes and Performapal would get some interesting support here,
focused on the cover card of the set, `Odd-Eyes Pendulumgraph Dragon`,
the first ever Ritual Pendulum monster, who could either be Ritual Summoned or Pendulum Summoned
from the hand, provided a spell in the gate (heard as such, not resolved)
by moving itself from the field to the Pendulum Zone,
and searched a Ritual Spell while in the Pendulum Zone, bouncing itself to hand after.
While all of these effects were interesting, Odd-Eyes would continue to have no presence in the meta
due to the lack of power coming from Pendulum at the time,
though it was not from a lack of trying:
in the same set, Pendulum strategies received `Beyond the Pendulum`,
a Link monster that provided scale searching and potential dual spot removal with its effect,
while also providing two zones the Pendulum Summoned Extra Deck monsters too,
being the new star Link monster for Pendulum strategies
(with "electromite" still banned, not resolved).

Libromancer got their second wave of support here,
centralizing their strategy on being a ritual engine of sorts,
able to search for their pieces reliably and Ritual Summon out `Libromancer Doombroker`
and `Libromancer Intervention` with it.
From there it was seeing rogue play.
`Alba-Lenatus the Abyss Dragon` was the newest Albaz Fusion,
requiring Albaz and any number of Dragons, able to be contact Fused with either board
and could attack monsters up to the number of materials used,
searching a Fusion Spell at the End Phase.
This was mainly notable for giving Branded decks an answer to Dragon Link,
though it would see play mainly at a single copy, if at all, as it wasn't really needed at the time,
but would get better soon.
Much more notably for Branded was the addition of `Branded Banishment`,
which revived either a Despia or a Level 8 or higher Fusion,
then let you have a `Super Polymerization`-like effect, banishing materials from either field.
This would be slotted in far more reliably,
as a searchable Super Poly was extremely good for the deck.

"Psychic and Punisher" (not resolved) and `Noh-P.U.N.K. Deer Note` would help push Punk
as a standalone
strategy into the meta, while also making the Punk engine far better,
now having a much stronger body to summon with `Noh-P.U.N.K. Foxy Tune` for certain lines,
as Deer Note revived the Punk when sent from field to Graveyard.
"Gray Punisher" (not resolved), on the other hand, gave Punks a game-ending monster to use,
as it was exceedingly easy to put a Level 11 Synchro on the board with the deck,
with its access to Level 8s and Level 3 Tuners.
`Exosisters Magnifica` would be the end boss of the Exosister strategy,
being an Xyz of two Exosister Xyz monsters,
which could banish a card the opponent controlled and tag itself back into one of its materials
on a quick effect.
This still wouldn't make Exosister playable, but it was once again a step in the right direction.
`Illegal Knight` was another searchable interaction point for the Adventure engine,
able to bounce two cards the opponent controls on a quick effect to give itself to the opponent,
which saw some play in certain builds with the Adventure engine,
but not nearly a staple play as Gryphon before it.
Dinomorphia got `Dinomorphia Frenzy`, which let them Fuse from the Deck and Extra Deck,
and `Dinomorphia Rexterm`, the boss monster of the deck,
who shuts off the effects of all opponent's monsters whose ATK is greater than or equal
to your Life Points
and could make all monsters the opponent controlled have ATK equal to your current Life Points.
While this certainly boosted Dinomorphia's playability,
it would continue to see little success overall,
as dropping your Life Points in half repeatedly does put you in an extremely precarious situation.
Lastly, Predaplant got a new wave of support here that would see splash play in certain brews
of Branded,
but no long-term success due to its perky nature.

#### YCS (May 27th)

With all of these changes hitting the meta at once,
there was bound to be a little bit of a shake-up,
but how much was still to be seen.
The next major YCS would take place a week later, on May 27th, 2022,
being the first major event following the release of Dimension Force.
Surprisingly for most, Punk had risen into the meta substantially with the release of Deer Note,
as the one card made Punk lines with Ze Amin far more powerful than they had been prior,
thanks to `Noh-P.U.N.K. Ze Amin` alone getting you to `Chaos Ruler, the Chaotic Magical Dragon`
and Halqifibrax with very little issue.
Couple that with the deck's easy access to Mills, and with Chaos Ruler
it made it a perfect engine for multiple decks.
It was seeing play in BASED as a way to start your various engines,
and in Adventure as a way to put `Water Enchantress of the Temple` and various other good cards,
like Snow, into the Graveyard for a quick start line.
They were also useful in combination with Therion,
as the number one issue of Therion at this stage was the fact that most of them required
Ethereon (heard as such) or Machine Engraved to start, which Chaos Ruler alleviated easily.
Branded and Tenyi Swordsoul continued to see major success in top cut representation,
with Floowandereeze also putting up good results.

The standout of the tournament was unquestionably Po Jiang, who won the event with Sky Striker,
of all things.
Po was on a card that would quickly become infamous over the next few months, being `Mystic Mine`,
which was incredibly powerful going into this particular meta.
In addition, he was main decking three `Demise of the Land` to be able to access Mine
on the opponent's turn in response to their Special Summons,
meaning it could shut down almost every major deck in the meta mid-combo.
Mystic Mine was also present in Justin Singh's top four Drytron list,
which seemed to be keen on turning out Mystic Mine and `Vanity's Ruler` at a rapid pace
to counter out the meta of the YCS.
These two tops were a catalyst of sorts, as moving forward Mine became a central talking point:
what needs to be added to decks to counter it out,
or whether the card was actually healthy for the game
in the first place.
This piece of discourse would only grow in the coming months with more results and sets releasing,
as people now realized Mine's power in the meta, and it wasn't going to just simply go away.

### Legendary Duelists: Duels From the Deep

- Release date: June 17th, 2022
- Set type: Legendary Duelists
- Major strategies: Marincess, Umi
- Impact: an overly expensive reprint

So, seeing how this particular set did, it's no surprise how
the Legendary Duelists sets ended up from here.
Duels From the Deep was an absolute bomb of a set,
and everyone knew it was going to be a bomb from the get-go,
which is probably why Konami decided to reprint `Forbidden Droplet` here.
As the surprise reprint, it actually gave some decent purpose to the set at first.
It was quickly realized that Droplet was short printed here very heavily,
marking its second short printed reprint in the last year,
with the previous reprint being in Brothers of Legend,
meaning that even then the set was not worth opening in the slightest.
Umi control and "morences" (not resolved) would see some experimentation on the YCS stage
but no long-term success at all, as both were already considered outclassed by the current meta,
though could still put up some interesting results in the short term.

#### North American WCQ (July 15th)

The North American WCQ was about a month later, on July 15th, 2022,
and this tournament shook up a good bit of the meta while reinforcing known points.
Punk decks had taken the position as the most represented deck in the top cut,
though this was split between the 11 Punk Adventure lists and the six Punk Therion lists,
with Punk Adventure representation being the highest single deck in the extremely diverse top cut
for this particular event.
The trend of the event was what most players had feared: Mystic Mine had taken over,
being in both finalists' main decks, and seeing the top four be almost built to target out
the Punk decks
that had rapidly risen.
Han Sol Aguiro would win the event with Swordsoul Tenyi,
notably main decking three Mystic Mine and three `Rivalry of Warlords` to counter out the meta,
as those cards specifically were able to target out Punk, Branded Despia and various other decks
between those two floodgates alone.
Ryan You would make second with Sky Striker,
dropping the previously popular Demise of the Land for more targeted removal-like offerings
of the doomed ("the doomed" is not resolved: probably a card or archetype name).
Pascal Monyat would make third with Floowandereeze,
utilizing a Simorgh engine to generate further Normal Summons for the Floowandereeze monsters,
and Elijah Green would shock many by finishing fourth with Marincess,
able to make `Marincess Aqua Argonaut` to control the game with her negation.

After the top cut showing here, Mystic Mine clearly had a target painted on its back,
and most players were not playing a main deck out to the card,
which would change over the next month,
as we began to see more and more players main decking a copy of `Harpie's Feather Duster`
or `Cosmic Cyclone` just to not lose to the Mine matchup.
With all of that in line, the meta would change before the next major tournament came around,
as arguably the most powerful single set of the year was just around the corner.

### Legendary Duelists: Season 3

- Release date: July 22nd, 2022
- Set type: reprint set
- Major strategies: Dark Magician, Predaplant, Evil HERO
- Impact: cheaper Souls

Yeah, you probably forgot this set came first too.
Season 3 literally was seen as a reprint ground for cards like `Magicians' Souls` and Predaplant,
the latter only being expensive because of a certain two big name players
bringing Predaplant brews to major events and spiking the price without even topping with it.
Regardless, the set would also highlight a massive issue with Yu-Gi-Oh's product yet again:
Magicians' Souls was short printed, marking yet another major set that was marred by short printing
the most valuable reprint.
This particular issue was starting to cause issues in the sales of these particular sets,
as a player only really wanted them for reprints and the rest of
the cards were not particularly relevant
in the meta, only to find out the reprints were short printed.
So what's the point of even opening the set?
This issue was becoming more and more prevalent as we got deeper into the year,
but the set bombing was the least of players' worries:
in the next two weeks the meta was going to be completely upended anyways,
and the set that followed would blow the current meta apart to make way for its new overlords.

### Power of the Elements

- Release date: August 5th, 2022
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Spright, Tearlaments, Ghoti
- Impact: set of the year

After seeing the results from the OCG, we all had to grit and prepare for the release of this set,
and it without question had an impact out the gate.
Spright and Tearlaments were the talk of the meta instantly.
Spright was able to go through various Level 2 lines and end on `Toadally Awesome`
and `Spright Elf`, who could revive the Toad after using its negate.
Tearlaments, which fused using monsters sent to the Graveyard by card effect,
made insanely powerful and varied boards, including cards like `Predaplant Dragostapelia`,
`Tearlaments Kaleido-Heart` and `Time Thief Redoer`, with the ability to trigger a Fusion
on the opponent's turn.
Spright would see meta play in its own pure variants, in combination with the Adventure engine,
and even some experimentation with engines like the Punk engine to grab their starting cards
with Chaos Ruler.
Tearlaments would see experimentation with practically every engine under the sun
that benefited from sending cards to the Graveyard, including Branded, Danger!,
and even its rival in Spright.
These two cores of cards would form the backbone of the new meta landscape,
though they were not the only decks present.

Exosister would finally find a meta worth playing in,
in addition to receiving `Exosister Martha`, who was a one-card Exosister Xyz to start plays
far more reliably than before, in addition to having a more Grave reliant meta at the time.
Flourishing Punk would grab two more pieces for their Pure variant,
being `P.U.N.K. JAM Extreme Session`, which gave the archetype a Draw 2 and a free summon,
and `P.U.N.K. JAM Dragon Drive`, who searched or dumped a Level 3 Psychic on summon
and could revive itself if the opponent responded to a Punk effect.
These would see experimentation in the rising Punk deck that had been popularized lately,
but wouldn't be as powerful as the other contenders.
Ghoti were a new TCG exclusive archetype of Fish monsters aimed at banishing themselves
for Synchro Summoning their boss monsters on the opponent's turn
to trigger their boss monsters' effects, such as `Ghoti of the Deep Beyond`,
who banishes the entire board if he is Synchro Summoned on the opponent's turn.
Though the archetype was not quite good enough with only half of their support.
Vernusylph were an archetype of Earth Fairies that discarded themselves and another monster
to perform an effect and Special Summon an Earth from the Graveyard,
which did nothing on release but were notable due to synergies
with an archetype released in the OCG by this point, which we'll get to soon.

Mathmech got an insane boost to their viability in `Mathmech Circular`,
who set up your Graveyard for Mathmech plays and searched for `Mathmech Superfactorial`
to make `Primathmech Laplacian` on the opponent's turn for prime interaction,
leading to a major boost in popularity for the archetype.
`Ultimate Slayer` was a new spell that let you remove specific Extra Deck threats without responding
by sending a monster of the same supertype from your Extra Deck to the Graveyard,
which saw play in some side decks as an out to specific meta threats,
such as the Toad Elf board and Fusions from Tearlaments.
`Garura, Wings of Resonant Life` was a TCG exclusive that, without exaggeration,
changed absolutely everything about the game compared to the OCG,
as it could be summoned with `Super Polymerization` using any two monsters
with the same Type and Attribute but different names.
This made almost every board breakable with Super Poly:
thanks to this and `Mudragon of the Swamp` both existing,
Super Poly turned overnight into one of the best tech choices in the game.
Lastly, `Morphtronic Telefon` had an FTK loop associated with it,
but its inclusion here was far more alarming than initially thought:
with the inclusion of all the Morphtronic support slated for the next Legendary Duelists set,
this indicated that the TCG would not be receiving that set at all,
rather that its pieces would be split and sold in other sets.
Normally this wouldn't be an issue, as most Legendary Duelists sets are subpar all around,
with maybe one or two standout cards, but this particular case was different.
In the OCG at this stage, a certain set of cards from this canceled set were terrorizing the format
without any sign of relief in sight,
and if we were receiving these in one of our upcoming sets then the meta would have to prepare,
as it would upend everything.

#### European Championship (August 19th to 20th)

Without question, though, Power of the Elements changed almost everything about the game,
ushering in a new meta overnight, and this was on full display just a few weeks later.
The European Championship happened from August 19th through 20th of 2022,
and all eyes turned toward it to see how the new set would completely upend the meta.
Unsurprisingly, Spright and Tearlaments took the majority of the top 128,
with 98 spots going to these archetypes alone.
Beyond that, though, what was absolutely shocking were the decks that made the top four.
Coming in fourth was Maddie Oguni on Live Twin Spright, which was the highest placing Spright list
of the tournament, as it turned out `Super Polymerization` for `Garura, Wings of Resonant Life`
completely punished suboptimal openings from Spright players,
which ended up being extremely popular with some players in the tournament.
Third was Lars Jungenir on Altergeist, which was main boarding a ton of hate
for the two big decks of the format in the form of `Lava Golem`, `PSY-Framegear Gamma`,
`Demise of the Land`, `Metaverse` and the boogeyman of the format, `Mystic Mine`.
Mine was extremely popular here following its breakout success in the previous YCS top cuts,
as it was poised to counter out Spright entirely
while also holding a great matchup against Tearlaments, allowing off-meta decks to shine.
And second was Zeo Mundry on Danger! Tearlaments,
which was quickly garnering the nickname of "Casino Tear",
as the deck had a significant number of random discards and Mills to start their plays off of,
thanks to the Tearlaments monsters and the Danger! monsters,
whose discard effects could similarly trigger the Tearlaments monster effects.
The standout card here was specifically curious (not resolved),
as he allowed a specific dump with his on summon effect to trigger whatever effect was still needed,
and this would be iterated on in future tournaments to take better advantage
of this particular effect.

Winning the entire event here and taking the world by surprise was Marcus Patel
with Rikka Sunavalon,
who used the newly released `Rikka Konkon` to Tribute
the opponent's monsters for his own Rikka effects
to control the board effectively.
While the deck was powerful, it heavily benefited from a surprise factor in this tournament,
as people had prepared so heavily for Tear and Spright that the Rikka matchup was an unknown factor,
which would be supported by the fact that this was one of the last tops Rikka Sunavalon
would see on a major event stage this year.
With these results in the bag, people began to shift their focus away from Spright,
as Tear had outperformed here and was clearly going to be the better deck in a few months
with further support on the way.
But that's not the end of this tale: another side set was just around the corner,
and while many had already written it off as not powerful,
it was going to shake up the meta significantly.

### Tactical Masters

- Release date: August 26th, 2022
- Set type: deck building set
- Major strategies: Vaylantz (heard as "valence"), Labrynth, Runick
- Impact: "Wait, you're Level 2?"

On the initial release, Tactical Masters was written off by most of the community,
as the major archetypes of the set had no success in the OCG.
However, this would rapidly change in the TCG with Runick,
a control archetype focused on vanishing cards from the top of the opponent's Deck
by sacrificing their Battle Phases, aimed at making the opponent deck out.
Two factors would change how this particular archetype was received in the TCG compared to the OCG.
The first and foremost of these being `Mystic Mine`:
in the OCG, Mystic Mine had been banned for some time,
but in the TCG one of the best stalling and control options in
the game was still available at three copies,
meaning that Runick, a deck entirely about controlling the game date to Mill the opponent out,
heavily benefited from this, seeing a rise in popularity of Runick Mine decks.
The second was the fact that the best Fusion of the deck, `Hugin the Runick Wings`, was a Level 2.
This little detail meant that every Quick-Play Runick spell could be used
as a starting spell for Spright,
forming the deck Runick Spright off of this interaction,
which would quickly become the best Spright variant in the format.
As for the other two archetypes, Labrynth was a trap deck that played heavily into a control
standpoint
but was just too slow to be a meta threat compared to the big decks at the time.
Vaylantz, on the other hand, were just lacking about everything needed to be metafiable,
not seeing a lick of competitive success compared to its contemporaries.

#### YCS Rio, Oceanic Championship and YCS Niagara

The true power of Runick Spright would be seen soon enough.
YCS Rio took place the same weekend as the Tactical Masters release,
and as such the meta would still be fairly tuned to Power of the Elements specifically,
as many had not yet tested the new Tactical Masters cards.
Interestingly enough, Tearlaments had completely overtaken Spright in terms
of top cut representation,
and as variants like Danger! Tear and Punk Tear were putting up stronger results than Spright
overall.
A little more surprising was the result from Mathmech,
as the addition of `Mathmech Circular` to the deck had essentially solved all
of its consistency issues,
seeing about half play it pure, while the other half played it in combination with adding Nabla
(`Mathmech Nabla`) for the extra power boost from access to `The Arrival Cyberse @Ignister`,
which most decks had no out to.
While Mathmech would perform well here, taking second place piloted by Marcus Lorenco,
the true success story was Xiao Pulau Lima winning the entire event with Mystic Mine Burn,
which notably played absolutely no main deck monsters,
instead looking to lock the opponent up with cards like `Ojama Trio` and `Ojama Duo`
and old floodgates like `Gozen Match` and `Rivalry of Warlords`.
This particular win sparked outrage in the community, clamoring for Mystic Mine's ban,
as it had been a major piece of now three event winning decks in the span of a couple of months.
This even sparked what was probably the most memed Yu-Gi-Oh tweet of the year, from Jesse Cotton,
where he went on to criticize how players were still not preparing for Mystic Mine,
even though it had now won three major events.

The Oceanic Championship would follow just a week later, with Tearlaments taking the entire top four
of the championship, seeing Dinos Pasovsky win the entire thing with a mostly pure Tearlaments list,
opting to play more hand traps like `D.D. Crow`, `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring` and
`Nibiru, the Primal Being` than supplementary engine pieces.
Although second and third place were both on Danger! variants,
what caught most people's attention was Po Jiang and Jonah Singer's top 16 finish
with Runick Spright,
being on the exact same Main and Extra Deck,
showing the synergy the two cores had together in a new way that hadn't been talked about yet.
Lastly, in the span of tournaments, YCS Niagara happened a week later
and would once again see an influx of Spright in the top cut,
once again taking over Tear's position.
However, once again Spright would fail to clear into top four,
where second through fourth place was once again claimed by Tearlaments variants,
with Exosister winning the entire event, piloted by Raymond Dye.
Raymond would defeat Jesse Cotton in the finals after dropping a `Dimension Shifter` on him,
leading to Jesse's previous tweet getting memed into the ground by the community.
This was one of the only big finishes Exosister had at this point in time,
finally showing how the deck had a good matchup in the current meta,
but its overall power ceiling was still below that of Tear, which continued to see major success,
and its consistency was way less than that of Spright,
whose list was beginning to really shift into the Runick variant at this stage.
This long string of event after event would finally be broken in mid-September
as the next major reprint set was finally given to us to help bring down the price of the meta.

### 2022 Tin of the Pharaoh's Gods

- Release date: September 16th, 2022
- Set type: Mega-Tins
- Major strategies: almost everything from the 2021 core sets
- Impact: lowering the barrier to entry

The 2022 Mega-Tins were released to us in mid-September,
and with them came a major lowering of the cost of the meta.
While major decks like Spright and Tear were still extremely new,
major staples of the format had been out for some time now,
and these tins brought some much needed reprints to bring their prices down,
including `Pot of Prosperity`, `Forbidden Droplet`, `Lightning Storm`, `Diviner of the Herald`,
various hand traps and the Branded Despia core.
In addition, the tins also brought a couple of new cards to support the Egyptian God cards,
most notably `The Revived Sky God`, and also a couple of other odd support pieces
like `Link Devotee` and `Rainbow Bridge of Salvation`.
Salvation would actually see some experimentation in Tearlaments builds for a time,
as its search was for any Field Spell and a Crystal Beast monster,
being able to search for `Primeval Planet Perlereino` and `Crystal Beast Emerald Tortoise`,
which was an Aqua for your Tear Fusions.
Link Devotee, on the other hand, was seen as useful almost exclusively for Magical Musketeers,
who would go on to see rogue level play from this one card's release.
Beyond these, nothing would really change meta wise from this release,
just make the game a little bit more affordable to newer players,
which could also be said for the release that happened two weeks later.

### Structure Deck: Legend of the Crystal Beasts

- Release date: September 30th, 2022
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Crystal Beasts
- Impact: double down reprints

While this deck sure did release, Legend of the Crystal Beasts was set to be an interesting
structure deck for the TCG solely because it was reprinting `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring`
and `Dimension Shifter`, but that impact was taken away from it slightly due to both seeing reprints
in the Mega-Tins just two weeks prior.
Beyond that there's nothing of note from this particular release,
outside of the fact that it was a precursor to
the long-awaited ban list update that followed just three days later.

#### Ban list (October 3rd)

The TCG ban list was officially updated on October 3rd, 2022,
after calls for updates for months from the community.
Newly banned were `Fairy Tail - Snow`, `Ronintoadin`, `Crystron Halqifibrax`,
`Chaos Ruler, the Chaotic Magical Dragon` and `Red Reboot`,
with Snow being a Tear and Branded hit, Ronin Toadin being clearly meant for Spright,
Halqifibrax being targeted at the Punk engine,
and Red Reboot just being a generally good hit to help Trap decks in the meta.
Newly limited were `Blackwing - Steam the Cloak`, `Cyber Jar`, "Ohlion" (not resolved),
`Fusion Substitute`, `Spellbook of Judgment` and `Appointer of the Red Lotus`.
"Steam the Cloak" and "Ohlion" were originally banned because of Halqifibrax,
Cyber Jar and Judgment's time as meta threats had passed,
Substitute could be released if Ronin Toadin was banned,
and Appointer was one of the most annoying cards to face when playing into Spright,
which was the main abusing deck at this point.
Completely released from this list this go-around were `Dinomight Knight, the True Dracofighter`,
as True Draco was past its prime, `Cyber Angel Benten` to help boost Drytron,
`Shooting Riser Dragon`, as it shouldn't have been limited in the first place,
`Fusion Destiny`, as Verte was now banned, `Nadir Servant`, as Dogmatika had left the meta,
`Trickstar Light Stage`, as Trickstar had left the meta,
and `Wall of Revealing Light`, as the cards it had enabled, like `Last Turn`
and `Self-Destruct Button`, were banned.

The big takeaway from this list, though, was that Tearlaments was poised to be the best deck
in the meta by a wide margin.
The banning of Ronin Toadin made the level of commitment needed to make `Toadally Awesome`
and Spright far greater, as you either needed to summon two `Swap Frog`
or play a variant like Live Twin or Paleozoic.
Meanwhile, the only major hit to Tear was the banning of Snow,
which had been a problem card in other decks like Branded Despia before,
so it's not like it was a direct hit to Tear.
While the pure Spright variant absolutely took a big hit here,
notably Runick Spright seemed to be the way
to move forward with the deck into the new climate,
as a heavy control plan versus the resource game of Tear seemed to be extremely powerful going into
YCS Utrecht (heard as "ycsu direct", not resolved).

#### YCS Utrecht

Two weeks later, YCS Utrecht was a big stage for the format following the October ban list,
as it would be the one big event before Darkwing Blast a week later.
The top cut for this event showed the world that Runick Spright was absolutely the best way to play
the deck after the loss of Ronin, and this was further backed up by a mirror match finals
between Runick Spright players Joshua Schmidt and Den Caboy,
with Joshua taking the win for his third YCS win.
Both players were on similar lists, playing new inclusions to the deck like "a PIRA" (not resolved)
for a free draw, `Capshell` for a free draw and `Wind-Up Kitten` for a bounce
to hand off Gigantic Spright.
Joshua had an interesting inclusion in this deck, `Number 29: Mannequin Cat`,
who let him summon specific threats to shut down matchups directly from his Deck,
which he exited in like "tetsudo irat numen" (not resolved) against Tearlaments
and `Thunder King Rai-Oh` against Spright.
In addition to this showing, both players beat Live Twin Spright lists to make it into the finals,
showing the variant's dominant nature.
This event set the stage for Runick Spright to be the big deck to beat in this format,
but if that would hold was yet to be seen, as the next core set was only a week away,
and it brought with it cards that would upend the meta as we knew it.

### Darkwing Blast

- Release date: October 21st, 2022
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Blackwing, Bystial, Kashtira, Ghoti
- Impact: `D.D. Crow`'s big brothers

Darkwing Blast was an interesting core set, to say the least,
and it absolutely shook up the meta in multiple ways,
primarily through the releases of Bystial and Kashtira.
Bystial was a set of Dark and Light Dragons that mostly shared the effect
of Special Summoning themselves
from the hand by banishing a Light or Dark monster from either player's Graveyard,
which became a Quick Effect if the opponent controlled the monster.
Each had their own unique effects too:
`Bystial Magnamhut` triggering on summon to search a Dragon in the End Phase,
`Bystial Druiswurm` drew a card (heard as "drew a swarm"),
sent a Special Summoned monster to the Graveyard when sent from field to Graveyard,
and `Bystial Baldrake` dumped the Branded Spell/Trap from Deck when sent to the Graveyard.
The outlier of the archetype was `The Bystial Lubellion`, who lacked the summon effect,
could be discarded to search for any other Bystial monster,
could summon itself from hand or Graveyard by Tributing a Level 6 or higher Dragon,
and could activate a Branded continuous Spell/Trap from Deck once per turn.
These would immediately see play in practically every deck in the format, to varying ratios,
with smaller ratios of Magnamhut and Druiswurm in most decks,
and heavier ratios including Baldrake and Lubellion in Branded decks like Branded Despia,
as they had the ability to banish Darks from the opponent's Graveyard in response to effects,
so they specifically could be used to counter out Tearlaments monsters using their Fusion Summon
effects.

Kashtira was the other major archetype introduced here,
being a series of Level 7 monsters that could Special Summon themselves if you controlled no
monsters,
each having an effect to search a Kashtira card and another effect that banished a card from
the opponent
face-down when attacking or when the opponent uses a monster effect:
`Kashtira Fenrir` searching a monster and banishing a face-up card on the field face-down,
`Kashtira Unicorn` searching a Spell and banishing from the opponent's Extra Deck,
and `Kashtira Ogre` searching a Trap and banishing a card from the top five of their Deck.
Of these, Fenrir would immediately jump out to people as being comparable
to `Dinowrestler Pankratops`,
as it could summon itself and remove a threat by battle and by its effect,
with Fenrir also being able to search for an additional copy of itself,
as its search effect did not exclude itself.
Fenrir would go from here to see splash play in various decks,
also breaking the record for the most expensive core set Ultra Rare on opening weekend,
breaking seventy dollars a copy at its height.

In addition to these, Spright would gain a new Link monster in `Spright Sprind`,
who could send a Level 2 from Deck to Graveyard on summon
and could detach a material from an Xyz you controlled when a monster was Special Summoned
to bounce a monster on the field to hand.
This would instantly find a slot in Spright decks to send a copy of `Nimble Angler` from Deck
to Graveyard,
which in turn could summon two `Nimble Beaver` with its effect,
essentially turning two Level 2s into three monsters,
as you could then use Sprind to Overlay with a Beaver to make `Gigantic Spright`.
In addition to this, it would also be slotted into Tearlaments,
as its ability could send a `Tearlaments Merrli` from Deck to Graveyard
to trigger her Fusion effect when needed.
Spright would also gain `Spright Double Cross`, a Trap that let them steal an opponent's monster
and either summon it to a zone a Link-2 points to or attach it as a material to a Rank 2 Xyz.
Tearlaments would get a new Fusion in `Tearlaments Rulkallos`,
which could negate effects that Special Summoned
and revived herself from Graveyard when sent by a card effect,
giving Tearlaments an option to prevent Nibiru.
`Tearlaments Heartbeat` operated as a searchable Mystic Mine out for the deck,
and `Tearlaments Scream` operated as an additional Mill 3 and a Trap search when sent to
the Graveyard.
`Spellbound` operated as a weaker version
of `Dimensional Barrier` that could also be used against Link monsters,
while also being a blanket toward all summoning mechanics,
but only using monsters already on the field.

Naturia would find minor success with `Naturia Camellia` and `Naturia Mole Cricket`
when used in combination with the Vernusylph cards, which also gained new pieces,
`Vernusylph of the Misting Seedlings`, which searched an Earth Fairy with its effect,
and `Vera the Vernusylph Goddess`, who was the boss monster of the archetype,
able to steal an opponent's monster and make it Earth on your turn,
revive an Earth on the opponent's turn, and the Gator monster effect on resolution
(heard as such, not resolved) if you have five or more Earth monsters,
not going on to the chain to do so.
Ghoti received further support but would still be missing that last enabling piece to be metafiable.
Lastly, `Destructive Daruma Karma Cannon` was a new TCG exclusive Trap
that flipped the board face-down and then had each player send monsters that are still face-up to
the Graveyard,
being one of the few ways to remove cards unaffected by card effects,
as it forces the player to do it, not Karma Cannon itself.
With all of these new tools released into the meta, there was sure to be an impact on the YCS
happening the same weekend.

#### YCS Minneapolis

YCS Minneapolis took place the same weekend as the Darkwing Blast release,
and as such the new releases would directly impact the top cut of this event.
The split between Spright and Tearlaments was clearly moving to favor Tearlaments more,
even with the release of the new Bystial cards.
Bystial would be more readily slotted into Tearlaments builds,
as the deck could always restock their milled names with their Fusion effects,
and they helped counter out the other Tear variants.
Spright had seen a pivot back to being pure focused,
thanks to Sprind's interactions with the Nimble monsters
being worthwhile for field presence.
The top four of this event would see no repeat deck types,
seeing some good variation in what was topping:
Spright Tear, Floowandereeze, Danger! Tear and Runick Spright all taking a top spot each.
Chris LeBlanc would win the event with Spright Tear,
taking strong advantage of the newly released Sprind to access both Spright and Tear plays,
in addition to enabling Extra Deck locks with `Knightmare Corruptor Iblee`,
by dumping her with Sprind and then reviving her with `Spright Elf`.
Michael Thomas would claim second with Floowandereeze,
being one of the best performances the deck had in the year,
seeing the growing power of both `Dimension Shifter` and `Barrier Statue of the Stormwinds`
in the current meta, in addition to the generally powerful nature of Mystic Mine in the format
as it stood.
The meta seemed decently balanced out, with the various deck types vying for top cut spots,
and nothing could possibly ruin that, right?
Right?

### Magnificent Mavens

- Release date: November 4th, 2022
- Set type: import reprint
- Major strategies: Sky Striker, Witchcrafter, Mayakashi, Ishizu
- Impact: Milling for days

Oh, Mavens, the set that 100 percent put the nail in the coffin for the Legendary Duelists packs.
At this stage the Legendary Duelists sets that had come before, being focused on three archetypes
used by characters from the anime, had been split into various OCG import cards,
with Morphtronic being placed in Power of the Elements,
the Amazoness cards being split between Darkwing Blast and the upcoming Crystal Revenge,
and, most notably, the Ishizu cards being placed into this set.
In addition to this, Mavens would import a couple of the new support pieces from
the OCG's holiday set from 2021,
but all eyes were placed firmly on the Ishizu cards.
The monsters split themselves into two sets:
one that milled five from both players, being `Agido the Ancient Sentinel`
and `Kelbek the Ancient Vanguard`,
and one that could return three to five cards from Graveyard back to Deck,
being `Mudora the Sword Oracle` and `Keldo the Sacred Protector`.
These monsters form the backbone of what became known as the Ishizu engine,
which aimed to quickly Mill cards and control the Graveyard with the return effects.
The reason this specifically was so powerful was its clear synergies with Tearlaments,
who heavily benefited from the additional milling effect,
creating the deck known as Ishizu Tearlaments.
Immediately many were extremely concerned about this,
as in the OCG, Ishizu Tear had already taken a tier zero position,
completely icing out Spright from the meta,
and many were worried that the same was about to happen here in the TCG.

#### YCS Pasadena

YCS Pasadena would take place the same weekend as Mavens' release,
and the results would speak for how much these Ishizu cards had already changed the meta,
with 26 of the 32 going to Ishizu Tearlaments alone.
We had officially entered a tier zero metagame, which had already heavily split the player base.
On the one hand, the format was clearly extremely skill expressive,
as a good majority of the top 32 were big names in the game that could use the format
as a strong form of skill expression.
On the other hand, chain links and non-linear lines were so rampant
that many watching the games on stream, including the commentators,
were finding it difficult to keep up
with what effect was happening,
with many community members joking about a specific moment in top 32
where Pack and Hani reached chain link 8 in their interaction,
having so much of their deck laid out on the table that it looked like a deck profile.
Hani would go on from that match to win the entire event with Ishizu Tearlaments,
defeating Jesse Cotton in the finals in an intense mirror match
where Hani would resolve `Abyss Dweller`'s effect four times in game three to lock it up.

Aside from Ishizu Tearlaments being the top deck, the other big takeaway from the event
was the power of the Bystial once again,
as every player in the top four was playing a Bystial package of some size in their main deck
to help counter out Tear.
Fourth place Dakota Angelov took it a step further by playing two copies of `Mask Change II`
to access `Masked HERO Dark Law` while going first, to auto-win the mirror match.
The only non-Tearlaments deck in top four was Vincent Wong's Spright,
which was main decking a massive eight Bystial cards, as well as `Skull Meister`
and `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring`, to counter out Tearlaments at every angle.
YCS Pasadena had shaken the metagame to its core and ushered in a new tier zero format,
being the game's first since 2017 with SPYRAL,
and there was no sign of that changing with Dortmund directly after.

#### YCS Dortmund

YCS Dortmund was the following weekend, on November 12th, 2022,
and similarly to Pasadena was completely dominated by Ishizu Tearlaments,
with 71 of the top 64 (as heard) being the new tier zero deck.
Floowandereeze would have a stronger representation in the top cut this time,
in addition to multiple other decks making the cut,
but Tear was still strangle-holding the majority of the top cut,
keeping over the percentage needed to be considered tier zero.
The finals was not a mirror match, at least, being Vincenzo Orofino on Tear against Mario Aguiro
on Floowandereeze, with Vincenzo winning the day and Tearlaments taking yet another event.
The big talking point of the top cut, however,
was the sudden appearance of an archetype from the past,
being Madolche.
The deck had received some serious boost in viability thanks to the Vernusylph cards,
which enabled summoning with Dolce monsters and could even search for `Madolche Anjelly`
and `Madolche Petingcessoeur` with the effect of `Vernusylph of the Misting Seedlings`,
which, when combined with the Ishizu cards and Bystial, gave them a strong matchup
specifically against Tearlaments.
With the YCS in the books, many turned to the next set release,
the final pack of the year, to shake anything up in the meta,
but judging by the set list that was unlikely.

### Battles of Legend: Crystal Revenge

- Release date: November 18th, 2022
- Set type: import reprint
- Major strategies: Crystal Beasts, Doodle Beasts, G Golem
- Impact: Edison reprints

Unsurprisingly, this set's new cards were almost immediately discarded as worthless.
Crystal Beasts would get seven new names to the archetype, but only maybe one or two were playable.
G Golem was dead on arrival, and Doodle Beasts, while actually pretty decent for Dinosaurs,
was supporting a deck that hadn't been relevant in quite some time.
The set was mainly looked at for reprints of cards that had gotten unnecessarily expensive as
of late,
cards like `Accesscode Talker`, `Selene, Queen of the Master Magicians`, `Borreload Savage Dragon`
and `Number F0: Utopic Draco Future`,
but also went out of its way to reprint cards for either the collector's market or the Edison format
that had gotten needlessly expensive, like `Toon Black Luster Soldier`, `D.D. Crow`,
`Revived King Ha Des` and "Blackwing value" (not resolved).
Meta impact wise, the set had none,
which is apropos for the YCS San Jose directly after.

#### YCS San Jose

Even though this event just happened, we have very little coverage of the event itself.
Unsurprisingly, the event was won again by Ishizu Tearlaments,
but, as finally fulfilling a prophecy, the winning pilot was none other than Jesse Cotton,
who had come in second in two different YCSs earlier this year.
The only confirmed breakdown we have is of the top eight,
of which five players were on Ishizu Tear, two were on Spright and one was on Floowandereeze,
although looking over what confirmed decks were in
the top 32 shows just how dominant Ishizu Tear was
compared to its competition.

#### Ban list (December 1st)

With all of these results set in stone, a new ban list was just around the corner
and was set to shake up the meta once again, just not in the way many were expecting.
The TCG ban list officially updated on December 1st, 2022,
and this update is probably one of the most odd ban lists we've received in recent years.
With Ishizu Tear clearly being the best deck, many expected to see a direct hit to the deck,
but instead we saw mainly buffs to its main counter, Floowandereeze, too.
Banned were `Mystic Mine` and `Curious, the Lightsworn Dominion`,
which were the most hated card in the format and a combo piece that had long since fallen out
of favor, respectively.
Limited was `Herald of Orange Light`, taking away Ishizu Tear's main out to Dimension Shifter.
Semi-limited was `Lyrilusc - Recital Starling`, giving Bird Up its main combo enabler back.
Newly unlimited were `Tellarknight Ptolemaeus` (heard as "patola mayus"), `Fire Formation - Tenki`,
`Dimensional Fissure`, `Metaverse` and `Macro Cosmos`,
which were undoubtedly the biggest portion of the list.
Ptolemaeus started getting tossed around as an answer to Tear through the use of
`Stellarknight Constellar Diamond`, but many neglected to realize you could only make Diamond
from turn two onward, due to its restriction of a Main Phase 2 Summon,
making it more niche than initially expected.
Tenki was an inconsequential removal, as it was already at two before this,
but the three main talking points of the list were Fissure, Metaverse and Macro,
all three of which were essentially either floodgates or floodgate enablers,
and each one had a direct counter to Tearlaments built into them.
While we have not yet had an in-person event to test this out,
all eyes have turned to the Remote Duel YCS at the end of the year
to be the testing grounds for these new changes.
With that in mind, there was one more release this year that added cards to the pool,
though their meta impact has yet to be seen.

### Structure Deck: Dark World

- Release date: December 2nd, 2022
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Dark World
- Impact: Danger! reprints

Ending the year with a bit of a dud, the Dark World structure deck is, for the most part,
not meta relevant and changed nothing overall.
While the new Dark World cards are great for their archetype and do bring the deck up
in metafiability,
it just has an awful matchup into both Tearlaments and Floowandereeze,
which are currently dominating forces in the meta.
The main draw to the structure deck is more so the reprints of the Danger! monsters,
who've been steadily rising over the year with decks like Casino Tear getting big,
although they're not currently viable in the meta,
which brings us to the end of 2022.
