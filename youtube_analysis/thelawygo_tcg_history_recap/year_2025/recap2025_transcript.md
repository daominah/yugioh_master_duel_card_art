Diego Zilia would take the event on Odion Primite a true underdog run to be certain.
The deck was an insane metacall for specifically Mitsurugi and Dracotail,
both of which were in the top three decks represented with a sideboard targeted at swapping out hate
for the yummy matchup to handle the last top deck.
By concentrating your game plan into two self-sufficient seven card engines,
the deck could then opt to absolutely max out on hand traps.
Seeing some less common options like `Ghost Belle & Haunted Mansion`
and `PSY-Framegear Delta` in the main for targeting its specific matchups.

#### YCS Anaheim

Kamal Krooks Valdez would take the event on Crystron K9, marking his sixth YCS win.
Crystron, despite the hits,
was still a solid deck to pilot with the K9's thanks in large part
to `K9-04 Noroi` allowing instant access to set up for your rank fives.

But this particular deck was also marred by a very specific interaction.
By linking into `Artifact Dagda` during your Crystron lines,
you could set `Artifact Mjollnir` to your board
and pop it with one of your various Crystron effects.
From there, with access to a rank five,
you can make `Imperial Princess Quinquery` to then revive the `Artifact Mjollnir`
to your opponent's side of the field,
mandatorily triggering its effect despite the opponent having no targets
and locking all special summons until the end of their next turn.
Effectively, similar to the gimmick puppet lock that Branded used to pull,
this card was inherently toxic now that the line was openly available
and would become yet another piece in the growing arsenal of summon lockers available at relatively
low investment points for the format.

### The Chronicles Deck: The Fallen & The Virtuous

- Release date: October 24th, 2025.
- Set type: Chronicles deck.
- Major strategies: Branded.
- Impact: a new search target for Albaz.

The Fallen and the Virtuous would be the first in a new line of pre-constructed decks that aimed
to reprint a popular series of cards with a couple of new additions, all in ultra rare,
all for the purpose of advertising Konami's new short form anime series known as Yu-Gi-Oh!
Card Game: The Chronicles.
We had previously received some cards meant
to tie into this in the form of random pieces of Sky Striker support to advertise the first arc,
but with this set, it seemed that Konami aimed to isolate the new cards
to these decks exclusively from now on.
For the inaugural set, Branded was selected as the archetype of choice,
namely thanks to being the second arc chosen for the series,
notably with Spirit Charmers
and Majestice following with one of those already confirmed
to have a Chronicles deck coming in the next year.
In this product, Branded would receive two new cards.
`The Fallen & The Virtuous` is is always considered both a branded and Dogmatika card
and can either dump an Albaz fusion from extra
to pop a card or if you have `Dogmatika Ecclesia, the Virtuous` on the field or engrave can revive a
monster from either graveyard.

`The Dragon that Devours the Dogma` is a fusion of Albaz,
a light or dark monster and an effect monster,
gains 500 attack and is card effect immune while `Dogmatika Ecclesia, the Virtuous` is on fielder
and grave.
Shuffles back two cards from any grave or banishment on summon
and searches a Dogmatika or tri brigade card in the end phase it sent to grave.
So, `The Dragon that Devours the Dogma` was simply not going
to cut it for the current version of the branded deck due
to the lack of `Dogmatika Ecclesia, the Virtuous` in their lineups.
But the Fallen and the Virtuous was almost exactly what the deck needed at this point,
namely in terms of the Dracotail variant.
By giving a card that was effectively `Dogmatika Punishment` without the extra deck lock that you
could set off Albaz on the end of your opening turn,
you added yet another interaction point to the board that could potentially grab another later,
which was notably desirable.

#### Emergency ban list of October 27th

What was not expected was a new ban list that went into effect on October 27th,
only just over a month after the previous one,
being marked accurately by the community as an emergency ban list.
The first we've seen since 2016 or 18 depending on who you ask.
Newly banned were `Archnemeses Protos`, `Artifact Mjollnir`, `Barrier Statue of the Inferno`,
`Fossil Dyna Pachycephalo`, `Evilswarm Ouroboros`, and `Dimensional Barrier`.
Every single one of these cards had a common factor between them.
They promoted extremely unfun game states.
`Archnemeses Protos`, `Artifact Mjollnir`, Drought, Inferno,
and Patches were all floodgates that were easily accessible via extra deck monsters with
`Archnemeses Protos` and Inferno being found by `Infernal Flame Banshee`,
Drought and Pachephilo being found by `Gallant Granite`,
and `Artifact Mjollnir` being found by a combination of `Artifact Dagda` and King Carry.

`Evilswarm Ouroboros` was banned due
to the consistency of getting its hand ripoff multiple times in a rank four deck thanks
to `Eclipse Twins`.
Lastly, `Dimensional Barrier` was effectively an auto-in button against so many decks in the format
that was also searchable with `Triple Tactics Thrust`.
This ban list was universally praised by the community with even some going as far as
to say that Konami should aim to do these kind of lists more often
to start pre-boxing out the unfun interactions
and floodgates from the format before they become issues in the future.

### Phantom Revenge

- Release dates: December 5th, 2025, and December 19th, 2025 (the Americas).
- Set type: deck building set.
- Major strategies: Hecahands, Enneacraft, Kewl Tune.
- Impact: a resounding thud to end the year.

Phantom Revenge would be the last set of the year,
once again seeing a two-week delay for its release in the Americas compared
to its overseas counterparts,
though notably did not affect any YCS level events for the period in question.
After the insane level of success that followed the release of Justice Hunters,
many looked to the set to be another breath of fresh air into the format.
Instead, all three of the archetype present here were considerably halfbaked.
Overall, though did hold potential based on support and upcoming releases.
All of the Hecahands monsters have the illusion gimmick.
`Hecahands Ibtel` can shuffle herself from hand into deck to summon a different Hecahands
and could revive a different Hecahands when sent to grave.

`Hecahands Yadel` can shuffle herself from hand into deck to search a Hecahands spell trap
and can reset a spell trap from grave when sent to grave.
`Hecahands Godos` can summon itself from hand when the opponent adds a card
to hand outside of the draw phase
and can revive a monster from the opponent's grave on a quick effect in their main phase.
`Hecahands Gaigas` shares the same summon condition
and can excavate three from the opponent's deck to summon a monster from it to your field.
`Hecahands Breus` shares the same summon condition
and can look at a random card in the opponent's hand to summon it to your field if it's a monster.
`The Hidden Hecahands` searches a Hecahands monster.
Can also send a card you control to grave to add a spell trap set from deck to that effect.

And can banish itself from grave to let the opponent draw and discard one.
`Hecahands Tartaros` either bounces an opponent's spell trap or fuses a Hecahands by banishing
monsters from field or grave, treating the opponent's stolen monsters as Hecahands monsters.
`Ib'al-Hecahands` negates and destroys a monster effect, then summons it to your field.
`Yad'al-Hecahands` does the same for spell traps, setting it to your field.
`Hecahands Jauzah` is a fusion of a Hecahands and an illusion.
Can also summon itself by tributing an illusion and a monster owned by the opponent on your field.

And searches or recurs a Hecahands once per turn.
And `Hecahands Xeno` is a fusion of three Hecahands.
Can quick effect look at two random extra deck monsters from the opponent
and summon one to your field.
And if destroyed by the opponent's card effect, steals every monster the opponent controls.
A strategy with a solid grind game that can put up true board annoyances in large part thanks
to yada giving you access to your main lines easily.
Generally able to set up a minimum of a `Hecahands Gaigas`
and a counter trap with the extended ability to the other counter
and `Hecahands Jauzah` if `Hecahands Gaigas` hits its excavation.

Also able to start directly from `Hecahands Ibtel` though loses the first counter trap if you do.
The issue comes up in what happens if `Hecahands Gaigas` misses.
Without a `Hecahands Gaigas` hit, the deck flounders to get going at first,
leaving you with maybe one point of interaction before the opponent gets a crack back.
The long and short is that with this deck,
you need to see multiple pieces
to get a combo truly going without risking it all on a one out of three hit.
And at this point in time, it was just too unreasonable of an ask for a strategy to make.
All of the Enneacraft monsters share an effect to reveal themselves in hand
to special summon a monster from hand face down,
locking you to only special summoning face down monsters for the turn
and share a pendulum effect to gain an EC counter when a monster is flipped up.

The low-level Enneacraft in the pendulum zone can pay 900
to reveal three Enneacraft cards from deck to randomly add one to hand
and all have monster effects that flip themselves face up when the opponent performs an action
to search an Enneacraft card.
`Proto Enneacraft - "orgIA"` flips when the opponent uses a monster effect in hand,
popping a monster the opponent controls on flip.
`Deftero Enneacraft - "alazoneIA"` flips when the opponent uses a card that adds a card from deck
to hand, banishing a random card from the opponent's hand face up for the turn when flipped.
`Ekto Enneacraft - "tromarIA"` flips when the opponent uses a card or effect to special summon,
negating an opponent's monster's effect on flip.
And `Enato Enneacraft - "oknirIA"` flips when the opponent uses an effect in grave or banishment,
shuffling back up to three opponents cards from graver banishment on flip.
All of the higher level Enneacraft share a pendulum effect
to pop a monster with less attack than themselves at the end of the battle phase if you have another
any scale and share a monster effect to flip themselves up in response to an opponent's action
to perform a disruption effect.

`Enneacraft - Aiza.LEON` flips when the opponent chains to your effect,
bouncing up to three cards on field
and burns the opponent for 900 anytime they add a card to hand by effect after it's flipped up.
`Enneacraft - Asta.PIXEA` flips when the opponent targets one of your cards with a card effect,
negating it and banishing a random card from their hand face down,
and locks the opponent from targeting cards on field or engrave after it's flipped up.
`Enneacraft - Atori.MAR` flips when the opponent activates a card or effect on field,
negating and destroying it, and books the entire opponent's board when flipped,
locking them from changing battle positions.
`Enneacraft - Archa.TAIL` flips when the opponent uses a card that would destroy cards,
giving your monsters and spell traps destruction immunity for the turn
and burns the opponent for 900 anytime a monster is sent
to the opponent's grave after it's been flipped up.
`Enneapolis` is always considered an Enneacraft.
Removes all EC counters from the board in the end phase to burn the opponent for 900 for each.

Can bounce any number of Enneacraft pendulums once per turn
and can either bounce or move the scale in Enneacraft monster after it's flipped face up during the
main phase.
`Enneacraft Release` places the Enneacraft in the pen scale from deck
and can banish itself from grave to recur an Enneacraft in the face up extra deck.
And `Enneacraft Reverth` shuffles back any number of Enneacraft from hand or field to deck
to draw that many cards
and can banish itself from grave when the opponent summons a monster
to either summon an Enneacraft from hand face down or flip a set any on the board.
By far and away the most complicated of the three strategies,
any aims to create a new axis of control strategy that a flip archetype has never done before
and as such has a lot of interesting points going with its strategy.
Because of the nature of their reveal and hand effects being repeatable,
you can effectively set every In Enneacraft in the hand off of one card,
leaving your normal set for the last to set one of the low levels
to give a board of mystery interaction points for the opponent to have to chew through.
On top of that, the field spell adding a substantial burn to each of the flips adds a clock
to the strategy that promotes the idea of playing a slower control game as nine triggers of the burn
results in a burn for gain.

The problems for the deck lie mostly in their effects not always resolving the way you want them
to and the bricky nature of the strategy itself as unless you're playing three copies of a card,
you have no way to reliably access any of them,
and you can't very well max out on everything without entering Brick City.
Still a strategy to keep an eye on though, as it does show promise in its complexity.
All the main deck Kewl Tune are tuners that share an effect
to use one other tuner in hand with it as synchro material while on the field
and have an effect that triggers when sent a grave is synchro material.
`Kewl Tune Mix` searches or recurs a non-level two Kewl Tune on summon
and pops an opponent's monster when used as material.
`Kewl Tune Clip` can summon itself in the opponent's main phase
to quick synchro a tuner synchro monster
and banishes a random card from the opponent's extra deck face up when used as material.
`Kewl Tune Reco` searches or recurs a non-level three Kewl Tune on summon
and pops a spell trap when used as material
and `Kewl Tune Cue` can summon any tuner from hand deck or grave on normal locks you
to summoning tuners for the rest of the turn
and excavates two from the opponent's deck banishing one of them
and puts the other back on top or bottom of the deck when used as material.

`Kewl Tune Synchro` searches a Kewl Tune and can quick synchro in the same effect,
locking you to tuners for the rest of the turn, being a hard twice per turn spell.
`JJ "Kewl Tune"` lets you normal an extra tuner each turn,
boost loudness war by 3,300 while the opponent has a tuner on field or engrave,
and contribute a tuner to search or special a Kewl Tune from deck,
locking you to tuners for the rest of the turn.
And `Kewl Tune Playlist` either recurs a Kewl Tune from field or grave or triggers that monster's
material effect being a hard twice per turn trap.
`Kewl Tune Track Maker` is a level four synchrotuner that takes a Kewl Tune and oneplus tuners.
Has the same material and hand effect as the main deck pieces.
Searches a Kewl Tune card on synchro summon and bounces an opponent's card if used as material.

`Kewl Tune Remix` is a level five taking mix
and one plus tuners gaining 1500 while the opponent has a tuner on field or engrave
and on the opponent's turn can quick effect recur a non-synchro tuner
and revive a non-synchrot tuner.
Then quick synchro a synchro tuner.
`Kewl Tune RS` is a level five taking Rico and one plus tuners.
Gains 300 attack for every tuner in either grave. increases all opponents monsters level by one if
they have 1,700 or less attack
and can quick effect banish a tuner in grave to negate an opponent's face up card for the turn
and `Kewl Tune Loudness War` is a level six taking two plus tuners gives other tuners targeting an
effect destruction protection
and can banish a Kewl Tune from grave when the opponent activates a card or effect
to copy its material effect.
This is the main strategy that holds the potential to be meta viable in the future.
Thanks to Q being able to summon any tuner with her effect, not just Kewl Tune,
you can opt to summon the RDA designed `Red Nova Dragon`,
who in turn lets you access `Kewl Tune Track Maker` for its search,
as well as summoning a Rico straight out of the deck,
who then in turn searches for either clip or mix
to combo straight into `Kewl Tune RS` or `Kewl Tune Remix`.

`Kewl Tune Remix` also holds significant power in the strategy thanks to its adback allowing you
to hit tuners that aren't in archype as well,
meaning that hand traps like `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring` are actually recurable, too.
The main limitation of the strategy, though,
is your extra deck lineup. as being all tuners means that you can only make all tuner synchros,
which effectively limits you to only the Kewl Tune ones or if you're not tuner locked,
something insane like prime heart.
As such, their primary limitation is going
to be what in-archetype extra deck options they have access to,
which in turn means that they'll need additional options in future support waves
to truly break them into the meta.
Though, at a minimum, they're a solid option for Genesis for the time being.
