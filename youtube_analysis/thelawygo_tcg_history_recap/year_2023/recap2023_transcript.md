### Intro

I'm going to say it right now: 2023 was a weird year for the history of Yu-Gi-Oh.
In retrospect, while a lot did change over the course of the year,
it also felt like very little change for long stretches,
until the entire format was upended and changes happened practically weekly for a bit.
That isn't to say we had a bad year in the game, far from it.
For every dud release we got, we also received another absolute banger of a set,
with one set released this year easily taking my pick for the best set of all time,
but I'm getting ahead of myself.

In total we saw four core sets, three deck building sets, three structure decks,
two world premiere sets, two reprint sets and even a duelist pack.
I thought you were dead. My death was greatly exaggerated.
As well as three ban lists, 15 YCS, 5 WCQ and one World Championship reappearance
to pack out this incredible year in the history of the game we all love.
Without further ado, let's get down into it: this is your Yu-Gi-Oh TCG 2023 recap.

#### YCS Sydney

Beginning the year, we had a YCS to remind the player base at large
that exiting 2022, nothing had changed.
YCS Sydney took place on January 7th and, as was expected,
Tearlaments was still the absolute dominant mega threat we all knew.
Tearlaments took first place, piloted by Jesse Cotton, marking his fourth YCS victory,
placing him now tied for the record of most total YCS victories with Chris LeBlanc.
It's no secret that this particular format was considered to be
one of the most skill intensive of the era.
That is primarily due to the nature of Tearlaments itself being
a massive web of decision point junctures that make the mirror match
one of the most complicated puzzles to navigate,
naturally meaning that the more skilled players in the community
that regularly perform well would bubble up to the top of play here.

Looking beyond Tearlaments for a moment, there were a couple of other faces in the top cut
worth mentioning.
Floowandereeze would make it all the way to second place here,
showcasing how the deck was actually thriving in the extremely slimmed down matchup spread,
fine tuning their build to swiftly deal with Tearlaments
with cards like `Dimension Shifter` and `Evenly Matched`.
Spright similarly held a portion of the top cut here,
thanks to the Bystial package countering out Tearlaments quite effectively,
but notably only one cleared into top 16, who didn't share their list for this event.
Runick would also see a couple of breakouts here in pure variants
focused on getting Majesty's onto the board as fast as possible to lock out Tearlaments
and the "ashisu" effects.
Swordsoul had adapted to main decking the Bystial lines as well,
mixing the level six bodies well with the Swordsoul tokens to hit level 10,
like `Baronne de Fleur`, `Swordsoul Supreme Sovereign - Chengying`
and `Swordsoul Sinister Sovereign - Qixing Longyuan`.
This would serve as our reintroduction to the meta right at the start of the year,
and shortly hereafter we'd receive our first set release,
and with it would be wisps of the meta game for the remainder of the year,
just not in the way we expected.

### Amazing Defenders

- Release date: January 19th, 2023.
- Set type: deck building set.
- Major strategies: Rescue-ACE, Purrely, Mikanko.
- Impact: "careful who you bully in high school" (the speaker's one-line impact, as captioned).

What Amazing Defenders is, is probably the textbook case of "careful who you bully in high school".
While the set was an absolute dumpster fire on release,
it would easily become the absolute best deck building set of the year,
with all three of its archetypes becoming meta in their own unique ways.
That isn't to say it was immediate though:
on release all three archetypes looked interesting
but were absolutely unplayable in the current meta environment,
resulting in probably one of the cheapest sets for a period of time.
After these, the archetypes were Rescue-ACE, Purrely and Mikanko,
each having their own period of experimentation, from unplayability to sudden meta threat.
So let's dive into each.

Rescue-ACE was the primary archetype of the set,
focused on their boss monster `Rescue-ACE Turbulence`,
able to set four different Rescue-ACE quickplay spells and traps directly from the deck
to form a massive control board instantly.
This was further boosted by `Rescue-ACE Air Lifter`,
able to search a Rescue-ACE spell on summon
and to send itself from hand or field to summon a Rescue-ACE from hand
in response to an opponent's monster effect on field.
`Rescue-ACE Fire Engine` is able to summon from hand when a Rescue-ACE is summoned,
and able to summon a Rescue-ACE from deck when the opponent summons a monster.
`Rescue-ACE Impulse` can negate the strongest monster's effects for a turn
and holds the same summon effect as Air Lifter.
`Rescue-ACE Hydrant` searched a Rescue-ACE monster once per turn
and let you use your quickplay spells and traps the turn they are set,
easily being the most important piece of the archetype,
not only for this effect but also because each of the spells and traps
gains another effect if Hydrant is on the board.
These included `RESCUE!`, which revives a Rescue-ACE from the grave,
or a monster from the opponent's, if you control Hydrant;
`ALERT!`, which recurs a Rescue-ACE from the grave or adds one from deck if you control Hydrant;
`CONTAIN!`, which negates a monster's effects and blocks it from attacking,
and can also lock it from being used as material for extra deck summons if you control Hydrant;
and `EXTINGUISH!`, which pops a monster then negates it
and all same name monster effects for the turn if you control Hydrant.
Lastly, `Rescue-ACE HQ` boosts Rescue-ACEs by 500,
lets you extra normal summon a Rescue-ACE,
and shuffles back a Rescue-ACE that's in the grave or banished to draw one.

Well interesting in theory, Rescue-ACE wouldn't move to do anything here,
due to the fact that aside from Hydrant and Turbulence all of their monsters did effectively
nothing,
requiring multiple turns of setup for their game plan,
at which point you get blown out of the Tearlaments infested waters.
But notably, they held potential if they found a way around their 50% brick rate.

Purrely is a level one fairy, `Purrely`,
that can add a Purrely spell or trap from the top three of the deck to hand on summon,
and is able to reveal a Purrely quickplay spell in hand to evolve itself
into an Xyz named on that spell, attaching that spell as material,
all of which have an on field effect tied to the ability to summon a level one Purrely from deck
by discarding a card.
`Epurrely Happiness` is able to protect a card once that turn
and gives its attached monster an added attack in the battle phase,
summoning Epurrely Happiness this way is able to search a Purrely card
and halve the attack of an opponent's monster after it battles.
`Purrely Pretty Memory` boosts both players' life by a thousand on activation,
giving `Epurrely Beauty` the ability to send a card you control in the grave
to snatch an opponent's card as material, summoning it able to negate
an opponent's monster effect once per turn.
`Purrely Delicious Memory` prevented a monster from battle destruction on activation,
boosting the monster it's attached to by 300 attack and defense for each material it has,
summoning `Epurrely Plump`, who can suck up two spells or traps from the grave as material per turn.
All three of these Xyz also have the effect to attach an activated Purrely quickplay spell
to itself as a material as a quick effect,
able to easily build up five materials for their boss monsters,
`Expurrely Happiness` and `Expurrely Noir`,
with both able to overlay onto a rank two with five or more materials.
Happiness is able to detach one to negate all monster effects on the opponent's field,
being unresponded to if it has a level one Purrely as material,
dealing 1500 burn if it attacks while it has five plus materials.
Noir is unaffected by cards while it has five plus materials,
able to detach two to put an opponent's card from field or grave on the bottom of the deck,
being a quick effect if it has a level one Purrely as material.

As for other pieces, `Stray Purrely Street` is a field spell
that gives your Purrely targeting protection,
floats your Xyz into a Purrely in deck on destruction,
and gives the ability to attach a Purrely quickplay from the grave to an Xyz in the end phase.
`My Friend Purrely` is a continuous spell that, by paying 500 life points,
gives your opponent three options of Purrely cards in deck to randomly add one to hand,
and when a Purrely Xyz is destroyed, adds three Purrely quickplays from grave to hand.
`Purrelyeap!?` is a trap that's a quick Xyz rank up for two turns
and can banish itself from the grave to shuffle three Purrely monsters in the grave back into the
deck.
While the concept was there for Purrely, it was clearly lacking a lot in terms of consistency.
While you wanted to make Noir as fast as possible, that usually required access to Plump
and a really well positioned opening, which, with all of their search options being inconsistent,
left Purrely in the hypothetical camp for now.

Mikanko was the latest crack at making a ritual archetype playable yet again,
with `Ha-Re the Sword Mikanko` either blocking or reflecting damage
based on if an equip is on her, searching a Mikanko equip when a spell is equipped to her.
`Ni-Ni the Mirror Mikanko` has the same damage effect
and is able to, as a quick effect, steal an opponent's monster if equipped.
`Mikanko Fire Dance` summons a Mikanko from hand and revives an opponent's monster to their field,
protecting its equipped monster from effect destruction.
`Mikanko Purification Dance` blocks effect destruction
and is able to bounce both a Mikanko and an opponent's monster in response to a summon.
`Mikanko Water Arabesque` prevents effect destruction
and is able to bounce the equipped monster to summon another Mikanko from deck
and swap the equip to that monster.
`Mikanko Reflection Rondo` is able to snatch the opponent's monster while you control a Mikanko.
`Ohime the Manifested Mikanko` is their ritual,
able to search a Mikanko card while in hand and discarding a card after,
enabling you to equip an equip spell from the grave to a monster once per turn on a quick effect.
`The Great Mikanko Ceremony` is able to cheat out a Mikanko in hand until the opponent's next end
phase,
and able to be banished from the grave to dump a Mikanko card from deck.
Mikanko on first release showed easily the most promise among the three,
namely being a splashable ritual engine,
as Ohime alone represented a full combo that resulted in a quick effect snatch
on the opponent's turn, thanks to searching Ceremony and Ceremony being able to dump Reflection
to set up Ohime's effect, seeing some experimentation
with other strategies already using ritual monsters.

Reprints here were extremely light, bringing "renow", `Isolde, Two Tales of the Noble Knights`
and Durendal, but not much outside of those.
On the whole, Defenders came out with a thud,
leaving the primary meta shakeup for the set following three weeks later,
and it would give an actual shakeup to the meta that would be felt for months to come.

### Photon Hypernova

- Release date: February 9th, 2023.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Photon, Kashtira, Chaos.
- Impact: the counter arrives.

Photon Hypernova was the first core set of the year
and immediately would be cracked open for one specific archetype in its support,
which we might as well address right here, right now.
Kashtira would receive its second wave of support here,
in the form of `Kashtira Riseheart`, a level four that can summon itself from hand
if you control a Kashtira and can banish a Kashtira card from deck
to banish the top three of the opponent's deck face down, making itself level seven if you do so.
`Scareclaw Kashtira` can summon itself by banishing a Kashtira or Scareclaw card from hand or grave,
can attack in defense mode, and negates the effects of anything a Kashtira or Scareclaw battles.
`Tearlaments Kashtira` can summon itself by banishing a Kashtira or Tearlaments card in hand or
grave,
milling three on summon from either player, and milling two when sent to the grave.
`Kashtira Overlap` is a quickplay spell that banishes a 1500/2100 monster from hand,
grave or either field
to boost another monster by 1500, and if banished while you control a Kashtira monster,
negates the effects of an opponent's monster for the turn.
`Kashtiratheosis` summons a Kashtira from deck with a different attribute than one you control,
and if banished can recur a banished Kashtira card to hand.
`Pressured Planet Wraitsoth` is a field spell that adds a Kashtira monster to hand on activation,
boosts your monsters by 100 for each attribute on field,
and pops a card on field after Shangri-Ira uses its effect once per turn.
`Kashtira Big Bang` is a trap that makes a player banish monsters until they only control one
if a Kashtira Xyz is on field, and if banished detaches the material from a Kashtira Xyz you control
and summons it.
Lastly, and by far the most important, `Kashtira Arise-Heart` is a rank seven Xyz
that takes either three materials or can be overlaid onto a Kashtira
if `Kashtira Shangri-Ira` used its effect that turn.
Banished any card to the grave, it attaches a banished card to itself as material once per chain,
and could on a quick effect attach three to banish a card on field face down.

So where do I even begin?
Arise-Heart alone is a house, coming from a hero one trick,
giving the deck the ability to summon a monster that is effectively Dark Law and DPE rolled into one
is already scary enough, but it's made worse by the fact that Kashtira could easily make this
in one card, thanks to `Kashtira Unicorn` being able to search `Kashtiratheosis`,
able to summon `Kashtira Fenrir`, able to search Riseheart, and so on and so on.
Arise-Heart on its own is a terrifying force because of what it does to the remaining meta,
as the presence of an easily accessible macro effect with built-in interactions
and board lockdown thanks to Shangri-Ira would do many things to shape the meta around it.
But probably more terrifying is what it did to the already present meta game.
Tearlaments might not like a macro, but the fact that Tearlaments Kashtira is both
a named Tearlaments monster gives the deck mills on turn zero
and merges the pieces of two strategies together,
made Kashtira a force to be reckoned with, both pure and with Tearlaments,
which we'd easily see the results at the following YCS.

Photon Galaxy got a major wave of support here, seeing a new end boss for the strategy,
but as beaters were not what the strategy was missing, they saw no impact on release.
Chaos would see a new overhaul to the archetype itself,
with multiple monsters focused on summoning multiple light and dark attribute bodies
to facilitate their new Synchros, specifically with `Chaos Archfiend` and `Chaos Beast`,
but would similarly see no play for the time being.
The various Branded lore archetypes would see roughly one new support piece each.
Bystial got `Bystial Baldrake`, a fourth Bystial name to add to lineups,
who contributes another light and dark when an extra deck monster is summoned, to banish it.
Dogmatika got `Dogmatika Alba Zoa`, a ritual boss that is immune to extra deck monster effects
and can make the opponent send seven cards from hand or extra deck to the grave on turn one,
and `Dogmatikamatrix`, a continuous spell
that searches a Dogmatika ritual monster or spell on activation
and another Dogmatika card if your opponent controls a monster,
and rips a card from the opponent's extra deck if you control a ritual Dogmatika, once per turn.
General Fusion support came in `Granguignol the Dusk Dragon`,
a fusion of `Blazing Cartesia, the Virtuous` and a light or dark monster
that dumps a level six or higher light or dark monster from deck or extra deck
to the grave on summon,
and can, if the opponent special summons a monster by monster effect,
banish itself from field or grave to summon a Dogmatika from deck or a Despia from extra deck,
and `Rindbrumm the Striking Dragon`, a fusion of `Fallen of Albaz` and a tri-type
who can negate the effect of an extra deck monster and bounce a card on field,
able to revive either itself or Albaz on the opponent's turn, banishing the one not chosen.
Springans and Therion received `Gigantic "Champion" Sargas`,
a rank eight that can overlay itself onto a Springans Xyz,
adds a Springans or Therion card from deck to hand once per turn,
and can destroy or bounce a card on field when a material is detached from an Xyz, once per turn,
and `Tally-ho! Springans`, a quickplay spell that optionally detaches up to three materials
from your monsters, adds a Springans from deck to hand,
then if you detached any materials, summons that many Springans from hand or grave.
Tri-Brigade received `Tri-Brigade Arms Bucephalus II`, a rank five
that can only be summoned if you have three or more Tri-Brigade spells or traps in the grave,
locks the opponent from responding to special summons,
and can banish itself when a monster attacks to banish all of the opponent's cards,
and is able to send a tri-type from the extra deck to the grave when sent to the grave.
Each of these would find their places in the meta,
with Bucephalus II slotting into almost every deck playing Bystial at this stage,
Alba Zoa forming a rogue deck with the remaining Dogmatika pieces,
Granguignol and Rindbrumm slotting into the standard Branded fusion lineups,
Sargas slotting into any deck wanting to search for Regulus and Borea,
seeing uses as an "AAR" servant or Punishment target that could still get you to a Granguignol pitch
without compromising the attack value for a searcher pop.

Gishki would receive a few legacy pieces here, with `Gishki Grimness`,
a level two fish that summons a Gishki from deck on summon
and can be the entire tribute for a Gishki ritual summon.
`Evigishki Neremanas` is a level 10 ritual
that can summon a water monster from the grave on ritual summon,
has battle protection from extra deck summoned monsters,
and can bounce a Gishki ritual monster to hand to negate a monster effect
and shuffle it into the deck.
`Gishki Nekromirror` is a ritual spell that summons a Gishki by tributing the exact levels,
or one monster the opponent controls regardless of level, losing life points equal to the attack
of the summoned monster, and able to cycle itself back into the deck
by placing a Gishki ritual monster from the grave on top of the deck.
`Gishki Aquamirror` searches a Gishki monster,
and in the end phase could banish itself from the grave
if you control a water ritual to set a Gishki Aquamirror from deck except itself.
Gishki support would be a solid line thanks to `Gishki Abyss` being a search for whatever you need
to complete the line, but more specifically would become a rogue level splash for Spright decks in
the meta,
thanks to Grimness and Abyss both being level two,
meaning you can set yourself up into `Gigantic Spright`
before committing to your gigantic line, seeing some experimentation.

Gold Pride would be the new TCG exclusive archetype,
with all pieces able to summon themselves if your life points are lower than your opponent's.
`Gold Pride - Leon` is a level three tuner summoning a Gold Pride from the grave on summon
and can synchro summon on the opponent's turn.
`Gold Pride - Nytro Head` is able to summon a Nytro token in the opponent's standby phase
to their field,
locking it from link material usage, and can pop the token to pop all cards in adjacent zones
on a quick effect.
`Gold Pride - Captain Carrie` searches a Gold Pride trap on summon,
and when sent to the grave can banish up to three Gold Prides from the grave
to boost an extra deck summoned Gold Pride by 500 for each.
`Gold Pride - Star Leon` is a level six synchro that can gain attack equal
to an opponent's monster's
on a quick effect, also destroying that monster if your life points are lower,
returning to the extra deck the turn it uses the effect to summon Leon from deck or grave.
`Gold Pride - Nytro Blaster` is a link two that can pop an opponent's card,
popping adjacent cards if your life points are lower, tagging into Nytro Head in the end phase
if this effect was used that turn.
`Gold Pride - The Crowd Goes Wild!` is able to reveal a Gold Pride
to search a different Gold Pride in deck,
then summon a Gold Pride from hand, taking damage equal to its attack.
`Gold Pride - Start Your Engines!`, when the opponent summons a monster,
reveals three Gold Pride monsters
in deck for the opponent to randomly add one to hand, then pops the monster summoned.
Gold Pride, just like every TCG exclusive archetype in the last two years,
showed a lot of promise on feel but had clear flaws,
particularly in that getting your life points lower needed out of archetype pieces to be consistent,
as their only piece that accomplished it was The Crowd Goes Wild, which itself was not consistent.
While it needed a second wave to potentially be meta,
it would for the time being see experimentation
with P.U.N.K., as the strategy paid life points a ton to use their own effects,
enabling the Gold Prides.

Other one-offs from the set worth mentioning included `Gravekeeper's Inscription`,
a spell that could at the start of main phase one lock either card effects banishing
or summoning from the grave until the end of the opponent's next turn,
being clearly designated as a counter for Tearlaments.
`Plunder Patrollship Jord`, the fabled Earth ship, can search a Plunder Patroll card
when the opponent summons a monster, able to summon a Plunder Patroll from deck if equipped
with one,
and can move itself into the pendulum scale by recurring a Plunder Patroll from the grave to hand.
Able to, in the scale, declare an attribute, send itself back to the extra deck
and summon a token of that attribute to both fields in defense mode,
which not only plugged a major hole in Plunder's extra deck
but also enabled you to access other attributes through the token summon.
`Big Welcome Labrynth` summons a Labrynth from hand, deck or grave, bouncing a monster you control
to hand,
which does trigger the removal by trap condition on most Labrynth bosses,
and can be banished from the grave to bounce a fiend you control to hand,
or, if you control a level eight or higher fiend, an opponent's card instead,
giving Labrynth a major play starter.
`Diabolantis the Menacing Mantis`, a TCG exclusive, can send insects or plants to the grave
up to the non-tuners used for its summon, used in some strategies as a way to set up Therion lines
by dumping `Therion "Lily" Borea` and being half of the materials needed for Sargas,
mixing with the P.U.N.K. engine for easy Therion access.
And last but certainly not least, `Triple Tactics Thrust`, a spell that's similar
to Talent before it:
it can activate if the opponent used a monster effect that turn, able
to set a spell or trap from deck
unable to activate it that turn, or, if the opponent controls a monster, can add it to hand instead
with no activation restrictions, being a massive staple in the meta thanks to being able to
generically search literally any normal spell or trap in the game.

#### YCS Lyon

With this support wave, Kashtira would push for a powerful showing at YCS Lyon the same weekend,
being the first deck to truly give Tearlaments a challenge,
although Tearlaments would continue to be the top deck in the meta.
As a note, there was also a team YCS in Mexico City on the same day,
but unfortunately due to poor coverage we only have a couple of lists out of the entire top cut
there,
so we're going to have to skip it for this look back.
Kashtira took its new pieces here to not only perform their one card Arise-Heart setup
but also now to completely lock the opponent out of the game before it started.
With all of the level seven swarming options now provided to them,
Shangri-Ira and `Kashtira Ogre` boards became a lot more possible than before,
resulting in many boards losing all monster or spell trap zones before the opponent even got a turn
to play,
which, while not common, was entirely possible for the deck to achieve.
Now, "subti" would have an odd top cut appearance here,
utilizing the nature of floodgates and stun to try and counter out Tearlaments that way,
with `Dimension Shifter` already proving to be a valid counter option for the strategy.
Jesse Cotton wouldn't hold the tie for most YCS victories for long though,
as Chris LeBlanc would win the event on Tearlaments, taking back the title with his fifth YCS win,
pushing that bar up just a little higher.
Although this event was a clear showing of Tearlaments being the best deck still even
with the new release,
it would be disingenuous to say that the showing here was 100% uncompromised,
as, though it had been announced earlier in the week of Hypernova release,
a new ban list took effect on February 13th, directly following YCS Lyon.

#### February 13th ban list

With it came the culling wave to the tier zero meta threat.
Newly banned were `Artifact Scythe`, firmly ending the Sanctum lockout play,
`Barrier Statue of the Stormwinds`, removing Floowandereeze's lockout line,
and technically `Kashtira Akstra`, but no one was playing it yet,
`Spright Elf`, one of the most egregious combo enablers ever printed,
and `Tearlaments Kitkallos`, the best card in the Tearlaments deck by a country mile.
Newly limited were "aido, kelc, keldo, mudora", `Tearlaments Havnis`, `Tearlaments Merrli`
and `Tearlaments Scheiren`, all hits to the Tearlaments, placing all
but one of their 2022 monster lineup
on the limited list.
In addition, `Ancient Fairy Dragon` was being released from the list,
with a caveat of not taking effect until a future set release due
to an errata coming down the pipeline.
Lastly, unlimited were `Destrudo the Lost Dragon's Frisson`, `Jet Synchron`, "Orion",
"servant of Emian",
`True King Lithosagym, the Disaster`, "yada" and "spiral Resort",
being a large cleanup wave to cards that no longer were issues,
either with the passage of time or with their other problem cards being banned.

With Tearlaments officially quelled, the meta game had opened up in such a way
that it was and wasn't clear what was about to happen.
While we knew that Kashtira was clearly going to be the best deck moving into this new frontier,
anything else could rise up in this newly open meta game space.

#### Team YCS Las Vegas

A team YCS would be held in Las Vegas the next weekend
and it would give us an idea of what to expect moving into this meta environment,
though not a perfect picture since this was a 3v3 event.
As expected, Kashtira would be the primary threat of the event,
notably being the deck played by all three members of Team Back for Seconds,
who would win the event.
Without the threat of Tearlaments present,
the deck had entered a space where it could be built fully
into its own game plans rather than having to counter out Tearlaments,
resulting in bringing more protective options for board lockout strategies,
specifically `Forbidden Lance`, which could counter out the primary counters to Shangri-Ira,
`Book of Moon` and `Book of Eclipse`.
Branded would see a bit of a resurgence here,
taking up the second largest percentage of the top cut,
primarily due to the fusion space being a lot more open now than before thanks
to Tearlaments being missing,
but also because a new tech had been discovered for the deck, that being `Gimmick Puppet Nightmare`.
`Branded Expulsion` from Power of the Elements had found a target worth putting on the opponent's
board,
and with it players could lock special summoning from the opponent for an entire turn,
which would cause the trap to become a staple in the strategy for a long period,
after being without question one of the best things you could be doing in Branded,
much to the dismay of Branded fans.
Labrynth would also see a resurgence here, primarily thanks to `Big Welcome Labrynth`
giving the deck a powerful new start option,
notably also playing the new `Tri-Brigade Arms Bucephalus II` for use with `Dogmatika Punishment`,
so you could still access Granguignol draws without sacrificing your attack power for the pop.
Spright would see some of the more interesting adjustments here following the ban of their primary
enabler,
with some opting for a Live Twin splash and others fully switching gears into Kashtira Spright
and even a pivot back into the Runick path for the deck.
This would set the stage for this new meta landscape,
being followed up by a structure deck released the following week
which would introduce yet another strategy into the pool worth experimenting with.

### Structure Deck: Beware of Traptrix

- Release date: February 23rd, 2023.
- Set type: structure deck.
- Major strategies: Traptrix (captioned as "trap tricks").
- Impact: "so why don't you take a seat right over there".

Beware of Traptrix was the first of three structure deck releases this year,
and it would be an attempt at revolutionizing the Traptrix deck,
a trap deck from the end of Zexal and early Arc-V eras,
attempting to bring them into the modern day.

Beware of Traptrix was the first of three structure deck releases this year,
and it would be an attempt at revolutionizing the Traptrix deck
from the end of Zexal and early Arc-V eras, attempting to bring them into the modern day.
This would include the new cards `Traptrix Pudica`,
who searches `Traptrip Garden` on normal summon
and temporarily banishes an opponent's special summoned monster on special summon;
`Traptrix Arachnocampa`, who protected backrow from destruction once per turn
and could special summon herself from hand on a quick effect if you control a Traptrix;
`Traptrip Garden`, a field spell that lets you normal summon an extra Traptrix each turn,
protects a plant or insect from battle destruction once per turn,
and can banish a monster to summon a Traptrix from hand or grave;
`Traptrix Holeutea`, a trap that summons itself as a level four normal monster,
can be activated the turn it is set by discarding another trap,
and can be banished from the grave to summon a Traptrix from the grave;
`Terrifying Trap Hole Nightmare`, which, if the opponent has special summoned that turn,
pops a monster with 2000 or more attack they control,
and if you have another "Hole" normal trap in the grave, banishes a monster from their grave;
and `Traptrix Pinguicula`, a rank four that is unaffected by trap and monsters who share a type
with its materials, can detach a material to search a Traptrix monster from deck,
and can attach a monster your opponent controls that is sent to the grave or banished
by card effect once per turn.

This support would do a lot in terms of speeding up Traptrix for the modern environment,
specifically in Pudica and Holeutea making it far simpler to use effects,
usually now able to trigger both effects in a turn to generate massive amounts of advantage.
Reprints here included "merello", "diona", `Gadarla, the Mystery Dust Kaiju`,
`Kumongous, the Sticky String Kaiju`, `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring`,
`Harpie's Feather Duster`, `Floodgate Trap Hole`, `Artifact Sanctum`, `Naturia Sacred Tree`,
`Evenly Matched`, "Ria", "aleris" and `Traptrix Sera`.
While this would introduce Traptrix as a viable strategy in the meta,
it would be poorly positioned overall, as Traptrix were already being cited against in major ways
due to Labrynth already being a potent meta threat,
leading to Traptrix's home resting in the range of a rogue deck.
Shortly after this we'd see the next big world premiere set for the TCG,
and it would make its attempt to shake up the meta with the Traptrix, could not.

### Maze of Memories

- Release date: March 9th, 2023.
- Set type: import reprint, world premiere.
- Major strategies: Gate Guardian, remaining history archetype pieces.
- Impact: wake up, wake up.

Maze of Memories is an odd set to say the least,
as in a weird way it did many things that various other sets did, rolled into one:
a reprint set of major cards from the last few years,
an OCG import set of all remaining history archetype collection cards we were still missing,
and on top of it all, a world premiere of a new TCG exclusive series of cards
retraining various cards related to Gate Guardian.
So let's start there.

Gate Guardian would receive a series of contact fusion monsters
able to banish the original three Gate Guardian materials from hand, field or grave to summon.
`Gate Guardian of Thunder and Wind`, a fusion of `Sanga of the Thunder` and `Kazejin`,
able to add a spell or trap that mentions the trio from deck to hand,
floating into a banished Sanga or Kazejin on removal.
`Gate Guardian of Wind and Water`, a fusion of Kazejin and `Suijin`, able
to negate a spell or trap twice per turn,
floating into a banished Kazejin or Suijin on removal.
`Gate Guardian of Water and Thunder`, a fusion of Suijin and Sanga,
able to drop an opponent's attack to zero twice per turn,
floating into a banished Suijin or Sanga on removal.
And `Gate Guardians Combined`, a fusion of Sanga, Kazejin and Suijin,
able to negate and destroy a card that targets your cards thrice per turn,
able to float into any of the other fusions or the original `Gate Guardian` on removal.
In addition to these, they also received retrains of various cards used by "perah and docs"
in the anime, like `Labyrinth Heavy Tank`,
able to be normal summoned without tributing, able to place a Gate Guardian piece
from hand or deck into the spell and trap zone once per turn,
popping a card on field if you control a Labyrinth Wall card when you do.
`Shadow Ghoul of the Labyrinth` is able to be discarded to add a Labyrinth Wall card from deck
to hand,
and able to pop a battling opponent's monster by banishing itself from the grave
while you control a Labyrinth Wall card.
`Labyrinth Wall Shadow` is a field spell that gives non-high-level monsters summoning sickness,
can place a Gate Guardian piece from deck into the spell and trap zone once per turn,
and can pop a low attack monster at the start of the opponent's battle phase.
`Double Attack! Wind and Thunder!!` is able to pop a card if you control a Gate Guardian monster
and can banish itself from the grave to shuffle back a Gate Guardian piece.
"Roku Guardian" is able to halve the opponent's life points to boost a Gate Guardian monster by
that much,
with the same grave effect as Double Attack.
And `Prey of the Jirai Gumo` is able to be summoned as a monster
and pop a monster in the same column,
with the same grave effect.
While an interesting thought experiment, the Gate Guardian cards would do absolutely nothing,
with their biggest experimentation being Heavy Tank being teched into Kashtira
solely as a level seven normal summon body.

As for the imports, `Wake Up Your Elemental HERO` revolutionizes the Hero strategy
by both replacing Trinity in the extra deck for an attacking finisher
and being a turn one extender, by being able to be popped with DPE
to access `Elemental HERO Shadow Mist`,
who in turn accesses your Mask Change on turn one.
That's right: it's an OTK enabler that also gets you to `Masked HERO Dark Law`.
Needless to say, this, along with another card released later in the year,
would actually do a lot to make Hero far more playable,
though still a rogue option for the regional level at the time.
`Accel Synchro Stardust Dragon`, or Accel Stardust Dragon for short,
revives a level two or lower tuner
on synchro summon and can, on a quick effect, tribute itself
to summon `Stardust Dragon` from the extra deck,
then synchro summon using monsters you control.
It became a solid option for various synchro strategies,
namely for being a level eight that instantly gets you to a level 10 on its own,
making `Baronne de Fleur` that much easier to access.
`Firewall Dragon Darkfluid - Neo Tempest Terahertz` is a link five that negates the effects used by
an opponent in the battle phase, can send a Cyberse from deck or extra deck
to the grave on a quick effect
to gain its attribute and boost by 2500 once per turn, and can attack up
to the number of attributes it has,
being an option for Mathmech to use as a finisher.
Reprints here included `Wind-Up Kitten`, `Nimble Angler`, "Rick aedle", `Mudan the Rikka Fairy`,
`Guardian Chimera`, `Ancient Fairy Dragon` with its new errata, `Baronne de Fleur`,
`Teardrop the Rikka Queen`, `Maxx "C"` and `Solemn Judgment`,
with Chimera and Baronne being massive reprints here, dropping their price very significantly.

#### YCS Lima

YCS Lima would take place the same weekend, and
with it we'd see the meta settling in a strange direction.
While Kashtira was still the top deck in representation, it wouldn't be by a large margin here,

able to quickly make your Naturia synchros backed up by the Runick spells for a massive control
game plan.
Traptrix would see a top here thanks to the interactions of Pudica and Holeutea and Sera,

Plunder Patroll would make a crack into top eight here thanks to Jord

#### The 250th YCS: YCS Bogota, YCS Los Angeles and YCS London

Three weeks after Lima, the largest event of the year would be held, being the 250th YCS,
being such a major event that it was held at three places at once,
being Bogota, Los Angeles and London on March 31st.
These events would not only be some of the highest attended YCS events ever held,
with London and Los Angeles being the second and third largest events separately,
but they would also spark a massive revolution in the YCS space known as Time Wizard events.
While Time Wizard formats had technically been a sanctionable event for OTSs to run,
being older format tournaments, they had not really been recognized by Konami at an official event
level
until this point, with officially sanctioned tournaments being held at these YCS
for the ever growingly popular Edison format,
in addition to their curated format selections from the previous Milestone YCS,
which ironically were held during not quite as popular formats,
and as such did not get the pull of players quite like Edison events did.
As such, Konami would make it a regular occurrence after this for YCS that had the event space
to hold what were called Time Wizard grand tournaments on the side of the main event,
usually for Edison format, which would be effectively a mini YCS for that format,
becoming one of the most popular side events ever for these tournaments.

As for the events themselves, Bogota, Colombia would be the least attended of the three
but would still have over a thousand players, which is nothing to scoff at.
Kashtira would take the majority of the top cut as expected, including the tournament itself,
piloted by Estabon Vquez, notably playing more unique to the deck hand traps like
`PSY-Framegear Gamma` and `Ghost Mourner & Moonlit Chill`.

Los Angeles, California would be the middle child of the three
but would still be the third most attended YCS of all time with 3225 players.
We would see a massive spread of decks across the top 64
but it would boil down to one deck taking the lion's share of representation,
being the same Kashtira deck we're used to seeing at this stage, taking first place,
piloted by Paulie Aronson.
Taking a dive into some of the more unique lists here, "ad axis" would crack the top 64,
being a deck focused on swarming the board with various level eight threats
to enable large damage pushes or rank eight mega threats,
being tooled as a blind second pick with cards like "Alpha the Kaijus" and `Lightning Storm`.
Marincess would see a top spot here thanks to the power of `Marincess Aqua Argonaut`,
able to flood the board on the opponent's turn and "a gate" a spell or trap on the same effect.
Code Talker would get a top here thanks to the Mathmech package,
as `Mathmech Circular` provided so much by itself for the strategy as a starter
that the rest can pile on for maximum advantage generation and power.
Scareclaw P.U.N.K. Kaiju Adventure is yet another pile deck,
this time taking advantage of the ability for the Scareclaw cards to combo off themselves
into `Scareclaw Light-Heart` for severe advantage generation.
Pure Rikka would see a top here as well,
being different from the various other Rikka Sunavalon tops we'd seen by not playing any copies of
"Lokai"
in the deck, instead utilizing Rikka in their purest state.

London, UK would be the largest of the three, being the second largest YCS in history,
only sitting behind 2012's Long Beach.
While Kashtira would be a dominating strategy for the top 64 here too,
there was a lot more fight over the middle chunks of representation here,
with Branded, Labrynth and Rikka Sunavalon all taking three slots each,
Mathmech taking four, and Spright taking a shocking 10 slots between its more pure
and Live Twin variants,
with Samir Bakkar taking the event on a more pure variant with a "mouy" splash,
with a single copy each of "Catty Penny" and "Melffy of the Forest"
to access `Herald of the Arc Light` on the opponent's turn.
Looking at the results for all three together, Kashtira had clearly come out on top,
with Spright and Naturia Runick throwing up results for second and third place,
with multiple other strategies close behind.
But whether or not those results would hold up following the next core set release
was what remained to be seen.

### Cyberstorm Access

- Release date: May 4th, 2023.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Superheavy Samurai, Mannadium, Purrely.
- Impact: "hotly anticipated release" (the speaker's one-line impact, as captioned).

Cyberstorm Access was a hotly anticipated release,
as it contained primarily support for previous archetypes
that would change their status in the meta game,
not only for their own decks but as an engine to use in practically everything.
That engine would be the Superheavy Samurai engine,
which was primarily made possible thanks to `Superheavy Samurai Prodigy Wakaushi`,
a pendulum that can, if you have no spells or traps in your scale,
set another Superheavy Samurai scale
from deck to summon itself.
99 times out of 100 you're going to use this to set `Superheavy Samurai Monk Big Benkei`,
who can, while you control a Superheavy Samurai, add a Superheavy Samurai Soul from deck to hand,
which in turn can let you go practically anywhere you want to go,
be it a level eight synchro through a combination of `Superheavy Samurai Soulgaia Booster`
and Wakaushi,
which, when you weave in a `Superheavy Samurai Motorbike` with it, can get you to `Baronne de Fleur`
via Accel Stardust Dragon, a rank four Xyz through the same method,
and all sorts of link climbing shenanigans thanks
to their previous link monster `Superheavy Samurai Scarecrow`,
which can discard a card to revive a Superheavy Samurai to its link arrow.
Not only was this super splashable, but it also had an FTK attached to it for the pure variant
that could very simply win the game straight away but died to a single hand trap.
But even still, the deck in its pure variant could get away with a metric ton of non-engine
thanks to its starters being so powerful.

Mannadium was the fourth of the Visas lore archetypes,
primarily being a series of level two tuner monsters that summon themselves from hand
if you control a Visas or a 1500/2100 monster,
floating into more copies of themselves on destruction with an added effect:
`Mannadium Fearless` boosting your synchros by 500 in battle that turn,
and `Mannadium Meek` boosting the newly summoned Meek by two levels.
Their Visas variant was `Mannadium Riumheart`, who could pop a Mannadium or 1500/2100 monster
to summon itself from hand, adding a Mannadium card from deck to hand on summon,
being used with the tuners to make `Mannadium Prime-Heart`, notably not requiring a non-tuner,
which can attack each turn up to the number of tuners used to summon it,
is untargetable if made with a Mannadium tuner,
and if it leaves the field floats into a 1500/2100 or `Visas Starfrost` in the grave or the
banished zone.
They also had `Mannadium Imaginings`, which reveals the Mannadium or Visas in hand to draw two
and place one in hand on the bottom of the deck, able to banish itself from the grave
to treat a 1500/2100 as a tuner that turn;
`Mannadium Abscission`, which pops a monster you control to add a `Peaceful Planet Calarium`
from deck to hand, or any Mannadium spell or trap if you already control Planet,
able to banish itself from the grave to summon a Visas or 1500/2100 from hand;
`Peaceful Planet Calarium`, a field spell that adds a Mannadium or Visas on activation,
boosts light monsters by 100 for each tuner in the grave,
and special summons a tuner that is destroyed once per turn;
and `Mannadium Reframing`, an omni negate counter trap if you control a synchro,
only destroying if you have Visas or a 1500/2100 in the grave,
and can be banished from the grave to shuffle back up to three Mannadiums in the grave.
While an interesting direction for the synchro line to take for Visas lore,
Mannadium on release wouldn't be heavily played,
still lacking the power level of Kashtira or Tearlaments before it,
but it would be experimented with as it received more support.
Though technically not a Mannadium related to the archetype was `Vicious Astraloud`,
a fusion of Visas and a 1500/2100 that must be contact fused by banishing materials from field or
grave
and pops a monster on field on summon, gaining attack equal to half of either
that monster's attack or defense,
being a powerful piece for any pile deck using Visas in any of its variants,
particularly good with Mannadium and Scareclaw.

The final Albaz lore drop is here for now, bringing us `Guiding Quem, the Virtuous`,
who dumps an Albaz card that mentions it from deck on summon
and revives an Albaz card that mentions it if a card leaves the extra deck.
`The Bystial Aluber` is primarily interesting for being a level four Bystial tuner.
`Albion the Sanctifire Dragon` is a fusion of Albaz and a light spellcaster who can't be targeted,
can on the opponent's turn summon two monsters from the grave, one to each side of the field,
and can revive itself by tributing a monster in each extra monster zone
and the center column on each side.
`Bystial Dis Pater` is a dragon locked level 10 synchro that can summon a banished light or dark
to your field and can shuffle back a banished card when the opponent activates a monster effect,
destroying it if the shuffled back card went to your deck, or negating the effect if it went
to the opponent's.
And `Despian Luluwalilith` is a level 12 synchro that requires a level four tuner,
who can permanently boost all of your monsters by 500
and negate the effects of a face up card on field when a monster leaves the extra deck,
and floats into a light spellcaster from hand or deck who has equal attack and defense
in the end phase of the turn it is sent to the grave.
All of these would see some variation of play throughout the remainder of the year for various
reasons,
but for the time being wouldn't change much in terms of Branded's placement in the meta game.

Cyberse would get a powerful new boss monster in `Firewall Dragon Singularity`,
a rank six that bounces cards the opponent controls up to the number of monster super types
you control or are in the grave, being ritual, fusion, synchro or Xyz,
boosting by 500 for each return, and revives the Cyberse in the grave
if it destroys a monster it points to in battle.
This would be a great mesh with the previous `Firewall Dragon Darkfluid - Neo Tempest Terahertz`,
thanks to it providing monsters needed for the bounce to go through,
in addition to receiving `Cyberse Sage` and `Cyberse Desavewurm` to fulfill the ritual
and fusion conditions.

Purrely would receive its first wave of additional support here, in `Purrelyly`,
which searches a non-quickplay Purrely spell on normal summon and can tag into a Purrely Xyz
by using a quickplay spell in the grave, attaching it as material to the summoned Xyz;
and `Purrely Sleepy Memory`, which drops the next battle damage you take to zero,
has the standard summon effect, and lets its Xyz draw one during the opponent's standby phase.
Both of these would help to make Purrely a potent meta contender,
with Purrelyly in particular making your lines far more powerful,
allowing you to search for `My Friend Purrely` on normal summon,
which in turn would reliably get you to `Purrely Delicious Memory` by revealing three copies in
deck,
giving you reliable access to Noir on turn one.

Mikanko would receive `Hu-Li the Jewel Mikanko`, with a standard battle damage effect,
giving targeting protection to all Mikankos while equipped with a card,
and searches a Mikanko trap when it is initially equipped with a card,
and `Mikanko Dance - Mayowashidori`, which protects its equipped monster from effect destruction,
bounces a card after a Mikanko battles, and can, while in the grave,
special summon a Mikanko from the grave
and equip itself to it.
Mayowashidori in particular was an excellent addition to the pure Mikanko deck,
giving the deck a reliable way to recur the smaller bodies,
which in turn fueled more digging into the deck with their effect when equipped,
boosting the deck's viability but not quite enough to break into the meta yet.

Gold Pride received their second wave with `Gold Pride - Roller Baller`,
with a standard summon effect and the ability to fuse on a quick effect for a Gold Pride fusion.
`Gold Pride - Pin Baller` is a fusion of Roller Baller and at least one other Gold Pride,
able to equip opponent's cards to it on summon up to the materials used,
the effect being unnegatable if your life points are lower,
and tags into Roller Baller from the grave if the effect is used.
`Gold Pride - Chariot Carrie` is a rank three Xyz
that can search a Gold Pride spell by detaching one,
allowing you to dump a Gold Pride if your life points are lower,
tagging into Captain Carrie in the grave if this effect was used.
`Gold Pride - That Came Out of Nowhere!` is a quickplay spell that special summons a Gold Pride
from hand or grave, letting the opponent do the same for any of their monsters,
negating the effects of whatever the opponent summoned,
and `Gold Pride - Better Luck Next Time!` searches a Gold Pride on activation,
dealing damage to the user equal to that monster's attack,
drawing one when a Gold Pride extra deck monster tags out once per turn.
This wave was honestly about everything that Gold Pride needed to stabilize itself,
cementing the P.U.N.K. engine as part of the deck,
as it now provided access to your full line thanks to Chariot Carrie also paying life points
needed to start the Gold Pride engines.

Other noteworthy cards here included `Sakitama`, a spirit that can reveal itself in hand
to normal summon a spirit from hand in addition to your normal summon that turn;
`Ringowurm, the Dragon Guarding the Hundred Apples`, a level two tuner
that can special summon itself from hand
if you control a non-effect monster and can banish itself from the grave if you synchro summon
that turn
to summon a level two token that can be treated as a tuner;
`Chaos Angel`, a level 10 light and dark locked synchro
that can consider a light or dark monster as its tuner,
banishes a card on synchro summon and gains protections based on the materials used,
with light protecting your synchros from all of the opponent's activated effects
and dark protecting all of your monsters from battle destruction;
and `REINFORCE!`, a Rescue-ACE trap that boosts Rescue-ACEs by 1500 attack and defense,
making it unaffected by the opponent's monster effects and giving it a one-time battle protection
that turn,
able to be banished from the grave to reset a Rescue-ACE spell in the grave.

#### YCS Santiago

YCS Santiago would be the first testing ground of this new release two weeks later,
but unfortunately we have extremely poor coverage of the event overall,
with only 11 of the top 32 known to us.
One of the big breakouts here was, as to be expected, Superheavy Samurai,
taking three of the known 11 slots, notably being able
to play half their deck as non-engine hand traps
due to the raw consistency of the core engine,

however unfortunately he never made his list public,

#### YCS Philadelphia

YCS Philadelphia would be a far more clear showing the following weekend,
and with it we'd see a clear picture of diversity in the meta, with 14 decks in the top 64.
Superheavy Samurai would perform well here, taking 12 top slots and clearing into the top eight,
similarly chunking the deck up with various hand trap lines in addition
to the Superheavy Samurai core.
Labrynth would clear into the top eight as well, claiming six of the top 64,
but more notably, a shift had occurred in the deck's composition here,
with almost all variants swapping off of the 20 plus backrow lineup
to instead be more centralized around roughly 10 traps and a suite of the furniture Labrynth pieces,
coining the deck as Furniture Labrynth.
This variant would move from here to become the primary variant appearing in future events,
being far less susceptible to the previous Labrynth counters like heavy backrow removal.
Branded had added Quem to their monster lineups, but beyond that nothing had really changed
from Quem's release, with the deck only clearing into top 32.
Purrely, now with a reliable way into Noir on turn one,
had four players clear into the top cut here,
though none made it past top 32.
The consistent access to Delicious Memory now meant that they could make their towers on turn one
far more reliably, thanks to Plump's attach effects.
Gold Pride managed to take two top spots thanks to their second wave of support,
with one pure variant using various rank three enablers like `Tour Guide From the Underworld`
and `Ready Fusion`,
and one P.U.N.K. variant even going so far as to play `Dark Spirit's Mastery` for access to
`Noh-P.U.N.K. Ogre Dance`, which in turn accesses the entire P.U.N.K. side of the deck,
which now could access the entire Gold Pride side of the deck too,
being a cavalcade of search cascades.
Dennis Nus would take the event on Kashtira,
notably having trimmed the deck's Kashtira count down to eight monsters and 10 spells and traps,
leaving the rest of the deck for non-engine to counter out the other popular options.
This would lead into the next deck building set the following week,
and though we've grown used to these sets being sleepers for the past few years,
this one would give a bit of pause to that concept.

### Wild Survivors

- Release date: June 1st, 2023.
- Set type: deck building set.
- Major strategies: Transcendosaurus, Vanquish Soul, Nouvelles.
- Impact: "$300 for a tier 2 strategy".

Wild Survivors was the second of the deck building set releases this year,
and at this point deck building sets had established a trend of sorts:
with practically any release of a deck building set you'd have one strategy
that was playable in the meta
directly out of the box to varying levels of success,
with at least one other strategy from the set picking up steam after a core release
to bring them more support.
We'd seen it with Grand Creators between Adventure, P.U.N.K. and Exosister,
we'd seen it with Tactical Masters between Runick and Labrynth,
we were even seeing it live at the time with Amazing Defenders between Mikanko, Purrely
and what was about
to be Rescue-ACE.
However, with Wild Survivors that trend appeared to be on its way out,
as very clearly from how the set was even printed, we were only looking at one strategy here
being a standalone potential meta deck.

Vanquish Soul was that strategy: a series of fire, earth and dark monsters
that all have effects that require you to reveal one or more attributes in your hand for a specific
effect,
themed after button combos you'd have to input for fighting games.
These include `Vanquish Soul Razen`, a fire warrior
that searched a non-warrior Vanquish Soul on summon,
could reveal a fire to become immune to effect destruction that turn,
and could reveal a fire and dark to destroy all other monsters in its column.
`Vanquish Soul Pantera` is an earth beast warrior that could summon itself if you have no monsters
in your main monster zones, could reveal an earth to make itself immune to battle destruction,
and could reveal an earth and fire to destroy all spells and traps in its column.
`Vanquish Soul Heavy Borger` is a dark machine
that can summon itself from hand by bouncing a non-machine
Vanquish Soul, could reveal a dark to draw a card, and could reveal an earth and fire
to burn the opponent
for 1500.
`Vanquish Soul Dr. Mad Love` is a dark fiend that searches a Vanquish Soul spell or trap on summon,
could reveal a dark to drop an opponent's attack and defense by 500,
and could reveal a dark and earth to bounce the lowest defense monster on field to hand.
`Vanquish Soul Pluton HG` is a fire zombie that could summon itself if you controlled no monsters
or only Vanquish Souls in the main monster zones, could reveal a fire
to boost itself by 3000 defense,
and could reveal a dark and earth to boost itself by 3000 attack.
`Vanquish Soul Caesar Valius` is an earth dragon that could bounce a non-dragon Vanquish Soul
to hand
to summon itself, could reveal an earth to make itself unaffected by the opponent's effects,
and could reveal an earth, fire and dark to destroy a card on field.
`Rock of the Vanquisher` is a link one requiring a Vanquish Soul monster that locks the opponent
to only attacking the strongest monster on field
and can either special summon a Vanquish Soul from hand
or recur one from the grave to hand once per turn.
`Stake your Soul!` is a spell that reveals a monster in hand to summon a Vanquish Soul
with the same attribute but a different name from deck, bouncing it in the end phase.
`Vanquish Soul Dust Devil` is a quickplay spell that can swap the battle position of a Vanquish Soul
to flip an opponent's monsters face down, up to the number of different Vanquish Soul monsters
you currently control.
And `Vanquish Soul - Continue?` is a quickplay spell that pays 500
to either special summon a Vanquish Soul
from the grave in defense or add it back to hand.

If the strategy seems a bit complicated, it is at first,
but quickly Vanquish Soul players figured out
optimal lines based on what attributes they opened, spawning a popular tier 2 to rogue level pick.
The main issue lied in two factors.
Number one, its performance in the OCG could not be easily translated to the TCG
due to the lack of `Maxx "C"` in our format,
meaning that Vanquish Souls needed a different reliably accessible earth monster
to run in their deck
to fill out that reveal slot, as the fire slot was already filled nicely by
`Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring`
and "curri Cara", which some players would fill with `Kashtira Fenrir`
and `Ghost Belle & Haunted Mansion`.
Number two, for some ungodly reason the 10 ultra rare slots in this pack,
which were usually divided three, three and four between the different archetypes of the set,
were not this time,
as Wild Survivors had given seven slots to Vanquish Soul,
meaning that the mid-tier deck was roughly $300 for just the core,
causing a backlash in the community due to a blatant upping of the rarities,
knowing that the other two archetypes present were simply just not as good.

Speaking of, Transcendosaurus was the second of these archetypes,
a series of retrains for vanilla dinosaur monsters from the past,
such as "frostos surus" getting `Transcendosaurus Glaciasaurus`,
"megazor" getting `Transcendosaurus Gigantozowler`
and "L fornicus" getting `Transcendosaurus Drillygnathus`,
but most of these would see little to no play at all in the competitive space.
However, two other cards from the archetype here would see play:
`Transcendosaurus Meteorus`, a level six Dino tuner that could summon itself from hand
if a card is destroyed
and can destroy a Dino on field or in hand to summon a normal Dino from hand or deck,
and `Ground Xeno`, a spell that adds a dinosaur normal or tuner monster from deck
to hand then pops a card in
your hand, able to be banished from the grave
to fuse monsters on field or in hand into a dinosaur fusion monster.
These two would be splashed into various dinosaur decks following release as enablers for the baby
pops
and a searcher for `Scrap Raptor`, one of the key Dino combo pieces.

Lastly, Nouvelles was a series of warrior
and beast warrior rituals focused on tributing attack position monsters
from either field to attack into bigger and bigger chefs,
with their main monster `Baelgrill de Nouvelles` able to negate and tribute an opponent's board
if summoned by a Nouvelles, and can, on quick effect, tribute an opponent's attack position monster
to summon
`Hungry Burger` from the deck.
"Welcome to Good Burger, home of the Good Burger, can I take your order?"
But simply, the concept of the deck was indeed interesting, with an actual built-in reason
to play the classic
Hungry Burger, but due to their nature as ritual monsters
and the clunkiness of requiring the opponent
to play into them to get their true value, Nouvelles was not meta in the slightest on release.
Reprints here included `Ultimate Conductor Tyranno`, `Miscellaneousaurus`, `Souleating Oviraptor`,
`Babycerasaurus`, `Petiteranodon`, `Fire Formation - Tenki`, `True King Lithosagym, the Disaster`,
the incantation monsters, `Double Evolution Pill`, `Fossil Dig`, `Lost World`, and more.

#### June 5th ban list

Four days later, on June 5th, the June 2023 ban list would take effect,
which extremely notably was dropped during round two of YCS Philadelphia.
Newly banned were `Cyber-Stein`, a necessary ban due to all the FTKs present from this one card,
from Vaylantz to now Superheavy Samurai, all able to FTK with a single card thanks to this;
`Number 89: Diablosis the Mind Hacker`, which was only being used at this stage
to cause problems in Kashtira,
specifically the enabler of the Shangri-Ira turn one lockout;
`Superheavy Samurai Scarecrow`, a massive hit to the new meta presence
which heavily limited how consistent
the deck could start its play lines by effectively removing their ability to search the combo;
`Appointer of the Red Lotus`, which was being used to snipe out hand traps
and was a bit of an issue card;
and `Branded Expulsion`, which was only being used at this stage
to lock out players from playing the game
with its various summon targets.
Newly limited were `Kashtira Arise-Heart`, meaning you only have to out it once now
to end the macro lock;
`Mathmech Circular`, the one card combo troublemaker that was too consistent for its own good
but was still playable due to being searchable by cards like "CET mining" and `PSY-Framegear Gamma`,
one of the most annoying hand traps to play against in the meta;
`Purrely Delicious Memory`, which while not a very big hit on paper absolutely killed Purrely's
consistency
on turn one, as now you couldn't reveal three Delicious off of My Friend;
`Naturia Sacred Tree`, absolutely obliterating Naturia Runick out of the meta by limiting their
searches
to game to once per duel effectively;
and the return of `Blaster, Dragon Ruler of Infernos` and `Denglong, First of the Yang Zing`,
two cards that have been on the list for quite some time,
with Blaster being the second worst ruler after
`Tempest, Dragon Ruler of Storms` and Denglong being previously a synchro combo piece
that ended on a search for
nine pillars as an omni negate, but with a "rodon" ban was not nearly as problematic.
Newly semi-limited were `Kashtira Unicorn`, which hits the deck's best starting monster;
`Lightning Storm`, which hits the best generic board breaker;
`Runick Fountain`, being a slap on the wrist for Runick Spright starter;
a small hit to Spright to keep them from rising into the meadow with a weakened Kashtira;
and `Herald of Orange Light` and `Sky Striker Mobilize - Engage!`,
with Herald being released due to the Yisu names all being limited,
and Engage due to upcoming Sky Striker support.
Lastly, unlimited were "steam the cloak", "Birdman", `Lyrilusc - Recital Starling`, "samera Lotus",
`SPYRAL Quik-Fix`, `Draco Face-Off` and `Sky Striker Mecha Modules - Multirole`,
all being either list cleanup or buffs to decks with support coming down the line.

This particular ban list, going into the national season, was a weird one to say the least,
hitting both Purrely and Superheavy Samurai before they even had a real chance to shine,
and hitting the most annoying aspects of Kashtira but not enough to keep it from being
the arguably best deck in the format.

#### Central America WCQ

The first real test of the meta would be the Central America WCQ held less than a week later,
and right off the bat it was clear that the hits were not enough to knock Kashtira off its throne,
taking 14 of the top 64, simply adapting the extra deck and moving on with its life.
Spright would be the more interesting breakout here thanks
to its pivot towards more supplemental engines,
with Carlos Mot taking the event with Live Twin Runick Spright,
mixing the various level two engines together to form one cohesive strategy.
Vanquish Soul would have a solid first appearance with three players clearing into top 64
and a top out being top eight, notably playing a couple more generic tools in their lineups
to flush out the attribute count,
like `Kashtira Fenrir` and `Ghost Belle & Haunted Mansion` for your earths,
`Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring` and "curri Cara" for your fires,
and one player even using the DPE engine to up their dark count.
This would be the official start to the national season,
with one more set release between here and the remaining WCQs,
and judging by the set list, it wasn't going to change much, but it might hold some surprises.

### Battles of Legend: Monstrous Revenge

- Release date: June 22nd, 2023.
- Set type: import reprint.
- Major strategies: Synchron, Hero, Chaos.
- Impact: a couple of meta sleepers.

Monstrous Revenge would be an important set for the year,
not because of what it brought but for what it started.
With the release of Monstrous Revenge, the 25th anniversary celebration of Yu-Gi-Oh would begin,
primarily punctuated by the inclusion of the new quarter century secret rare rarity into all sets
for the next year, being placed onto both new cards and reprints within the set.
As for new cards, `Assault Synchron` would be the toast of the town on release,
being a level two tuner that can summon itself from hand by burning the player for 700,
able to banish itself from the grave when a dragon synchro is tributed to revive it.
While not a staple, Assault Synchron would be a piece that was heavily experimented
with in various decks,
but primarily in Spright Adventure Peru,
as its level two nature opened up a lot of avenues for the deck.

`Elemental HERO Flame Wingman - Infernal Rage` was a fusion of any two differently attributed
Elemental HERO monsters, adds a favorite card from deck or grave to hand on summon,
and can be tributed to summon another level seven or lower Elemental HERO
that can't be normal summoned or set
from either deck or extra deck, ignoring their summoning conditions.
`Skydive Scorcher` is unassuming on the surface, as it really did appear to just be another search
to weave into Hero combos, but Infernal Rage solved a massive issue
with a recent wave of Hero support
from Power of the Elements, that being that there were no good Wingman fusions for Neos Wingman.
This solved not only that issue but also searched `Favorite Contact`
to make `Elemental HERO Shining Neos Wingman`
on the opponent's turn, meaning now a standard Hero combo could make Dark Law, DPE
and Favorite Contact in the back row to have as an additional piece of interaction.

`Duality` and "Shadows Light" were both adaptations of Chazz's spells from the GX manga,
with Duality able to tag out a light or dark monster for a same type
and level target in either extra deck
or main deck of the other attribute, also able to banish itself from the grave
to shuffle a light or dark from the grave back into deck to draw one,
and Shadows Light able to target a dark to summon a light from deck or extra with the same type
and level,
able to be banished from the grave to get an additional normal summon of the light or dark
that turn.
Shadows Light was a little too specific and slow, but Duality saw experimentation immediately,
and in Branded as a way to tag out a spent `Aluber the Jester of Despia` into
`Herald of the Arc Light`.
Reprints here included "IBL", "Nessie", "Sigma", the P.U.N.K. cards, the Adventure engine,
"Shin Buster",
`Divine Arsenal AA-ZEUS - Sky Thunder`, `Infinite Impermanence`, `Dark Hole`, `Terraforming`,
"deiser", `Super Polymerization`, `Book of Eclipse`, `Sky Striker Mobilize - Engage!`
with its alt art,
"equation macro", and lastly both `The Bystial Lubellion` and `Lady Labrynth of the Silver Castle`
exclusively as quarter century secret rares.

#### South American WCQ

The South American WCQ would be held the same weekend, and though we do have some results here,
this particular event is not very well covered, with 41 of the top 64 being unknowns.
Vanquish Soul would have a fairly successful outing, with six of the known 23 top spots,
clearing into the top eight and cutting the non-engine down substantially in terms of other
attributes,
though one was playing a small Kashtira engine to access Arise-Heart for the extra power.
Mathmech would see a couple of surprise tops here, with one leaning into the Spirit engine
to access `Albion the Sanctifire Dragon` quickly and the other leaning into the Firewall pieces
for Neo Tempest Terahertz pushes.
Santiago Marin would take the event on Kashtira, similarly playing the slimmed down engine
to make room for plenty of non-engine interruptions and search power.

#### European WCQ

The EU WCQ would follow a week later, and unsurprisingly Kashtira would once again take its spot
as the top deck of the format with 15 top spots.
Very notably, however, was the rise in various decks thought to be on the lower end of power
having shockingly powerful performances here, specifically in Rikka, Dragon Link, Floowandereeze
and Fur Hire.
Fur Hire managed to mix itself quite well with the Runicks,
as most of its pieces could get you access to
`Donner, Dagger Fur Hire`, which provided both the construction and board flooding with its effects,
useful for establishing a control setup, and doubly so with the added in Spright package,
with `Gigantic Spright` able to access the Fur Hire side of the deck through
`Rex, Freight Fur Hire`.
Floowandereeze found itself consistently bouncing in and out of top cuts throughout the year,
primarily in a single slot spot, but while it was unassuming, the deck had a layer of consistency
to it,
able to counter out various decks in the format by both playing into and using macro effects easily,
meaning it had a solid matchup into Kashtira specifically,
as the macro effect of Arise-Heart was not an issue.
Dragon Link was rising back into the meadow rather rapidly thanks primarily to `Bystial Dis Pater`,
able to recur the dragons you banished for your Bystial summons to provide even more board swarming
to get quick and easy access to "borin Dragon".
Rikka Sunavalon, though, was the breakout star of the event,
piloted to a first place finish by Jessica Robinson,
showcasing the resilient power of Rikka and Sunavalon when played together,
especially with the extra interaction points provided by `Rikka Konkon` and the Therion cards.

#### Oceania Championships

The Oceania Championships would be held the same day and we'd see similar results to the EU WCQ
with a couple of notable exceptions.
Purrely, fresh off its loss of Delicious Memory, topped not once but four times,
with one pilot taking the deck as far as second place, opting
to max out all the remaining quickplays
for ease of access to the monster line.
Kunkun Lee would take the event on Kashtira, playing a fairly standard lineup to what we've expected
at this stage.

#### North American WCQ

The NAWCQ would wrap up WCQ events for the year a week later,
and once again Kashtira would dominate,
taking the event, piloted by Jeremy Mitchell,
with the deck almost entirely standardized at this point,
only varying in the non-engine play.
Probably the most talked about deck outside of this though was Hero,
which took one spot in the top 32 here,
sporting the new Wake Up, Infernal Rage and Neos Wingman packages,
letting the deck reliably set up a turn one board comparable to Kashtira's.
This would be the last event before this year's World Championship,
but prior to that the third core set of the year would be given to us,
and any hope of a meta shakeup before Worlds would entirely rest on its shoulders.

### Duelist Nexus

- Release date: July 27th, 2023.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Synchron, Illusion, Unchained.
- Impact: the start of a new era.

While technically Duelist Nexus is not referred to as an era starting point,
as no new show or summoning mechanic accompanied it,
the shift in the pack's design layout, as well as its nature as a green pack,
heavily implies that Duelist Nexus is the start of the eighth era of Yu-Gi-Oh.
As such, it does one thing that a few previous eras have done: introduce a new monster type.
That would be the Illusion type, based on the various custom monsters from the older non-Yu-Gi-Oh
Yu-Gi-Oh games like Dark Duel Stories, with the first archetype of these being tied into Yugi's
original
monsters of Gazelle, Berfomet and Chimera.

This initial wave gave us `Gazelle the King of Mythical Claws`, who can add a level five fiend
or Chimera fusion to hand on summon, searching an Illusion monster when sent
to the grave for a fusion;
`Big-Winged Berfomet`, who searches a level four beast or Chimera fusion to hand on summon
and summons an Illusion monster from the grave when used as fusion material;
`Cornfield Coatl`, sharing a common effect with its other Illusions in this group
of not being able to be destroyed or destroy others in battle,
can discard itself to search for a monster that mentions Chimera fusion
and can banish itself from field or grave while you control a monster named
`Chimera the Flying Mythical Beast`,
whom we'll refer to as the OG Chimera from now on, to negate and destroy an effect
that targets a card you control;
`Mirror Swordknight`, with the same battle effect, can discard itself to summon a monster from deck
that mentions Chimera fusion and can banish itself from field or grave while you control the OG
Chimera
to negate a monster effect;
`Chimera the King of Phantom Beasts`, a fusion of a beast
and a fiend whose name becomes the OG Chimera's
on field and in grave, rips a card from the opponent's hand in the end phase it's fusion summoned,
and can banish itself from the grave on the opponent's turn to revive a beast,
fiend or Illusion monster;
`Chimera the Illusion Beast`, a fusion of the OG Chimera and one or more Illusion monsters
whose name becomes the OG Chimera's on field and in grave, can attack monsters up
to its number of fusion materials,
has the same battle destruction prevention effect, and if it attacks a monster drops
that monster's attack to zero
and negates its effects at the end of the damage step;
and `Chimera Fusion`, a quickplay fusion spell requiring the use of a beast or fiend
and can, during your main phase while it's in the grave or you have the OG Chimera on field or in
the grave,
either add itself back to hand or banish itself from the grave
to summon the OG `Gazelle the King of Mythical Beasts`
and the OG `Berfomet` from deck or grave.

Where to begin with this wave?
If Illusion wanted to make an impactful first wave, this was the way to do it:
not by making it powerful on its own but by latching itself to a currently meta deck.
Because Gazelle searches any Illusion monster when used as fusion material and was a beast,
it meant that `Branded Fusion` could send it for "Ren bro", allowing you to make a full Chimera push
off of Branded Fusion, which would become a popular option coming the next YCS.

Synchron and the 5D's dragons in general would get a nice new wave of support in
`Revolution Synchron`,
who can be used from hand to synchro summon for a level seven or eight dragon or Power Tool monster
and can mill a card while in the grave, if you control a level seven or higher synchro,
to summon itself and make itself level one,
and `Crimson Dragon`, a level 12 synchro that can search a spell or trap
that mentions it on synchro summon
and can, on a quick effect, target another level seven or higher synchro summoned on field
to return itself
to the extra deck and summon a dragon synchro of the same level, treating it as a synchro summon.
Revolution and Crimson would both be slotted into various decks that liked synchro summoning,
as Revolution provided large amounts of extension and Crimson allowed you
to double up on desired levels
of monsters on the opponent's turn, such as by targeting a Baronne to summon Dis Pater
or a level 12 to summon `Hot Red Dragon Archfiend King Calamity`.

Mannadium's second wave would come here in `Mannadium Torrid`, with the same special summon effect,
floating into any of the Mannadium tuners,
as well as more Visas general support like `Veda Kalanta`,
who can summon itself if a card is destroyed by a card effect while you control Visas,
searching `Clear New World` when it does, and can pop a monster the opponent controls
when a card you control is destroyed by a card effect, gaining attack equal to its original attack,
and `Visas Amritara`, a level eight synchro that can be made with all tuners, whose name becomes
Visas Starfrost on field, searches a spell or trap that mentions Visas on synchro summon,
and can pop a monster you control to boost all synchros you control by 800.
Veda Kalanta and Torrid specifically would find their way into Mannadium builds,
giving a large boost of consistency through the synchro pool to back up its already powerful
search engines from all of the various Visas field spells.

Infernoble would receive a couple of important pieces in `Infernoble Knight Ricciardetto`,
who can banish himself from hand to summon a level four or lower fire warrior from hand as a tuner
and can summon a level four or lower fire warrior from the grave on summon;
`Infernoble Knight Turpin`, who allows its equipped monster to be used as a tuner,
can special summon itself from hand or grave if you have a monster being used as an equip card,
and can equip itself from the grave to a warrior monster;
`Angelica, Princess of Noble Arms`, a level five synchro that can add a `Horn of Olifant`
or a card that mentions Emperor Charles from deck to hand on summon,
can send a fire warrior from deck to the grave when targeted for an effect or an attack
to temporarily banish herself and summon a Roland monster from deck or extra deck;
`Infernoble Knight Emperor Charles`, a link one requiring specifically Infernoble Knight,
gaining its effect on summon and equipping the spent `Emperor Charles the Great` to itself,
and was able to send an equip spell from hand or face up field to the grave
to negate a spell or trap once per turn;
and `Noble Arms Museum`, a field spell that boosts all fire warriors by 500 attack,
lets you pay 1200 life to search a Noble Arms card,
and can, the same turn you use the prior effect, summon a Noble Knight monster from the spell
and trap zone
and also activate a different Infernoble Arms card from deck, and it's placed on activation,
destroying itself after, and if it's sent to the grave because the equipped monster was sent
to the grave
can target a banished or grave fire warrior and add it to hand.
Infernoble support here would be outstanding, bringing more combo potential to the strategy
and a new and improved end boss, bringing the strategy back into the meta game.

Unchained would receive new support here in `Unchained Soul of Sharvara`,
who could destroy either a fiend or face down card you control to summon itself from hand,
setting an Unchained spell or trap from deck when sent to the grave;
`Unchained Soul of Shyama`, who can pop a card you control to pop a spell or trap on field,
able to revive itself from the grave by popping either a fiend or a set card you control;
and `Unchained Soul Lord of Yama`, a link two that adds an Unchained from deck or grave
to hand on summon
and can banish itself from the grave when a card you control is destroyed
to summon a fiend from hand or grave
then pop a card you control.
This small wave would do wonders for the strategy, revitalizing it into a meta mainstay,
but just how meta would have to wait for a YCS to see.

Rescue-ACE would get another couple of cards here to round out the strategy:
`Rescue-ACE Preventer`, who could summon itself from hand by banishing a Rescue-ACE in the grave,
can flip an opponent's monster face down on a quick effect if you control another Rescue-ACE,
and can summon a banished non-level eight Rescue-ACE when sent to the grave;
and `EMERGENCY!`, a quickplay spell that summons a Rescue-ACE from deck in defense
then tributes a Rescue-ACE from hand or field, able to be banished from the grave
to reset a Rescue-ACE trap in the grave.
These two did a lot to iron out the bricking of Rescue-ACE,
with Emergency getting it more reliable access
to either Hydrant or Turbulence, which once again we'd see with the next YCS.

Purrely would get another boost in `Epurrely Noir`, a rank two counterpart
to the previous Sleepy Memory,
able to discard a card to target up to two of the opponent's cards to bounce them to hand
with the same equip effect,
and `Purrely Sharely!?`, able to summon a level one Purrely from deck while you control a Purrely
Xyz,
then immediately overlay an Xyz with the same rank
but a different attribute from the one you have on field
onto the newly summoned level one, able to then attach a material from deck to it.
This support would revitalize Purrely into a viable strategy once again,
namely in giving another good rank up target for going first, thanks
to Sleepy Memory now having a valid target.

As for the one-offs, `Sleipnir the Runick Mane` was a new Runick fusion able to banish itself
and an opponent's
monster until the end phase as a quick effect in either your main phase or the opponent's battle
phase,
able to summon a Runick token when the opponent adds a card from their deck to hand,
being a solid piece of disruption and interaction,
doubly since its effect would also remove it from the
extra monster zone, allowing you to make more fusions.
`Evolzar Lars` is a new generic rank six that can't be targeted by monster effects while it has
materials
and can detach two materials, or just one if the materials are all reptiles or dinosaurs,
to target an opponent's face up card when they activate a card or effect to negate
that card's effects that turn,
being a solid generic rank six for the pool, doubly so for dinosaur strategies.
`Dark Corridor` is a search spell for Dark Worlds that also discards a card,
absolutely incredible for the strategy but unfortunately suffers from being a hard once per turn,
limiting its usefulness in a strategy that needs every draw to count.
Lastly, `Fusion Armament` is an Instant Fusion like spell for fusion monsters listed on other
fusion monsters,
being extremely niche but holding its use.

#### World Championship 2023

Following two weeks later would be the 2023 Yu-Gi-Oh World Championships, held in Tokyo, Japan,
being extremely important in a cultural sense for the game as it was the first time in four years
that a Worlds event took place, also being the first time a Worlds event held a Master Duel section,
which various top players who didn't quite make the main bracket would play in.
With the event being a combination of the TCG and OCG's card pool and ban list,
deck selections were far more limited than they had been prior,
with a decently balanced top cut focused on a couple of mid power decks that we've seen previously.
Paul Aronson with Dragon Link would take the world champion title this year,
being the first ever US player to claim the title,
showing the resilience of the strategy that would never truly die.
This would be followed up by another set just five days later,
and it would be an odd one to witness after the events of the last year, to say the least.

### Legendary Duelists: Soulburning Volcano

- Release date: August 10th, 2023.
- Set type: duelist pack.
- Major strategies: Salamangreat, Battlin' Boxer, Volcanic.
- Impact: pain can finally rest.

Legendary duelist sets were believed to be retired after the last one was broken apart for Maven
and other releases, so seeing Soul Burning Volcano actually releasing in the TCG,
rather than getting similarly broken up, was definitely a surprise.
It brought support for fire attribute strategies used in the anime.
The first of these was Salamangreat, which had consistently been in and out of the meta in the past,
so a decent wave of support could potentially bring the deck back in.
"Fire" searches a Salamangreat on summon and can banish itself from the grave when a Cyberse battles
to destroy your monster in the damage step.
`Salamangreat Weasel` can summon itself from hand
if you have two or more Salamangreats in the grave,
and while in the grave, when a Salamangreat ritual, fusion, synchro, Xyz or link is summoned
to the field,
you can put it on the bottom of the deck to revive a different Salamangreat in the grave.
`Salamangreat Tiger` can summon itself from hand by discarding another Salamangreat,
can be used as a non-tuner for a fire synchro summon,
and can modulate its level by one once per turn.
`Salamangreat Burst Gryphon` is a level eight synchro that can summon a level seven or lower fire
from the grave once per turn, reducing its level by that monster's,
and if it's synchro summoned using another copy of itself, summons a monster from your grave
in the next standby phase.
`Salamangreat Raging Phoenix` is a fire locked link four that can search a Salamangreat
if summoned using a copy of itself and can summon itself from the grave
when a fire monster is destroyed,
gaining its attack.
`Salamangreat Charge` is a quickplay spell that can shuffle back two banished or grave fires
to summon a third,
negating its effects and preventing attacking, or can pop a card on field if you control a ritual,
fusion,
synchro, Xyz or link fire with an attack different from its original.
And `Salamangreat Revive` is a trap that revives a fire in the grave
and can be banished from the grave
to shuffle a Salamangreat in the grave with the same name as one on field
to boost the field monster's attack by the shuffled monster's attack.
More swarming, searching and recursion is always appreciated for Salamangreat,
but this particular support simply just made Salamangreat better at what it was already pretty
great at,
seeing very little relevant movement in the meta from it.

Battlin' Boxer would receive its first support since the Zexal era,
with `Battlin' Boxer Uppercutter`,
who searched a Battlin' Boxer or counter counter trap on summon and when sent
to the grave can summon
another Battlin' Boxer from the grave or set a counter counter trap from the grave;
`Battlin' Boxer Chief Second`, who let you normal summon an extra Battlin' Boxer and,
when the opponent attacks
while you control a warrior or fire monster, can summon itself from hand, negate the attack
and banish a monster on field until the end phase;
and `Battlin' Boxer Promoter`, who can summon itself from hand if the opponent controls a monster,
can tribute itself to summon two other Battlin' Boxers from deck,
and can banish itself from the grave to level modulate all Battlin' Boxers by one.
`Number C79: Battlin' Boxer General Kaiser` is a three material rank five
that gains 200 attack for each material
it has, can detach to negate a special summon the opponent performs,
and if it has `Number 79: Battlin' Boxer Nova Kaiser` as material
when attack is declared involving a
Battlin' Boxer and an opponent's monster, you can send a Battlin' Boxer from hand or deck
to the grave
to absorb the opponent's monster as material.
`Battlin' Boxer King Dempsey` is a rank four
that searches or dumps a level four or lower fire warrior
or Battlin' Boxer spell or trap and can detach a material on a quick effect
to prevent targeting Battlin' Boxers
with card effects for the rest of the turn.
`Rank-Up-Magic - The Seventh One` is a quickplay spell that,
when a monster is destroyed in the battle phase,
targets a Number exceeding "great" and summons a Number C from the extra deck
that is one rank higher,
attaching the targeted monster as material, then able
to search a Seventh or rank up magic spell or trap
from deck to hand if you summon a 101 to 107 monster this way.
And `Battlin' Boxing Cross Counter` is a counter trap that negates a monster effect and destroys it
by destroying a Battlin' Boxer or Number monster you control,
then summons a Battlin' Boxer Xyz from the extra deck with a different name,
attaching Cross Counter as material.
While this support would be great for the archetype,
Battlin' Boxer has not been anywhere near meta relevant,
even back in the Zexal era, and the support wouldn't change that fact,
although notably King Dempsey being generic meant it could be used with other decks that would want
a low level fire warrior search, like Vanquish Soul or Infernoble.

Lastly, Volcanic received its first wave of support since Accelerator Reload in Secrets of Eternity
all the way back in 2015, and it would get a decent support wave for the archetype itself.
`Volcanic Rimfire`, when sent to the grave, can banish itself from the grave
to dump another Volcanic
or can banish a `Blaze Accelerator` from field or grave to place a different Blaze Accelerator card
from hand or deck face up on the field.
`Volcanic Trooper` can search a Volcanic card on summon and can discard a card
to summon a bomb token
to the opponent's field, burning the opponent for 500 when destroyed.
`Volcanic Emperor` must be special summoned from hand or grave by banishing three Pyro monsters
or a Blaze Accelerator from field or grave,
burning the opponent for 500 for each banished Pyro on summon
and setting a Volcanic trap from deck, burning the opponent for 500 each time they special summon
while it's on field.
`Volcanic Blaze Accelerator` was a continuous spell
that can be activated by dumping a Blaze Accelerator
from hand, deck or field to the grave, can summon a Volcanic from hand once per turn,
and can pop an opponent's monster by dumping a level one Pyro from deck to the grave.
`Fire Ejection` can send a Pyro from deck to the grave, then
if it's a Volcanic you can either burn the opponent
for its level times 100 or summon a bomb token to the opponent's field.
`Volcanic Emission` was a trap that can either search or summon a Volcanic monster ignoring its
summoning
conditions, returning it to the hand in the end phase,
or burn the opponent for either the attack of their
monster or half the attack of your monster.
And `Volcanic Inferno` was a continuous trap that could banish a Pyro from the grave
when the opponent activates
a monster effect on field to burn the opponent for 500 and negate the effect
if you banished the Volcanic,
and can place up to two banished or grave Volcanics on the bottom of the deck in the opponent's end
phase.
The support would be solid and revitalizing the Volcanic strategy into a competent burn strategy
for casual play,
with Emperor having a similar role to `Masquerade the Blazing Dragon` from the Branded Despia deck,
but would be nowhere near enough to make the deck a standalone meta threat.
This left the pack just as quickly as it had gotten there, with the new set coming a month later
to bring some levity to the price ceiling of the game as a whole.

#### September 25th ban list

Four days later the September 2023 ban list would go into effect,

but aimed to rock the meta to its core.

### Age of Overlord

- Release date: October 19th, 2023.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Snake-Eye, Horus, Armored Xyz.
- Impact: a massive shift to the game as a whole.

Age of Overlord would, without exaggeration, change everything about the meta,
introducing new archetypes that
would be meta defining, new singles that would be played in everything,
and support for previous archetypes that
would potentially change their positions in the meta: exactly what an impactful core set is supposed
to do.
Much of that would be centered around the newest lore focused monster, like Albaz
and Visas before her,
being `Diabellstar the Black Witch`, able
to summon herself from hand by sending another card from hand or field
to the grave, setting a Sinful Spoils spell or trap from deck on summon, and able
to resummon herself if sent to
the grave on the opponent's turn by sending a card from hand or field to the grave.
She would also be searchable by `WANTED: Seeker of Sinful Spoils`, able
to add a Diabellstar monster from deck
to hand and can be banished from the grave to place a Sinful Spoils spell or trap
that is either banished or in
the grave on the bottom of the deck to draw one.
She would even be tied to the new archetype of Snake-Eye, a series of mostly level one fires:
`Snake-Eye Ash` adds a level one fire from deck to hand on summon and can send itself
and one other face up card
to the grave to summon a Snake-Eye from deck; `Snake-Eye Oak` is able
to recur a grave or banished Snake-Eye on
summon with the same summon effect; `Snake-Eye Birch` can summon itself from hand
if you control a fire monster
with the same summon effect; `Snake-Eyes Flamberge Dragon` is a level eight dragon who can place a
monster in the
grave or on field into the spell and trap zone as a continuous spell,
can summon a monster being treated as a
continuous spell on the opponent's turn, and when sent
to the grave can summon two level one fires from the grave;
`Divine Temple of the Snake-Eye` is a field spell able to place a Snake-Eye monster from hand,
deck or grave face up
in the spell and trap zone as a continuous spell on activation,
boost level one fires by 1100 attack,
and can summon a monster being treated as a continuous spell when the opponent summons a monster;
and `Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye` sends a face up card to the grave
to summon a level one fire from deck,
able to be banished from the grave to search a level one fire from deck
and place a Snake-Eye or Diabellstar from
the grave on the bottom of the deck.
Sinful Spoils Snake-Eye, Diabellstar and Wanted would become an engine for any deck
that needed easy access to a
level one fire monster in the format, like Rescue-ACE, instantly rocketing Rescue-ACE up the meta
to far higher
levels of success than it had already seen, with the remaining Snake-Eye cards holding promise to go
with later
support.

Horus was a new archetype of level eights centralized around the continuous spell
`King's Sarcophagus`, which gives
Horus monsters non-targeting destruction prevention, can send a card from hand to the grave
to dump a Horus monster
from deck up to four times a turn, and can send a monster that the opponent controls
that a Horus monster battles
to the grave in the damage step once per turn.
All of the Horus monsters share the ability to summon themselves from the grave
when you control King's Sarcophagus,
with `Imsety, Glory of Horus` able to send himself and another card from hand to the grave
to search King's
Sarcophagus and draw a card, able to send a card from field to the grave
when another card you control leaves the
field by an opponent's card effect; `Duamutef, Blessing of Horus` boosting by 1200 attack
and defense for each Horus
monster you control, able to draw cards equal
to the number of different monsters in your main monster zone when the
opponent removes a card from the field by card effect; `Hapi, Guidance of Horus` able
to either add two banished or
grave cards to hand or shuffle them back into deck
when the opponent removes a card from the field by card effect;
and `Qebehsenuef, Protection of Horus`, who gives all of your Horus monsters targeting protection
from battle and
card effects when the opponent removes a card from the field by card effect.
Lastly, `Canopic Protector` summons a Horus monster from hand or grave
when the opponent activates an effect,
able to do it once per monster name and resets itself when sent from hand or field to the grave.
These cards would form a pseudo engine of sorts for decks
that don't mind playing out of the graveyard,
as you can use King's Sarcophagus to effectively trade your opening hand for a grave of resummon
level eights,
which in turn gives you access to the rank eight pool's various options, seeing success
with decks like
Tearlaments and Dragon Link.

Visas and Mannadium would get a wave of new cards focused around the Visas synergies,
but the two most important
of the wave came in `Visas Samsara`, able to be considered a Visas Starfrost on field
and in the grave, can
summon itself from hand by shuffling back any number of banished, grave or on field Visas monsters,
boosting by
400 attack for each different one shuffled, and can be treated as a non-tuner for a synchro summon,
and `Mannadium Trisukta`, a level six synchro tuner
that can summon a level two tuner from the grave on summon,
negating its effects, and can swap any number of tuners on field to level two that turn,
locking you into synchros
when you do.
These two would immediately be included into the various Mannadium pile decks that popped up,
which we'll talk about with the next YCS.

The Armored Xyz line would be a new set of generically splashable Xyz
that play off of each other for advantage
generation, with "torpedo", a rank three that can detach two materials to draw one
and gives a monster it's equipped
to effect stunning when they attack, and if it's an Xyz, targeting protection from effects;
"Fortress", a rank five who can overlay itself onto any other rank three or four,
cannot be used as Xyz material
unless it has no materials, can detach up to two materials
to add an Armored Xyz card for each material detached
from deck to hand, and gives a monster it's equipped to double battle damage;
`Full Armored Dark Knight Lancer`, a three material rank seven
that can overlay itself onto a rank five or six,
boost itself by 300 attack for each material and equip card on it,
can add an Xyz named card in the grave to hand
once per turn, and can, when an equip card becomes equipped to a monster you control,
absorb an opponent's monster
to itself as material; and `Full-Armored Xyz`, a trap
that while you control an Xyz monster Xyz summons using
monsters you control and can be banished from the grave to target an Xyz monster
and equip another Xyz from field
or grave to it as an equip spell with the effects of boosting the equipped monster by its attack
and can destroy
itself to protect the monster from destruction.
These cards would be extremely generically splashable, with any deck able to make a rank three able
to use the
full line with one copy of Full-Armored Xyz in the deck
to use the summon Dark Knight Lancer on the opponent's turn
using Fortress, and any deck that can make a rank three or four
that can lose all of its materials can do the same
line without Torpedo, with Mathmech being a particular beneficiary thanks to "Alan Bion".

Vanquish Soul would receive a new wave here in `Vanquish Soul Jiaolong`, a fire wyrm
that can summon itself if a
Vanquish Soul is revealed for a card effect while it's in hand, can reveal a fire
to change a monster's battle
position, and can reveal two fires to search a Vanquish Soul card, and `Vanquish Soul Snow Devil`,
a trap that can
reveal one of each of earth, fire and dark in hand to apply effects in sequence based on how many:
with one or more
burning the opponent for 400 and summoning a Vanquish Soul from hand, two or more burning for 600
and protecting a
Vanquish Soul from effect destruction that turn, and three or more burning for 800
and destroying all monsters on
the field.
This would be a solid wave thanks to Jiaolong filling a very specific hole in the strategy by being
the second good
fire monster, allowing `Stake your Soul!` to be a lot more alive for the deck, in addition
to giving Razen a better
target for its pop effect.

`Ken the Warrior Dragon` and `Gen the Diamond Tiger` were TCG exclusives that could summon the other
to the
opponent's board, with each having an effect when summoned by the other:
Ken making the opponent draw two and
discard one, and Gen discarding a card from your hand.
But remember, these will be summoned to the opponent's board,
so these will be activating for your opponent,
meaning in reality it'll make you draw two and discard one,
or your opponent discard a card from their hand.
These two nifty little guys do a lot more than they say on the tin,
being enablers for decks like Dark World, or
much more importantly for turning on `Triple Tactics Talent` and `Triple Tactics Thrust`,
as your opponent will
have activated a monster effect and will currently control a monster,
seeing a lot of hype on reveal for those
applications.

On to the one-offs. `Arias the Labrynth Butler` can, on a quick effect,
send itself from hand or field to the grave
to summon a Labrynth or set a normal trap from hand, making the trap able to activate that turn, and
when the
opponent responds to a normal trap or Labrynth card can summon itself from the grave,
giving Labrynth the option
to effectively play on turn zero when the opponent is going first, but being limited
to what was in the opening hand
for options.
`Berfomet the Mythical King of Phantom Beasts` is a fusion of two different types of monsters
between beasts, fiends
and Illusions whose name becomes the OG Chimera on field and in the grave, can dump a beast,
fiend or Illusion from
deck to the grave on fusion summon, and can banish itself from the grave on the opponent's turn
to summon a banished
beast, fiend or Illusion, being a solid one-of in the already popular Branded Chimera build.
`Super Starslayer TY-PHON - Sky Crisis` is a rank 12
that can overlay onto your strongest monster the turn or turn
after the opponent summons two or more monsters,
locks you from summoning the remainder of the turn, locks the
effects of monsters with 3000 or more attack, and can detach a material
to bounce a monster from field to hand,
being very clearly set up as the anti-`Divine Arsenal AA-ZEUS - Sky Thunder`, able to come down
and completely
counter the popular board wiping threat.
`Infernal Flame Banshee` is a rank four that can detach one to either search or bin a Pyro from deck
and is able to
summon itself if banished while you control a Pyro, gaining attack equal
to the number of your banished monsters
times 100, being a generic rank four Pyro searcher or dumper for various strategies.
`Exceed the Pendulum` is a rank three requiring a pendulum monster
that gains 100 attack for every pendulum you
control, can add a pendulum from the face up extra deck to hand on link summon,
and can special summon a pendulum
from hand or grave whose level is between your pendulum scales once per turn in the main phase on a
quick effect,
being a critical extension piece for Pendulum strategy similar
to its previous iteration of `Beyond the Pendulum`.
`Angelica's Angelic Ring` can only be equipped to a monster that already has an equipped card,
negates the effects
of the first spell the opponent uses that resolves that turn, and could pay 500 to destroy it
to give its monster
targeting protection permanently, seeing play in Infernoble as an added control option.
Lastly, and unquestionably most importantly, `S:P Little Knight` was a rank two that,
if link summoned using an extra
deck monster as material, banished a card on field or in the grave, and could,
when the opponent activates a card or
effect, banish two cards on field until the end phase including at least one card you controlled.
This one link monster would completely redefine the extra deck, as it not only did Unicorn's job
when tagged into
with IP, but better, it also provided either more disruption
with a DPE like effect or protection from cards like
Imperm, able to remove the monster from the board so it can resolve safely.
The number of applications this one card had in the meta are too many to list,
so let's just simply see it in action.

#### YCS Indianapolis

YCS Indianapolis would take place the same weekend as Age of Overlord's release, and
if it isn't apparent just from
the pie chart, Age of Overlord's pieces would completely upend the established meta,
so let's dive into what exactly
has changed.
Vanquish Soul had adapted to Jiaolong and Snow Devil, instantly slotting both in
to smooth out their fire lines, in
addition to adding in a copy of `S:P Little Knight`.
Get used to me saying that last line, by the way: it won't be the last time you hear it.
Resonator Bystial Horus was a newer evolution of Dragon Link that aimed to use the Horus cards
to get you to
`The Zombie Vampire` to mill for your grave setup, or if you are already sufficiently loaded thanks
to the Horus
cards, `Number 38: Hope Harbinger Dragon Titanic Galaxy` for spell negation, with Vision Resonator
and Crimson Gaia
able to fully shine in a deck full of level six darks, giving them easy access to level eight
and 10 synchros.
Also Little Knight.
Exosister would make a surprise appearance here,
sporting a line we had seen at the regional level a couple of times
this year, being the `Sakitama` `Aratama` line that allows you
to extend yourself into rank fours without special
summoning a monster that isn't an Exosister, allowing you to still use `Exosister Martha`'s effect.
Little Knight is here.
Mannadium would see two tops here, as Samsara had effectively turned itself into a one card combo,
able to access a
level eight synchro on its own thanks to being Visas on the field, able
to link off into a Light-Heart and get play
lines rolling.
Say hi to Little Knight.
Tearlaments would take three top spots here, playing similar lines to its previous outings,
but it would continue to
rise a bit as an extremely potent meta option in the new landscape.
Oh hey, Little Knight.
Purrely would not see too much of a shift in their lineup, simply changing what hand trap lines
to play, with
`Nibiru, the Primal Being` and `Droll & Lock Bird` becoming extremely popular picks yet again.
The puppy loves Little Knight.
Rescue-ACE would be unquestionably the biggest winner of the new set release,
taking 12 top spots including first
place, piloted by Steven Santoli, seeing a massive spike in playability thanks to Diabellstar,
as now Wanted or any
other card in your opening can get you to Hydrant, who can search Air Lifter,
who can search Emergency, which can
pop the Air Lifter to summon Turbulence and set four Rescue-ACE spells and traps
with Hydrant on field for their

This would completely revolutionize the deck's lines by giving them reliable access
to not only Hydrant on turn one

positioning the deck as an incredibly powerful threat.
Also this time, there's two Little Knight.
With the meta revitalized, Little Knight was without a doubt the star of the show, spiking in price

#### YCS Santa Cruz

YCS Santa Cruz would be a week later, and following the upheaval that was YCS Indianapolis,
the meta appeared to
have restabilized between various different strategies, with no clear best deck in the format,
as even the decks
with high percentages didn't hold a convincing margin.
Infernoble would see a top spot here, sporting the new equip spell `Angelica's Angelic Ring`
to provide an extra
spell negate.
Swordsoul would take two top spots here, one playing the standard Tenyi Swordsoul
and one playing Superheavy Samurai
Tenyi Swordsoul, showing that older decks
with lower power levels can top in the format as long as players who are
comfortable with them can pilot them effectively, with the meta so wide open
that almost anything competent can top.
One of the most massive upsets here was unquestionably Diabellstar Mikanko taking first place,
piloted by Michael
Verissimo da Silva, which would be the first deck to fully use Ken and Gen to their fullest extent:
by triggering
Ken or Gen you can place a monster on the opponent's field in the center column,
and in doing so you can combo using
any other pieces of extension into Isolde,
which in turn can loop you through your Mikanko lines for setup, summoning
"geonor transversa", summoning an `Number 30: Acid Golem of Destruction` and swapping it
to the opponent's board,
which in turn locks them out of special summons, and
with the Mikanko monsters would reflect battle damage back to
the opponent, being extremely powerful in the unfocused meta game.
This would lead into the next set a week later, and though Age of Overlord was absolutely powerful,
this one would
give it a run for best set of the year, being an absolute crowd pleaser
that we could have only dreamed of.

### 25th Anniversary Rarity Collection

- Release date: November 2nd, 2023.
- Set type: reprint set.
- Major strategies: staples.
- Impact: the bottom out of the staple market.

Rarity Collection was a first of its kind set,
bringing reprints of 79 of the most popular cards in the game, two of
which got both regular and alt art printings, and seven different rarities each,
ranging from the low rarity of
super all the way up to the max rarity of quarter century secret rare.
Because every card was printed in every rarity, the secondary market was flooded
with all of these staples, making
some variants extremely cheap to obtain while others being pricier.
As the nature of this set was reprinting meta staples, it would be next to impossible for me
to read off all of the
major reprints here without going on for a long time, so here are a couple of highlights:
`Effect Veiler`,
`Tour Guide From the Underworld`, `Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring`, `Dimension Shifter`,
`Borreload Savage Dragon`, `Baronne de Fleur`, `Time Thief Redoer`, "Seline", `Change of Heart`,
`Called by the Grave`, `Pot of Extravagance`, `Dark Ruler No More`, `Lightning Storm`,
`Nadir Servant`,
`Triple Tactics Talent`, `Pot of Prosperity`, `Dimensional Barrier`, `Evenly Matched`
and `Infinite Impermanence`
all receiving reprints here with the max rarity.
Hunting for them is probably one of the most fun sets ever to open.

#### YCS Richmond

YCS Richmond was held the same weekend, and similar to previous events, the meta was so volatile
that there would be
no clear top deck, with Unchained taking the most representation here
but not being the undisputed best deck in
format, with Tearlaments, Rescue-ACE, Infernoble, Purrely and Labrynth all putting up numbers
to make a case for
themselves too.
Joseph Bellor would win the day on Unchained, playing a fairly standard lineup
to what we've expected to see at this
point, with a slightly heavier hand trap line.
This would lead into the last pack of the year, and it seemed to be poised to do something similar
to what Wild
Survivors did earlier that year, for better and for worse.

### Valiant Smashers

- Release date: November 17th, 2023.
- Set type: deck building set.
- Major strategies: Memento, Centur-Ion, Vaalmonica.
- Impact: the Vanquish Soul treatment.

Valiant Smashers was the last pack of the year, and though it was poised
to bring us another solid archetype in
Centur-Ion, the other two archetypes felt absolutely abysmal on release, so it was clear
that this particular set
was not going to be well received.
The first of these was Memento, a series of retrains of various DM era vanilla monsters focused on
popping their own
cards to access other Mementos, with `Mementotlan Tatsunootoshigo` able
to summon itself from hand while you only
control Mementos, and can pop a Memento you control to dump Mementos from deck
to the grave whose total levels equal
the one you popped; `Mementotlan Dark Blade` able to discard a Memento on summon
to pop an opponent's spell or trap
and able to destroy a Memento you control to summon a level three or lower Memento from deck;
`Mementotlan Angwitch` able to search a Memento on summon and can pop a Memento
to revive a level two or lower Memento
in the grave; `Mementotlan Mace`, who can discard itself
if you control a `Mementoal Tecuhtlica - Combined Creation`
to take an opponent's monster until the end phase and while on field can pop a Memento
to search a Memento card;
`Mementotlan Goblin`, who can discard itself if you control Combined Creation
to give Mementos effect targeting
protection and while on field can pop a Memento to dump up to two different Mementos from deck;
and their payoffs being `Mementotlan-Horned Dragon`, who can summon itself from hand
if you have three or more
different Memento monsters in the grave and when popped on field can pop three other cards on field
including a
Memento you control, and Combined Creation,
who can only be summoned by returning five other Memento monsters from
hand or grave to deck or extra deck, can attack all monsters once
if it's the only monster you control, and if the
opponent activates a card or effect can summon a Memento from hand or grave once per turn.
For spells and traps, they receive `Mementomictlan`, a field spell that stuns backrow,
when a Memento battles summons
a Memento from hand or grave with a lower level than the one destroyed
and could reset a Memento spell or trap in the
end phase; `Mementotlan Bone Party`, a quickplay spell that pops a Memento on field or in hand
to search or summon one
with a different name, able to be banished from the grave to give a Memento piercing that turn;
`Mementotlan Bone Back`, a quickplay spell that can,
when the opponent removes a Memento from field, summon a Combined
Creation from hand or deck ignoring its summoning conditions, and can banish itself from the grave
if the opponent
moves a Memento out of the grave to summon as many different Mementos as possible from hand or deck;
`Mementotlan Fracture Dance`, a trap that can pop a card on field if you control a Memento
and another if you control
Combined Creation, and if a Memento battles an opponent's monster can be banished from the grave
to drop all
opponent's monsters by a thousand attack; and `Mementotlan Cranium Burst`, a continuous trap
that forces the opponent
to only attack the strongest Memento on field and can drop a Combined Creation's attack
and defense by a thousand each
to destroy and negate a monster who activates its effect on field once per chain.
While the deck has an extremely capable combo line and setup, the payoff just wasn't there,
leaving this as one of two
archetypes completely forgotten on release.

The other one of these was Vaalmonica, a duo of pendulum monsters that share an effect to,
while in hand, discard
another card to set both themselves and their counterpart from deck into the scales,
can banish a Vaalmonica spell or
trap to copy an effect from it, with a life point gain effect in the case of `Angello Vaalmonica`
and the damage effect
in the case of `Dimonno Vaalmonica`, both gaining counters in scale when you control the other scale
and either gain
life or take damage, and each had one other scale effect:
Angello link summoning a Vaalmonica link using monsters you
control when the opponent attacks, and Dimonno dropping the opponent's monsters' attack by 100 for
each counter on
herself.
Their links were `Duralume, Vaalmonican Heathen Hallow`, a link one that can only be summoned
if you have three or
more counters on Angello, can destroy opponent's monsters on summon up
to the number of counters in your scales, and
can remove three counters from your scales to attack three times that battle phase, and
`Zebufera, Vaalmonican Hallow Heathen`, a link one that can only be summoned
if you have three or more counters on
Dimonno, can remove three counters from your scales to protect cards from destruction,
and can apply a Vaalmonica
spell or trap effect from the grave on the opponent's turn on a quick effect.
Their spell and trap line includes `Vaalmonica, the Agathokakological Voice`, a field spell
that searches the
Vaalmonica monster on activation and steals an opponent's monster
if one of your scales hits three counters;
`Vaalmonica Scelta`, which either gains 500 life to place a card in hand on the bottom of the deck
to draw two or
takes 500 to search a different Vaalmonica spell or trap; `Vaalmonica Versare`,
which either gains 500 life to excavate
until you hit a Vaalmonica card to add to hand or deals 500 to dump a Vaalmonica card from deck
to the grave;
`Vaalmonica Intonare`, which either gains 500 life
to summon a monster from the grave of the opponent's choice or deals
500 to add a level four monster from the grave to hand; `Vaalmonica Followed Rhythm`, a trap
that can either gain 500 to
pop a spell or trap or take 500 to bounce a monster on field to hand, needing
to control a Vaalmonica to activate and
able to do both if you control a Vaalmonica link; and `Vaalmonica Chosen Melody`,
which either gains 500 to give
Vaalmonicas targeting protection from effects that turn or takes 500
to negate a monster effect on field that turn,
needing to control a Vaalmonica to activate and able to do both if you control a Vaalmonica link.
To put it bluntly, Vaalmonica on their own were completely worthless, needing to be splashed
with a strategy like
Performage to even get off the ground, and even then it's just link one and rank four spam,
with other strategies
doing infinitely better in that regard, leaving the strategy dead on arrival.

The last archetype was Centur-Ion, a series of monsters able to be turned into continuous traps
that all share the
effect of summoning themselves once per turn if they're in the backrow,
with `Centur-Ion Primera` being a tuner that
protects high level Centur-Ions from card effect destruction while as a trap
and can search a Centur-Ion on summon;
`Centur-Ion Trudea`, able to place itself and a Centur-Ion from deck in the backrow,
locking itself from summoning for
the rest of the turn, able to boost its level by four
when summoned from the backrow by its own effect;
`Centur-Ion Emeth VI`, able to bump a Centur-Ion from the front line to the backrow
to summon itself from hand or
grave; and `Centur-Ion Legatia`, a level 12 synchro that gives battle protection
to 2000 or lower attack monsters,
draws a card on summon, can destroy the highest attack opponent's monster,
and in the end phase places a non-synchro
Centur-Ion from hand or grave into the backrow.
Their spell and trap line include `Stand Up Centur-Ion!`, a field spell
that protects itself while you control a
Centur-Ion, can send a card from hand to the grave the turn it's activated
to place a Centur-Ion from deck in the
backrow, and lets you quick synchro summon after summoning a monster using at least one Centur-Ion
monster;
"emblema oath", which either places a Centur-Ion from deck in the backrow or sets a Centur-Ion
spell or trap from deck;
`Centur-Ion Bonds`, which places a Centur-Ion from hand or grave into the backrow
and can banish itself from the grave
when a Centur-Ion synchro is summoned to place a Centur-Ion from the grave in the backrow;
`Centur-Ion Phalanx`, which banishes a monster on field until the standby phase of the next turn
and can banish itself
from the grave to summon a Centur-Ion synchro from the grave but drops its attack by 1500;
and `Centur-Ion True Awakening`, an omni negate counter trap that sends monsters from the backrow
to the grave for cost.
Needless to say, this was the strategy among the three that held potential,
being adept at swarming the board
repeatedly for synchro eight and 12 pushes that recurred their pieces regularly
to do it again the next turn,
seeing some experimentation on release.
The issue lied in, once again, Konami printed most of the deck's good pieces in the ultra rare
slot, which completely
shafted the rest of the archetypes, with five of the seven slots going to Centur-Ion, two going
to Vaalmonica and none
going to Memento, being the most overly blatant ultra rare rarity scaling done since earlier this
year when they did
it to Vanquish Soul.
Though this was the last pack of 2023, it was not the last set release,
as we still had one more structure deck to hit
the masses, and it was aimed at giving one last shakeup to finish the year off with a bang.

### Structure Deck: Fire Kings

- Release date: December 8th, 2023.
- Set type: structure deck.
- Major strategies: Fire King.
- Impact: "sacrifice the Fire King? Well, that's not very advanced".

The Fire King structure deck was the final set release of the year, giving us one last batch of toys
to play with
before the final YCS of the year, aiming to revitalize Fire King into a proper modern strategy.
This support included `Sacred Fire King Garunix`, able to summon itself from hand or grave
when a fire monster is
destroyed, popping a fire tri-type from deck on summon and gaining that monster's attack;
`Fire King High Avatar Kirin`, able to summon itself from hand by popping a fire on field or in
hand in the main
phase on a quick effect, able to revive a Fire King when destroyed, destroying a card on field
when you do;
`Fire King Avatar Rangbali`, able to summon itself from hand when a face up Fire King is destroyed,
able to destroy
another fire on field or in hand to negate a spell or trap effect;
`Legendary Fire King Ponix`, able to summon itself from hand if a fire is destroyed, able
to search a Fire King spell
or trap on summon, returning itself to hand the standby phase after it's destroyed;
`Fire King Sanctuary`, a continuous spell able
to activate `Fire King Island` from deck on activation, can pop a fire
monster to protect your field spell from destruction, and
when the opponent special summons a monster can Xyz summon a
fire Xyz using Fire Kings you control;
`Fire King Sky Burn`, a quickplay spell able to pop an equal number of Fire Kings you control
and cards the opponent
controls, able to banish itself from the grave
to prevent a Fire King from being destroyed by card effect;
and their new Xyz `Garunix Eternity, Hyang of the Fire Kings`, a rank seven
that nukes all monsters on summon, can detach
a material to pop a spell or trap on field, boosting by 500 attack when it does, and
when destroyed while it has
materials summons that many Fire Kings from the grave.

This wave of support would bring Fire King into the modern era successfully,
with Ponix specifically being an insane
one card starter for the strategy, able to set up the deck to end on Island, Sanctuary
and two level sevens to make
Garunix Eternity on the opponent's turn when they special summon.
Because Ponix alone is a full combo for the deck, players would experiment
with various other engines to go with it:
from Tri-Brigade, thanks to the fire tri-type synergy with Sacred Garunix able to pop a "kidden";
Dogmatika, since the
deer servant could get you to Ponix, plus floor interaction thanks to `Dogmatika Maximus`,
"shur egg" and
`Titaniklad the Ash Dragon`; and most importantly Diabellstar, being able
to summon Ponix through Sinful Spoils
Snake-Eye, or instead potentially summoning `Snake-Eye Ash` to search Ponix, or a full board setup
that could
realistically end on four plus interaction points.

#### YCS Barcelona

YCS Barcelona would be held the same weekend, being the last YCS of the year,
and from it we see even more decks enter
the field of viable decks, shifting the top cut distribution even more,
with now seven different decks taking at least
four top spots and 17 different decks making top cut in total.
Kashtira would make a crack into top cut here, playing a pseudo stun game
with a heavier focus on Shangri-Ira and
another Kashtira to lock up the board and stun out opponent's options, similarly
to what they were doing pre Arise-Heart.
Centur-Ion would take four top spots here with three different variants:
a Horus variant providing additional level
eight bodies for easy level 12 synchro access, Bystial giving variety to the lines
with level 10 access and grave
control, and even the Superheavy Samurai variant for quick and easy starting lines in Wakaushi.
Fire King would take four top spots on its first outing as well,
with all four playing the Diabellstar engine as an
additional way into Ponix to start your lines,
with one player adding in the Snake-Eye pieces for the "amow whale"
setup line.
Joshua Schmidt would take the event on a deck no one was expecting, being Bystial Runick,
using Quem for access to
Cartesia for synchro plays, `The Bystial Aluber` as a searchable level four tuner,
Branded Regained for its interactions
with `S:P Little Knight`, able to turn its temporary banish into a permanent one,
and most uniquely Duality, which
could access a variety of different options in the extra deck off of the various levels
and attributes in the deck,
such as Albion off of "hugan" to dodge Imperm, Unicorn off of "Gary"
to lock the opponent out of the game, and
`Scarlight Red Dragon Archfiend` off of Bystial "Bellion" to burn for game.
This win would also put Schmidt at four total YCS wins,
placing him in spitting distance of the most YCS wins record,
at third place behind Cotton and LeBlanc's five.
