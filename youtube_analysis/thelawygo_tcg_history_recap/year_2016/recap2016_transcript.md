### Intro

When we last left off, the meta was in a state of flux following the release of DOCS
and the subsequent banlist that took everything pre-core out of the meta.
Kozmo was left a lone wolf at the top of the meta with a couple of pendulum brews starting
to pop up here and there under the name of Pepe, short for Performapal Performage.
Those piles had grown substantially in power, too,
with the release of the Master of Pendulum Structure Deck,
bringing pendulum magicians and support for Performapal and Odd-Eyes,
allowing the strategy to rival and even surpass Kozmo in the meta.
But, this was kept exclusively to the regional level,
as the only potential YCS to showcase this was canceled at the end of 2015.
Because of this, Pepe would have to show its growth following the next core set release.
And to anyone who knows about Pepe, this set would instantly make the pendulum magician pieces
obsolete.

### Breakers of Shadow

- Release date: January 15th, 2016
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Performapal, Dracoslayer, Kozmo
- Impact: YOU'RE GOING TO DIE, CLOWN!

BOSH is probably one of the most infamous sets of the Arc-V era,
which is kind of funny in retrospect, as on the surface,
it doesn't look like it would shift too much.
No major revolutions and new archetypes, a couple of pieces of support for existing ones,
but nothing as game-warping as you'd expect from a set that caused a tier zero takeover.
In reality, the set may have only had a bunch of smaller pieces for various strategies,
but all of those smaller pieces could be utilized by the same centralized pile at the center
of the meta, which would result in a complete hostile takeover of Pepe on the format
as a tier zero mega threat, arguably the worst we've seen since Tele-DAD.

Let's start with the aforementioned Performapals, who saw three pieces of relevant support here.
Monkeyboard is a scale one that searches a Performapal the turn it scaled,
which in practice means that Monkeyboard on its own was full pendulum scales
as it covered the low scale and allowed you to search for a high scale such as Lizardraw
or the new scale six of Guitartle, who draws a card when the Performapal scale is activated
while it's already in scale.
This would be extremely useful alongside Lizardraw specifically as you could scale the turtle,
scale lizard to draw one off the turtle, then send lizard for its effect to draw one,
effectively giving you a pot of greed style effect.

Lastly, `Performapal Pendulum Sorcerer` was an OCG import that
on special summon popped up the two cards you control to search for a Performapals up
to the number of cards you popped.
This was an incredibly powerful effect that standardly would let you pop scales to search
for either follow-up or the lizard turtle combo,
but there was a pretty major oversight on this particular card.
On its initial release, Pendulum Sorcerer was mistranslated stating that it searched up
to the number of monsters destroyed, not cards.
As such, because it happened at the same stage of the effect as the pop,
if you popped your scales with this effect, it would consider the pop cards spells,
meaning you wouldn't get the search off.
This would be a pretty major nerf to the cards applications,
specifically in regards to popping cards like Plushfire in the scale,
which was a very common play at the time due to Plushfire's float effect.

`Buster Blader` would be the newest attempt at retraining a monster used by Yugi,
giving us `Buster Blader, the Destruction Swordmaster`,
who's treated as `Buster Blader` on the field and in grave,
equips opponents monsters destroyed by battle to himself and can send an equipped monster
to grave to pop all same type monsters the opponent controls, as well as the Whelps,
a series of dragon tuner companions for `Buster Blader`.
Buster Whelp would be the main one, able to search a Destruction Sword card on normal,
contribute itself to summon `Buster Blader` from hand or grave,
and can summon itself from grave by discarding a Destruction Sword card
if you control `Buster Blader`.
This was a quick and easy way to access one of the deck's primary bosses, being `Buster Blader`,
the Dragon Destroyer Swordsman through a search for `Destruction Swordsman Fusion`,
a quick play spell that can recur itself by discarding any card
when fusion summoned gains a thousand attack for every dragon in the opponent's field or grave,
swapping all dragons to defense, locking their effects, and gains piercing.
Or the Synchro, `Buster Dragon`, who turns all opponent's monsters into dragons,
can summon a `Buster Blader` from grave if you do not control one,
and can equip a Destruction Sword monster from grave to `Buster Blader` once per turn,
being effectively a companion piece for the anti-dragon bosses.
As for the other Whelps, each can equip from hand or field to a `Buster Blader`,
with each having a flood gate effect and an effect that move the Whelp off of `Buster Blader`
for an immediate bonus.
With Dragon Buster locking the opponent's special summons from extra deck,
and can summon itself while equipped.

Wizard Buster locking the opponent's grave effects,
and can send itself to grave to recur a Destruction Sword monster.
And Robot Buster locking face-up spell and trap effects,
and can send itself to grave to boost the equipped monster by a thousand for the turn.
This would be an extremely comprehensive and solid way of legacy support for `Buster Blader`,
giving the deck a solid direction.
But it suffered pretty heavily from the Retrain not having the name `Buster Blader` in hand or deck,
meaning half of the support couldn't be used with it until the Retrain was on the field
or in the grave, leading to the deck bricking to high heaven more often than not.
Although it would see rogue level play in formats with high dragon representation
for obvious reasons.

Dracoslayer would receive easily its most impactful support yet,
receiving another Dracoslayer main deck name in Master Pendulum,
being a normal pendulum that can pop another pendulum scale on field while it's in scale,
and the fusion boss of Dinoster Power, a contact fusion of a Dracoslayer pendulum
and any other pendulum.
Can revive a Dracoslayer pendulum once per turn,
and blocks all pendulum cards you control from destruction of any kind.
However, there was one more piece of support this time that would take Dracoslayer over the top,
being `Draco Face-Off`, a quick play spell that revealed a Dracoslayer and a Dracoverlord from deck,
had the opponent select one at random, and
then the selected monster could either be special summoned or set in the scale
with the other moving to the extra deck.
This was insanely powerful, giving you easy access to your Luster lines at best
or at worst giving you a free level four body and Vector Pendulum while placing Luster
in the extra deck for the eventual Pendulum summon back,
making this an absolute staple for all Pendulum decks moving forward.

Dinomist would be the third Dracoslayer tie-in archetype,
a series of level four and five machine Pendulums focused on protection and scales
with all the low scales being able to destroy themselves in place of another Dinomist card
and all of the high scales being able to negate a card that targets a Dinomist
by popping itself with each having unique monster effects.
With Stegosaur popping both sides of any combat involving another Pendulum you control,
Plesios dropping the opponent by 100 for each Dinomist card you control,
Pteran searching a Dinomist when it destroys a monster in combat,
Brachion able to summon itself from hand if your opponent has the highest attack on field
and you control no Brachions, Ceratops able to summon itself from hand
if you control only Dinomists and no Ceratops,
and Rex able to tribute a Dinomist at end of damage step it attacks to either attack again
with piercing or spin back a card from the opponent's hand or field and gain 100 attack.
They also had two relevant spell traps in Charge,
which searched a Dinomist on activation and added a Dinomist sent to face-up extra deck back
to hand once per turn, and Rush, which summons a Dinomist from deck,
making it unaffected by card effects and destroys it in the end phase.
While Dinomist was absolutely interesting and constructed,
it didn't have what it needed to compete on the meta stage consistently,
being relegated to the rogue level at best.

Shiranui would be a new zombie archetype aimed at cheating out their synchros
with their tuner Spectralsword, who can banish itself and another zombie non-tuner
in grave to summon a zombie synchro whose total level equals the two.
This would be complemented by their main deck pieces of Smith,
who searched a Shiranui card when sent to grave, a synchro material,
and prevents zombie battle destruction the turn he's banished, Spiritmaster,
who summons a Shiranui from hand on normal and pops a card when banished, and Samurai,
who can banish a zombie from grave to boost itself by 600 and banish any monster it battles
that turn and recurs a Shiranui monster when banished.
Their synchro bosses here were Samuraisaga,
a level six that can shuffle back a banished zombie to swap all opponent's monsters
to defense with less attack than the returned monsters and drops an opponent by 500 when banished,
and Shogunsaga, a level eight that banishes a zombie from grave on summon to boost itself
by that monster's attack and floats into a banished zombie with zero defense on destruction.

Shiranui had the bones of an interesting strategy for sure,
unique way of utilizing the standard zombie stick of filling up the grave and recursive payoffs,
but unfortunately was just not consistent enough at this juncture to become meta.
Seeing some rogue level experimentation with `Uni-Zombie` for helping set up Spectralsword lines.
Kozmo would see its third wave of support here in Tincan,
a pilot who can pay 500 to choose three Kozmo cards from deck,
then add one at random to hand while dumping the other two to grave.

Soartroopers, a pilot who can pay 1,000 to revive a pilot in grave.
Delta Shuttle, a ship that can dump a Kozmo from deck to grave to drop a monster's attack
by its level times 100.
Dark Eclipser, a ship that can't be targeted and can banish a Kozmo monster from grave
to negate traps, notably not floating into a Kozmo on death, rather searching one instead.
And `Kozmojo`, a trap that pops a Kozmo to non-targeting banish a card on field.
This wave would spark an interesting change in direction
for Kozmo's deck building over the next couple of months,
mainly centering around the new pilot of Tincan.
As by using its effect, one could actively set up the grave with multiple ships and
then revive them using cards like `Call of the Haunted` and `Oasis of Dragon Souls`.

This direction of the deck's building style would receive the colloquial name of Tin Can Turbo.
And while it wouldn't be super popular for now,
it would fundamentally shift Kozmo's place in the meta over the coming months
with new releases and the strategy's last support wave.
Kaiju would receive their most interesting support wave yet, receiving Jizukiru,
the now strongest Kaiju body at 3300, but also a new reason to actually play it.

`Interrupted Kaiju Slumber` was effectively `Dark Hole` that summoned two Kaiju from deck,
one to each side of the field, and could be banished from grave on the following turn
to search another Kaiju.
This would spark a movement of using the Kaijus as an engine that was effectively dark hole
but better, wiping the board and giving you an immediate 1,100 damage to push
to the opponent by attacking over the Gameciel with Jizukiru,
then being able to search another Gameciel on a following turn
to out whatever the opponent built after.
Majespecter would receive a more minor support wave here in Ogama,
who sets a Majespecter spell trap from deck on summon, and Sonics,
which doubles a Majespecter's attack and defense but halves the damage it inflicts that turn.
These two would find a home in Majespecter decks,
namely for the added flexibility they gave to Majespecter setups,
with Ogama providing a search for either pool of responses the deck had access to.

`Guiding Ariadne` could, when destroyed, reveal three counter traps in deck
to have the opponent pick one to add to your hand, shuffling the rest back in.
This would be yet another option for Pepe,
as not only could it potentially search `Solemn Warning` if the opponent was daring enough
to give it to you, but also the new counter trap of `Solemn Strike`,
which can negate and destroy either a monster effect or inherent special summon
for the cost of 1,500 life points, being a massive staple in the meta almost immediately thanks
to it being a way to block pendulum summon pushes.
Another option was the more budget friendly `Ultimate Providence`,
which was an omni negate at the cost of discarding the corresponding card super type from hand,
which would see some experimentation.

`Twin Twisters` was yet another variant of MST,
taking a discard to use but popping up the two spell traps instead.
This would be an instant staple, especially in a meta game centered around pendulums,
as the versatility of being able to snipe both scales or remove multiple back row threats
while also potentially setting up the grave was too good of a deal to pass up for most players,
seeing mass play almost immediately.
`Quaking Mirror Force` was the third of the elemental variants of the classic `Mirror Force`,
flipping any affected monster face down permanently instead of destroying them,
seeing experimentation immediately as the ability to lock up the opponent's zones
with this was actually surprisingly useful.

`Fiendish Rhino Warrior` was an OCG import that blocked other Fiends from being destroyed,
able to banish a Fiend from Deck when sent to Grave.
Being an instant staple in Burning Abyss as it blocked the BA self-pop
and let you `Foolish Burial` one after using it.
`Neptabyss, the Atlantean Prince` could send an Atlantean from Deck to Grave
to search an Atlantean card and can, if sent to Grave for a WATER monster effect,
revive an Atlantean monster.
This piece would completely revamp Atlantean, or more accurately Merlantean,
into a rogue-level contender for the current metagame.
As by sending Dragoons with his effect, you could not only search another Dragoons,
but also search for a Mermail to pair with it, being the perfect starter for the strategy.

Lastly would be two new Xyz in the OCG import slots that would revitalize the staple Rank 4 pool
in extremely different ways.
`Traptrix Rafflesia` is unaffected by traps while she has materials and could detach
to send a `Trap Hole` from Deck to Grave when its effects would be able to be applied
to trigger that effect.
Rafflesia would become an instant staple Rank 4 in the format in large part thanks
to `Treacherous Trap Hole`.
As her effect became a two-monster pop on a quick effect with that.
Seeing use for that as well as for applying Bottomless after the fact.

The other, and far more interesting application card was `Cyber Dragon Infinity`,
a light locked three-material Rank 6 that can also summon itself over `Cyber Dragon Nova`.
Gains 200 attack for each material, can suck up an attack position monster on either field
as material once per turn, and can detach one for an Omni negate.
You may be remembering what I said right at the start of this, and no, I did not misspeak.

`Cyber Dragon Infinity` would be a staple piece of the Rank 4 toolbox due

### Emperor of Darkness Structure Deck

- Release date: January 29th, 2016
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Monarch
- Impact: Stunner Swarm, you decide

Emperor of Darkness in the OCG was the beginning of a new structure deck series known
as structure deck R, which took the concept of an older structure deck,
in this case the Dark Emperor or Advent of the Emperor in the OCG,
and revitalizing the idea into an entirely new design space aimed at making the previous structure
deck viable in the current metal landscape.
This concept's first outing would be quite the impactful one,
revitalizing the Monarch strategy with a number of new additions.
Edea and Eidos would be new low-level additions.
With Edea on summon specialing an 800/1000 from your deck,
and when sent to grave returns a banished Monarch spell trap to hand.
And Eidos giving you an additional tribute summon the turn it's summoned,
and can be banished from grave to summon an 800/1000 from grave.
Both of these would fuel the new bosses of Aether and Erebus,
with both being able to be tribute summoned over any single tribute summoned monster.

Aether able to tribute summon itself in the opponent's main phase
by banishing a Monarch spell trap from grave,
and able to summon a 2400/1000 from deck by dumping two Monarch spell traps from deck
to grave on tribute summon.
And Erebus able to dump two Monarch spell traps on tribute summon to spin away a card from hand,
grave, or field the opponent controls.
Enabled to discard a Monarch spell trap while in grave to recur back a 2400 or higher attack
and a 1000 defense monster in grave.

The Monarch spell traps would also see an overhaul in Pantheism,
which discards a Monarch spell trap to draw two,
that can be banished from grave to reveal three Monarch spell traps in deck
to have the opponent pick one to add to your hand.
`The Prime Monarch`, a continuous trap that can shuffle two Monarch spell traps
from grave back into deck to draw one, and can while in grave,
banish a Monarch spell trap to revive itself as a level five light fairy, and Domain,
a field spell that boosts your tribute summon monsters by 800 while attacking,
can reduce a 28,000's level by two in hand for the turn,
dropping their tribute summon requirement by one,
and while you have no cards in your extra deck and a tribute summoned monster on the field,
locks the opponent from special summoning a monster from the extra deck.
To say this wave would make Monarchs meta was an understatement,
seeing the deck's surge in playability with new draw and search tools, new board flooding,
and new bosses worth the tribute requirements.
With Aether in particular being a house of a card thanks to being able to summon Kuraz
with her effect, which is one of the only 2400 Monarchs that does something on special summon,
popping two cards on field and giving their controllers the ability to draw one free.

Each.
Domain though would cost some very heavy concessions in the base build as it was designed.
As giving up your extra deck not only reduces your options in any scenario,
but also flags to your opponent exactly what deck you were playing.

When starting a match, the count for your extra deck is public knowledge prior

On top of that, Domain Monarch was a bit of a brick fest,

And with no extra deck to back it up, that could leave one dead in the water.
That isn't to say the deck wasn't powerful, as it was,

But, there was another version of Monarch waiting below the surface

#### YCS Atlanta

YCS Atlanta would be held a week later on February 6th, and that's right.
Pepe had hit the most representation in a top 32 cut a deck had ever had at this point in history,

Pendulum as a strategy in general was rampaging as the tipping point had clearly been hit,

`Guiding Ariadne` would be a tech that saw success thanks to searching for `Solemn Strike`

giving another layer to the end board of the rank four toolbox.
Dark Pepe would also be a variant that rose here
thanks to `Armageddon Knight` and Zephyros the Elite,

Jose Lagunes would take the event on Pepe, notably opting to not load up on any fancy techs,

Monarch would manage to take two top spots here, but falling completely out by top 16.
With `Majesty's Fiend` being the big counter pick for the various pendulum swarming effects

Kozmo would also squeak in a single top spot here,
notably playing `Anti-Spell Fragrance` in the main to potentially block out the pendulum scales

It was clear that Pepe was a menace and needed to be dealt with, and interestingly enough,
Konami would deal with it in the most bizarre way we've seen yet.

#### Adjusted list (February 8th)

Back on February 4th, Konami would release a blog post to their official site,
notably including what they were referring to as an adjusted list
that took effect directly following YCS Atlanta on February 8th.
This list would be in force for all official events at the regional level and above,
but leave it as optional for all card shops,
allowing them to keep the previous list without losing their OTS status.
The adjusted list would make six hits in total, being three bans and three limits,
all targeted at Pepe, banning Plushfire, Damage Juggler, and Ptolemaeus,
and limiting Skullcrobat Joker, Monkeyboard, and Luster Pendulum.
This was a massive knee cap to the strategy as the Performage engine as it was died with this list,
leaving the only real option still worth considering being Trick Clown for certain strategies,
as well as the death of Infinity's usage in rank four decks.
However, this would not be the end of Pendulum as the strategy was still left with plenty
of options to rebuild with, and that would be helped along by the announcement of an errata
to Pendulum Sorcerer, correcting its mistranslation and now letting it search
for all cards popped rather than just monsters, fixing its major error.
This would leave the game in completely uncharted territory with the vast majority
of shops adopting the adjusted list immediately due to it dealing with Pepe's oppression
with only a handful not adopting it, leaving most to question why the list was done this way at all,
leaving the entire community to watch the next couple of set releases and events
to see what direction the game would take.

### Wing Raiders

- Release date: February 12th, 2016
- Set type: deck building set
- Major strategies: Phantom Knight, Raidraptor, Super Quant
- Impact: a new hybrid threat will rise

Wing Raiders would be the third attempt at bringing the deck building set concept to the masses,
and it would not disappoint, bringing two new archetypes in the main block of support needed
for an existing one.
Raidraptor was the existing archetype, debuting back in Secrets of Eternity consisting
of a series of level four Winged Beast aimed at Xyz Summoning,
then repeatedly ranking up into their more impressive bosses with Rank-Up-Magic spells.
Tribute Lanius can dump a Raidraptor card from deck to turn it Summon
and searches a Rank-Up-Magic in Main Phase 2 if it destroyed something in battle that turn.

Last Strix can summon itself when a Raidraptor battles and you have a spell or trap
on field or in grave.
It can tribute itself to summon a Raidraptor Xyz from Extra Deck with its effects negated,
returning it in the End Phase.
Force Strix is a Rank 4 that can detach to search a level four Dark Winged Beast.

Meant to tie in their anime users boss monster of `Dark Rebellion Xyz Dragon` from New Challengers.
Their traps fall into two categories, those that summon themselves as monsters and those

With Fog Blade able to negate a monster's effect on field and blocking it from attacking

Lastly were the Super Quants, a series of Power Ranger inspired monsters and their mechs,

Red Layer is the level five, can special summon itself if you control no monsters,

Green Layer is the level four, summons a Super Quant from hand when summoned,

`Super Quantal Fairy Alphan` is the enabler for the deck,

to Xyz summon `Super Quantal Mech King Great Magnus`,

With two or more letting it detach to quick effect spin a card back into deck,

and six or more blocking the opponent from adding cards from deck to hand by card effect.

Magnus was without question what you were building towards,
being effectively a Towers if you could get it to four materials,

### Millennium Pack

- Release date: April 15th, 2016
- Set type: import set
- Major strategies: cards from the original anime
- Impact: how far can you go for a draw three?

Millennium Pack would be yet another side set aimed at printing niche cards from the original anime,
similar to Dragons of Legend before it.
And for the most part, would be forgotten in the long term as just another side set
in the sea of new products from the Arc-V era.
However, there were two specific cards that appeared on this particular set
that would catch the eye of the casual observer.
And they would both provide a significant benefit to the user for a heavy cost.

The first of these was `Left Arm Offering`, allowing you to,
if you have two or more cards in hand and are fine with a lock from setting cards that turn,
banish your entire hand to search for any spell from your deck.
While this was insane on paper, the practicality of it would be immediately suspect.
As locking you from setting first meant you couldn't save any part of the hand,
as it all had to go for the search, which would see some experimentation,
but for the time being was too heavy a cost for the insanely powerful upside.
On the other hand, get it?

`Card of Demise` could draw until you had three cards in hand.
Blocks the opponent from taking more damage that turn,
locks you from special summoning for the entire turn, and discards the entire hand in the end phase.
This card, though, didn't block you from setting,
which opened an entire can of worms onto the format known as Demise decks.

Decks that specialize in setting a ton of back row, getting up to a draw three off,
and then setting more.
Standardly paired with a strategy that could take advantage of the back row
to either play a stun game more often than not, like in the case of Demise Qliphort,
or to fuel massive recursion plays, such as the case of Demise Kozmo.
Both of these strategies would be seen as immediately meta-viable thanks to the spell.
Though, it would take a little to truly catch on as Qliphort was decently out
of the meta at this point, and Kozmo had a whole different thing going on with Tin Can Turbo.
Though, soon would adapt.

### Shining Victories

- Release date: May 6th, 2016
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Blue-Eyes, Amorphage, Lunalight
- Impact: a custom-built meta breaker

Shining Victories would be the second core set of the year,
and it was pretty much billed as the Blue-Eyes set.
So, let's just dive into them.
Blue-Eyes would see a massive overhaul to its support,
seeing an entire archetype of sorts spawn around its previous pieces from the old saga
of Blue-Eyes structure deck back in the 5D's era.

`Dragon Spirit of White` was always considered a Blue-Eyes card,
is treated as a normal monster in hand and grave, banishes a spell trap when summoned,
and contribute itself on quick effect to summon the OG Blue-Eyes from hand.
`Sage with Eyes of Blue` searches a level 1 light tuner on normal.
Yes, that does include Veiler, and can be discarded to turn an effect monster you control
into a Blue-Eyes monster from your deck, which conveniently triggers Maiden's summon effect,
too, since it targets an effect monster.

`The White Stone of Ancients`, the turn it's sent to grave,
summons a Blue-Eyes from deck if it's still in grave at the end of the turn,
and can banish itself from grave to recur a Blue-Eyes monster.
`Blue-Eyes Twin Burst Dragon` must be fused with two original Blue-Eyes or by sending two
to grave from field, can't be destroyed by battle, can attack twice on monsters,
and banishes anything it attacks but doesn't destroy.
`Blue-Eyes Spirit Dragon` is a level nine Synchro that takes a tuner and a Blue-Eyes,
blocks both players from summoning multiple monsters at the same time,
can negate a grave effect once per turn,
and tribute itself on quick effect to summon a light dragon Synchro from extra deck except itself,
destroying it in the end phase.
This support was honestly really solid.

Sage had insane versatility in that it could search for a stone or Veiler depending on the open,
or could be discarded to convert a monster into Dragon Spirit from deck,
which in turn let you answer backrow in a safer manner since Spirit could just tag out.
Stone also meshed well with the previous White Stone of Legend as an insanely valuable Cards
of Consonance target, getting another body at the end phase or recursion for a burned piece,
which could both be used on different turns.
Spirit Dragon, though, was the major talking point,
as its effect was almost hand-crafted for the current meta.

Blocking multiple special summons at once was a clear middle finger to Pendulums as a mechanic,
and the grave negation effect seemed tailor-made for a match-up like PK Fire.
But at this time, Blue-Eyes would be nothing more than a rogue option.
While all this sounds good on paper, at the end of the day,
you're still very heavily reliant on the original vanilla Blue-Eyes,
which reminder is a level eight vanilla, a brick in most cases, as well as Maiden,
who used to be a decent option to stall or set up with,
but now most decks might be running engines that require bricks at this stage,
but it doesn't mean that they're always chomping at the bit to use them.
And without a consistent way to access Spirit Dragon outside of Sage and Pray,
the deck wasn't going anywhere for now.

Lunalight is a series of Beast-Warriors centered around combat and Fusion Summons,
with each of their Fusions ranking up off of each other to get more more powerful combat effects,
but quite notably they were clunky and not worth the investment due to being easily dealt
with before reaching the battle phase.
The only notable points to the support here were Black Sheep being a searcher for Poly
that was itself searchable with Tenki and Tiger,
whose revival effect being a soft once per turn meant
that it could theoretically provide an insane amount of recursion with cards
that bounce it back to hand like Zephyros.
But, for now, was left with no good targets to revive.

Amorphage was a series of Dragon Pendulums that all lock special summons from the extra deck
when pendulum summoned or flipped face-up for the lower levels and in general for the higher.
Because this lock didn't require a pendulum summon for the higher levels,
Sloth here would see some play in decks thanks to it being a one tribute monster
that you could search with various pendulum search cards that you could tribute summon at the end
of a combo.
`Performapal Odd-Eyes Light Phoenix` and Unicorn's effects mostly don't matter outside
of niche circumstances where Light Phoenix stays in scale to chump block for you.

What matters here is that they are both Performapal and Odd-Eyes monsters that fill both a low
and high scale requirement, meaning that `Sky Iris` can find its way back in a pendulum list now
that it can search something relevant that isn't named Pendulum Dragon.
`Kozmoll Dark Lady` is a level five pilot that can pay a thousand to negate
and destroy a monster effect with a standard pilot tack out.
While disappointing that Dark Lady was the only relevant new card Kozmo would receive in this wave,
Dark Planet looks so cool, but it's just so bad.
It did solve a massive issue in the Tin Can Turbo variant of the deck.
That being a monster that you can search with Tincan
to immediately summon after before Tincan gets banished by E-Tele's drawback.

Dark Lady giving the Kozmo board a monster negate also provides them more versatility
in their revival game plan as you can negate a monster with Dark Lady without needing
to waste a valuable Dark Destroyer pop on them.
Then, potentially tack her into a Dark Destroyer later anyways.
Thunder King, the Lightning Strike Kaiju, is now the strongest Kaiju at 3,300 attack.
That's it.
It's just a better card to summon with IKES now.

`Angel Trumpeter` is a level four normal tuner,
meaning that for decks already experimenting with `Unexpected Dai`, namely that of Pendulums,
they could easily tech this in as a one-off for easier access to things like Ignister Prominence,
which with a limited Luster is appreciated.
`Lector Pendulum, the Dracoverlord`, destroys both itself and a Pendulum at the start
of the damage step when battling, giving another solid Dracoverlord target for `Draco Face-Off`,
which would have probably been more relevant had the spell not just been limited.
Ghost Reaper and Winter Cherries can, if the opponent has more monsters,
discard itself to reveal an Extra Deck monster and banish all copies of it
from the opponent's Extra Deck.
This second addition to the Ghost Girl lineup held some interesting applications
in the current meta game thanks to the Extra Deck standardly centralizing pool as of late.

Primarily with applications against Burning Abyss specifically,
as by cutting off a BA player's access to Dante, you also cut them off of Beatrice.
Still, most decks in the meta were not as heavily reliant on one specific card in the Extra.
So, for now, she'd be relegated to a side deck pick at best.

`Crystal Wing Synchro Dragon` is a Level 8 Synchro requiring a Tuner and a non-Tuner Synchro.
Can negate and destroy a monster using its effect once per turn and gain its attack for the turn.
And if it battles a Level 5 or higher, gains that monster's attack.
Talk about an upgrade.

Crystal Wing was everything Clear Wing was and more,
with the only downside being that it now requires a Synchro as material to make,
making it much harder to access than desired.
But luckily, there was already a solution to that in the card pool.
Because it was a Level 8 Dragon Synchro, Tzolkin had found its target,
able to cheat out Crystal Wing in decks that could make it,
though none had really become known by this point.

`Digital Bug Corebage` would be one of the payoffs to the Digital Bug archetype that debuted here,
being a Rank 5 that could also rank up over a Rank 3 or 4 Insect by detaching two materials.
Able to detach one to spin away a defense mode monster,
able to reattach a material from grave if a monster changes battle position on field.
This, interestingly enough, could rank up over Gigabrilliant,
a generic Rank 3 that was already seeing the occasional play in Burning Abyss builds,
giving the Rank 3 powerhouse yet another generic tool.

Raidraptor Ultimate Falcon requires three Level 10 Winged Beasts to summon, or more simply,
any number of the Rank-Up-Magic Spells being used on a Rank 8 Raidraptor is unaffected

drop all opponents by 1,000 attack or burn the opponent for 1,000
if they control no face-up monsters.
`Rank-Up-Magic Skip Force` lets you rank up a Raidraptor Xyz two ranks and can banish itself
and a Raidraptor in grave to revive a Raidraptor Xyz.
Between Skip Force and Soul Shave Force, Raidraptor had seen a boon in new good Rank-Up Spells,
and Ultimate Falcon was finally a payoff worth ranking up to,
being a Towers that burned each turn or dropped the burn to go for the kill.
While this wouldn't make Raidraptor meta,
it would solidify the deck as a solid rogue to tier three option,
not able to rise up further due to the ever-present Kaijus.

`Pre-Preparation of Rites` can search a Ritual Spell from deck and a monster listed on
that Ritual Spell from grave or deck.
That's right, we buffed old Rituals.
Take that, Nekroz.
In reality, a buff to the old way of Ritual Summoning is honestly a really great design,
but it still sat in the space of useless for now.

Almost all of the relevant Rituals in 2016 were either tied to an archetype
of generic Ritual Spells, ala Nekroz or Gishki,
or were far too slow or weak to justify running in the first place.
This would grow in power, though, as new Ritual designs would keep this particular card in mind,
specifically in the fact that it doesn't have to mention the monster in the summon effect,
it just needs to mention it, period.
`Drowning Mirror Force` completes the Elemental Retrains of `Mirror Force` series by only
being activatable when a direct attack is declared, spinning away all attack position monsters.
Without question, the best payoff for any of these cards,
even beating out the shockingly good `Quaking Mirror Force`,
but with the downside of needing the opponent to attack directly,
who is going to play something like this?
Yeah, so Demise decks already want to draw almost entirely backrow and very little to no monsters,
so Drowning would fit perfectly into their lineups, which we'd see soon enough.

Lastly, `Fire King Island` could pop a monster on field or in hand to search a Fire King monster
or could summon a Fire Winged Beast from hand once per turn.
Nuking your monsters have sent to grave or banished.
While a solid card for Fire Kings, giving the deck the ability to search for its key pieces
while triggering their pop effects, the strategy still wasn't going anywhere
with its current game plan.
However, Island popping cards in the hand was such a unique effect to give
to an easily accessible field spell that it got the wheels turning.

What all cards can trigger when destroyed by card effect while in hand?
That's right, you bet.
Kozmo.

Every single ship's floating effect did not require it to be on field first,
meaning you could pop a ship in hand to float into something like Tincan,
which in turn started your entire play line.
But it couldn't be that powerful, could it?

#### YCS Providence

YCS Providence would be held 3 weeks after Shining Victories release

Kozmo had taken 13 of the top 32, with at least four of those being a new variant
in Fire King Kozmo.
Island had clearly made an impact on the strategy,
seeing a single copy played to where it could be searched by `Terraforming`
and it was a full access to your Kozmo engine without the need to commit your normal summon
or play E Tele.
In addition, it was fairly simple to get the Island off the board
so it wouldn't be a detriment later.
As by popping a ship with it to float into Sliprider, you could pop the Island with it,
which in turn would pop the Slip to then float into a Pilot
to start your lines without the Island potentially nuking the board later.

Demise Kozmo would also see its first real success here,
playing a slimmed-down Pilot and Ship spread to make room for more Revival Traps,
Floodgates, Responses, and of course `Card of Demise`,
which could net massive draws for the deck given the right opening.
Speaking of Demise, Qliphort would find a top spot here sporting Demise as well,
also loading up on plenty of Qliphort tailored floodgates
like `Re-qliate` and `Rivalry of Warlords`,
in addition to the new `Drowning Mirror Force`.
Pendulum strategies would see a wide variety of tops here,
ranging from your more standard Draco Pals variant to a couple of Majespecter variants
and even a Pendulum Magician variant.

The Draco Pals and Magician variant specifically though were now sporting `Sky Iris`,
not only as a way to protect the scales,
but also to access the new Odd-Eyes scales of Light Phoenix and Unicorn,
rounding out their scale lines and giving yet another way to pop a pen scale.
Extra Deck Monarchs had overtaken their Domain brethren to rule the slice of the pie chart,
but a very specific development here would shift the deck structure even more
into the insane pile it was becoming.
`Brilliant Fusion`, a card we've mentioned a couple times at this point,
but not really had a reason to focus on in the meta game, now had a true home and purpose.
Because the engine sent a light for part of Seraphinite's requirement,
you could send an Edea for the summon, which
in turn would cycle back a Monarch spell trap that's banished,
but that's easily the more minor of the three benefits.

Seraphinite providing an extra normal summon was also insanely powerful for the strategy,
giving access to multiple Monarch bodies on the first turn,
which also meant that Erebus could now reliably hand rip twice on the first turn,
which was also a great benefit.
The arguably strongest part, though, was one that was pretty basic, but effective.
Seraphinite is a level five.
Because of this, and Prime Monarch already abusing the rank five pool,
Seraphinite could easily be turned into half of Pleiades once the extra normal was used.
And as such, the engine would become far more popular for Monarchs as the WCQ season spun up.

Tommy Zamen would take the event on PK Fire,
playing a mostly standard build to what we've come to expect from the strategy.
Notably, trimming the Ragged Gloves count down to one as it was easily the worst
of the three Phantom Knight monsters in the main.

#### Dueling Network shutdown

While we do need to still talk about two more WCQs and a set release,
I'm going to pause here to specifically talk about a historic moment
in the Yugioh community's history here that needs to be at the very least mentioned.
Many simulators for online play have risen and fallen in the history of the game,
as they either get outpaced by advancing technology, the dev teams leave them to rot,
or in very rare circumstances, a company orders them to shut down.
Dueling Network had been around since 2011,
and up until this point in history had been the go-to platform for testing and deck theorizing,
but had over the last year undergone some legal issues stemming around the usage
of the official card art for the game from the company Nihon Ad Systems, or NAS for short,
who is the production company in charge of the Yu-Gi-Oh! anime.
Though they had tried to comply by removing all art of the game from the site,
a cease and desist motion was filed, and on July 6th, 2016, Dueling Network,
as it was known, was fully taken offline.
This would send a shockwave through the community.
As well some other simulators existed for testing,
none were as widely used and accepted as Dueling Network.

And while a successor would launch about 6 months later, being Dueling Book,
which is still used to this day, it would still be a moment that hit almost everyone
in the community.
With all of that out of the way, there's also a set released just 2 days after the DN takedown.
And this would be the one chance of shaking things up
in the meta before the remaining two WCQs could take place.

### Rise of the True Dragons Structure Deck

- Release date: July 8th, 2016
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: dragon
- Impact: a single new spell for Blue-Eyes

Rise of the True Dragons would be the second of the structure deck R series in the OCG,
modernizing the Felgrand structure, and man, did it fail hard.
The main goal of the deck is to use their level 4 warriors to equip one of their bosses
from deck to themselves, then tribute themselves off to revive them.
As the target of their revival effect isn't chosen until after you tribute.
This is done to facilitate Arc Brave Dragon,
who banishes all of an opponent's back row on summon from grave,
and can revive a level 7 or 8 dragon the turn after it's put in grave,
and `Divine Dragon Lord Felgrand`, who banishes the monster on summon from grave,
and revives a level 7 or 8 dragon on destroying something in battle.
While interesting, the issue stood in that the warriors can't activate their effects until you have
at least one dragon in grave, which the deck did nothing to really circumvent outside
of reprinting `Foolish Burial`, which was limited.
However, a new spell that would be interesting here was `Return of the Dragon Lords`,
being a `Monster Reborn` for level 7 and 8 dragons,
and can be banished from grave to prevent a dragon's destruction.

This would be recognized as a perfect addition to Blue-Eyes specifically,
as the revival conditions matched up perfectly with Blue-Eyes main deck pieces
and gave the dragons great protection to use after.
Though Blue-Eyes still wasn't meta viable with this.

### The Dark Side of Dimensions Movie Pack

- Release date: July 22nd, 2016
- Set type: import set
- Major strategies: Blue-Eyes, Gadget, Cubic
- Impact: skewing the balance of Worlds

The DSOD movie pack is interesting to talk about in retrospect because of the timing of it all.
In the OCG, it was released alongside the movie of the same name,
bringing the cards from the movie to the OCG on the same day as the movie's release.
For other countries though, DSOD wasn't set to be released until 2017.
So, why debut the tie-in movie set so early in the TCG?

Well, the prevailing theory is that Konami rushed this set out because of a certain set
of support that would affect of decks performance at Worlds.
Blue-Eyes would receive the side of its new major support wave here just in time.
Neo Ultimate is a retrain of the original Ultimate, taking the same materials,
able to send a Blue-Eyes fusion from extra to grave after it attacks while it's your only monster
to attack again, able to use this effect twice in a turn,
and can banish itself from grave to negate and destroy anything that targets a Blue-Eyes.

Chaos Max is a level eight ritual that can't be targeted and deals double piercing damage.
Lastly, Alternative White Dragon must first be summoned by revealing an original Blue-Eyes in hand,
becomes the original Blue-Eyes on field and in grave,
and can pop a monster once per turn in exchange for its attack.
This changed everything for Blue-Eyes, as you now had a reliable monster
to summon alongside your level one tuners to access Spirit Dragon on the first turn.
This also pulled Melody of Awakening Dragon out of the bulk pile at last because it searched
for Alternative and the OG Blue-Eyes, giving you the ability to summon it out immediately.
On the more niche side of things, Chaos Max was also a boss worth considering
as it could be summoned with `Advanced Ritual Art` by dumping an OG Blue-Eyes from deck,
giving an option to absolutely obliterate more defensive decks.
It's fair to say that this support was the reason the pack was rushed out so fast,
making the strategy completely legal for Worlds, which we'd see soon enough.

The rest of the pack was dedicated to other strategies from DSOD, specifically that of Cubic,
a stall focused strategy focused around Vijam,
who can't be destroyed in battle and locks up monsters at battles, and the Magician Girls,
a series of spellcasters that loop with one another to fill up the board.
The only other notable cards here would be that of `Gold Gadget` and `Silver Gadget`,
who summoned a level four machine from hand on summon and float into any other gadget
on destruction, which would see some experimentation for now,
but not widespread success until much later in the year.
This wouldn't be the only new set before worlds though,
as the next core set would also debut beforehand,
but whether it would be impactful to the world scene would have to be seen.

### The Dark Illusion

- Release date: August 5th, 2016
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: `Dark Magician`, Metalfoes, Paleozoic
- Impact: laying the groundwork for future metas

The `Dark Illusion` was the third core set of 2016,
and while it wouldn't be as explosive as BOSH or the fourth core set of the year,
TDIL would lay the groundwork for multiple meta archetypes that would take hold later
in the year and beyond.
Following the archetypes spawned around Blue-Eyes,
Dark Magician would receive a wave of legacy support centered around the titular card.
`Magician of Dark Illusion` becomes `Dark Magician` on field,
can summon itself on the opponent's turn when you use a spell trap effect,
and can summon a `Dark Magician` from grave when you use a spell trap effect once while on field.

`Magician's Robe` during the opponent's turn can discard a spell trap to summon `Dark Magician`
from deck, and can summon itself from grave on the opponent's turn when you use a spell trap effect.
`Magician's Rod` searches a spell trap that mentions `Dark Magician` on normal,
and can recur itself on the opponent's turn when you use a spell trap effect by tributing a monster.
`Ebon High Magician` is a spellcaster locked rank seven that can use spell traps from hand
on the opponent's turn by detaching material for each,
and floats into a Dark Spellcaster in deck on removal, then pops a card on field.
Their spell traps all revolve around the original `Dark Magician`,
with `Illusion Magic` able to tribute a spellcaster to search up to two Dark Magicians
from deck or grave.

Dark Magic Expand gaining more effects for each `Dark Magician` or girl on field or in grave,
with one plus boosting a Dark Spellcaster by a thousand,
two plus locking the opponent from chaining to spell trap effects and preventing them
from destruction, and three plus making dark spell casters you control immune to card effects.
And `Magician Navigation`, summoning a `Dark Magician` from hand and a level seven
or lower dark spell caster from deck, able to banish from grave to negate the effects
of a face-up spell trap while you control `Dark Magician`.
Lastly, `Dark Magical Circle` looks at the top three of the deck on activation,
adding a `Dark Magician` or spell trap that mentions it to hand from those
and places the others back in any order.
And while on field, banishes an opponent's card once per turn when you summon a `Dark Magician`.

The strategy had a clear direction here,
with Circle Control easily being the name of the game with its banish effect.
But the issue lied in consistent access to `Dark Magician` reliably.
A lot of the decks plays relied heavily on Circle and Navigation,
which in turn meant you consistently needed a `Dark Magician` in hand to trigger Nav
in the first place, which naturally led to bricking galore.
This would leave Dark Magician in the position of a rogue deck at best for now,
but a note should be made here about the requirement to search cards
that listed `Dark Magician` specifically.
This was clearly put in place to make the new support able
to search older cards like `Thousand Knives` and `Dark Magic Curtain`,
but left a very specific casualty in the funniest way possible.

`The Eye of Timaeus`, despite having `Dark Magician` written on the card,
could not be searched with any of the support due to it not listing `Dark Magician` specifically,
as it said a `Dark Magician` monster.
This would be a bit of a logistical mess in the short term,
but a funny trivia fact looking back now.
Metalfoes was a new series of normal Pendulums,
all sharing an effect to pop a face-up card once per turn in the Pendulum Scale
to set a Metalfoes spell trap from deck.
With Steelen being a level two scale eight, Silverd a level three scale one,
Goldriver a level four scale one, and Volflame a level seven scale eight.
Their traps were all focused on furthering the popping effects,
with Counter able to summon a Metalfoes from deck when a card you control is popped,
able to be banished from grave to recur a Metalfoes in face-up extra,
and Combination able to summon a Metalfoes from grave
with a lower level than a Fusion Summon Metalfoes on summon,
and searches the Metalfoes monster when sent to grave.
Their primary game plan involves using the various pops and pendulum summons
to facilitate their main spell `Metalfoes Fusion` with a standard Fusion effect
and the ability to put itself from grave on bottom of deck to draw one.

Adamante is a vanilla Fusion of a Metalfoes and a 2500 or less attack monster.
Crimsonite is a vanilla Fusion of a Metalfoes and two 3000 or less attack monsters.
Orichalc is a Fusion of any two Metalfoes that gives all Metalfoes double piercing
and pops a card when sent to grave.

Metalfoes here poised a compelling strategy,
being similar to Igknight but with a set of bosses that didn't hamper the deck's long game.
Though at this stage would be relegated to tier three as its bosses weren't the most meta relevant,
but would see experimentation as cards that can pop your monsters that want to be popped such
as Kozmo or even Yang Zing as the pop and set effects were simultaneous,
meaning that Yang Zing's wouldn't miss timing.
With a third block of sets for the era, once again,
the TCG exclusives would change over to two new archetypes
with both receiving four cards a set now rather than one getting five while the other gets three.

The first of these was Subterror, bringing yet another attempt at revolutionizing flip monsters
for the modern day with a bunch of high-level behemoths.
Each of the behemoths shares an effect to special summon themselves from hand
when your last face-up monster on field flips face-down
and can flip themselves face-down once per turn.
Umastryx on flip banishes an opponent's monster and Stalagmo on flip discards a Subterror monster
to draw two.

Nemesis Warrior would be the starter of sorts, able to dump a Subterror from deck,
tribute himself and another monster whose level are equal to or more than the sent monster,
and summon it either face-up or face-down.
Able to summon itself from grave if a behemoth gets flipped face-up.
Lastly, the Hidden City.

THEY'RE CRAZY.
THEY THINK THEIR CITY'S HIDDEN.
BRO, IT'S RIGHT THERE.
I SEE IT JUST FINE.

Searches a Subterror on activation, can flip a Subterror face-up once per turn,
and can negate an attack by flipping a set Subterror face-up.
While this set of cards was interesting for sure,
there wasn't a clear direction to take the strategy out the gate like there was with BA or Kozmo,
and it was clear that Konami was favoring this archetype at the time by giving them two ultras
and a secret rare between these four.
So, most would invest immediately into this archetype with the hopes that waves two,
three, or four would get it there.
As for now, it wasn't even good enough to be rogue.

The other archetype was SPYRAL, which was clearly centered around Super Agent,
who could summon itself from hand if you called the super type of the top card
of the opponent's deck correctly, popping a spell trap if summoned by a SPYRAL effect.
GEAR - Drone, able to rearrange the top three of the opponent's deck on summon,
contributed itself on quick effect to boost a SPYRAL by 500 for each card the opponent controls,
and can banish itself and a SPYRAL card in grave to recur a Super Agent.
Quik-Fix can search a SPYRAL GEAR on summon,
and can summon itself from grave for a discard while you control Super Agent.
And GEAR - Big Red can revive a SPYRAL and blocks it from being destroyed in battle.

SPYRAL was underplayed in its hype on reveal compared to Subterror,
but on initial inspection looked to be the better of the two archetypes,
being a far more generically usable engine of sorts.
With Super Agent providing board swarm and spell trap removal,
and Quik-Fix for some reason being a machine type,
meaning that it was abusable with `Machine Duplication`.
While unassuming now, many would pick up a set of these on release thanks
to the potential these initial cards held with just a little shaping.
In the OCG import slots though, we'd also see two archetypes.

One of which was a favorite of the community at this stage that we'd been expecting
for far too long.
Trains were a series of cards not really designated by an archetype,
but rather a designation of being level four or 10 Earth Machines.
These had been introduced back in Zexal and popularized slightly by Gustav Max
as a burn tool for FTK decks.
But here they get the couple of pieces the strategy had been missing for a while now.

`Heavy Freight Train Derricrane` can summon itself from hand if you summon an Earth Machine,
and can pop a card when detached from an Xyz.
`Revolving Switchyard` is a field spell that summons a level four Earth Machine from deck
when you summon a level 10, then boost its level up to 10,
and can discard a card to search a level 10 Earth Machine.
Number 81 Superdreadnought Rail Cannon Super Dora is a rank 10 that can detach
on quick effect to make a monster unaffected by card effects that turn.
While it seems odd that I'm focusing on these so much,
the OCG got these cards back in Secrets of Eternity.
And off of that, a deck known as Shaddoll Trains was heavily popularized
and actually did quite well for the time thanks to Shekhinaga,
who itself is a level 10 Earth Machine.
And the deck had been popular for online simulators in the TCG as well for some time.

But with the hits to Shaddoll already present and these coming to us too late,
it was clear that trains would be relegated to a rogue strategy at best.
The other OCG import strategy, oddly enough being a Korean exclusive import, was Paleozoic,
a series of traps that all share the effect to summon themselves back as level two water aquas
from the grave and chained to a trap, with all being unaffected by monster effects when you did.
Olenoides pops a spell trap, Hallucigenia halves a monster's attack and defense that turn,
Canadia flips an opponent's monster to face-down defense, Pikaia discards a Paleozoic to draw two,
and Anomalocaris is a three-material rank two that's unaffected by monster effects,
lets you excavate one and add to hand if it's a trap or send to grave otherwise
when a trap is sent to grave, and can detach to pop a card on quick effect if it has trap
as material.
While interesting for sure, Paleozoic didn't do enough on their own right now
to be considered for the meta, though many would be keeping an eye on them for the future
if the remainder of their support got imported.

`D/D Savant Thomas` in the scale can recur a face-up D/D pendulum from extra deck,
and on field can pop a D/D in scale to summon a level eight D/D/D from deck,
giving D/Ds an option to actually access Oblivion King Abyss Ragnarok semi-reliably,
giving D/D a slight boost into the meta.
Blackwing Gofu the Vague Shadow could summon itself from hand while you control no monsters,
optionally summoning two tokens when it did,
and could banish itself and any number of non-tuners to revive a Blackwing Synchro of that level,
making it a tuner.
I don't believe anyone has ever used that last effect,
as the first effect was actually surprisingly useful for various decks.

The new Metalfoes clearly could use it with the tokens as fodder for their search pops
and a level five tuner could be used to access level seven and eight synchros reliably.
Monarch wanted it as a better level five tuner to play than Quickdraw,
immediately being slotted into variants for Tzolkin,
and even those without it as a Cyber Dragon-like card,
and a couple of other decks aimed at summoning multiple copies of Omega and Trishula
to hand loop going first.
And this will not be the last time we talk about this card.
As while it was already solidly useful,
within one year it would become one of the most ban-worthy cards the game had seen in a long time.
But I'm getting ahead of myself.

`True King Agnimazud, the Vanisher`, would be the first of the new True King archetype,
able to summon itself from hand by popping two monsters on field or in hand, including a fire.
Banished a monster when summoned this way and recurred a non-FIRE Wyrm if both pops were fire.
Though interesting for sure, this card was a bit too early to the party to be useful yet.
But let's put a pin in that and we'll circle back to it in 2017.

`Shiranui Solitaire` contribute a zombie to summon tuner with zero defense from deck,
and if banished can summon a banished Shiranui monster.
This was the only card Shiranui needed to be relevant, and boy was it.
By tributing itself, you could summon `Uni-Zombie` from deck,
who in turn put a Spectralsword from deck into grave to make it level four.

From there, Spectralsword could banish itself and Solitaire to summon a level six synchro,
namely Samuraisaga, then use Solitaire's other effect to resummon the Spectralsword
to synchro into an eight and set up the grave for a Shogunsaga the following turn.
While this sounds like it's just a great day for zombie lovers all around, of course,
there's some degeneracy attached to this.
While one would assume they'd summon more in-archetype pieces this way,
or maybe even a cool zombie or two, this combo was used to reliably access multiple copies
of Omega and Trishula on turn one to hand rip the opponent.
I mean, they really did make it easy, didn't they?

One card access to a level eight synchro and a level four or five tuner,
what more could you ask for?
This would, unfortunately, be a decently popular option in the upcoming meta game
and would be a deck that would have to be accounted for in multiple meta games moving forward.
Speaking of, `Block Dragon` can be special summoned by banishing three Earths from hand or grave,
blocks all destruction except battle destruction,
and if sent to grave searches up to three rocks whose total level equals eight.
This card, on release, was a complete flop as there was not a single rock archetype
or deck that was good enough to include this and be meta.
Though some would try this with Triamids from the same set and with the prior rock stunt,
but both would come up short.
Though this card would be seen again.

`Fairy Tail - Snow`, on summon, flips an opponent's monster face down and can be summoned
from grave by banishing seven cards from hand, field, or grave.
Oh, I get it, like the seven dwarves.
Snow is a weird card to talk about in a vacuum as it was recognized as good out the gate,
being constantly recurrable by banishing used cards,
but for the time being was relegated to an option for decks that specialized
in milling like Lightsworn.
And that is something we will have to discuss later as it's a whole 'nother can of worms.

`Coral Dragon` is a level six synchro tuner that can discard a card to pop a card and
when sent to grave draws a card.
Simple, effective, and gave decks with easy level six synchro access to Tzolkin.
Yeah, because of `Stardust Charge Warrior`,
a common practice moving forward for decks that can make two level six synchros is
to make these two to access Tzolkin and Crystal Wing thanks to the draws they provide,

`Cosmic Cyclone`, for the cost of a thousand life points, can banish a spell trap,

in a far stronger way, which would see serious play following release.
`Card of the Soul` could search any monster whose total attack

`Floodgate Trap Hole` could flip any summoned monster face down permanently,

Lastly, and without question the most hotly debated card of the time,

This would be a card that caused massive divide in the player base immediately on whether

### Structure Deck: Yugi Muto and Structure Deck: Seto Kaiba

- Release date: October 21st, 2016
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Magnet Warrior, ABC
- Impact: The Machine Uprising

Structure Decks Yugi and Kaiba were incredibly interesting releases
as the last time we had seen a structure deck explicitly tied to a character was Marik back
in the 5D's era.
Although you could make an argument that Master of Pendulum was that for Yuya just last year.
As they were tied to the titular characters,
many expected on initial announcement that these would be further support
for both's key strategies of Dark Magician and Blue-Eyes,
but they were actually instead a complete overhaul to key archetypes they both used back
in Battle City, being Magnet Warrior for Yugi and XYZ for Kaiba.

Starting with Yugi, the Magnet Warrior archetype would get an overhaul
with the Electromagnet Warriors, each able to tribute themselves on the opponent's turn
to summon a level four Magnet Warrior from deck with Alpha searching a Magna Warrior on summon,
Beta searching a Magna Warrior on summon, and Gamma summoning a Magnet Warrior from hand on summon.
These also combine together to form Berserkion, the Electromagna Warrior,
able to summon itself by banishing the three Electromagnets from hand, field, or grave,
can banish a Magna Warrior in grave to pop a card,
and when destroyed summons the three Electromagnets from banishment.
There was even a fusion of the two Magna Warriors, both Berserkion and the original Valkyrion,
being `Imperion Magnum the Superconductive Battlebot`,
who has a once-per-turn omni negate and destroy,
and if removed by opponent's card effect summons the two Magna Warriors from hand or deck.

`Magnetic Field` was a field spell that could summon a Magnet Warrior from grave
if you controlled an Earth Rock once-per-turn and can bounce a monster that battles
with an Earth Rock but isn't destroyed.
Lastly, `Magnet Conversion` can recover back three Magnet Warriors on activation
and can banish itself from grave to summon a banished Magnet Warrior.
While absolutely interesting and seemingly competent,
meshing well with the recent `Ties of the Brethren`,
Magnet Warrior would suffer due to three major reasons.
Number one was the reliance on the original Magnet Warrior monsters,
as they were the only level fours available to tag into with the Electromagnet effects,
meaning that in order to have the tag out effect be live, you had to play bricks.
Number two was that while Berserkion was a solid presence, you had to find it to summon it,
and its effect was slow and required the opponent to remove it to float,
being way too reliant on the opponent not knowing how to play around it.
Number three was that Imperion Magnum, while reasonable to get out thanks
to Alpha searching its materials, was extremely resource intensive
for a single negation point whose float effect requiring to hit both targets meant
that you had to play at least two copies of Valkyrion in your deck,
a card that was by all metrics a brick.

And I know I said three reasons, but in reality, there's four.
Because released at the exact same time was a very similar concept to the Magnet Warrior retrains,
but done far, far better.
The Kaiba Structure Deck focused on the XYZ Unions from Battle City,
giving them far better counterparts in A, B, and C, each being a Union monster.
Wow, it's been a while since we've heard that term, and can equip onto a Light Machine,
each having a destruction-tanking effect for its equipped monster, an effect while equipped,
and an effect when sent to grave.
With A making its equipped monster immune to monster effects and recurring a Union
from grave to hand other than itself when sent to grave,
B making its monster immune to spells and searching a Union when sent to grave,
and C making its monster immune to traps and summoning a Union from hand when sent to grave.
These all facilitated the summon of ABC Dragon Buster,
a Fusion who could be summoned by banishing the A, B, and C from field or grave,
could discard and banish a card on a quick effect, and could, on the opponent's turn only,
tribute itself to summon three banished Light Machine Unions with different names.

Similar to Magnet Warriors, A-to-Z Dragon Buster Cannon was a banished contact Fusion
of ABC and the original XYZ.
That has a repeatable omni-negate at the cost of a discard each time,
and can banish itself to resummon both ABC and XYZ from banishment.
`Union Hangar` was their new Field Spell, able to search a Light Machine Union on activation,
and can equip an appropriate Light Machine Union from deck to a Light Machine Union
that is summoned once per turn.

Lastly, `Union Scramble` can summon up to three banished Light Machine Normal or Union monsters
on activation, and can banish itself from grave to recur another to hand.
ABC, to put bluntly, was way better than Magnet Warriors in almost every way.
Their Field Spell let them turbo out two monsters at a time,
their payoff didn't need to be searched first, the payoff's removal was a quick effect,
and in general, Level 4 Machines already have way more existing support than Level 3 Rocks too,
like Gold and `Silver Gadget` for example,
which in turn makes it easier to trigger things like the grave effects of the union
since you can just overlay the equipped monster to put the unions attached in grave.

ABC was immediately recognized as an actually potent deck with ABC itself being the kicker
as once you've used all the unions to go through and summon various rank four bosses,
you cap it off with ABC to add a discarding banish and the ability to re-flood the board again.

#### YCS Liverpool

This deck was entirely predicated on the idea of hand looping the opponent out of the game

The only things holding the deck back from a full meta takeover was the chance of dead hands,

as this deck had so many varying lines to go through that learning it

### Invasion: Vengeance

- Release date: November 4th, 2016
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Metalfoes, Paleozoic, Frog
- Impact: Well, if it isn't frog-ally amazing

I MESSED IT UP.
INOV was the last core set of the year, and its job was fairly simple.
Support the strategies introduced in `Dark Illusion`,
which it handled more effectively than I think any of us at the time were ready for.

Starting with Metalfoes, their support here would include Bismugear,
a new scale eight with the same scale effect, but if popped,
search a Metalfoes monster in the end phase, notably being level one, meaning it was,
at the time, impossible to pendulum summon back out from the extra.
Alkahest, a fusion of a Metalfoes and a normal monster that can quick effect relinquish a monster,
gaining defense equal to its attack, able to use its equipped monster as material
for a Metalfoes fusion.
Mithrilium, a fusion of a Metalfoes and a pendulum,
able to shuffle back two Metalfoes cards in grave to bounce a card,
floating into a Metalfoes pendulum in face-up extra or grave when sent to grave.
And `Fullmetalfoes Fusion`, a quick-play fusion spell for Metalfoes.
This support would change practically everything about the more pure variants of Metalfoes,
giving them interaction on the opponent's turn in Alkahest,
who can now be summoned on the opponent's turn with `Fullmetalfoes Fusion`,
but also in Mithrilium, who not only provides great removal,
she recycles your used spells and traps to then reset with the scale effects,
and her float effect also goes off when used as fusion material,
making her easily the most important piece of the deck now.
This would shoot Metalfoes up the ranks of the meta into a tier one to two position,
able to compete solidly with ABC already in the meta.

SPYRAL would receive a couple of interesting new pieces in Master Plan,
able to search a SPYRAL MISSION and search a `SPYRAL Resort` and a SPYRAL monster when sent
from field to grave.
And `SPYRAL Resort`, which gave your other SPYRAL cards targeting protection,
search to SPYRAL monster once per turn,
and shuffles a monster from grave into deck as maintenance cost to prevent destruction.
While Well, wouldn't do anything in the short term,
Master Plan would be theorized as an insanely powerful piece for the strategy,
able to search three cards by itself.
With the caveat being the deck currently had no reliable way to summon her
or get her off the field reliably.
But if those two hurdles could be cleared,
she had the potential to make SPYRAL a meta threat by herself.

Paleozoic would receive the remainder of its cards here, being Eldonia,
able to boost a monster by 500, Dinomischus, able to banish a face-up card for a discard,
Marrella, able to dump a trap from deck, Leanchoilia, able to return a banished card to grave,
and Opabinia, a rank two that's unaffected by monster effects,
lets you activate Paleozoic traps from hand, and if it has a trap as material,
can detach to search a Paleozoic trap once per turn.
This would do a lot to iron out the flaws in Paleo as a deck,
giving them a proactive play in Opabinia to activate from hand,
as well as better Paleos in Dinomischus and Marrella.
But to say this was all they received would be disingenuous.
As well this was all the archetypal support they got,
there was also another Xyz released here that should be talked about with them.

`Toadally Awesome`, TCG localization at the time was a joke,
is an Aqua locked rank two that can detach in the standby phase to summon a frog from deck,
can once per turn tribute an Aqua for an omni negate and destroy,
then you can set that card to your field, and if sent to grave recurs back a water monster.
This card would be a massive meta shifter,
as with it Paleozoic had an omni negate it could access thanks to the entire archetype
being Aqua level twos.
In addition to a perfect rank two engine it can now access for going first place,
being that of frog.
In addition, `Toadally Awesome`'s omni negate didn't require materials,
meaning that by cheating it out with `Bahamut Shark` you could tribute itself
for its omni negate effect, then put it back in extra with its recursion,
meaning that not only was it a rank two threat,
but any deck with `Bahamut Shark` access could also use it,
which we'd see the implications of with the upcoming YCS's.

`Doki Doki` can discard a rock to summon another from deck with the same level and attribute,
being a decent option for Magnet Warriors for now,
but would be far more useful in a couple of years.
`Mare Mare` was a level seven tuner that could lower its level by one to summon a level one token.
Being an extremely interesting combo enabler when used with Yazi,
and would get far more popular with the next era of the game.

`Starving Venom Fusion Dragon` would be the fourth of the mechanic dragons.
Taking two dark monsters on the field, gains the attack of an opponent special summon monster
on fusion summon, and can copy a level five or higher name and effect for a turn,
and nukes the opponent special summoned monsters when destroyed.
This would be notably the weakest of the four dragons for now,
as the only real card that could make it without sinking too many resources
with `Super Polymerization`, which was currently banned.
Leaving Starving Venom in a weird spot for now.

`Meteor Black Comet Dragon` is a fusion of a level seven Red-Eyes and a level six dragon.
Can dump a Red-Eyes from deck on summon to burn for half its original attack,
and revives a normal monster when sent to grave.
And `Red-Eyes Insight` dumps a Red-Eyes monster from deck to search another Red-Eyes spell trap.
These two would help form an FTK of sorts for Red-Eyes.
Specifically in that `Meteor Black Comet Dragon` summon with `Red-Eyes Fusion`,
and then two copies of `Inferno Fire Blast` would be 8200 damage.
Which while an extremely inconsistent FTK thanks to `Inferno Fire Blast` not being searchable,
was still one to be aware of.

Denglong, first of the Yang Zing, is a level five synchro that searches a Yang Zing card on summon.
Can dump a Wyrm to copy its level, and floats into a Yang Zing when it leaves field.
This first effect would standardly be used to search their new counter trap Nine Pillars.
Which by popping a Yang Zing at the end of its effect was an omni negate and shuffle back.
Making Denglong a level five synchro with a generic omni negate,
which would be attractive for many strategies.

Vermilion Dragon Mech was a TCG exclusive level nine synchro that could banish a tuner
and grave to pop a card, and if destroyed by card effect or card to banish tuner.
Being a solid option for Blue-Eyes.
Lastly, `Dimensional Barrier` could lock the summon and effects of either rituals,
fusions, synchros, Xyz, or pendulums for a turn.
Being an extremely powerful floodgate to an entire mechanic of your choosing,
but was also seen as problematic immediately because it listed all of the mechanics it hit,
meaning that if a new mechanic came up within a year,
as many expected would happen as we were coming up on the three year mark since Pendulums,
Barrier wouldn't affect it.
Still, for now, it was an extremely useful floodgate,
which would find its way into various decks immediately.

#### YCS Rio

YCS Rio would be a week later, and though ABC was clearly still the top dog in the format off
of this, a shakeup had happened with Metalfoes rising up the ladder.
With the additions of Alkahest and Mithrilium,
Metalfoes had ditched some of the more niche options in its arsenal
for more standard pendulum pieces like the Majespecter package and Luster Pendulum,
turning it into the go-to pendulum deck of the format.
`Painful Decision` was also starting to get more popular in the deck
for accessing basically whatever scale you needed, as now with Mithrilium,
the dump scale wasn't gone for good, as you could just float back into it later
or cycle it back to the deck for her bounce effect.

Paleozoic could find its first top here, too,
utilizing the frogs as an engine to access rank twos on the first turn,
either for Toad backed up by a series of traps or for Opabinia to churn out the Paleos
on the first turn without needing to set them first.
As the deck was super reliant on traps in the first place,
it made sense that its lineup would be chock-full of floodgates and counters to top strategies,
such as `Anti-Spell Fragrance`, `Floodgate Trap Hole`, `Quaking Mirror Force`,
and `Barrier Statue of the Torrent`.
Most interesting here was `Waboku`, which served the purpose of being protection
for your summoned Paleos as well as being chainable to any removal,
which in turn also triggered a Paleo in grave, making it an incredibly important inclusion.

Galileo De Obaldia, the 2010's world's winner, would take the event on ABC,
with the only real change to the deck being the inclusion of `Dimensional Barrier`,
as ABC was one of the best decks to utilize it since it could pivot between fusions
and Xyz better than some other strategies.
This would lead us into the last set of the year, and with so much changing so fast,
many were curious if the last set could bring another shakeup before the year closed out.

### Destiny Soldiers

- Release date: November 18th, 2016
- Set type: deck building set
- Major strategies: Destiny Hero, Abyss Actor, Darklord
- Impact: one new rogue level threat

Destiny Soldiers was the last set of the year bringing three dark attribute archetypes
to try and shake things up one last time.
Abyss Actor would be the first of these being a series
of pendulums focused around their spells the Abyss Scripts which had different effects based
on whether they're activated or destroyed while set
which unfortunately were not successful at the time in the slightest.
Even as pieces in a pendulum pile.

Destiny Hero would be the second of these bringing new support
to the strategy focused around their cards Aster Phoenix used in the recent stint
of the Arc-V anime.
Drilldark can summon a D-HERO from hand on summon with attack less or equal to itself
and deals piercing.
`Dark Angel` can discard itself if you have three or more D-HEROes in grave to summon a D-HERO
from grave to the opponent's board negating and destroying any spells activated by its controller.
It can banish itself and another D-HERO from grave in your standby phase
to have both players stack a normal spell from deck on top of their deck.

Celestial can pop an opponent's face-up spell when it attacks to burn for 500.
It can banish itself and another D-HERO while you have no cards in hand to draw two.
And Dystopia is a fusion of two D-HEROes that can burn for half of a level four
or lower D-HEROes attack in grave on summon.
It can turn its attack back to normal if changed to pop a card on field on a quick effect.

Needless to say the support lacked a lot of focus but it would give Hero a couple
of interesting tools for the future to play around
with not really changing the strategies position for now.
Lastly and most importantly were the Darklords most of which shared an effect
to pay 1,000 life on quick effect to copy a Darklord spell trap effect from grave
then shuffled it back into deck.
Ixchel could discard herself and another Darklord card to draw two.

Tezcatlipoca could discard itself to prevent the destruction of Darklord monsters.
Nasten could discard two other Darklord cards to summon itself from hand.
And Amdusc could discard itself and another Darklord to recur a Darklord card.

Morningstar was the only one here without the copy effect,
able to summon Darklords from deck on tribute summon up to the number
of effect monsters the opponent had, had targeting protection while you controlled another Darklord,
and can mill cards up to the number of Darklords you controlled to gain 500
for each Darklord card milled that way.
As for their spell traps, Banishment searched a Darklord card, `Contact` revived a Darklord,
`Rebellion` could send a Darklord from hand or field to grave to pop a card,
and Enchantment could do the same to brain control a monster for the turn.
With the traps notably skipping their costs if used by the Darklord copy effect.
This would be a bold departure from the GX era cards.
Although Desire and Superbia specifically would also be useful for this new version of the deck,
giving more swarming and easy spot removal for the strategy.
Though it was a competent deck for sure,
Darklord being a deck of high-level monsters with only two reliable ways to get one
on field initially left the deck in a position of bricking a lot,
which in turn meant that for now Darklord would be relegated to the ever-growing gallery
of rogue-level decks.

#### YCS Anaheim

YCS Anaheim would follow two weeks later,
and with the new pool of cards from INOV new strategies would start popping up.
Toad and its consequences would result in the appearance of not only Mermail,
taking advantage of the new `Bahamut Shark` target to regain some of its relevance lost
with the shuffling of the meta, but also in, of all places, Hero.
Because `Bahamut Shark` could access Toad, the deck had resorted back to an old favorite,
being Bubble Man, as it could be half of a `Bahamut Shark` very easily either with another copy
of itself or with `Tin Goldfish`, which had always been considered for a Shadow Mist enabler,
but never really had a reason to be played over something like Goblinberg until now.
As such, the end board of Dark Law and Toad would become more and more common
as Hero rose back into a tier three position in the meta.

Darklord would take two top spots on its first outing,
being able to mitigate its bricks in a Swiss setting, but crumbling a bit in single elimination,
falling out in top 32.
Though notably playing three copies of `Darklord Zerato` from Phantom Darkness as It's tribute nuke.
Yang Zing would see a top here mostly thanks to Denglong in addition
to the Metalfoes enabling the Yang Zing float effects in a major way,
which in turn allowed for the deck to set up both Denglong and Zefraniu,
which resulted in multiple Nine Pillars to form a defensive line.

Elvis Vu would take the event on ABC utilizing the `Brilliant Fusion` engine as a way
to flood board and to access the ABC pieces on the light dump for easier access
to the fusion ABC itself.

#### YCS Bochum

YCS Bochum would be two weeks later being the last YCS of the year and
with it we'd see the meta actually balance out again between its top four decks,
being that of ABC, Metalfoes, Paleozoic, and Hero.
Paleo had shown the most growth here over time since release as it had trimmed its monster
and spell count down to about as low as it could go, being that of a frog engine,
Maxx "C", and Desires, leaving the rest of the deck to be impact traps and Paleos
with probably the most interesting pivot here being that of `Reckless Greed`,
filling a similar niche to how it was used back in Dark World
to stack multiple together without getting multiple of the drawback.
Only this time it could also revive a Paleo when you used it.

Awesome Performage Lightsworn would be a pile
of pile decks combining the previous Lightsworn game plan with that of using `Tin Goldfish`
as half of `Bahamut Shark` with the other half usually being `Gigobyte`,
which was still searchable with `King of the Feral Imps` and easy
to summon between the Performages and Minerva.
Zombie would take the turn going down the hand loop trail we all expected utilizing the Shiranui
engine in addition to various level one and two tuner options alongside `Beast of the Pharaoh`
to loop through their Omega and Trishula with `Beast of the Pharaoh` able to revive `Zombie Master`
when sent for a synchro summon, Master then being able to discard to revive Beast,
and then with a level one tuner makes Omega or a level two makes Trishula,
reviving the Master with Beast to do it all over again.
Billy Brake would take the event on Metalfoes marking his fourth YCS win,
which at the time made him the player with the most total YCS wins,
which would be his last YCS win as he would retire from the competitive side of the game
to work for Konami in their development of the game sometime after August of 2017.

### Outro

And with that, 2016 Yu-Gi-Oh! would come to an end.
But what an insanely packed year it was.
We'd seen the rise and immediate fall of a tier zero titan,
multiple formats with at least four decks vying for the top position,
and so many shifts in the game that it's hard to believe it all happened within a single year.
But unfortunately, a storm was coming.

We were about to enter 2017, and with it would come more than just a new tier zero menace.
It would also bring a shift that many would doom cry as the end of Yu-Gi-Oh! as we knew it.
But I mean, come on.
They said that about every new mechanic so far.

How bad could it possibly be?
