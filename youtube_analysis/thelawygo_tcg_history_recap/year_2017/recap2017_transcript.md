### Intro

When we last left off, the Arc-V era was nearing its climax.
Over the course of 2016, we had seen the rapid rise and fall of a tier zero mega threat in Pepe,
a surprisingly diverse and interesting metagame through the World Championship Qualifier season,
and a recent shakeup into more options with the advent of Metalfoes, ABC and Paleozoic.
As we enter 2017, Yu-Gi-Oh as a whole was on the move in a couple of other ways
outside of the standard TCG output.

Starting the year off, on January 11th,
Konami would hold the worldwide release for their new mobile game adaptation of the TCG,
Duel Links, which brought with it the advent of the Speed Duel format,
using three zones each for monsters and spell traps, as well as a 20 to 30 card deck.
The early days of this new format's metagame is actually extremely intriguing,
and if you want to learn more about it, I'll direct you to the first video I ever uploaded here,
covering the first year of the game's metagame development.
But as for the impact on the TCG, the wider appeal of Yu-Gi-Oh on mobile
actually would do a decent amount for bringing in older players to the game once again,
coupled with the English release of the Dark Side of Dimensions movie,
which was coupled with a re-release of the movie pack from last year, now in gold rarity.

#### YCS Sydney

YCS Sydney would be held the same weekend as the DSOD release, on January 14th,
and from it we'd see that INOV format was still in full swing,
with Metalfoes taking up the top spot in representation,
as well as first place, piloted by Michael Duke.
But there were many other decks filling the format besides Metalfoes.
ABC was continuing to be a fan favorite, taking seven of the top 32 here,
still promoting its rank four into Dragon Buster game plan,
and it still remained an effective strategy.
There were also a couple more odd inclusions, such as Abyss Actor lines,
and Metalfoes using `Abyss Actor - Wild Hope` as a pop target
to search `Abyss Actor - Sassy Rookie`,
Blue-Eyes now using `The Fabled Catsith` as a search target for Sage
to pull more value out of Twin Twisters and Melody,
and Fire King Kozmo popping back up here
with the inclusion of `Fire King Avatar Yaksha` for the ship pops.

Following this would be the release of the new Shonen Jump promo, being `Odd-Eyes Persona Dragon`,
being another decent Odd-Eyes low scale to use with Sky Iris.

With all of the preamble out of the way,
all eyes turned to the first structure deck release of the year,
and given how it had been delayed time and time again,
finally seeing it come out would be a boon to a strategy that's been in the fringes
for some time now.

### Pendulum Domination

- Release date: January 20th, 2017
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: D/D/D
- Impact: a tuner to save the strategy

The Pendulum Domination structure deck is a weird one to start with,
because in the OCG it actually caused a tier one takeover for the strategy of D/D/D,
and it was set to be released in the TCG back in July of 2016.
However, to help the TCG's schedule coincide better with the release of the Blue-Eyes support,
this was actually the listed response,
the Rise of the True Dragons structure deck was moved up the schedule
and Pendulum Domination was bumped back.

So with that said, what support did Domination bring to the so far floundering D/D/D strategy?
`D/D/D Dragonbane King Beowulf` was a fusion of a D/D/D and a D/D that gives all D/Ds piercing
and can Heavy Storm the board in your standby phase.
`D/D/D Cursed King Siegfried` is a level eight D/D-locked Synchro
that can negate a face-up spell trap until your next standby phase
and regains life points for each Dark Contract you control when destroyed.
`D/D Savant Copernicus` is an Armageddon Knight for D/Ds and Dark Contracts,
and can pop itself in scale to prevent burn from a spell card.
`D/D Orthros` can special summon itself from hand when you take burn damage,
locking you to Fiends for the turn,
and can in scale pop a spell trap and a D/D or Dark Contract you control.
`D/D Nighthowl` is a level three tuner that on normal can revive a D/D,
locking you to Fiends for the turn, setting its attack and defense to zero.
Lastly, and most importantly, `D/D Lamia` is a level one tuner
that can send a Dark Contract or D/D from hand or field to grave to summon herself.

Many of these pieces were absolutely critical parts of the D/D/D line,
and with them now available,
D/D/D was one of the most powerful and complicated decks in the metagame.
The lines for each possible opening had so many different variations and paths
that it could go through that the community ended up developing a spreadsheet
for every single possible opening,
which in turn spurred the meme of the deck's complicated nature further.
Many honestly expected that with these cards,
D/D would rapidly rise to the status of best deck in format,
but there was a very major complication with that, because it had been delayed for so long.
3 weeks later, with no major events in between, we'd receive the first core set of the year,
and with it would come the identity of Yu-Gi-Oh for almost the entirety of the next year.

### Raging Tempest

- Release date: February 10th, 2017
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Zoodiac, Zoodiac, Zoodiac
- Impact: Zoodiac

Raging Tempest, or RATE for short, is probably one of the most infamous sets in the game's history,
and it all has to do with its headlining archetype,
but for the sake of everything else, we are going to save it for last on the archetype coverage.

The Windwitches were a series of WIND spellcasters designed to synergize with each other
to Synchro climb entirely by themselves into a level seven Synchro,
and then into an eight with that seven.
`Windwitch - Ice Bell` could special summon herself if you controlled no monsters,
then summon a Windwitch from your deck, burning the opponent for 500,
and locking you to level five and up WINDs from the extra that turn.
`Windwitch - Glass Bell` on summon searches a Windwitch, locking you to WINDs when you do.
`Windwitch - Snow Bell` can summon herself from hand if you control two or more WINDs
and only WINDs,
and makes a Synchro made with it unable to be destroyed by card effect.
`Windwitch - Winter Bell` is a level seven Synchro
that burns the opponent for the level of a Windwitch in grave times 200,
and can summon a monster from hand with a lower level than a Windwitch you control
in the battle phase.

These cards formed a self-contained line thanks to Ice Bell,
who could summon herself, summon Glass Bell out of deck and burn the opponent for 500,
then have Glass Bell search for Snow, who could then summon herself.
From there, Ice and Glass make Winter,
who burns the opponent for another 800 thanks to Glass in grave,
and they can be Synchroed with Snow into Crystal Wing,
who now also can't be destroyed by card effects.
The primary downside to this line was that it WIND-locked you,
but given time, this wouldn't be an issue in the slightest.

Ancient Gear would get a new wave of support, but in a completely new direction for the archetype,
based on their usage in the Arc-V anime as the default villain deck.
`Ancient Gear Hunting Hound` burns for 600 on normal, has the Ancient Gear spell trap stun,
and can Fusion Summon an Ancient Gear once per turn.
`Ancient Gear Howitzer`, a fusion of any two Ancient Gears, is unaffected by card effects,
can burn the opponent for a thousand once per turn,
and if destroyed floats into any Ancient Gear in deck, ignoring summoning conditions.
`Chaos Ancient Gear Giant` is a fusion of any four Ancient Gears, is unaffected by spell traps,
locks the opponent from using monster effects in the battle phase,
can attack all monsters once, and deals piercing.
The idea of unrelenting damage from the anime was adapted quite well here, being fair,
but it's a far cry from a good strategy when the starting life points are at 8,000 and not 4,000.
Still, it did bring some attention towards Ancient Gears as a rogue strategy,
alongside a structure deck release later this year.

SPYRAL would receive another wave of support,
`SPYRAL Tough`, who becomes Super Agent on field and in grave,
and can attempt to pop a card by calling the card type of the opponent's top deck right,
`SPYRAL GEAR - Utility Wire`, which spins a card to top of deck if you control a Super Agent,
and `SPYRAL MISSION - Recapture`, which steals an opponent's monster
the first time you summon a SPYRAL each turn,
and can banish itself from grave to prevent a SPYRAL's destruction.
While a solid wave, SPYRAL would continue to see no play as its own standalone deck,
though many were picking up on the idea that SPYRAL as an engine for another deck could be useful,
thanks to the vast amounts of advantage the deck can generate off of its smaller pieces.

Okay, fine, let's talk about Zoodiac.
Zoodiac was a new series of Beast-Warriors aimed at cheating the Xyz mechanic
by allowing the user to overlay any Zoodiac Xyz onto a single Zoodiac monster,
with each Xyz able to do that once per turn.
The trade-off here was that the Xyz had no attack and defense on their own,
inheriting the stats from all of their Zoodiac materials,
with each main deck monster also giving an effect to a Beast-Warrior Xyz with it as material.
`Zoodiac Ratpier` Foolish Burials a Zoodiac on normal,
and gives its Xyz the ability to detach to summon another Rat from deck.
`Zoodiac Bunnyblast` recurs a Zoodiac when destroyed,
and lets its Xyz detach to negate a spell that targets it.
`Zoodiac Whiptail` can attach itself to a Beast-Warrior Xyz from hand or field,
and banishes any monster that battles its Xyz.
`Zoodiac Thoroughblade` can discard a Zoodiac to draw one and gives its Xyz piercing,
and `Zoodiac Ramram` summons a Zoodiac from grave when destroyed,
and lets its Xyz detach to negate a trap that targets it.

As for the Xyz, each gains the stats of its Zoodiac materials,
can overlay onto a single Zoodiac monster once per turn,
and has its own unique effects beyond that.
`Zoodiac Boarbow` can attack directly, and if it has 12 materials when you do,
nukes the opponent's hand and field and swaps itself to defense mode.
`Zoodiac Tigermortar` can detach to attach a Zoodiac from grave to any Xyz you control.
`Zoodiac Broadbull` can detach to search a Beast-Warrior monster that can be normal summoned or set,
and `Zoodiac Drident` can detach on quick effect to pop a card on field.
Lastly, while most of their spell traps were completely irrelevant,
`Zoodiac Barrage` was the exception,
being a continuous spell that could pop a card to summon a Zoodiac from deck,
and can attach itself from grave to a Zoodiac Xyz if destroyed by a card effect.

So where do we begin with Zoodiac?
I guess we'll start with the Rat.
Ratpier on its own represented all sorts of degenerate lines,
namely stemming from the basic premise of cycling its effect multiple times
to end on at least Drident and a rank four.
Basics, there was: overlay a Rat into Boarbow, into Tigermortar,
detach the Boarbow to summon Rat, overlay into Broadbull, detach to summon Rat, detach to search,
overlay the two Rats into `Daigusto Emeral` to draw one,
and finally overlay the Broadbull into Drident.
But that was the very basic level of it all.
Through all sorts of recursion and kooky lines,
a single Rat could cascade into five plus draws and all sorts of advantage on top of that.
But I'm getting ahead of myself.
Drident being able to pop anything on a quick effect was an insanely good effect
for what was effectively a generic rank four at the time,
which put the previous option of Rafflesia in a worse position,
as you no longer needed a brick to get a quick effect pop off of her,
on top of the fact that Drident was usually just the cherry on top.

Broadbull was, in hindsight, the far more problematic of the Xyz pool,
because its search effect had such a wide range of targets,
in addition to being a soft once per turn, meaning if you could get out two or more,
it was repeatable,
made better by it being the only two-material Zoodiac Xyz in the first place,
meaning that it was actually likely that you could summon them out multiple times.
Whiptail was probably one of the more fair options, but only by comparison,
as it gave Drident more fuel for popping cards,
in addition to an extra layer of protection by disincentivizing monsters attacking into her,
by threatening a banish after the fact.
Barrage posed a very serious issue, though,
as through its effect popping itself,
you could perform the entire Zoodiac line without the use of your normal summon,
and with its inclusion, you were technically playing nine copies of Rat to start lines,
between three Rat, three Tenki and three Barrage.
Oh, I'm sorry, did I say nine? I meant at least 12.
`M-X-Saber Invoker`, being one of the earliest Xyz printed to the TCG,
can summon any level four EARTH Warrior or Beast from the deck with any two level three materials.
You get where this is going, right?
`Speedroid Terrortop` now represents a full Zoodiac combo without the normal summon,
being able to access Invoker, who accesses Rat, who combos through that way.
Zoodiac would descend upon the meta and insert themselves into literally every deck in the format
to some extent, which we will talk about more in depth with the next YCS.

All right, time to rapid fire the one-offs.
`True King Lithosagym, the Disaster` was by far the best option of the True Kings,
popping EARTH for summon and being able to rip three cards from the opponent's extra deck
if both pops were EARTH, floating into a non-EARTH Wyrm in grave on destruction.
While not super relevant right now outside of a tech play in Zoodiac,
Lithosagym would find itself in a very comfortable home soon.
`Spiritual Beast Tamer Winda` is both sides of the Ritual Beast sub-archetypes
and floats into any Ritual Beast in deck or extra on destruction,
being a massive boost for the archetype,
but unfortunately coming out at just the worst possible time for playability.
`Miscellaneousaurus` can be discarded on a quick effect
to make your Dinosaurs unaffected by opponent's activated effects for that main phase,
and could banish itself and any number of Dinosaurs from grave
to summon a Dinosaur of that level from deck.
While an incredible boon for Dinosaur decks, the strategy was nowhere near playable at the time,
though this would find a home within a couple months.
`Foolish Burial Goods` was a Foolish Burial for spell traps.
While that sounds mediocre, this actually filled a much-needed space in the game as a whole,
as players were resorting to cards like Magical Hats to put spell traps
in grave up until this point,
so the card was a welcome addition to the pool.
`Sea Monster of Theseus` is a tuner Fusion of any two tuners with no other effects.
Most of you watching are probably confused why I bring this one up,
and it's because of the fact that Konami was proudly bragging on their blog
that they were going to be releasing the best card of the year in the TCG exclusive slot
for this set,
referencing this card.
Yes, Theseus was always considered niche.
It did have uses, specifically for those involving Instant Fusion where you needed a tuner,
or in Tzolkin's case, a level five tuner,
but calling this the best card of 2017 was laughable even then,
as the Zoodiacs were in the same set, and we have a whole lot more to cover from the year.
`Lost Wind` negates the effects and halves the attack of a special summoned monster on the field,
and can set itself from grave if the opponent specials a monster from the extra deck.
This sounds niche in a lot of respects,
but in actuality would be an insanely powerful card for specifically Paleozoic,
as a trap that was a repeatable trigger for your Paleo summon effects is always appreciated,
and the attack halving and negation is actually permanent,
so it covers a lot more situations than one expects at first.

And it got even better with the last card we're going to talk about, `That Grass Looks Greener`.
When you activate this, if you have more cards in your deck than your opponent,
you mill cards from the top of your deck until your deck count is the same as your opponent's.
This spell was theoretically a 20 card mill, and all it took to play was running a 60 card deck.
Grass caused arguably one of the biggest shifts in deck composition in the game's history,
as on the surface, it changed decks that wanted to mill from being 40 cards to 60 cards,
but it's actually a lot more complicated than that.
If you were a Grass deck and you matched up into another Grass deck, you'd both be at 60 cards,
meaning that Grass is now a completely dead card.
As such, even decks that weren't Grass decks would consider playing higher deck counts
specifically to dodge the Grass matchup, whether that be a full 60 or even a midpoint like 50 cards.
The usual trade-off to playing 60 was a lower consistency,
but now, with the best engine in the game being a one card combo
you can see off of a minimum of 12 cards in your deck,
the loss in consistency for playing larger than a normal deck was actually decently offset.
Rather than just talking theoretical on all this, let's look at some results.

#### YCS Seattle

YCS Seattle would be a week after RATE's release, on February 18th,
and the meta that had come before was completely obliterated in one massive strike
with the release of RATE.
On the surface, the format does look rather diverse, with 10 different decks making the top 32 cut,
but if we look below the surface, we can see that a grand total of 31 of the top 32 decks
were actually playing Zoodiac in some capacity.
Zoodiac had completely taken over the format.
Commonly, any way into Zoodiac Rat here meant that you were going to have a Drident,
an additional draw, and then plenty of wiggle room beyond that
to set up a board of options to play into the opponent's next turn,
which in turn resulted in most decks playing options to get Rat,
both on the normal summon and without burning the normal,
specifically in the case of Barrage and Terrortop.

This resulted in a number of different variants of Zoodiac specifically charting
into the top cut here,
between the pure variant, an Artifact variant to capitalize on `Artifact Moralltach` and Scythe,
a Kozmo variant utilizing the power of Barrage to pop your ships for setup,
and the most popular variant behind pure, Kaiju,
utilizing Interrupted Kaiju Slumber and the Kaiju names to iron out your going second matchup,
as Drident on the normal summon was a pretty standard killer for the mirror match.
Alexis Rodriguez would take the event on this variant,
capitalizing heavily on the base level understanding of setting up Drident with no protection
being the go-to line for most players.

Beyond the Zoodiac-focused decks, however,
almost every other deck would still be playing Rat lines to play with their own strategies,
as adding a quick effect pop to any board was still killer.
Infernoid would be the most popular strategy outside of a strict Zoodiac variant,
and it would be the deck to show off the new power of the spell Grass.
The ability to mill 20 plus cards is an insane boon for the strategy,
and as such could set up any number of waves of Infernoids directly out the gate.
As such, a couple of cards that had been present for some time at this point
would finally start seeing significant play,
mainly `Void Imagination`, being useful now as a way to get multiple bosses on the board
on your first turn now that fuel wasn't a concern,
but also Left Arm Offering, as sacrificing your hand
to get a mill 20 actually was a decent trade-off.
Even with the deck's primary play being that of Grass into Infernoids,
Zoodiac would still be present here thanks to Terrortop and Barrage,
allowing a Drident to be set up prior to going down your Infernoid lines,
playing a fairly minimal line of enablers there.

Volcanic would see a top spot here, which makes sense,
as the Scattershot engine standardly needed a lighter package to latch onto to be effective,
which the new Zoodiac cards would fill out nicely.
Metalfoes would see a top here, notably playing a 50 card lineup
to sort of shoot the gap between the consistency needed to beat Zoodiac
and the deck size needed to shut off a significant Grass mill,
which in turn helped in allowing them to play all of the options needed for the strategy,
including the newly added Zoodiac engine.
The only deck in the entire top 32 to not be playing Zoodiac cards, period, would be Burning Abyss,
leaning into the 60 card variant as well to primarily cut off access to opposing Grasses,
though it was playing a single copy themselves to capitalize off the grave effects.

With the meta substantially shifted at its very core,
the next deck building set would arrive just a week later,
and while it wouldn't be as fundamentally game-warping as Zoodiac in the short term,
it would help to introduce another combatant to play into this new tier zero menace.

### Fusion Enforcers

- Release date: February 24th, 2017
- Set type: deck building set
- Major strategies: Predaplant, Fluffal, Invoked
- Impact: a normal summon engine of epic proportions

Fusion Enforcers, coming just two weeks after Raging Tempest, had a massive uphill battle to win,
and while it wouldn't dethrone Zoodiac by any means,
it would at the very least introduce a new option into the metagame.
Starting with the two that didn't quite land,
both Predaplant and Fluffal would get new options for their strategies,
with Predaplant getting `Predaplant Chimerafflesia` as an in-archetype boss monster,
and Fluffal getting a couple of smaller pieces in `Fluffal Octopus` and `Fluffal Penguin`
and the new boss `Frightfur Kraken`,
but neither of these strategies would have staying power in the recently heightened power floor.

Invoked would be the last of the three,
and be a completely new archetype centered around the sole main deck monster
of `Aleister the Invoker`,
who can be discarded to buff a Fusion by a thousand,
and searches the archetype Fusion spell `Invocation` on normal or flip.
Invocation can fuse materials in hand for any Fusion monster,
or can banish materials from field or either grave for an Invoked Fusion monster,
and can return a banished Aleister the Invoker to hand to shuffle itself from grave back into deck.
`Magical Meltdown` is the archetypal field spell
that searches Aleister the Invoker on activation,
prevents the activation of your Fusion Summon cards and effects from being negated,
and blocks responding to the Fusion Summon of a monster.

As for the archetype's Fusions,
there would be one for each of the six primary attributes in the game,
taking Aleister and its attribute as material.
`Invoked Caliga` takes a DARK and locks both players
to only one monster effect activation and one attack per turn.
`Invoked Raidjin` takes a WIND and can quick effect flip a monster face-down once per turn.
`Invoked Cocytus` takes a WATER, can attack in defense using its attack stat,
and can't be destroyed or targeted by card effects.
`Invoked Purgatrio` takes a FIRE, gains 200 attack for each opponent's controlled card,
can attack all monsters once each, and deals piercing.
`Invoked Magellanica` takes an EARTH and is just a large body at 3K,
and `Invoked Mechaba` takes a LIGHT and can negate and banish any card activated
by discarding a card with the corresponding card type.
Lastly, `Invoked Elysium` is a fusion of an Invoked monster and a monster summoned from the extra,
is considered all six of the primary attributes on field,
and can banish an Invoked monster from field or grave
and all monsters the opponent controls of that attribute.

Aleister the Invoker would represent one of the most consistent normal summon engines in the game,
in that with access to Invoker, you had access to a Fusion Summon every turn between your cards
and your opponent's,
but there were clear outliers in which Fusions were the best.
Mechaba was obviously the best on the surface,
being an omni negate that can be made with one of the most common attributes,
but each attribute had their strengths and weaknesses depending on the metagame.
As such, Aleister would find itself splashed in decks that didn't require the normal summon
and played attributes that meshed well with it,
and with that, another deck would rise up into the fringe of the meta
thanks to Aleister's interaction with a so far unimpactful engine, which we'll discuss soon.

#### YCS Atlanta

YCS Atlanta would be held 2 weeks after Enforcers' release,
and from it we would see just the slightest movement in the outer fringes of the top cut.
Invoked had proven to be an interesting slot into the Zoodiac core,
seeing most of the normal summon reliant options for Zoodiac here shaved
for the Invoked package of Meltdown, Aleister and Invocation.
The primary benefit to the addition was, on top of the usual Drident from the Zoodiac combo,
you could also make a Raidjin quite easily between Ratpier and Emeral used for the combo,
meaning an added Book of Moon effect to your end board.

This would also be one of the primary focuses of the new strategy in the fringes of the meta,
known as Artifact Windwitch Invoked,
taking the prior released Windwitch package for accessing Crystal Wing on the first turn,
and then pairing it with the normal summon of Aleister to access Raidjin,
as you would still be under the WIND lock.
In addition to this, the Artifact package proved to be a solid inclusion with Invoked,
thanks to providing game-winningly strong effects when summoned with Sanctum,
or to be used with Aleister to make Mechaba, providing an omni negate to the end board.

Beyond that, what we've come to expect from the new Zoodiac dominance once again came to pass,
seeing 29 of the top 32 running the engine in some regard,
though shockingly, it would not take the event overall.
Cory Roa would take that honor on Paleozoic Frogs,
proving that the correct combination of traps and Frogs
could actually be a sufficient answer to the newly dominant threat.
Lost Wind in particular would be a standout,
as the trap was both repeatable for your Paleo effects and its debuffs were permanent,
meaning that if you could land it onto a Drident,
that effect was off for as long as she remained on the board.

#### Star Pack Battle Royal, Raging Tempest Special Edition

Directly following YCS Atlanta would be a couple of minor set releases.
The first of these was the Star Pack Battle Royal, which was mostly unimpactful
outside of a reprint for Speedroid Terrortop, which would become a lot more valuable as of late.
The other would be Raging Tempest Special Edition,
which would continue the tradition of these sets printing a couple of cards
from the next core set early,
but this time would actually make a difference.
`Baobaboon`, if normal or special summoned, draws a card
and stacks a card from hand on top or bottom of deck,
and if destroyed, summons any number of Baobaboon from deck.
This would actually make a very specific version of Zoodiac
from the previous YCS a lot more powerful,
being that of Metalfoes Zoodiac,
as now, by popping a Baobaboon for a Metalfoes search, you could summon two additional from deck,
stack your hand with two more draws, and overlay them for M-X-Saber Invoker for a full Zoodiac line.

#### YCS Guatemala City

Guatemala City would be the first testing grounds for this new tech,
and without skipping a beat, Metalfoes Zoodiac would adapt,
slotting the new option in to further promote the synergy between Zoodiac and Metalfoes,
also notably playing 60 cards to not only fit in all the new options,
but also to counteract the rising popularity of Grass variants like Infernoid Zoodiac.
Gerald South Chavez would take the event on just that,
not only seeing the Zoodiac package and varied Infernoid names,
but also three copies of `Lullaby of Obedience`.
The power spell had found a format where it could dominate,
as with over 80% of the top cut on the Zoodiac package,
calling Ratpier with it could prove insanely valuable,
especially as that would then provide a total of three activations for the effect,
since you didn't need to use your own Rat for the startup.

#### YCS Prague

YCS Prague would be the same weekend,
and though we've unfortunately lost a major portion of the coverage for this event,
one thing that was apparent is that Paleo was not about to be left behind
by the new tier zero menace.
Joshua Schmidt would take the event on Paleo Frog,
bringing the first successful variant of Grass Paleo to the masses.
While the concept of using Grass with Paleo had been kicked around since the card's reveal,
its impact was more often than not subpar,
as you could easily load tons of Paleos into the grave,
but not really trigger them all that successfully,
since you only had a limited number of traps to be used after the fact.

There were a couple of cards here to help counteract that problem.
The first was a long-known card that would be reintroduced here in Card of Demise.
One of the hardest parts for Paleo with Demise is the lock on special summoning,
meaning that you can't set up a Toadally after using it,
but with more focus being placed on loading up the grave with traps, that goes away a bit,
as it's clear we're setting up for a longer game with it.
The other, and far more apparent departure, was `Absolute King Back Jack`.
Back Jack had already been experimented with for Burning Abyss strategies,
thanks to its ability to set up Fire Lake pushes,
but here, when dumped off Grass, it can set up your top deck
and gives you effectively an additional trap to your backrow line,
as its excavate ability made the trap live from then on.
Because of this, you can now also play cards like `Fiend Griefing` for disruption,
which also in turn is a trigger for your Paleos, and now is a setup for more triggers.

With innovation still present in the metagame, a shift was in the air.
The spring import set was right around the corner,
and it wouldn't just bring imports this time around.
It was about to bring a shift that is still hotly debated to this day.

### Duelist Saga

- Release date: March 31st, 2017
- Set type: import set
- Major strategies: Shark, GX manga cards, erratas
- Impact: rewriting the game's history

Duelist Saga would be a replacement of sorts for the annual Gold Series release,
with this being the first year that Gold Series would not be printed since its debut back in 2008.
With it, we'd receive a bunch of anime-centric cards,
including a set of OCG imports for various anime and manga characters,
such as Rio's Sharks with `Silent Angler`,
Kotori's Numbers with `Number 70: Malevolent Sin`,
manga Aster's Vision HEROes with `Vision HERO Vyon`,
a tribute to Jaden's HEROes with `Elemental HERO Honest Neos`,
manga Bastion's Zombies with `Gozuki`,
manga Belowski's Darklords with `Darklord Ukoback`,
and a new version of Yusei's boss in `Cosmic Blazar Dragon`.
However, most of these would have no impact in the short term.

The far more talked about portion of the set would be a series of erratas
made to famously powerful cards from the game's history,
some of which had been banned for quite some time at this stage.
These erratas were intended to bring the power level of these cards
more in line with what is considered acceptable for the game's current context,
and as such, the following cards were permanently errata'd to have the following updates.
`Brain Control` could now only affect normal summonable and set monsters.
`Imperial Order` no longer had the option to refuse to pay its maintenance cost:
if you had the life points, you had to pay it.
`Necrovalley` now also negated type and attribute changes in the grave,
and now also negated the effects of cards that move themselves out of grave.
`Future Fusion` now delays its deck dump by one turn to allow the opponent a chance to counter it.
`Rescue Cat` now negates the effects of the monster it summons,
and now is also a hard once per turn.
Bionic is now a hard once per turn.
And lastly, `Goyo Guardian` now requires an EARTH tuner to summon.
Many of these erratas are understandable for the power level the cards provided,
but some were also extremely contentious, specifically in that of Goyo Guardian.
Goyo had already been legal in the TCG again for some time before the errata
and had done absolutely nothing of note, so errata-ing it here seemed pointless for many.

#### Ban list (March 31st)

To help drive these erratas in, a new ban list would take effect
the same day as Saga's release, on March 31st, 2017,
and on top of all the errata releases,
a couple of extremely interesting hits would take place for the fully taken over tier zero metagame.
Newly banned were `Majespecter Unicorn - Kirin`, the primary power play of most pendulum strategies,
`Vanity's Emptiness`, the staple floodgate that's been around for far too long at this point,
and `The Tyrant Neptune`.
Wait, what?
Tyrant Neptune's a very odd hit, but it will make a lot more sense with an upcoming set,
as the hit now was to prevent a very degenerate interaction with it
that would have become available with the next core set, which we'll discuss with its release.
Newly limited were Maxx "C", down from two,
and Bionic, Rescue Cat, Brain Control, Future Fusion and Imperial Order,
all with their new erratas in tow.
Newly semi-limited were Wisdom-Eye Magician, up from one,
Interrupted Kaiju Slumber, a hit to the rising staple board breaker,
and `Zoodiac Ratpier`, the combo enabler for so many decks in the tier zero format.
Lastly, unlimited would be `Sangan`, who had also received an errata back in 2016,
but was still banned until now.
Easily the most talked about change here was Ratpier,
as bringing the copies in deck down from 3 to 2
would seemingly solve the issue of it being used as a generic combo enabler for any strategy, right?

#### YCS Denver

YCS Denver would be held just over a week later, and, oops.
So, as it turns out, while hitting Rat to 2 did absolutely curb its use as it was used initially,
it was by no means knocked out of the meta, as crafty players quickly adapted Zoodiac lines
to do far more interesting things.
For example, with a little bit of labbing,
players realized that with the way into Rat and `Instant Fusion`,
the Zoodiac combo could be extended far beyond what was done before,
ending with five draws, Drident and Emeral.
This was primarily thanks in part to Lunalight Black Sheep,
a Beast-Warrior searchable with Broadbull, being able to search for Polymerization,
which could actually be Fusion Substitute, thanks to the fact that it was always considered Poly.
With this, you could actually fuse two of your used Xyz into `Elder Entity Norden`.
That's right, we're fusing Norden correctly, the way it is written on the card.
Then, once Norden was used up, you could put it back with Fusion Sub's grave effect to draw,
and get another Norden proc later.

This, in addition to other advancements,
such as the reintroduction of Brilliant Fusion into the metagame as a rank four and normal enabler,
with `Performage Trick Clown` and Seraphinite respectively,
`Retaliating "C"`, both as a way to counter Zoodiac when Barrage or Instant Fusion comes down
and to search Maxx "C" afterwards,
and the ever-growing popularity of 60 card decks to prevent Grass interactions,
led to an interesting period of the game
that would still remain a tier zero format with Zoodiac at the clear center of it all.

Beyond this, there were a couple of other changes that did come out of the ban list.
Metalfoes Zoodiac was probably the hardest hit variant with the ban of Kirin,
but it was still a powerful variant option with access to Baobaboon,
and as such would double back on an old option of `Qliphort Scout` and `Qliphort Monolith`
to access Cyber Dragon Infinity with your opening board, in addition to the Zoodiac lines.
Kozmo would also sneak back into top cut here with a similar list
to the prior Tin Can Turbo variants,
now also rocking `Dimensional Barrier` as a way to lock out the Xyz onslaught.
Anderson Saying would take the event on Infernoid Zoodiac,
and even with the semi-limit and the rise in the number of players on 60 cards,
the variant continued to be a solid choice for the current metagame,
with every Infernoid doubling as D.D. Crow for critical combo pieces,
and the near infinite recursion they gain from Grass.

This would lead us into the next structure deck a week later,
and seemingly the Structure Deck R train was set to keep going,
as we weren't just getting one this time, it was a duo.

### Machine Reactor and Dinosmasher's Fury

- Release date: April 14th, 2017
- Set type: structure deck
- Major strategies: Ancient Gear, Dinosaur
- Impact: kick the baby, don't kick the goddamn baby

Next up on the Structure Deck R train, we revisit the old structure decks
of Machine Revolt and Dinosaur's Rage,
two structures that were initially failures in promoting new decks to the scene,
given a new chance at life here.

Machine Reactor, without knowing the contents, probably held the most promise,
as with the recent Ancient Gear support in RATE,
a little support going in the right direction could spell a meta
for the longtime floundering archetype.
`Ancient Gear Wyvern` on summon searches any Ancient Gear card except itself,
and locks you from setting cards for the rest of the turn,
with the monster effect stun on attacking.
`Ancient Gear Gadget` on summon gains a stun effect for a card type of your choice
for all of your monsters that turn,
and can make its name that of any Gadget monster until the end phase once per turn.
`Ancient Gear Hydra`, if summoned by tributing an Ancient Gear monster,
banishes anything it battles but doesn't destroy,
and if summoned by tributing a Gadget, can attack all monsters once each,
with a spell trap stun on attacking.
`Ancient Gear Reactor Dragon` deals piercing, is tributed over an Ancient Gear monster,
and can attack twice if tributed over a Gadget,
stuns spell traps when attacking, and pops a spell trap after attacking.
`Ancient Gear Fortress` gives Ancient Gears effect targeting and destruction prevention
for the turn they're summoned, locks the opponent from responding to Ancient Gear cards and effects,
and summons an Ancient Gear from hand or grave when destroyed,
then locking you to Ancient Gears when it does.
`Ancient Gear Catapult`, while you control no monsters,
pops a face-up card you control to summon an Ancient Gear from deck, ignoring summoning conditions,
and can be banished from grave to pop a face-up card you control to summon an Ancient Gear Token.

A lot of disjointedness all around,
and feeling more like it's trying to make up for the over-restrictive nature of past releases.
Ancient Gear here feels less like trying to blaze a new path forward for the archetype
with their new Fusion support,
and instead feels too focused on making some of the original high-tribute pieces work,
which ends up making the new cards fall flat immediately.
Only one of these new cards would even see any form of immediate experimentation,
being that of Wyvern, being a Stratos for the archetype,
meaning that the Fusion burn strategy now had a searcher.

As for Dinosmasher's Fury, the direction was far more open-ended,
as the original structure was more so just bringing an amalgam of Dinosaur pieces together
with no real rhyme or reason.
`Petiteranodon` would be a counterpart to the much older `Babycerasaurus`,
able to summon a level four or higher Dino from deck when destroyed by card effect,
as opposed to the original's four and lower effect.
`Souleating Oviraptor` on summon either searches or pitches a Dino from deck,
and can pop a level four or lower Dino other than itself to summon a Dino from grave in defense.
`Ultimate Conductor Tyranno` must be special summoned by banishing two Dinos in grave,
can pop a monster in hand or on field on a quick effect to Book of Moon the opponent's board,
can attack all monsters once each,
and if it attacks a defense position monster, burns the opponent for a thousand
and sends it straight to grave.
`Lost World` debuffs all non-Dinos by 500,
can summon a Jurraegg Token to the opponent's board when you summon a Dino once per turn,
locks the opponent from targeting Dinos with card effects while they control a token,
and can prevent a normal monster's destruction once per turn
by popping a Dino in hand or deck instead.
Just a quick reminder that tokens count as normal monsters.
`Survival's End` pops as many normal monsters on field as possible
to summon a level four or lower Dino from deck up to the number destroyed,
destroying them in the end phase,
and can banish itself from grave to pop a Dino you control and any card the opponent controls.
There was also a new vanilla Dino here with 2K attack named `Megalosmasher X`,
and a reprint of Rescue Rabbit, in case Dino Rabbit was still on the brain.

This new direction for Dinosaur was a bold step, to say the least,
and while it wouldn't make massive waves in the meta,
it was still an extremely competent rogue-level deck on a serious budget,
as realistically, the deck was actually competent enough to make using three copies
of the structure deck
a bold move in the face of the growing expenses of the tier zero metagame.
Ultimate Conductor Tyranno, or UCT for short, was an incredibly powerful boss monster,
and a quick effect blanket Book of Moon's power cannot be overstated,
as Raidjin was already seeing play being a single-target version.
Oviraptor promoted a gameplay loop of popping baby for baby,
cycling Babycerasaurus mostly to flood the board with options to make big pushes,
whether that be Xyz lines with Evolzar or Synchro lines with `Jurrac Aeolo`.
Speaking of which, Oviraptor also represented a one card level five Synchro,
thanks to Miscellaneousaurus from RATE,
able to search Miscellaneousaurus on summon, immediately pitch it for its protection effect,
and then immediately after, banish Miscellaneousaurus to summon Aeolo from deck,
with Oviraptor and Aeolo accessing a level five like Denglong.
With almost all the ideal pop targets also being EARTHs,
Lithosagym's name would be thrown into the ring,
able to pop the babies to summon itself and potentially rip up the opponent's extra deck,
then trigger the babies in grave to swarm out more Dinos.
While I could go on, we'll leave it here for now,
as Dino was a legitimate budget strategy at the time thanks to this structure deck,
and because of that, though it wouldn't be nearly as successful as Zoodiac,
it was quite beloved for the strategy it built at the time.

#### YCS Bochum

YCS Bochum would be held the same weekend,
and with it, not much would shift in terms of deck representation or builds here.
Canan Ash would take the event on pure Zoodiac,
still playing the Fusion Sub combo, but notably trimming most of the fat
to cut the deck down to 41 cards.

At the start of May, we'd be graced with a new Weekly Shonen Jump promo,
this time being `Apprentice Illusion Magician`,
a level six spellcaster able to summon herself from hand by discarding a card,
searches a Dark Magician on summon,
and can send herself from hand or field to grave in the damage step
to boost a DARK spellcaster by 2,000.
While the two latter effects would rarely be used outside of specifically Dark Magician decks,
Apprentice Illusion would see minor experimentation as a level six swarm tool
for decks aiming to utilize the rank six pool, like Beatrice.

This would lead into the next core set 4 days later,
and though Zoodiac clearly had left its impact on the meta as a whole,
another massive threat was on the horizon,
and if you thought Drident's pop was a good effect to make fast, you haven't seen anything yet.

### Maximum Crisis

- Release date: May 5th, 2017
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Supreme King, True Draco, Zoodiac
- Impact: ending the era with a bang

Maximum Crisis would not only be a massive core set release
that would reshape the metagame yet again,
but would also be the final core set of the Arc-V era.
As such, a lot of anime-focused archetypes would see waves of support here,
namely those focused around the final antagonist of Z-ARC.

So let's talk about Supreme King first.
Supreme King was a new archetype of pendulum enablers for extra deck bosses,
all aimed at summoning the new big boss `Supreme King Z-ARC`,
a fusion of four dragons, one Fusion, Synchro, Xyz and Pendulum,
that nukes the opponent's board on summon, is immune to effect destruction and targeting,
cheats out a Supreme King Dragon from deck or extra when it destroys a monster in battle,
and when destroyed in the monster zone, moves to the pendulum zone,
where it locks the opponent from using Fusion, Synchro or Xyz effects
and pops a card added from deck to hand once per turn.
Obviously, this was insanely powerful,
but the caveat was it was an absolute pain in the ass to get out.

As for other members, `Supreme King Gate Zero` is the first ever scale zero.
In the scale, it blocks damage to you while controlling Z-ARC,
and can pop itself and Infinity in the other scale to search Poly or a Fusion spell,
and on the field can pop itself and another monster you control
to summon a Dragon Fusion or Synchro from extra,
dropping their stats to zero, negating their effects
and blocking it from being used for a Synchro or Xyz summon,
and able to float into a pen scale when destroyed.
`Supreme King Gate Infinity` was the first ever scale 13.
In the scale, it locks pendulum summoning if you control a monster,
and gains life from an opponent's monster's attack while you control Z-ARC,
and on field can pop itself and another card
to summon a Dragon Xyz or Pendulum from the extra,
dropping the stats to 0, negating the effects and locking it from being summon material,
and floats into scale when popped.
`Supreme King Dragon Darkwurm` in scale can activate a Gate from deck,
when summoned searches a Gate, and from grave can summon itself if you control no monsters.
But bluntly, Z-ARC was way too cumbersome to get out,
but Gate Zero and Darkwurm were actually really solid potential additions to the pendulum pile,
namely Darkwurm, being a level four DARK Dragon that searched a zero scale
and could be used with Foolish Burial. It checked all the boxes.

Predaplant would get two new notable pieces in `Predaplant Ophrys Scorpio`
and `Predaplant Darlingtonia Cobra`, who themselves formed a pseudo package.
Ophrys Scorpio on summon pitches a monster to summon a Predaplant from deck,
and Darlingtonia Cobra, if summoned by a Predaplant effect,
searches Polymerization or a Fusion spell card, once per duel.
Ladies and gentlemen, meet the new Zoodiac enablers.
While not as ideal as some of the other Zoodiac enablers, thanks to needing the normal summon,
the fact that they performed the Zoodiac setup and searched Instant Fusion
made them an instant consideration, as now the massive combo was a one card combo,
either by starting with Ophrys or `Lonefire Blossom` to access Ophrys.

Lyrilusc was a new series of level one Winged Beasts aimed at swarming the board
for quick Xyz plays.
`Lyrilusc - Cobalt Sparrow` searches a level one Winged Beast on summon,
and gives a WIND Xyz it's used to make targeting protection.
`Lyrilusc - Sapphire Swallow` can summon herself and another level one Winged Beast from hand
if you control a Winged Beast,
and gives its Xyz the ability to attach a Lyrilusc from grave to it on summon.
`Lyrilusc - Turquoise Warbler` can summon herself if you control no monsters,
and summons a Lyrilusc from grave on special summon.
`Lyrilusc - Assembled Nightingale` is their key Xyz, taking two plus level ones,
gains 200 attack for each material, can attack directly,
can attack a number of times equal to its materials,
and can detach on quick effect to protect Lyriluscs from all destruction and battle damage.
Lastly, `Lyrilusc - Independent Nightingale` is a fusion of Assembled and a Lyrilusc.
If summoned with a Lyrilusc Xyz on field, boosts its level by the materials it had,
gains 500 attack for each of its levels, is unaffected by other card effects,
and can burn the opponent for its level times 500 once per turn.
And there's the reason Neptune had to be banned.
By using Zoodiacs to access King of the Feral Imps,
one could search Neptune, summon Independent with Instant Fusion,
and tribute summon Neptune over her, copying her effect onto itself,
and making it an unaffected 6,000 attack monster, at least,
that could burn the opponent for 5K once per turn.
Needless to say, this was not okay to have in the metagame,
and thankfully it was banned before we got this interaction,
but unfortunately, this will not be the only time we mention this interaction this year.

True Draco would be a counterpart archetype to the True Kings
that had been rolled out slowly over the last few sets,
being tied almost directly to the previous Dracoslayer archetype in lore,
able to tribute summon themselves by tributing continuous spells and traps
instead of, or in addition to, monsters,
with each of the important one-tributes also having an effect
when the opponent activates a card or effect after their tribute summon,
with `Majesty Maiden, the True Dracocaster` searching a True Draco or True King monster,
`Ignis Heat, the True Dracowarrior` searching a spell,
and `Dinomight Knight, the True Dracofighter` searching a trap,
with Ignis and Dinomight also having the option to activate the cards directly from deck.
`Master Peace, the True Dracoslayer` would be the archetypal boss,
being a level eight, so it needs two tributes,
gives itself blanket immunity to the card types that it tribute summoned over,
and can banish a continuous spell trap from grave to pop a card on field once per turn
on a quick effect.

In addition to their monster line, their spell trap line was almost entirely continuous spell traps,
each of which has the ability to tribute summon an additional monster,
with the spells being for your turn and the traps on the opponent's,
each having a once per turn effect on field,
and each also popping a card on field when sent to grave,
with the spells popping spell traps and the traps popping monsters.
As for their unique effects,
`True Draco Heritage` lets you draw one for every card type of True Draco or King
sent from field to grave that turn,
`Disciples of the True Dracophoenix` let you shuffle back three True Draco or King cards in grave
to draw one,
`True Draco Apocalypse` lets you pop a True King or Draco
to halve the attack and defense of all opponent's monsters permanently,
and `True King's Return` revives a True Draco or King and locks special summons
for the rest of the turn.
Lastly, their field spell `Dragonic Diagram` boosts all True Kings and Dracos by 300,
prevents tribute summoned True Kings and Dracos from being destroyed once per turn each,
and can pop a card on field or in hand to search for a True Draco or King once per turn.

Put bluntly, this was the strongest tribute summon archetype ever conceived, and for good reason.
For starters, the continuous spells and traps trading for cards when tributed over
was insanely powerful,
as they effectively maintained their on-field advantage simply by being fodder.
Secondly, you could actually tribute over them while still set,
meaning you didn't need to show your hand to the opponent before going in on a summon.
Thirdly, the deck actively punished the opponent for attempting to push back,
specifically thanks to Dinomight Knight,
as if the opponent responds after he's on the board,
he can activate True King's Return directly out of the deck,
which then gives them the ability to reflood and keep going.
Fourth, Diagram was unbelievably strong.
The ability to pop a card in hand has for a while now been considered a very strong effect,
even if the searches lacked luster, and this one actually searches cards worth playing,
able to add any and all cards in the archetype other than itself,
while providing blanket protection for all the archetypal cards,
meaning the opponent needs to burn more effects to remove them,
which can potentially trigger the True Draco response effects.
Lastly, and certainly most importantly,
Master Peace was without question one of the most powerful boss monsters ever printed at this stage,
with an effect similar to the already meta-dominant threat in Drident,
blanket protection to two of the three card types in the game of your choice,
and stats that could actually win combat effectively.
Master Peace was instantly one of the most powerful cards in the meta,
and we will be talking about this further with an upcoming YCS.

Zoodiac, as powerful as they already had been,
actually would get another relevant wave of support here,
with not one, but two new relevant extra deck pieces.
`Zoodiac Hammerkong` has the standard Zoodiac overlay effect,
blocks effect targeting to Zoodiacs while he has material,
and must detach one in the end phase of each turn.
`Zoodiac Chakanine` has the standard Zoodiac overlay effect,
and can detach to revive a Zoodiac, blocking its effects
and from being used as material for an Xyz summon for the rest of the turn.
These two actually formed a decent loop in themselves,
as you could detach Hammerkong in a combo, revive it with Chakanine later,
and then use Tigermortar to load a material onto it,
with its end phase detach being negated the turn it's revived,
meaning that for the next turn, your Zoodiacs had targeting protection,
specifically useful for keeping the Drident up for a turn,
seeing play in the deck almost immediately.

The TCG exclusives would each get a little more support
to try and push themselves into the meta with their last efforts.
Subterror would get `Subterror Fiendess`,
able to discard herself or send herself from field to grave
when the opponent activates a card or effect,
to negate that card and flip a Subterror face-down,
and can, on the field, flip a monster you control face-down
to summon a Subterror from hand or grave face-down,
and `Subterror Final Battle`, able to either flip up a Subterror, set down a Subterror,
combine the attack and defense of a Subterror,
or prevent the negation of Subterror effects that turn,
setting itself back face-down after being used instead of going to grave.
This last wave would actually be really powerful for Subterror,
but unfortunately would do too little too late,
as Subterror was just not in a position to make any kind of waves into the oppressive meta.

As for SPYRAL, `SPYRAL Sleeper` must be special summoned by banishing three SPYRAL cards from grave,
can pop a SPYRAL card you control and up to two opponent's cards on a quick effect,
and if destroyed, nukes your board and summons a Super Agent from hand, deck or grave.
`SPYRAL GEAR - Last Resort` can equip itself from hand or field onto a SPYRAL,
prevents them from being destroyed or targeted,
and can send a card you control to grave to let its equipped monster attack directly that turn.
`SPYRAL GEAR - Fully Armed` can only equip to a Super Agent, boosts its attack by a thousand,
banishes the monster it destroys in battle, as well as another card of your choice when you do,
and when destroyed, summons a Super Agent from grave.
`SPYRAL MISSION - Rescue` recurs a SPYRAL monster once per turn,
destroys itself after three of the player's end phases pass,
and can be banished from grave to revive a SPYRAL monster.
This support would actually be significantly powerful for SPYRAL,
giving a legitimate option for actual presence on their opponent's turn with Sleeper,
in addition to the insane amount of protection from Last Resort,
and Rescue being a valuable card to pitch off of Quik-Fix's revival effect.
While still not powerful enough to be meta-viable on their own,
given the much higher bar than when they started,
SPYRAL was now at least competent enough to be considered as a splash engine for Zoodiac decks,
and potentially a meta pick once Zoodiac's reign ends.

Moving on to the one-offs now.
`D/D Ghost`, if sent to grave, can dump an additional D/D or Dark Contract from deck
with the same name as one in grave,
and if banished, can return a banished D/D or Dark Contract to grave except itself.
While on the surface unassuming, Ghost would be a piece in a few different D/D combos,
being yet another addition to the spreadsheet.
`Zefraath` in the scale can put a Zefra from deck into face-up extra
to change its scale to that monster's,
can only be summoned from face-up extra by tributing all monsters you control,
including at least three Zefra,
gives an extra pendulum summon for Zefra the turn it's summoned,
and can tribute a monster to summon a Zefra from deck.
While a massive boon for the Zefra archetype,
what this would actually see play as was a modular scale for pendulum piles,
as there were enough decent Zefra pieces at this stage to play them to modulate the scales,
then summon some of them back out for their benefits, without fully investing into Zefra as a whole.
`Fairy Tail - Luna` on normal searches a spellcaster with 1850 attack,
and can target an opponent's monster to make them either pitch another copy from deck or extra,
or bounce it, once per turn on a quick effect.
Luna would be a lot more niche than her sister Snow,
but would still see some usage,
namely in being a disruption tool that could actually search the aforementioned Snow.
`True King of All Calamities` would be the extra deck boss of the True Kings,
being a rank nine that can change all monsters on field to a declared attribute
by detaching on quick effect,
also locking attacks and effects of monsters with that attribute for the turn,
and allows True Dracos and Kings to pop opponent's monsters for their effects.
This would be a massive boss with a capital B,
becoming an instant build-around for any deck utilizing multiple level nines,
of which the only deck for the time being doing such was Dinosaurs,
who were already utilizing Lithosagym, able to take advantage of the secondary effect too,
but it would be a thorn for a long period of time after.
`Duelist Alliance` searches for any "Pendulum" card if you have a scale activated,
becoming a near staple for pendulum piles after this,
able to search a solid line of cards, including the ever-popular Luster Pendulum.
`Set Rotation` takes two different field spells from your deck and sets one
to each side of the field.
This card was insanely powerful due to a few different applications.
For starters, over the past couple of years, field spells have grown in power substantially,
almost to the point where finding them early could lock up a game on their own,
so any deck that runs multiple field spell names would instantly gravitate towards this.
But on top of that, there had been two field spells in particular released
that have mandatory search conditions tied to them,
those being `Gateway to Chaos` and `Oracle of Zefra`.
Because of the search being required, you cannot activate these if you cannot search,
and if set to your field spell zone,
you cannot activate a new field spell over them until they are properly activated,
meaning that against decks not teching a Zefra,
Black Luster Soldier or Gaia the Fierce Knight monster,
you could effectively lock them out of field spells for the game while setting up your own.
Set Rotation would become an almost instant staple for these reasons,
and we'd be seeing this card for quite some time after.
`Tornado Dragon` was a TCG exclusive rank four that can quick effect detach to pop a spell trap,
being effectively MST on a rank four,
which was basically guaranteed to see play for that reason alone.
`Bujin Hiruko` is a scale that can banish itself
to Xyz summon a Bujin over another Bujin Xyz you control,
seeing play namely for being a searchable low scale with Broadbull.
`Sylvan Princessprout` is a rank one that can detach one to excavate one,
able to add it to hand if a spell trap or send it to grave if it's a monster,
and can send another Plant from hand or face-up field to grave to revive a Sylvan,
a solid addition to the rank one pool,
only held back for now by rank ones being completely unfeasible in the game.
Lastly, and probably most importantly for the long-term implications for the game as a whole,
`Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring` can be discarded to negate any effect that would search a card,
summon a monster from deck, or send a card from deck to grave, on a hard once per turn.
Ash was one of the most talked about cards from the set, without question,
being a blanket answer to any mass combo that could go off,
and was shockingly underwhelming on release.
The problem stood that in the current meta, stopping one search or summon meant jack,
as with Zoodiac running around, they would be spewing searches and summons left and right,
but once the meta slowed down a little, Ash would find a home rather quickly.

### Pendulum Evolution

- Release date: June 23rd, 2017
- Set type: okay, look, I'm going to cut off the usual intro here,
  because I have a major gripe with this specific set

Pendulum Evolution as a pack did not exist in the OCG.
It was a complete structure deck over there.
When importing it to the TCG, somewhere along the line,
they decided that rather than bringing over the structure deck as it is,
it would be better to divide this solidly powerful structure into a set all its own in the TCG,
making the copies of new cards no longer guaranteed.
Luckily, it played out a lot better here than it could have,
as nothing was short printed and the new cards were actually decently easy to get a hold of,
but the practice itself was without question suspect,
and unfortunately, this practice would become more and more commonplace
as we entered the next era of the game,
taking price-efficient structure decks and splitting them up into import sets for profit,
a practice they hadn't done since the Upper Deck eras, for good reason.

Pendulum Evolution would be an import set exclusively targeting the OCG structure deck
of the same name,
being a massive wave of support for both the existing Pendulum Magician archetype,
but also towards the recent cover card of Maximum Crisis, Z-ARC,
providing a new way to access the boss.
Each of the four dimension dragons would get a Pendulum Magician counterpart.
`Double Iris Magician` is always treated as a Pendulum Dragon card,
in scale can pop itself to double the battle damage of a selected DARK spellcaster that turn,
and if destroyed, searches a Pendulumgraph card.
`Black Fang Magician` is always treated as an Xyz Dragon card,
in scale can halve an opponent's monster's attack and pop itself,
and if destroyed, revives a DARK spellcaster.
`White Wing Magician` is a tuner, is always treated as a Synchro Dragon,
in scale can negate a card on resolution that targets a DARK spellcaster and pop herself,
and if pendulum summoned and used for a Synchro summon, banishes herself.
`Purple Poison Magician` is always treated as a Fusion Dragon card,
can pop itself in scale to boost a DARK spellcaster by 1200 for a battle,
and pops a face-up card on field if destroyed.
There was also an updated version of Yuya's Tuning Magician, being `Harmonizing Magician`,
who is unable to be summoned from extra deck,
and when pendulum summoned from hand, summons a Magician pendulum from deck,
negating its effects and banishing it when it leaves the field.

`Astrograph Sorcerer` and `Chronograph Sorcerer` are the two outliers here,
tied to the two original Pendulum Magicians of Stargazer and Timegazer,
with each having a scale effect to pop themselves to either scale or summon one of the original two.
They share an effect to summon themselves from hand when a card you controlled is destroyed,
with Astrograph searching another copy of a monster that was destroyed that turn,
and Chronograph summoning a monster from your hand.
They also share an effect to banish themselves and one each of a Pendulum Dragon, Xyz Dragon,
Synchro Dragon and Fusion Dragon between hand, field and grave
to Fusion Summon Supreme King Z-ARC from the extra deck.
`Star Pendulumgraph` gives blanket spell targeting protection to your spellcasters,
and searches a Magician pendulum when one leaves the field, once per turn,
and `Time Pendulumgraph` gives blanket trap targeting protection to your spellcasters,
and can pop a Magician pendulum monster on field or in scale and an opponent's card once per turn.
Lastly, `Timebreaker Magician` is a Magician pendulum-locked rank four
that can detach to add a DARK spellcaster from deck, grave or face-up extra to hand,
and if a pendulum card would be destroyed, once per turn,
can dump a spellcaster from deck to grave instead.

Okay, so there is a lot to unpack here, but starting out, this support was insane.
The new Magicians actually did a lot to iron out
some of the natural weaknesses of the previous Pendulum Magician decks,
from mass summon negates like Strike being outed by Black Fang's revive,
Harmonizing allowing quick and easy Synchro and Xyz access by itself while also setting up,
and Double Iris being able to search Time Pendulumgraph
to set up easy destruction going into the opponent's turn.
With the release support from the ban list,
it was clear Konami was trying to push this deck to be a deck to beat for the WCQ season,
and probably just for the WCQ season,
as at this point, we as a player base knew what was going to be coming in July
with the new era of the game.
But for now, let the pendulum players have their fun and look at the pretty flowers.

### Battles of Legend: Light's Revenge

- Release date: July 7th, 2017
- Set type: import reprint set
- Major strategies: pendulums, dinosaur, Timelords
- Impact: the long-awaited reprint of a meta-relevant prize card

Battles of Legend, as the name suggests,
would be a successor to the Dragons of Legend packs
that we've received up until this point for the past 3 years.
After three Dragons of Legend sets, all the dragons had been printed,
and needing it to remain an import set for various OCG releases we simply didn't get,
specifically those of the Collector's Pack series at the time,
and needing a set in which we could receive reprints and erratas for specific cards,
the Legend sets would be transitioned into the Battles of Legend series,
which as of the time of writing this is still an active yearly series that continues
to see printings.
As such, while this isn't technically the inaugural set,
as technically it's the fourth in the series of the Legend sets,
it would be what marked the official start of taking the yearly sets more seriously,
as this one started to actually do reprints for the meta at large that actually mattered.

Quickly running through the imports,
pendulum piles would see a couple of new pieces in `Performapal Odd-Eyes Synchron`,

The Timelords would see their archetypal debut,

which were all boss monsters that can normal summon for no tributes if you control no monsters,
return themselves to the deck at the end of the battle phase they battled for some effect,
or in your standby phase for no effect,
and standardly had a reason to not remove them stapled on.
Honestly, these were never truly relevant,
though they would see the occasional pop-up in rogue-level stall strategies
that never saw any real success.
`Giant Rex` is able to summon itself if banished, once per turn, and gains 200 attack when you do,
and `Double Evolution Pill` banishes a Dino and a non-Dino from hand or grave

These two would be a pseudo pair for Dinosaur decks,

As for the reprints that need to be mentioned, there would be two relevant ones for the time being.

### Link Strike

- Release date: July 21st, 2017
- Set type: starter deck
- Major strategies: Cyberse, Link
- Impact: a shock to the core of the game

Link Strike would be the precursor to the first set of the new era,
and with it would come the new extra deck monster category of Link monsters,
and a shift to the game's rules, colloquially referred to as Master Rule 4.
With this change, the pendulum zones would be removed from the board,
instead now treating the left and rightmost spell trap zones as your pendulum zones,
and new zones above the center-left and center-right columns would be added,
known as the Extra Monster Zones.
With this change, from now on, a monster summoned from the extra deck
would now be required to be summoned to either an EMZ or to a zone
the new monster category of Links have an arrow pointing towards.
This meant that overnight, Fusions, Synchros, Xyz and Pendulums summoned from the extra deck
no longer could be used in the capacity they had been standardly for the past 15 years of the TCG,
and that change would cause a massive amount of backlash upon initial announcement
among the more casual bases of the game,
as it effectively made many strategies completely unplayable under these new rules.

As for the new Links, each has a summon requirement and a Link rating,
sending monsters equal to the Link rating to the grave to perform a Link Summon,
for which you can use a Link monster as multiple monsters equal to that monster's Link rating,
or as just one.
These Links have two distinct features that made them heavily different
from previous monster categories,
those being a lack of defense value,
meaning the monster could not be played in or changed to defense mode,
and the addition of Link arrows to the card itself,
which denote what additional zones can be used to summon extra deck monsters to while on the field.

At this stage, we only had three Link monsters, all sporting the new type of Cyberse.
`Link Spider` is the Link one that takes any normal monster, which can include tokens,
and can special summon a level four or lower normal to a zone it points to from hand once per turn.
`Honeybot` is a Cyberse-locked Link two
that gives monsters it points to battle destruction immunity and effect targeting protection.
`Decode Talker` is a Link three that takes two plus effect monsters,
gains 500 for every monster it points to,
and can negate an opponent's card that targets your card by tributing a monster it points to.
On initial inspection, Honeybot was discarded immediately,
Decode was slotted in as the token Link for decks to use,
and Link Spider probably had the most talk surrounding it,
thanks to its ability to convert tokens into Link material,
which brought back the classic DM era staple of `Scapegoat` into the conversation
as a massive Link material swarm,
but also Gofu the Vague Shadow became a one card Decode Talker
with Link Spider converting the tokens,
which would actually see some play for this period.
This initial weak period of Links wouldn't last long,
as the next core set would release 2 weeks later,
and with it, the Arc-V era would come to a close,
and a new era would begin, for better and for worse.

### Code of the Duelist

- Release date: August 4th, 2017
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Trickstar, Gouki, World Chalice
- Impact: setting the tone of the next year

Code of the Duelist would be the first set of the new VRAINS era of the game,
and as such, it had a lot of pressure placed on it to deliver or die,
as with the previous era's start, Duelist Alliance, the meta completely warped around its contents.
So how did it do?

Trickstar would be a new series of Fairies aimed at winning the game
in a death by a thousand cuts scenario, literally,
as almost every Trickstar card would have a small burn effect added on,
as a reactionary effect to most actions or as an additional effect to their standard effects.
`Trickstar Lilybell` can summon herself if added to hand except by drawing,
can attack directly, and adds a Trickstar from grave to hand on dealing damage.
`Trickstar Lycoris` summons herself on a quick effect by bouncing another Trickstar to hand,
and burns the opponent for 200 every time they add a card to hand, for each card.
`Trickstar Candina` searches a Trickstar card on normal,
and burns the opponent for 200 every time they resolve a spell trap.
`Trickstar Holly Angel` is a Trickstar-locked Link two
that burns the opponent for 200 every time a Trickstar is summoned to its zone,
gives Trickstars it points to destruction immunity,
and gains attack anytime a Trickstar deals effect damage to the opponent,
equal to the burn, until the end of the turn.
`Trickstar Light Stage` searches a Trickstar monster on activation,
can lock a set spell trap while it's on field until the end phase,
at which point it forces the activation of that set card or sends it to grave,
and burns the opponent for 200 anytime a Trickstar damages them.
Lastly, `Trickstar Reincarnation` banishes the opponent's entire hand
and has them draw the same number of cards,
and can be banished from grave to summon a Trickstar from grave.

Wait, what?
Yeah, so if it wasn't obvious, there was a massive outlier in the Trickstar card effects,
and that was specifically in Reincarnation.
Banishing the opponent's hand, regardless of if they get a new one or not,
is an incredibly useful and powerful effect to have in your arsenal,
and that "or not" part is actually possible to pull off thanks to `Droll & Lock Bird`,
a hand trap from all the way back in Star Strike Blast,
able to lock the opponent from adding cards from deck to hand
for the turn after they've done it once.
A common concern would be to Reincarnation after the first search,
then chain Droll, locking them from being able to draw after banishing their hand
with Reincarnation,
which almost never actually happened due to needing two specific cards to pull off,
but was still a theoretical concern sitting in the back of players' minds.
As for the Trickstars themselves, while a solid starting point was seen here
and a clear direction for the deck was present,
it was just too early to tell how this one was going to play out,
though more solid support could easily push this deck into a meta contender.

Gouki was the new archetype of EARTH Warriors,
that all share an effect to replace themselves with a search for any Gouki other than themselves
when sent from field to grave.
`Gouki Suprex` specials a Gouki from hand on normal,
`Gouki Twistcobra` tributes a Gouki on quick effect to boost another Gouki by its attack that turn,
and `Gouki Riscorpio` can normal summon with no tributes if you control no monsters or all Gouki.
`Gouki The Great Ogre` is a Gouki-locked Link three that debuffs all monsters' attacks
by their defenses,
and can pop a card it points to instead of itself on an attempted destruction.
Lastly, `Gouki Re-Match` can summon two Gouki from grave with different levels in defense mode.
An incredibly simple archetype on paper,
but the ability to cycle for other monsters when sent from field to grave for anything
made them an incredible set of cards,
specifically for Link climbing, or using as continuous material
to make higher and higher Link monster ratings.
While not quite there yet in terms of support to be meta,
it was clear that this was going to be an archetype to watch in the coming months
as more support comes down the pipeline,
as it was clearly going to be one of the better decks for summoning Links.

The Supreme King archetype would get four new variants of the dimension dragons.
`Supreme King Dragon Odd-Eyes` in scale can tribute a Supreme King monster to pop itself
and search a 1500 or less attack pendulum from deck,
can tribute two Supreme King monsters to summon itself from hand,
doubles the damage dealt by pendulums battling monsters,
and can tribute itself in the battle phase to summon two Supreme King Dragons or Gates
from face-up extra deck.
`Supreme King Dragon Dark Rebellion` is a DARK pendulum-locked rank four
that can detach in battle to steal the attack of its combat target, dropping that monster to zero,
and can return itself to extra in the battle phase for the same summon effect as Odd-Eyes.
`Supreme King Dragon Clear Wing` is a DARK pendulum-locked level eight Synchro
that nukes the opponent's face-up monsters on summon,
pops a monster it battles to burn the opponent for its attack once per turn,
and can summon itself from grave by tributing two Supreme King Dragon monsters.
`Supreme King Dragon Starving Venom` takes two DARK pendulums,
can summon itself from the extra by tributing materials,
can copy the name and effect of any monster on field or in grave for the turn,
and deals piercing damage for the turn that it does.
These four were all pretty solid additions to the Pendulum Magician deck at this stage,
all being easy summons with incredibly powerful effects,
but one of these was bound to cause problems.
Starving Venom's effect was almost identical to the previous Tyrant Neptune's,
meaning that the Assembled Nightingale burn line was once again available,
and far easier to pull off now as a proper FTK,
as Starving Venom's effect copy is not a hard once per turn,
with each copy able to burn for 4,000 damage, only taking two to end the game.
Luckily, this was held back a little by the recent Master Rule 4 changes,
but was bound to be an issue in the future if not handled relatively soon.

D/D/D would get a few more combo pieces to add to their ever-growing arsenal.
`D/D Vice Typhon` can tribute a D/D on normal to summon a level seven D/D/D from deck,
and in grave can Fusion Summon a D/D/D by banishing materials from grave, including itself.
`D/D/D Gust High King Alexander` is a level 10 Synchro
that gains 3K if there are three plus D/D/Ds on field,
and can revive a D/D when another D/D is summoned, once per turn.
`D/D/D Wave High King Caesar` is a Fiend-locked rank six
that can detach to negate and destroy anything that special summons a monster,
then boost itself and another D/D by 1800 that turn,
and searches a Dark Contract when sent to grave.
A lot of new powerful options for the D/D/D core,
but unfortunately held back massively by the new Master Rule changes,
needing some kind of easy access Link to do anything big.

World Chalice would be a pioneer in what I'm going to refer to as the lore archetypes,
those being a series of multiple archetypes all tied together under a centralized lore
that plays out over the course of multiple years of the game.
As for this one, the archetype is centered around three normal monsters,
`Crowned by the World Chalice`, `Chosen by the World Chalice` and `Beckoned by the World Chalice`,
which are important for this in that they are normal monsters with World Chalice in their name.
`World Chalice Guardragon` can be sent from hand or field to grave
to negate anything that targets a linked monster,
meaning a monster that has a Link arrow pointing to it,
and can be banished from grave to summon a normal monster in grave
to a zone a Link monster points to.
`World Legacy - "World Chalice"` would be the first of the new World Legacy archetype,
meant to be an anchor archetype between the various lore archetypes
that will be trickled out over the coming core sets.
It can tribute itself when a monster is summoned from the extra
to send that monster to grave,
if it leaves the field after being normal summoned or set,
summons two World Chalice monsters from deck,
and can be banished from grave to search a World Legacy card.
`Imduk the World Chalice Dragon` is a Link one that takes specifically a normal,
lets you extra normal a World Chalice once per turn,
destroys anything it battles that it points to,
and summons a World Chalice from hand when sent to grave.
`Ib the World Chalice Priestess` is a Link two taking different types and attributes,
can't be destroyed and has targeting immunity while linked,
can send itself to grave to protect a monster she points to,
and summons a World Chalice from hand when sent from field to grave.
`Auram the World Chalice Blademaster` is a World Chalice-locked Link two,
gains 300 for each different World Chalice in grave,
can tribute a World Chalice it points to to summon another monster from grave
to a zone it points to,
and floats into a World Chalice in hand.
`Ningirsu the World Chalice Warrior` is a Link-locked Link three,
draws cards up to the World Chalices it points to on Link summon,
can send a card from each field to grave once per turn,
and floats into a World Chalice in hand.
All in all, a solid intro course to Links as a whole,
but the archetype was severely lacking any kind of a real payoff
to do anything other than be a tutorial deck for now.

Lightsworn would get a whole new sub-archetype,
playing into their identity as history's number one chaos enabler,
being the Twilightsworn, DARK attribute counterparts to Lightsworn,
focused on banishing rather than milling.
`Jain, Twilightsworn General` can banish a Lightsworn in hand to drop a monster's stats
by its level times 300,
and mills two if another Lightsworn uses its effect, once per turn.
`Lyla, Twilightsworn Enchantress` can banish a Lightsworn in hand when a spell trap is activated
to pop a spell trap on field,
and mills three once per turn when another Lightsworn effect is used.
`Lumina, Twilightsworn Shaman` can banish a Lightsworn in hand
to summon a banished Lightsworn other than Lumina,
and mills three when another Lightsworn effect goes off.
`Ryko, Twilightsworn Fighter` on normal or flip can banish a Lightsworn in hand or grave
to banish a card on field,
and mills three when another Lightsworn effect goes off.
`Punishment Dragon`, being a counterpart to Judgment Dragon,
can summon itself if there are four different Lightsworn names banished,
can pay a thousand to shuffle all graves and face-up banishments into deck on a quick effect,
and mills four when a Lightsworn effect goes off.
`March of the Dark Brigade` recurs any Lightsworn with a level,
then banishes cards from the top of deck equal to its level.
`Twilight Twin Dragons` can recur a Judgment Dragon and mill four if you control Punishment Dragon,
and can recur a Punishment Dragon and banish four off the top of deck
if you control Judgment Dragon.
Lightsworn has almost always had a place in the meta,
from enablers to its own dedicated strategy,
so this support would be welcomed in quickly,
though Lightsworn wasn't quite viable at this point in time.

A new set block means new TCG exclusives,
and this round we'd see a fairly interesting new take on rituals with Vendread.
The primary gimmick of the deck is to revive their Zombie main deck pieces
in order to use them as ritual material,
passing effects from them to the ritual monsters in question.
`Vendread Houndhorde` can discard a Vendread card to revive itself,
and if used from field as ritual material,
lets its summoned monster quick effect banish a spell trap once per turn.
`Vendread Revenants` revives itself when destroyed,
and if used from field as ritual material,
lets its summoned monster quick effect banish a special summoned monster once per turn.
`Revendread Slayer` is the starter ritual monster here,
able to banish a Zombie in grave to boost itself by 300 in battle,
and if sent to grave, can dump a Vendread monster from deck.
`Revendread Origin` is the starter ritual spell,
able to banish Zombies in grave as material in addition to the standard ritual summon,
and can be banished from grave to protect Slayer specifically from destruction.
`Vendread Reorigin` tributes an opponent's monster with a level
to summon a token with the same level, locking you to Vendreads while on the field.
Vendread had an interesting first wave, for sure,
but was far from meta-viable at this stage, requiring a couple more waves to get going.
But a small detail here that would be a welcome sight
was the fact that despite Origin being a generic ritual spell for Vendreads,
by mentioning Slayer in a separate effect, it was in fact searchable with Pre-Preparation of Rites,
being a trend we'd see more commonly with ritual archetypes in the future.

The other TCG premier archetype here was F.A.,
being a successor to U.A. in many respects, namely in its lore and art style.
Each F.A. gains attack from its level,
can boost its level by one when an F.A. spell trap is used,
and gains a new effect once its level goes to seven or higher,
with `F.A. Sonic Meister` able to attack twice a battle phase,
and `F.A. Hang On Mach` putting a one-sided Macro effect on the opponent.
`F.A. Circuit Grand Prix` increases F.A. levels by two in the battle phase only,
draws a card when an F.A. wins combat,
and searches an F.A. card when destroyed by card effect.
`F.A. Downforce` boosts an F.A.'s level by two for a turn,
and can be banished from grave to do the same.
While definitely interesting, restructuring the rulings on monster levels, now able to go above 12,
F.A. wouldn't see play here, needing a lot more support to even be considered at all.

`Rescue Ferret` can summon exactly six levels of monsters from deck
to zones a Link monster points to,
with their effects negated, and destroys them in the end phase,
being a far more generic version of the previous Rescue monsters,
but requiring Links to be fully usable.
`Spellbook of Knowledge` can send a Spellbook or spellcaster from hand or field
to grave to draw two,
effectively turning the previous `Spellbook Magician of Prophecy` and `Spellbook of Secrets`
into a normal summon Pot of Greed and deck thinner,
with Prophecy or Secrets searching the other, the other searching Knowledge,
and converting the Prophecy into a draw two.

As for the proper introduction of generic Links, we'd have three here worth talking about.
`Firewall Dragon` is a Link four with generic materials
that can bounce monsters on field or in grave to hand once while on field,
up to the number of its co-links, meaning Link monsters it points to that also point back to it,
and when a monster it points to is sent to grave, lets you summon a monster from hand,
notably with no once per turn restriction.
This was a massive problem card, and the community knew that the moment this card was released,
being one of the most overtuned protagonist ace monsters we've seen since Stardust.
The fact that it let you just empty out your hand to use as Link materials,
then immediately do it again from that, was recognizably busted,
and was so generic that it was bound to cause some kind of an issue in the future,
clearly being a catalyst for some kind of FTK in the near future
once we got a couple more generic Links.
`Topologic Bomber Dragon` is another generic Link four
that nukes the main monster zones when a monster is summoned to a zone a Link monster points to,
and burns the opponent when it attacks a monster for that target's original attack.
Bomber Dragon was a go-to boss for this period of the game,
able to lock a zone from the opponent and constantly threaten a board nuke with any summon,
seeing play for these reasons initially.
Lastly, as a way to ease the player base into the idea of using Links,
the attribute stat boosters from Metal Raiders would be retrained here
into generic attributed Links,
seeing one release per core set for a period of time.
`Missus Radiant` would be the first, being the EARTH Link,
boosting EARTHs by 500, debuffing WINDs by 400, and can recur an EARTH when destroyed.
Why is the EARTH first? Why on Earth would you print the EARTH first?
So, remember how people thought that Links were going to be good for slowing down the game,
thanks to making decks unable to spam out extra deck monsters anymore?
Why don't we just instantly invalidate that point by releasing the piece that fixes that issue
for exactly Zoodiac?
We'll talk more about this in a second, but we've got a Worlds to cover first.

#### World Championship 2017

The 2017 World Championships would be held a little over a week after CODE's release,
on August 12th,
and notably, Master Rule 4 would be in effect,
but the only legal set with Links in it for this event would be the Link Strike starter deck,
heavily hampering many decks,
meaning that decks that use zero to one extra deck monsters at a time would be heavily preferred
here.
Also, extremely notably, Zoodiac was not at all a viable option here,
as Drident and Broadbull were banned and Ratpier was limited in the OCG,
taking the TCG tier zero threat off the table.
Another point of note here would be the single unknown slot on this pie chart,
as in top eight, Shinsuke Hayama of Japan, the two-time reigning world champion,
would be disqualified for slow play, which resulted in not getting to know his list,
as it was never shown on the stream for the event.
With all of that said, Sujimura Rosoki of Japan would take the event on True King Dino,
being without question the best performance the deck had seen thus far,
thanks primarily to the conditions of the event.
The deck didn't really care if it couldn't have more than one extra deck monster out at a time,
as primarily the only three you'd ever summon on the first turn would be Denglong
to get the counter trap,
or Trishula to hand rip, and then summon All Calamities.
A lot of the deck's power lied in the main deck with Lithosagym and UCT,
so it wasn't too big of a surprise to see it do well here.

#### Mega Tin

The 2017 Mega Tin would come down just 2 weeks later,
and as usual reprinted the core sets of the last year,
with the most notable reprints here being those of the Zoodiacs getting a full core reprint.
In addition to this, the promo slots would bring a couple of new cards as well,
bringing `Proxy Dragon`, a generic Link two,
and `Elder Entity N'tss`, a counterpart to Norden that was not nearly as good as Norden,
able to pop a card when sent to grave from anywhere,
being useful for cards like `Nekroz Kaleidoscope` that dump from extra deck.

#### YCS Toronto

YCS Toronto would be that same weekend,
though unfortunately, due to poor coverage, we do not have a top cut for this event.
Kristal LeBlanc would take the event on True Draco Zoodiac, marking his third YCS win,
and placing the player in spitting distance of Billy Brake's record of four wins,
seeing very little changes to the deck as a whole for the new Master Rule 4,
outside of a singular copy of Missus Radiant to enable multiple zones.
LeBlanc would also be the recipient of the new YCS prize card,
`Number 89: Diablosis the Mind Hacker`,
a rank seven that can detach to banish a monster from the opponent's extra deck face-down,
can banish a card from the opponent's grave face-down at the end of the battle phase
if it won combat that turn,
and can banish the top of the opponent's deck face-down
up to the number of their face-down banished cards, once per turn,
being seen primarily as a dissuasion from running Pot of Desires while it was in play.

#### YCS Rimini

YCS Rimini would be held the same weekend, and, oh, a rat.
Zoodiac was once again nearly uncontested as the tier zero mega threat it's been
for the last 8 months,
taking 24 of the top 32, including the event, piloted by Andreas Vos.
With Konami releasing Missus Radiant immediately in CODE,
Zoodiac completely ignored the Master Rule 4 change,
pivoting themselves into a tier zero mega threat yet again,
and unless something was done about it soon, we were in for yet another Zoodiac format.
With the game hemorrhaging players at this stage,
the next release needed to do something to stop that bleeding and bring in new players soon,
or we'd be seeing a doomsday scenario that many feared would be the end of Yu-Gi-Oh as we knew it.

### Legendary Duelists

- Release date: September 8th, 2017
- Set type: Duelist Pack
- Major strategies: Red-Eyes, Amazoness, Cyber Dark
- Impact: anime support with little impact

Legendary Duelists would be a reshift of the previous Duelist Pack series,
focused on providing support to various anime character decks in a single location.
For this inaugural set, we'd see support for Joey's Red-Eyes, Mako's Legendary Fisherman,
Mai's Amazoness, Bastion's Chemical Beasts, Zane's Cyber Darks and Syrus's Roids,
all of which would have no meta impact at large,
but it was still quite notable,
as this pack series would be a recurring sight in the coming years
for any and all decks seen from the anime getting a facelift with new support.

#### Ban list (September 18th)

The September 2017 ban list would take effect 10 days later, on September 18th,
and with it, massive changes would be felt across the game.
Newly banned were Daigusto Emeral, Denglong, Dinomight Knight, `Grandsoil the Elemental Lord`,
Lithosagym, Broadbull and Drident.
Newly limited were Ignis Heat, Miscellaneousaurus, Ratpier, Dark Hole, Interrupted Kaiju Slumber,
True King's Return, and the return of `Gateway of the Six` from the banned section.
Newly semi-limited, and all coming up from one,
were Black Luster Soldier - Envoy of the Beginning, Bionic, Luster Pendulum, `Mathematician`,
`T.G. Hyper Librarian`, Brain Control, Foolish Burial, `El Shaddoll Fusion`
and Pre-Preparation of Rites.
Lastly, unlimited were Debris Dragon, Honest, Rescue Cat, `Summoner Monk`,
Witch of the Black Forest with her new errata, `Charge of the Light Brigade`, `Dragon Ravine`
and Wavering Eyes.

All right, get lost, you're fired. You, get out of here, you movers.
That's right, keep moving. You know what? You know what? Yeah, check you, you stay.
Without question, this ban list was aimed at absolutely destroying the meta
of the last 8 to 9 months,
with a cleaving blade coming down onto specifically that of Zoodiac,
seeing its two core extra deck pieces banned,
and Rat, a card that needs to be at more than one copy to operate the way it wants to, limited.
True King Dino, being the deck that won Worlds, was bound to get hit too,
and losing Lithosagym and Denglong would be critical losses,
as well as the limiting of Miscellaneousaurus,
though it could still be useful thanks to Oviraptor being able to search it.
True Draco looked like it was also hit pretty bad,
losing Dinomight Knight and most copies of Ignis Heat in return,
but with Master Peace sitting completely untouched, the deck was far from gone.

#### Legendary Dragon Decks

Following that would be a super minor release, being the Legendary Dragon Decks,
being a set of three preconstructed decks themed to the three Legendary Dragons,
Cyber Dragons, and the four dimension dragons,
bringing one new card for each, being `Dark Magician the Dragon Knight`,
`Chimeratech Megafleet Dragon` and `Odd-Eyes Arc Pendulum Dragon`.
Of these, the only one to see any kind of use was Megafleet Dragon,
able to turn a Cyber Dragon into a removal for anything in the Extra Monster Zone,
leaving a 2400 attack monster in its wake.

#### YCS Guadalajara

YCS Guadalajara would be held the same weekend, and with the culling of Zoodiac,
the meta would shift rapidly,
with Pendulum Magicians filling the top deck position, taking the event, piloted by Gabrielle Romo.
Pendulum Magician received a massive boon from the last ban list,
taking out its primary competition,
and even playing well through the Master Rule 4 change,
thanks to the raw power of the new Supreme King Dragons
and the ability to get your materials back to hand for the pendulum summon.
True Draco would continue to see success thanks to the lack of hits towards Master Peace,
still being at three, with three Diagram making him incredibly reliable to establish still.
Trickstar would see its first top here thanks to the Windwitch engine,
as by setting up your lines using just a normal summon,
the deck doesn't mind locking its specials for the turn in exchange
for an indestructible Crystal Wing,
especially when the Droll Reincarnation combo is accessible.
SPYRAL would see its first ever pure YCS top here,
with the realization that Links solved SPYRAL's main issue,
getting Master Plan on and off the board.
By utilizing quick Link climbing tools like Gofu, and Quik-Fix with Machine Duplication,
accessing Firewall was a simple task, which could cheat out Master Plan
and then Link her off the board to trigger all of her searches on the same turn.
This realization would make SPYRAL a serious talking point for the meta at large,
but this was about to get a lot worse,
as the next core set was just around the corner,
and a certain set of imports was about to come to us far sooner than we were ready for.

### Circuit Break

- Release date: October 20th, 2017
- Set type: core set
- Major strategies: Rokket, Altergeist, Krawler
- Impact: out of one tier zero and into another

Circuit Break would be the final core set of the year,
and with it would come primarily support for strategies introduced back in CODE,
with a couple of new additions sprinkled in.
Rokket would be one of the new archetypes,
being a series of DARK Dragons focused on effects that pop themselves for some form of advantage,
though the initial wave of monsters here left a lot to be desired.
However, there were two pieces here worth talking about.
`Borreload Dragon`, the boss monster of the archetype,
was a generic Link four taking three or more effect monsters,
had targeting protection from monster effects,
can drop a monster's stats by 500 on a quick effect that's unrespondable,
and when attacking a monster, can steal it to the zone it points to,
sending it to grave at the end of the turn.
This would become a staple Link four in the pool, a growing roster, to be sure,
but this would be one of the most reliable ways to win combat
and potentially push massive amounts of damage in one go.
The other would be `Quick Launch`, which summons a Rokket from deck and pops it in the end phase,
which would be considerably better once we received good standalone Rokkets.

Altergeist was the other big archetype here,
being a series of monsters that form resource loops with their trap cards.
`Altergeist Marionetter` sets an Altergeist trap from deck on normal,
and can send an Altergeist card from field to grave to revive another Altergeist.
`Altergeist Silquitous` on a quick effect can bounce an Altergeist card and an opponent's card,
and if sent from field to grave, recurs an Altergeist trap.
`Altergeist Meluseek` can attack directly,
can send an opponent's card to grave after dealing battle damage,
and if sent from field to grave, searches an Altergeist monster.
`Altergeist Primebanshee` is an Altergeist-locked Link three
that can tribute an Altergeist to summon another from deck to a zone it points to,
and when sent from field to grave, recurs an Altergeist card.
`Altergeist Camouflage` equips itself to an Altergeist,
preventing it from being attacked and negating anything that targets it,
and can be banished from grave to protect an Altergeist from destruction.
`Altergeist Protocol` prevents the negation of Altergeist cards,
and lets you send an Altergeist from field to grave to negate a monster effect once per turn.
`Personal Spoofing` shuffles an Altergeist card from hand or field to deck
to search an Altergeist monster once per turn.
While not quite there yet in terms of power level,
Altergeist showed some real promise with this initial wave,
with Meluseek and Marionetter forming a decent line of disruption,
with Meluseek being removed resulting in Marionetter setting up a trap
and bringing back Meluseek to keep going.

Gouki would receive `Gouki Headbatt`, with the standard Gouki search,
and could send another Gouki from hand to grave while in hand
to summon itself in defense and boost a Gouki on field by 800.
More importantly for Headbatt was that it could summon itself if a Gouki was on field,
making it an excellent swarm option for the Link-focused deck.
`Amano-Iwato` brings Spirits back into the game,
locking all monsters except Spirits from activating their effects.
Some players would tinker with this as a way to block hand traps,
while others would look for a way to prevent Amano-Iwato from activating its effects,
either locking its return to hand effect and keeping an effect lock on the board.
`Destrudo the Lost Dragon's Frisson`, for a cool half of your life points,
can either summon from hand or grave if you control a level six or lower,
lowers its level by that monster's,
returning to the bottom of the deck when it leaves field after this effect is used,
effectively able to turn any low-level monster into a level seven Synchro.
Destrudo would be a very hotly desired option for many decks, for both AFD and Yazi combos,
especially now that Yazi for Mare Mare actually did something rather powerful
in flooding the board with Link materials, being a one card Firewall Dragon.
`Akashic Magician` is a Link two requiring same-type monsters and no tokens,
can only be Link summoned once per turn,
on Link summon bounces all monsters she points to,
and can declare a card name to excavate cards up to the number of monsters co-linked to her,
adding all copies of the declared card to hand and milling the rest.
Akashic would be a solid addition to the extra as a piece repeater, situational disruption,
and at worst, a generic Link two that points down, being played for all of these reasons.
`Mistar Boy` would be the WATER generic Link, boosting WATERs, debuffing FIREs,
and recurring a WATER on destruction,
being not as useful out the gate as Missus was, but still solid for WATER strategies.
`Metaverse` either searches or activates a field spell from deck,
being a great option for suddenly activating a counter field spell for various strategies,
such as Necrovalley and `Secret Village of the Spellcasters`.
`Evenly Matched` can be activated at the end of the battle phase,
and can be activated from hand if you control no cards,
forcing the opponent to banish their board face-down until they control as many cards as you,
which, if you're using the from-hand application, is one,
because Evenly will still be on the board when it resolves.
This was a massive tool for going second,
something we haven't really seen in a long time for the game,
being a way to equalize against a board that was able to fully set up.
`Lyrilusc - Recital Starling` is a rank one that can detach to search a level one Winged Beast,
meaning that any deck with rank one access now could search D.D. Crow,
which was actually shockingly relevant coming up here shortly.
`Number 41: Bagooska the Terribly Tired Tapir` is a generic rank four
that detaches a material in your standby phase or destroys itself,
in attack position can't be destroyed or targeted by effects,
and in defense position changes all monsters to defense and negates the effects
of monsters in defense.
Bagooska, commonly referred to as Plan B,
was an extremely common sight in any deck that could make rank fours moving forward,
as if their play lines get interrupted, you could always make a defense position Bagooska,
which could buy you a turn to regather your resources.

Lastly would be two OCG imports that we were not expecting to be brought over so soon,
being the OCG additions to the last two TCG exclusive archetypes,
notably being brought over months earlier than usual,
as we usually expected to receive them in our Gold Series-styled sets,
like Duelist Saga earlier this year.
`Subterror Behemoth Fiendess` was a flip-locked Link two,
gained attack for every Subterror level used to make it times 100,
can dump a flip monster from deck to summon a monster from hand face-down to a zone it points to
once per turn,
and if a card it points to is flipped, you can either search or recur a flip monster.
This was incredibly powerful support for Subterror,
but the only real drawback for it being the lack of enablers,
with so much of the archetype being high-level bosses,
causing Fiendess to see no real play out the gate.
`SPYRAL Double Helix` was a SPYRAL-locked Link two that becomes Super Agent on field and in grave,
can declare a card type to check the top of the opponent's deck,
and if called right, searches or special summons a SPYRAL from deck or grave.
This was beyond incredible for SPYRAL,
solving the main issue present with the strategy,
that being access to Master Plan and Super Agent on a whim,
as it was considered Super Agent on field and in grave,
making the strategy a complex powerhouse that was bound to make an impact at the next YCS.

#### YCS Dallas

And YCS Dallas would be held the same weekend,
and instantly, SPYRAL became the new tier zero mega threat,
taking 29 of the top 32, including first place, piloted by Imran Khan,
who played a mirror match against his brother, of all people, in the finals.
The deck was an advantage machine,
thanks in large part to Double Helix giving quick access to Master Plan
and for being considered Super Agent,
which in turn allowed the deck to cycle copies of Quik-Fix for extra searches,
which could be used to cycle them more,
providing Link material to climb up into Firewall Dragon in multiple quantities,
or can even be used to access more niche options,
like the new Recital Starling to search D.D. Crow,
Sylvan Princessprout to potentially get a spell trap draw, or Duan to answer a large attack threat.
This would also primarily be one of the first true showcases
of how powerful Link summoning actually was,
especially in the case of a deck that didn't have planning around it to begin with,
as, more so than Synchros or Xyz, Links as a summon mechanic had a host of monsters
that would prove to be problematic,
due to being designed long before the concept was even talked over for the first time.

As for what, if anything, could compete with SPYRAL at this stage,
the answer was not a whole lot on their own, but a couple of decks did manage here.
Trickstar would crack into the top 32 thanks to playing a suite of 13 hand traps,
with the primary goal being to stop a Double Helix push before it got massively out of hand.
Because of all the searching that was going on with SPYRAL,
both Droll & Lock and Ash Blossom were prime candidates,
which in turn meant that the Reincarnation lock had a far more natural place
in the current meta space than previously.
The other deck that cracked in was Invoked,
quite notably taking advantage of the variety of hand trap attributes to make their Fusions,
and the lack of need for main deck space to absolutely load up on hand traps
to stop the new tier zero menace.
One option that would appear here would be that of Set Rotation,
taking the Oracle of Zefra approach to lock the opponent out of SPYRAL Resort,
while also setting up your own Magical Meltdown,
which gave the Invoked player a serious advantage
by locking SPYRAL out of a critical piece of their support.

#### YCS London

YCS London would be the following weekend, and a very similar show would be present,
with SPYRAL once again clearing the tier zero threshold with 27 of the top 32 spots,
taking first place, piloted by Jesse Cotton,
a player who had been a familiar face in the field for some time now,
taking five YCS tops and seven premier event tops, but this would be his first YCS win.
One of the more interesting inclusions here would be that of `PSY-Framegear Gamma`,
which provided a layer of insulation at the start of your lines against hand traps,
such as Ash Blossom being used against your one-for-one,
which in turn gets you two more monsters to use as Link material in addition to your standard line.
With hand trap heavy decks becoming the norm,
a deck that would pull a surprising top cut placement here would be Burning Abyss,
playing so many starters that hand trapping one or two of them was by no means a turn end,
taking advantage of the meta landscape.
Pendulum Magicians would top here as well, seeing a very similar deck comp to before,
but now loaded up on hand traps for the SPYRAL matchup.

#### Ban list (November 6th)

Quite suddenly, a new ban list would go into effect on November 6th, just one week after London,
believed to be an emergency hit to SPYRAL to prevent another tier zero domination,
limiting Blackwing - Gofu, SPYRAL GEAR - Drone, SPYRAL Quik-Fix and Set Rotation,
limiting three of the most common play starters for SPYRAL,
as well as one of the best counters against the deck,
but whether this would be enough was still to be seen.

#### Spirit Warriors

Two weeks after this list, we'd receive the deck building set Spirit Warriors,
which brought new support for Six Samurai, and the Secret Six Samurai,
an archetype of quick-play and trap cheating, LIGHT Fiends,
and Magical Musketeers, and Fairies that gain effects to their columns
from their continuous spells and traps, in Weather Painters,
but none of these three would be anywhere near viable enough to crack into the meta at large.

#### YCS San Diego

Even with the supposed emergency list in effect,
YCS San Diego was the same weekend as Spirit Warriors,
and despite the hits, SPYRAL continued to appear as the dominant threat of the metagame,
though at a state where others could now compete, or so it seemed on the surface.
Fil Khan would take the event with SPYRAL,
being the brother of the YCS Dallas winner that lost in finals a month prior,
getting the run back of the century.
Even with the deck hit pretty bad,
the same line of Master Plan to search three is still an incredible power play,
especially now that Set Rotation was limited,
meaning getting locked out of combo was less likely.

#### YCS Prague 2

The final YCS of the year would be just a week later, in Prague,
once again receiving the title of Prague 2 to help differentiate.
Raphael Nevan would take the event with SPYRAL,
showcasing how even though the deck was more in line now,
it was still undoubtedly the best in the room.

I wish I could say that this was where 2017 ends,
with a nice open meta with a clear best deck, but in a way that was still beatable,
but unfortunately, there was one more release right at the tail end of the year.
The December Weekly Shonen Jump promo would become legal for use on December 1st,
being `Linkuriboh`, a Link one requiring a level one,
that can tribute itself to make an opponent's attacking monster drop to zero attack,
and can revive itself by tributing a level one monster.
All of the issues that were solved on Quik-Fix Link climbing immediately became present once more,
as you could summon Quik-Fix, search a SPYRAL card, Link it off for Linkuriboh,
pitch the searched card to resummon Quik-Fix, and do it all again,
able to do it up to six times between the actual summon of Linkuriboh
and the tribute to summon from grave effect,
meaning that with this, tier zero SPYRAL was back on the menu,
which sadly we will need to save for next time.

### Outro

As with this release, 2017 Yu-Gi-Oh came to a close,
with a year full of tier zero after tier zero, a player base that had been heavily fractured,
and a game teetering on the edge of yet another tier zero format.
Everyone still remaining held out hope that things would get better in 2018.
However, it's always darkest before the dawn,
and sadly, in this context, it could still get a good bit darker.
Buckle up, this is going to get really bumpy, really fast.
