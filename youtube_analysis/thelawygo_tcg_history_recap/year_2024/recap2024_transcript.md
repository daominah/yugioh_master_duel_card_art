### Intro

2024 in Yu-Gi-Oh, what an odd year to say the least.
Not only did we see a continuation of the 25th anniversary celebration
with new Rarity Collections and new quarter century printings,
but also a fundamental shift to the core of the meta with multiple big archetype releases.
This year we saw four core sets, two import sets, three reprint sets.
That's it. What a rip.

Okay, put bluntly, this year is going to be a bit of a rough one to cover,
because compared to last year nothing happened:
no structure decks, no duelist packs, and just a surprisingly low number of sets in general,
as we're used to getting two to three deck build packs a year.
This year generally just feels lighter in terms of new additions compared to the past,
yet somehow more expensive.
Without structure decks bringing affordable options to the table,
all new strategies are isolated to specifically packs,
which in turn means that anything new to build is going to be more expensive,
which leads us to a rather polarizing year in total.
Regardless, we still have a lot to cover, so let's dive in.
This is your 2024 Yu-Gi-Oh TCG recap.

#### January 1st ban list

Starting off the year quite literally on January 1st,
we'd see a new ban list take effect to try and address some of the decks from late last year.
Newly banned were `Agido the Ancient Sentinel`, `Kelbek the Ancient Vanguard`,
`Mathmech Circular` and `Eldlich the Golden Lord`.
Newly limited were "Airlifter", "Dry ass" (unresolved), `Sunvine Healer`, "Sharara",
`Gozen Match`, `Rivalry of Warlords`, "Tik bu" (unresolved), `Orcust Harp Horror`,
a "redo" card (unresolved) and `Snatch Steal`, all returning from the banned section.
Newly semi-limited were `Dinowrestler Pankratops`, `Speedroid Terrortop`
and `Purrely Sleepy Memory`.
Newly unlimited were `Infernity Archfiend`, `Kashtira Unicorn`, `Mind Control`,
`Pot of Desires`, `Spellbook of Judgment`, `Spright Starter` and `Upstart Goblin`.

Honestly speaking, this is a fairly light balancing,
clearly aimed at just toning down the outliers of the format:
specifically removing the "Isisu" Mill pieces entirely,
light hits to Ragnaraika (Raganara) and Unchained,
plants, limiting most of the major summon floodgates,
and effectively removing Infernoble Knight (Infernoy Knights) from the format for the sins of
another.
This is a historical moment here in Indianapolis.
This leads us into the first set of the year,
bringing back an idea from the previous year
to see if any more money can be drawn from the pockets of DM era nostalgia.

### Maze of Millennia

- Release date: January 19th, 2024.
- Set type: import and reprint, premiere.
- Major strategies: `Flame Swordsman`, Earthbound, Animation Chronicle imports.
- Impact: a sequel to Maze of Memories from last year.

Maze of Millennia is a sequel to Maze of Memories from last year,
aimed at bringing in all the imports from the Animation Chronicle set in the OCG,
as well as premiering a new archetype based on some pre-existing anime nostalgia.
This time bringing Flame Swordsman a full-blown archetype.

`Fighting Flame Swordsman` searches a Flame Swordsman mentioning spell trap on summon
and dumps a Flame Swordsman or mentioner from extra deck if sent to grave.
`Salamandra, the Flying Flame Dragon` equips itself from hand or grave to a warrior,
boosts the Flame Swordsman or mentioner by 700 attack while equipped,
and searches a Salamandra spell trap when sent to grave.
`Mirage Swordsman` summons itself from hand or grave when a fire fusion is destroyed,
pops itself (the monster) it battles in the damage step,
and if destroyed by anything but its own effect floats into a level seven or lower
Flame Swordsman or mentioner from deck or extra.
`Ultimate Flame Swordsman` is a fusion of `Flame Swordsman` and `Fighting Flame Dragon`
that can pop a monster to burn the opponent for 500,
can do it on a quick effect if it has an equip card,
and can double its attack in combat but pops itself at the end of the turn if you do.
`Fighting Flame Dragon` is a fusion of a dragon and a fire warrior,
can equip itself from board or grave to a warrior fusion,
boost a Flame Swordsman or mentioner by 700, and gives it a second attack each battle phase.
`Flame Swordsrealm` locks responding to a fire warrior normal summon,
sends a card from hand to grave to cheat out a Flame Swordsman once per turn as a fusion summon,
and on attack declaration can drop a warrior by 1000 to boost all other monsters you control
by 1000 for the turn.
`Salamandra Fusion` boosts a fire warrior by 700, sacks itself to protect its equip,
and can send itself and a fusion monster it's on to grave
to cheat out a Flame Swordsman or mentioner from the extra as a fusion summon.
`Fighting Flame Sword` searches a Flame Swordsman mentioner,
can pop a card when a fire warrior attacks,
or negates anything that targets a Flame Swordsman or mentioner.
`Salamandra with Chain` gives a fire monster it's equipped to 700 attack
and flips an effect monster face down on activation.
It can banish itself from grave to fuse a fire warrior or dragon
by shuffling back materials from hand, field or grave, but destroys it in the end phase.
`Flame Swordsdance`, in response to a special summon, cheats out a level seven or lower
Flame Swordsman or mentioner from extra deck and flips a monster face down.
It can be banished from grave and tribute a face down monster to cheat out another
without the level restriction from deck or extra.

A lot to unpack here, but the long and short is that the deck really wants you to get to
`Ultimate Flame Swordsman` equipped with `Fighting Flame Dragon` as fast as possible,
which, when done right, gives you a quick effect popper and a double attacker
that can double its attack on command and keep itself from going down
by popping the dragon instead, which can then just quick effect put itself back on from grave.
After the Gate Guardian pieces from last year were sort of a jumbled mess,
this time the TCG premiere actually feels coherent,
which is all the more disappointing that it wouldn't do anything in the meta.
Though arguably it wasn't the deck's fault this time:
being an archetype centered around fire warriors, the clear synergy here
would be to mix it with the Infernoble Knights, who had actually been decently relevant
in the last few months.
Unfortunately this is a solid had just been banned prior to this release,
meaning that accessing specific warriors out of the deck on command
wasn't really as simple as before,
and as such Flame Swordsman, while a competent archetype here,
was not going to be able to keep up with the rest of the meta.

`Earthbound Prisoner Ground Keeper` summons a level five or lower Earthbound

`Earthbound Prisoner Stone Sweeper` can summon itself if there's a field spell up

`Earthbound Prisoner Line Walker` searches an Earthbound Prisoner or `Harmonic Synchro Fusion`

then letting the opponent summon a new monster of the same name.
`Earthbound Prison` is a field spell that negates a monster's effects on activation
for as long as it's on the field, gives you an extra Earthbound normal summon each turn,
and if destroyed by the opponent while you have an Earthbound, halves their life
and negates all their face up cards for the turn.
`Harmonic Synchro Fusion` sends a tuner and a non-tuner from field to grave,

`Earthbound Servant Geo Gremlin` is a dark locked synchro that can, on quick effect,

and can quick effect fuse an Earthbound fusion by banishing materials from hand, field or grave.
`Earthbound Servant Geo Gryphon` is a dark locked synchro that can quick effect summon
an Earthbound from grave, locking you to fusions and synchros when you do,
and when destroyed by an opponent pops a card and burns for 300
for each different Earthbound on field and in grave.
`Earthbound Servant Geo Grasha` is a fusion of two Earthbounds,
searches the field spell on summon, nukes the opponent's monster when they special summon,
and burns them for 800 when you do.
"Geoga" (unresolved) takes an Earthbound fusion and an Earthbound synchro,
changes the monster it battles to 0, nukes the opponent's board when they lose a monster,
and floats into any Earthbound in deck or extra, based on their appearance in the Arc-V anime.

Earthbound's severely outdated play style of drop a boss with a field spell
has now been replaced by an interesting hybrid fusion synchro game plan,
and while it wouldn't be meta, it would spark a bit of discussion
around specifically `Harmonic Synchro Fusion`.
While there wasn't a clear deck that could use it now,

### Phantom Nightmare

- Release date: February 9th, 2024.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Yubel, Goblin Biker, Voiceless Voice, Snake-Eye support.
- Impact: one card to change the meta.

Phantom Nightmare on the surface seems like a fairly unassuming set in the grand scheme:
a couple of new archetypes, updates for some legacy archetypes,
and a couple of pieces to support recent releases.
But those couple of pieces would completely change the meta at large.
As usual, we're starting with the bigger archetype releases.

`Spirit of Yubel` has a standard Yubel protection, floats into a Yubel from hand, deck or grave,
can summon herself when the opponent attacks, and when special summoned sets a Yubel mentioning
spell trap from deck.
`Grinder Golem` can reveal a Yubel monster to summon itself from hand to the opponent's board
and the revealed Yubel to your board, gives you back 3000 when you slam a Yubel into it,
and can summon itself to the opponent's board from grave when you summon a Yubel.
`Samsara D Lotus` can tribute itself to summon a Yubel from deck, tribute on the opponent's turn
while you control a Yubel to change any monster's effect to "destroy Yubel",
and recurs or summons itself from grave in your end phase if you control specifically Yubel.
`Yubel - The Loving Defender Forever` takes a Yubel and any number of effect monsters on board,
burns the opponent for 500 for each material used, has the standard Yubel protections,
and after battle burns the opponent for their monster's attack and banishes it.
`Nightmare Pain` pops a dark in hand or on field to summon a Yubel or mentioner,
forces the opponent to attack Yubels, and reflects any damage involving Yubels back to the opponent.
`Mature Chronicle` gets a counter anytime a Yubel monster or mentioner is summoned.
It can remove one from the board to summon a Yubel from grave, two to recur a banished card,
three to banish a card from deck, four to pop a card, and five to search.
`Eternal Favorite` can either summon a Yubel
from banishment or grave, locking responses to the summon,
and can discard a card and send itself to grave to Super Poly using Yubels you control.
Super Poly wet dream aside, this is a really solid reinterpretation of a GX classic monster line.
While not the greatest on its own right now, due to the concessions you'd have to make
on your end board, standardly choosing between actual disruption with a Samsara Yubel setup
or properly setting up the greatest Super Poly target ever,
only getting both with an ideal opening,
Yubel would be considered as an option to load into the Unchained fiend box mega conglomerate,
as it were.
Not much else beyond that for the time being.

`Goblin Biker Dugg Charger` can detach a material from an Xyz on either board to summon itself
and search a Goblin Biker spell trap on summon.
`Goblin Biker Clatter Sploder` summons a Goblin from grave on summon

`Goblin Biker Boom Mach` can summon itself the same way from hand or grave
on either player's main phase on a quick effect.
`Goblin Biker Mean Merciless` on summon summons a Goblin Biker from hand

`Goblin Biker Big Gabonga`: have you ever Kabanga?

as material when a material is detached off it, and can detach a Goblin from deck to it
in the end phase.
`Goblin Bikers Gone Bonkers` (heard as "crazy beast") takes two plus level sixes,
can detach two on quick effect to suck up a spell trap as material,
can detach to prevent monster destruction,
and can revive itself and attach a Goblin from grave to itself if in grave once per duel.
`Goblin Biker Grand Bash` boosts Goblins by 3000 for each Goblin you control,
gives an extra Goblin normal summon once per turn,
and can target two Goblins to either match their levels to one another
or make both of their levels their combined total.
`Goblin Biker Grand Entrance` searches a Goblin, then detaches a material from an Xyz
to summon a Goblin from hand, and can be banished from grave to detach a material from an Xyz
to recur a Goblin.
`Goblin Biker Grand Stampede` Xyz summons using Goblins or can imperm a monster
by detaching material from an Xyz once per turn.
Hilarity aside, Goblins were a solid rank three archetype,
but sadly didn't do anything that rank threes didn't already do between the Punks and the
Gold Pride archetypes, and those aren't even meta at the moment,
so sadly these wouldn't be either.

Lo, `Lo, the Prayers of the Voiceless Voice` can be used as a whole tribute
for a light warrior or dragon ritual, places a Voiceless Voice continuous spell trap from deck
face up on board when summoned, and can summon herself from grave

`Sauravis, Dragon Sage of the Voiceless Voice` can summon himself

can bounce itself to hand in response to an opponent's card or effect
to cheat out a light warrior or dragon ritual from hand or deck,
spinning it away in the end phase of the next turn.
`Skull Guardian, Protector of the Voiceless Voice` gains 2050 while Lo is on field or in grave,
can search a Voiceless Voice or ritual warrior or dragon on ritual summon,

`Prayers of the Voiceless Voice` can ritual summon any light ritual.

to summon Skull Guardian or the original Sauravis or Saffira from deck.
`Barrier of the Voiceless Voice` locks the opponent to only attack rituals

Taking the old and forgotten one-off rituals of the past,

and did a fairly solid job in that sense.

Voiceless Voice would prove to be a potential slower control deck for the format,

As for the one-offs, only two are really worth talking about, but they're quite a doozy.
`Snake-Eyes Poplar` summons itself when added to hand except by drawing,
searches a Snake-Eye spell trap on summon,

This card completely revolutionized Snake-Eye as a meta strategy
by giving them far more material to work with in their combos
and by providing access to the other half of their spell trap lines.
Up until this point the only spell in the archetype that was searchable was
`Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`, and that was thanks to `Diabellstar the Black Witch`,
but now you had access to the field spell `Divine Temple of the Snake-Eye`,
which in turn gives you more extension by providing easy fodder for your lines
and also a way to push monsters out of the spell trap zones outside of
`Snake-Eyes Flamberge Dragon`.
As for the material to work with: by using OSS for `Snake-Eye Ash` to search Poplar,
this Poplar would instantly summon himself, search Divine Temple, be linkable for `Linkuriboh`,
then come back as a continuous spell to be fodder for Ash's effect
while maintaining a monster on the board.
This also meant that Flamberge could realistically be weaved into lines
far more reliably, thanks to material revival now having an easier to reach target than Oak was.
But that was also helped along with another card from this set.
`Promethean Princess, Bestower of Flames` is a link three taking two or more effect monsters,
locks your summons to fires while on board, can revive a fire once per turn,
and can pop a fire and an opponent's monster to revive herself on the opponent's turn
when they special summon.
Princess being able to revive a fire meant that you could link off Flamberge to get its material
summon off and then revive it to utilize both of its effects,
being the monster to continuous spell version to use on a piece in grave, standardly I:P
in early builds, and then summon it back to the field with its other effect
on the opponent's turn to make a reactive "SP" (unresolved).
Princess's fire lock only being active while she's on the field also made it fairly simple
to play around, as you could always link her off for something like
`Salamangreat Raging Phoenix` for accessing "Zantis" (unresolved)
or just drop down to Aita (unresolved) to continue comboing off
while now placing a bomb into the grave for later.
All of this was just scratching the surface of what was possible with these new cards,
and that's not even mentioning `Bonfire`, which can now search Ash to search for Poplar,
giving the deck an entirely new way to combo off that didn't even burn the OSS usage,
meaning that you could search it with Poplar and save it for some more interesting lines.

#### Team YCS San Jose

A team YCS in San Jose would be the first showcase of the deck's power,
and showcase it the tournament did, with Snake-Eye variants taking a whopping 39
of the potential 48 top spots for the top 16.
Here though this should be taken with a very heavy grain of salt,
as this was a team event, so the deck's results would be slightly inflated due to that factor.
Team Next Door would take the event, being all on Snake Eyes pure,
and also notably being the first team of the exact same three players to win a second team YCS.
The group's decks primarily focused on the newfound consistency of Snake-Eye thanks to Poplar
by loading up on hand traps and insulation beyond the core of the strategy,
but also quite notably played `Jet Synchron` as an option to access via
`Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`, with Jet accessing `Borreload Savage Dragon`
being a cakewalk thanks to it and `Snake-Eyes Diabellstar` getting the job done alone.
But also quite notably, by tuning with a Snake-Eye you can make `Formula Synchron` for the draw,
then continue on as normal, loading it into the back row with Flamberge to reset instead of I:P.
This option allows you to convert said Flamberge into `Baronne de Fleur` on the opponent's turn,
which in turn would also trigger Flamberge again to reflood the board,
now under an omni-negate insulation.
This would be a far more proactive version of the I:P line that had already been considered,
although that option was very much still on the table too.

Pure wouldn't be the only version of the deck to make waves,
as a Fire King variant would also be quite popular here.
As previously discussed in our 2023 coverage, `Diabellstar the Black Witch` accessing
`Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye` gives the deck access to the Fire King cards
thanks to `Legendary Fire King Ponix` being a level one fire,
which in turn means that now with Poplar in the mix,
starting with Ash for Poplar also allows you to not only access the full Snake-Eye line
but also the full Fire King line, by converting the searched OSS into Ponix.
While not leaving as much room for hand traps and non-engine as the pure variant,
this version had a lot more firepower in terms of interaction,
thanks to the Fire King cards, which were quite adept at constantly providing
meaningful interaction points on the opponent's turn.
The only other deck with a decent showing here aside from those two was Voiceless Voice,
whose main draw was not being named Snake Eyes.
Being a slower control deck allowed itself to play lower to the ground
in the face of mounting massive advantage generation,
which standardly did mean that the deck had a difficult time keeping up,
but under the right circumstances could be devastatingly difficult to out,
specifically in circumstances involving sticking a Skull Guardian in with the Lo.

#### Team YCS Las Vegas

Another team YCS would be held in Las Vegas just a week later,
and we'd see similar results to San Jose, with Snake-Eye variants taking 40 of the potential 48,
which again is slanted due to the nature of a team YCS.
The Jaari brothers (name as heard) would take the event, rocking two Fire King Snake Eyes
and one pure Snake Eyes, seeing similar builds to those used in San Jose.
But far more notably on this team was Chris LeBlanc,
which meant that his YCS win total ticked up to six,
marking him once again as the record holder for most YCS wins.

#### YCS Sydney

YCS Sydney, two weeks later, would be the first non-team YCS of the year,
and with the team format no longer slanting the results
we'd see that Snake-Eye had taken a comfortable tier one position
with a lot of other decks under it vying for tier two to three positions.
Heatsoul Kashtira Hero would be seen here, utilizing `Mecha Phantom Beast Dracossack`
as a way to convert their Kashtira bodies into Heatsoul via `Link Spider`
and `G Golem Crystal Heart`, giving the more control oriented deck a way to draw that much deeper.
Phantom Knight Adventure already had a reputation for being able to play effectively
entirely out of the graveyard, so adding in the Horus engine here makes perfect sense,
allowing the deck to set up massive draws to back up any discarded.
A Playmaker's Shark deck (unresolved) would see a top here,
utilizing the newer water pieces to access `Number 4: Stealth Kragen` quickly,
which when paired with `Gozen Match` is just a board lock for most matchups,
on top of having access to both the `Armored Xyz` line and `Bahamut Shark` for `Toadally Awesome`.
Branded isn't a new sight at this stage and unfortunately is still nightmare locking players
out of the game, specifically using Sanctifire (`Albion the Sanctifire Dragon`),
now that "Exposion" (unresolved) was banned.
Though it wouldn't be as powerful as Snake Eyes.
Salamangreat would also top here, utilizing the ever growing power of the fire link pool,
specifically that of `Promethean Princess, Bestower of Flames`,
able to climb into `Salamangreat Raging Phoenix` in archetype far easier.
Vanquish Soul would also see a top, taking advantage of the best anti-Snake Eyes option
in the format, being `Dimension Shifter` and `Summon Limit`,
which unfortunately would grow in popularity as the year goes on.
Jason Wang would take the event on pure Snake Eyes, with the entire top eight notably being on
Snake Eyes pure or Snake Eyes Fire Kings.

#### YCS Medellin

YCS Medellin would be three weeks later, and Snake-Eye, while clearly still the best deck
in the format, had dropped to only 50% representation in the top cut,
with still no deck though able to put up enough numbers to compete regularly
outside of a tier three level showing from Voiceless Voice and Kashtira.
Many players were experimenting with the more meta fringe decks to see if they could
break the format open.
Infernoid would top here, not only being one of the decks to benefit from the inclusion of
`Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`, thanks to `Infernoid Decatron` being a level one fire,
but also being able to quick effect banish the Snake Eye combo pieces while still in grave,
still utilizing the core of Snake Eyes, playing the Infernoid side in a similar manner
to how Fire King is used.
"Monum" (unresolved) would see a crack in here, still aimed at balls to the wall synchro play
to end on multiple level 10 bosses between `Baronne de Fleur`, "Disper" (unresolved)
and "Shangying" (unresolved), with not much changing in the build overall.
Runick would see a top, still sporting the stun package game plan,
with this outing also sporting `Time-Tearing Morganite` to give the deck the gas needed
to keep up with the flood of options Snake Eye sports, and `Synchro Zone` being used effectively
as `Messenger of Peace` without the maintenance cost.
Christian Urano (name as heard) would take the event with pure Snake Eyes, as to be expected,
with no real notable standouts in his build.

#### YCS Guadalajara

YCS Guadalajara (heard as "Squad laara") would be two weeks later,
and once again no real shakeups in the topping lists outside of Voiceless Voice overtaking
Fire King Snake Eyes in representation, though this is more so due to the player base

#### April 15th ban list

Even though it wasn't a tier zero level performance,
a solo tier one deck is still a massive issue when it comes to format diversity,
and as such a new ban list would go into effect directly following Guadalajara on April 15th,
which would not take shots at Snake Eyes specifically,
but rather the structure of meta decks for the past couple of years in general.
Newly banned were `Linkuriboh`, `Baronne de Fleur`, `Borrelsword Dragon`,
`Borreload Savage Dragon` and `Summon Limit`.
Newly limited were `Anti-Spell Fragrance` and a series of banned cards
all coming back to limited: `Archnemeses Protos`, `Tidal, Dragon Ruler of Waterfalls`
`Thunder Dragon Colossus`, "Kieran" (unresolved) and `Chicken Game`.
Newly semi-limited were `Armageddon Knight` and `Purrely Delicious Memory`, both up from one.
Lastly, unlimited here were `Destiny HERO - Malicious`, "Harror" (unresolved),
`Speedroid Terrortop` and "Engage" (unresolved).

An absolute head turner of a ban list.
`Baronne de Fleur` and `Borrelsword Dragon` biting the bullet in real time caught everyone's
attention.
The duo of synchros have been staple pieces of various meta end boards for years at this point,
and with these bans Konami made it clear that they were taking aim at the generic negation
bosses that had become staple, though many noted that they clearly missed one
by not banning `Apollousa, Bow of the Goddess`.
`Linkuriboh` would be the only real Snake Eyes specifically directed hit,
and though there were other options to slot into Snake Eyes like Apollousa,
`Linkuriboh` was more than just a link one, regularly appearing in the end board
as a way to block your pieces from simply getting walked over,
being a great hit alongside the generic negate bans.
A few concerns would also arise from the release of `Archnemeses Protos` and Colossus,
two cards that have been considered boogeymen in their respective times,
namely in locking out decks from being able to play, period,
with Colossus now having a far simpler way to summon and `Nemeses Corridor`.
Though we'd have to see if they'd make any real impact overall.

#### YCS Raleigh

YCS Raleigh would be the next weekend, and unsurprisingly Snake Eye remained the best deck
in the format, taking the event piloted by Cameron Neil,
pivoting the synchro slots towards "Disper" and "Omega" (unresolved)
to replace Baronne and Savage, now aiming to hand rip the opponent with the two
by banishing Omega for his effect then cycling it back to the board with Disper.
Clearly the ban list hadn't done anything to dethrone Snake Eyes,
but that was only part of the equation, as the next core set would release a week later
and if anything was going to be able to compete, it would have to be found within that set.

### Legacy of Destruction

- Release date: April 26th, 2024.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Shining Sarcophagus, Ragnaraika, Tenpai Dragon (also Melodious).
- Impact: one new player in the meta.

Legacy of Destruction is a bit of a weird set in retrospect,
as going into it there wasn't a whole lot of hype behind it.
In the OCG this hadn't done anything to change their Snake Eye infestation,
so how could it realistically fare better here in the TCG?

`Shining Sarcophagus` cannot be destroyed by monster effects, can search a card that mentions it
once per turn, and can discard a spell when the opponent revives a monster to put it back
in the grave.
I know it's a bit weird to start with the spells for us,
but it's important for us to know this card when going over the remainder of the archetype,
just because it's an anchor piece.
`Gandora-G the Dragon of Destruction` gains 300 for each banished card,
can summon itself if you control Shining Sarcophagus (heard as "submit itself"),
and can pay half your life to nuke the board other than itself, banishing those cards
and then summon a level seven or lower Shining Sarcophagus mentioner from the deck,
increasing its level by the cards destroyed.
`Silent Swordsman Zero` increases its level by one each standby phase,
gains 500 attack for each additional level it has,
and can negate an effect that targets a Shining Sarcophagus or mentioner,
increasing its level by one when you do.
`Silent Magician Zero` gains a level when the opponent draws,
for each card drawn gains 500 for each additional level it has,
and can negate a spell card or effect while you control Shining Sarcophagus,
to increase its level by one.
`Gadget Trio` searches the Shining Sarcophagus or mentioning spell trap on summon
and, if destroyed, sets `Stronghold the Hidden Fortress` from deck.
`Moremarshmallon` can't be destroyed by battle while Shining Sarcophagus is active
and is the only thing attackable, can summon itself from hand if you control Shining Sarcophagus
on a quick effect, and if destroyed by card effect summons another from literally anywhere
except itself, then burns the opponent for 1000.
`Turn Silence` boosts a monster's level by three,
then negates a monster effect if chained, and if Shining Sarcophagus is up it can finish itself
from grave to end the battle phase when a Shining Sarcophagus mentioner battles.
`Ties That Bind` summons two different level four or lower monsters
that mention Shining Sarcophagus from deck if you control Shining Sarcophagus and a mentioner,
but locks you from extra deck summons for the rest of the turn.
`Future Silence` searches a Shining Sarcophagus mentioning monster,
then if used in the battle phase while Shining Sarcophagus was up, makes both players draw
until they have six cards in hand.
`Stronghold the Hidden Fortress` summons itself as an effect monster that gains 1000 for each
Shining Sarcophagus or mentioning monster you control and can destroy an opponent's attacking
monster while you control Shining Sarcophagus once per turn.

Honestly, a really interesting archetype based entirely around Yugi's deck from his final duel
against Seto Kaiba at the end of the original anime, and its lines are really quite solid.
Starting from `Gadget Trio` you can search Sarcophagus, search Gandora with Sarcophagus,
summon Gandora and destroy the Trio, which will get you both one of the other monsters
out of the deck via Gandora and Stronghold via the Trio,
resulting in a battle popper and standardly a spell negation with `Silent Magician Zero`
coming down off Gandora, which will have 2000 attack by the time the opponent's turn starts.
While decent enough, the problem stood that the deck didn't really do enough for the current
landscape, as it really seemed like the deck could put up exactly one piece of interaction
per engine piece in the opener, and sadly that didn't do all that much
when other decks of the meta could do so much more with much less.

`Refrain the Melodious Songstress` can dump a Melodious from deck to boost a Melodious fusion
by its level times 200, searches a Melodious monster on summon,
and can scale herself from extra when a Melodious fusion is summoned.
`Couplet the Melodious Songstress` locks your pendulum summons to lights,
can search a Melodious spell trap if you only control Melodious monsters,
and if added to hand except by normal draw, special summons a level four or lower Melodious
from hand or grave, and shares the same rescaling effect as Refrain.
`Bacha the Melodious Maestra` takes any two Melodious monsters, blocks Melodious fusion effects
from being negated, summons a Melodious from deck when summoned,
and revives a Melodious other than herself when sent to grave.
`Flowering Etoile the Melodious Magnificat` takes a Melodious Maestra and two other Melodious
monsters, can quick effect banish any number of Melodious monsters to bounce that many
opponent's cards, returning the Melodious monsters at the end phase,
and if removed by the opponent floats into any Melodious in deck or extra.
`Melodious Concerto` fusion summons a fairy using monsters from hand, field or pendulum zones,
and if a Melodious fusion hits the grave while it's in grave places itself on the bottom of the
deck to draw a card.
Huh, breaking an old fusion from deck spell, I wonder why that seems familiar.
Making new fusions for the older spells can pose balancing issues if the new fusion is too
powerful or generic, specifically with targets like `Neos Fusion` and
the one heard as "Atinado" (unresolved).
All right, "Atinado": for many that would immediately come to mind, being a fusion from deck
spell that truly has no real downsides, allowing it to be used as an engine enabler for various
strategies, but most notably for decks with a pyro starter.
By starting with either Refrain or "Asado" (unresolved) one could weave a line through Bacha
without locking themselves, able to climb up to make `Apollousa, Bow of the Goddess`
thanks to cards like Xyz the pendulum, then pendulum summon back out two level fours to make
`Infernal Flame Banshee`, which in turn gets you to a pyro from deck,
meaning that Snake Eyes now have a way to get their starters while also accessing an Apollousa
for insulation on their play lines.
While this wasn't the only use for the Melodious cards, it was by far the most prominent,
and as such it would be an incredibly useful engine for any deck that needed access to
rank fours and insulation, which are most decks.
As for the archetype itself, "Atoy" (unresolved) was actually a pretty solid boss for the
archetype, able to tag out your board to ruin your opponent's just to have yours come back later,
and floats into the incredibly annoying to deal with `Bloom Diva the Melodious Choir` to boot,
as any Duel Links player can attest.
The best part of the deck is that while `Solo the Melodious Songstress` does lock you to
Melodious monsters, the rest of the archetype doesn't, outside of their link
that can be used for in-archetype explosivity,
which means that you can either divert yourself down any number of generic options
or opt for the in-archetype path to set up `Aria the Melodious Diva` and `Elegy the Melodious Diva`
at the same time.
All of this was incredible for Melodious but wouldn't rise them above the rogue camp for now,
thanks in large part to the still prevalent Snake Eyes and another archetype we'll discuss soon,
though its usage as a generic engine would be noted.

`Ragnaraika the Evil Seed` can summon itself from hand by pitching an insect, plant or reptile,
which I'll be referring to as the tri-type for this section,
and on summon searches two Ragnaraika cards from banishment or deck,
then banishes a card from hand.
`Ragnaraika Samurai Beetle` summons itself from hand by cycling back a banished tri-type
and, if sent to grave for a Ragnaraika link summon, revives a level four or lower tri-type.
`Ragnaraika Armored Lizard` summons itself from hand by banishing a tri-type from grave
and can discard a tri-type to bounce an opponent's non-tri-type monster.
`Ragnaraika Skeletal Soldier` is a link two requiring two monsters with at least one of the
tri-type, can revive a Ragnaraika once per turn, and can bottom a tri-type you control to summon
itself from extra.
`Ragnaraika Mantis Monk` is a link three requiring at least one of the tri-type,
can banish two tri-types from grave to search a Ragnaraika trap,
and has the same self-revive effect.
`Ragnaraika Chain Coils` is a link four with the same summon requirement,
can lock both players' in-hand monster effects for the turn in response to an opponent's
monster effect, and has the same self-revive effect.
`Ragnaraika Stag Sovereign` is a link five requiring two plus of the tri-type,
pops two monsters when the opponent summons from the extra deck, and has the same self-revive
effect.
`Ragnaraika Bloom` boosts the tri-type by 300 and drops all non-tri-types by 300,
and can either search a Ragnaraika monster then discard a card,
or special summon a Ragnaraika from hand, grave or banishment once per turn.
`Ragnaraika Hunting Dance` pops opponent's cards up to the number of different types in the
tri-type that you control or link for, and can be banished from grave when a tri-type is
destroyed to pop a monster your opponent controls.
While we always do love a new tri-type in this household, especially one with as much promise
as plants, insects and reptiles, the issue stood that Ragnaraika effectively just did more of
what plants and insects already could do, providing near infinite board swarming.
The main thing both sides needed was a good payoff for all of that swarming,
as standardly you'd lock yourself at some point in there and need to stay in type to build a board,
which this release did nothing to solve.
For now these would be disregarded, but as with all other plant and insect support,
would have an eye kept on it in case a payoff ever did arise.

`Tenpai Dragon Paidra` searches or sets a Sangen spell trap on summon,
blocks battle damage to you from your fire dragons, and can quick synchro in the battle phase.
`Tenpai Dragon Fadra` on summon, or when a monster battles in the damage step,
revives a level four or lower fire dragon, blocks fire dragons from battle destruction,
and can quick synchro in the battle phase.
`Tenpai Dragon Chundra` can quick synchro in the battle phase, can summon itself from hand
if you control a fire dragon, and summons a level four or lower fire dragon from deck
when a monster battles.
`Sangenpai Bident Dragion` is a dragon locked level
seven synchro that revives a fire dragon on synchro summon,
could revive itself on quick effect if three or more attacks have been declared that turn,
popping a card on field when you do.
`Sangenpai Transcendent Dragion` is a dragon locked level 10 synchro,
forces the opponent to attack if able, locks the opponent from using cards or effects in the
battle phase, swaps the board to attack mode on synchro summon,
and has the same self-revival effect as Bident Dragion, and summoning makes your fire dragons
unaffected by opponent's activated effects in the main phase.
`Sangen Summoning` can search a Tenpai Dragon then discard a card once per turn,
and if destroyed in the battle phase doubles the attacks of a dragon synchro.
`Sangen Kaimen` can either search a level four fire dragon, summon a fire dragon from hand,
or do both if you activated it in the battle phase.
While unassuming on the surface, due to the stigma surrounding the battle phase decks
up until this point, Tenpai was actually far more potent than any battle focused strategy
we'd ever seen before, namely because of its consistency to block the opponent out of stopping its
lines, then moving to the battle phase to end the game over four times in one turn.
If Tenpai gets to the battle phase they can output a maximum of 37,400 damage off of a single
card, being either Paidra or Kiman, meaning that in addition to the 15 main deck slots that
you need for the deck, the remaining 25 can be dedicated towards hand traps and consistency tools
to help ensure the beyond lethal hit, namely that of `Pot of Prosperity`,
as you can still OTK through the half damage downside.
This was partially thanks to the Edison format staple `Trident Dragion`,
who gave the new Tenpai bosses that big damage push needed,
but therein lied the main issue with the deck out of the gate:
Trident Dragion was a required card and only had an original printing from 2009.
The card was instantly bought out and the price skyrocketed,
prompting Konami to suggest to the community that if you don't have a copy
you could just borrow one from a friend, which got memed into the ground instantly.
Overall this deck, thanks to its ability to play so much non-engine, would be a contender
for the upcoming meta game, but just how much would have to be seen.

`Snake-Eyes Diabellstar` can move itself or an opponent's attacking monster to the spell trap zone
and can, while a continuous spell, place a fire monster from grave in the spell trap zone
to move itself back to the monster zone.
This would be a solid extension piece for the pure Snake-Eye variant,
giving you a way to convert Poplar and Flamberge's spell trap zone effects into even more
swarming potential, though notably it didn't really fit into the Fire King variant.
`Centur-Ion Gargoyle II` can send a face up Centur-Ion card to the grave to summon itself from hand,
if sent to grave for a synchro material adds itself back to hand,
and can summon itself if treated as a continuous trap, then reduce its level by four.
`Centur-Ion Auxila` is a level eight synchro that protects your face up continuous cards
from being destroyed, searches a Centur-Ion on summon, and in the end phase can place a
non-synchro Centur-Ion from grave or banishment on board as a continuous trap.
`Wake Up Centur-Ion!` can summon a level four or eight token if you have a monster card
in your spell trap zone, locking it to not be fusion or link material,
and can be banished from grave to dump a Centur-Ion from deck to grave.
Auxila on its own solves a ton of the issues in Centurion, namely in that it's a level eight
that you can actually go into on turn one for more access to interaction points
by searching the trap or by finding further extension pieces,
which also does mean that the Calamity lock that Centurion was already famous for is now even
easier to achieve.
It also makes a small Centurion package desirable for other strategies that need easy access
to synchro lines, in addition to any way into a level eight synchro now also being a Centurion
combo, making `Wake Up Centur-Ion!` a desirable option for the deck.
Damn, `Hot Red Dragon Archfiend King Calamity` has to get banned now, right?

`Varudras, the Final Bringer of the End Times` is a rank 10
that can detach for an omni-negate, an additional detach for a pop when you do,
if it attacks while it has material can pop a card, and if destroyed pops another card.
While a rank 10 omni-negate is a lot harder to utilize than a level 10 synchro was,
Varudras would actually have a home almost instantly in specifically Yubel,
as level 10s are fairly easy to access for that deck, especially now thanks to
`Nightmare Throne`, which searches or destroys from deck a Z00 fiend on activation,
searches Yubel when a Yubel monster leaves a field that is one level higher or lower
than the one that left, then summons it ignoring summoning conditions.
`Gruesome Grave Squirmer` can summon itself from hand if you control a fiend,
popping a Yubel or mentioner on field when you do,
and can be banished from grave to summon a fiend with Z00 from hand or grave.
Throne specifically would be massive for the deck, now able to search for
`Dark Beckoning Beast` and `Samsara D Lotus`, effectively giving the deck four more starters
between itself and terraforming, and Throne being able to pop `Spirit of Yubel` from deck itself
is another starter, as there's a slew of lines that you can now make through
Dark Beckoning Beast that makes Lotus almost irrelevant.
The only real issue is that Nightmare Throne mentions Yubel monsters multiple times on the card
but not Yubel herself, meaning that the field spell is not searchable within the archetype,
suffering from the same issues as `The Eye of Timaeus`.
Squirmer is also a great piece of extension and insulation in a way, as since its summon effect
is a quick effect you have the option to summon it in chain
to something like `Infinite Impermanence`
to remove the imperm target from the field so that your effects can properly
resolve in the grave.

#### YCS Rition Era

YCS "Rition Era" (as heard, unresolved) would be held just a week after Destruction's release,
and though Snake Eyes was clearly still the dominant deck in the room,
a couple of shifts had happened with the new set's release.
For starters, the Fire King variant had fallen off hard here,
dropping from its dominance to only taking two total slots in the top 32.
Secondly, while none would be prevalent enough to claim a true second best deck position,
a couple of decks would put up showings to back up a claim for that spot,
specifically Voiceless Voice, who continued to show promise through the entire last format too,
and newcomer Tenpai, who would actually do solidly with all of the generic omni-negate bosses
removed from the meta, though the deck had a difficult time playing into Apollousa still.
Speaking of which, the third shift would be the appearance of yet another set of engines
in the Snake Eye arsenal, with Melodious popping up here,
as both Refrain and "Asado" provided full one card combos for Snake Eyes,
providing them easy access to lines in addition to Apollousa for insulation against hand traps.
And Kashtira becoming a household name again, being an engine used by first place Rafael Marino:
by opening `Snake-Eyes Diabellstar` and `Kashtira Unicorn`, one could weave through the Kashtira
line to get Unicorn to search `Kashtira Birth`, which gives you material enough to access
the necessary material to hit Apollousa with an I:P protection,
all before the usage of `Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`,
which could then get you to the full Snake Eye combo.

### 25th Anniversary Rarity Collection 2

- Release date: about three weeks after YCS Rition Era (no exact date given).
- Set type: reprint set.
- Major strategies: none, retro format reprints.

### Battles of Legend: Terminal Revenge

- Release date: June 21st, 2024.
- Set type: import set.
- Major strategies: Genex, Ritual Beast, Yubel (also Ice Barrier, Infernoid).
- Impact: a newcomer to rogue and a kick up the rears.

Terminal Revenge, as a side set, held a lot of intrigue behind it,
specifically because it would be importing the Terminal World set from the OCG,
being a pack designed specifically around printing legacy support
for four of the Duel Terminal archetypes from between 5D's and the Arc-V eras,
which are some of the most beloved archetypes in the game's history.
Granted, with every one of these kinds of releases some archetypes are going to be treated
better than others, and sadly Ice Barrier was sort of doomed from the word go,
namely in that the cards introduced were supporting the newer structure deck support
for the archetype rather than the original game plan,
which to be fair was the correct thing to do, but at this stage the archetype wasn't really
worth salvaging.
Though it did give one specifically good card.
`Mirror Mage of the Ice Barrier` is a level two Aqua tuner that, when sent to grave,
can search for any Ice Barrier card from deck to hand,
which can search the previous `Freezing Chains of the Ice Barrier`,
which can revive an Ice Barrier on activation.
All of the stars align for this card's specific usage: being a level two water Aqua
that, if dumped from deck, searches the spell that revived it.
And just like that, `Swap Frog` once again became a power player for Sprite decks,
because Swap Frog can dump any level two Aqua: it can dump Mirror Mage,
who searches Freezing Chains, which revives it, and boom,
instant access to either a rank two or a level four synchro,
which means access to `Toadally Awesome` or `Herald of the Arc Light`,
once again bringing omni-negate access to Sprite in a much easier form.

`Infernoid Evil` is a fusion of two Infernoids, banishes an Infernoid in grave on fusion summon
to dump Infernoids up to the banished one's level from deck,
and if sent to grave searches a Void spell trap.
`Infernoid Flood` is a link four requiring at least one Infernoid, can tribute a monster to negate
an Infernoid special summon, banishing it, banishes a card on board if a card in grave is
banished, and if destroyed floats into an Infernoid from deck ignoring summoning conditions.
`Void Reignition` is an in-archetype card destruction for yourself only
and can be banished from grave to return up to 11 different Infernoids from banishment to grave.
`Void Breach` boosts an Infernoid by 100 for each banished Infernoid
and can, once per turn, either banish an Infernoid or special summon an Infernoid from hand
ignoring summoning conditions.
A solid bout of support for Infernoid, especially given their recent top via the Snake Eyes,
but sadly this support would miss the mark quite heavily,
due to requiring that the new spells be effectively played with pure Infernoid,
and because Evil has no real way to summon an archetype except `Void Imagination`,
which gives your opponent the agency to control an extra deck monster first,
meaning that you're in no way going to be able to resolve that spell.

`R-Genex Undine` can banish a Genex from grave to gain its attribute and tuner status
if applicable, and can recur itself and another Genex from grave if you control a Genex synchro.
`R-Genex Turing` can summon itself from hand if you control a Genex in the opponent's main phase,
then quick synchro summon a Genex synchro, treating itself as a one, two or three for that summon.
`Arms of Genex Return Zero` is a level 10 synchro requiring a dark tuner that can negate and
destroy a monster effect by banishing a like attributed Genex from grave,
able to use it once per attribute per turn, and can shuffle up to six Genex with different
attributes in grave or banishment to deck to pop that many spell traps on board.
`Repair Genex Controller` is a Genex locked link one that can recur a Genex on link summon,
and if a Genex is added to hand except by drawing, you can normal summon it once per chain.
Okay, this card is a Dark Nash wet dream, and yes, there is a near infinite looping combo line
that this can access thanks entirely to the R-Genex line searching another level on normal summon,
but this is also the most impermeable monster in existence, effectively locking in
that it's the coolest link one a deck could ever get,
but sadly won't be enough to make them meta.

`Ritual Beast Tamer Lara` (heard "Lura") has the same special restriction as Herkin,
can discard herself to extra normal a Ritual Beast, can banish herself from grave to protect a
Ritual Beast from destruction, and if banished can summon a Ritual Beast from deck.
`Ritual Beast Ulti-Nochiudrago` has the standard Ulti materials, can contact fuse from field or
grave by banishing materials, prevents targeting other Ritual Beasts, and can return itself
to extra to summon any one banished Ritual Beast, locking its direct attacks.
`Ritual Beast Ulti-Reirautari` is a generic link four, locks both players from tributing to
activate cards or effects, can spin back a banished Ritual Beast to normal summon another Ritual
Beast, and can banish a Ritual Beast and an opponent's card on their turn.
`Ritual Beast Inheritance` drops opponents by 200 for each type among your Ritual Beasts on board,
can reveal a Ritual Beast in hand to search a different typed Ritual Beast than discard a card,
and if two or more monsters are special summoned to your field at once you can swap a monster's
battle position.
Ritual Beast just got about everything they could have possibly wanted here and it's not even
funny. Lara by herself would have been an incredible piece of support:
the extra normal is both a Tamer and a Spiritual Beast for fusions, floods further when banished
for any number of Ritual Beast effects or for the new fusion, and it's just all around an
incredible support piece for Ritual Beast.
Nochiudrago being able to summon itself by banishing materials from grave is another Lara enabler
and gives repeatability to your Ulti Ritual Beasts since its tag out.
Though it only hits one, it can hit any Ritual Beast name, meaning that it can actually hit another
banished fusion, which in turn gives access back to two names if needed.
Reirautari is an odd floodgate, but it can just auto win certain matchups and can just banish
cards on a quick effect, which in turn lends credence to Nochiudrago's repeatability.
Another notable point is that it's summonable with `Ritual Beast's Bond`,
being a spell that can now cheat out a link four, which is just extra hilarious to me.
All in all, Ritual Beast absolutely made out best from the Duel Terminal support cards,
and hopefully with such a wave they can potentially see the rogue tier moving forward.

There were also a couple of other random OCG imports here that would be worth noting.
Specifically those of "Win Kuo" (unresolved), a level six being considered a "favorite" card,
meaning it could be weaved into the existing HERO lines, though is very much optional.
`Dragon Master Magia`, a quarter century secret rare exclusive, being extremely difficult to find,
as you can ask Rexen (unresolved), being a solid negation option for Blue-Eyes decks to tinker
with, but sadly locked at around a 750 dollar price tag.
And `Phantom of Yubel`, a fusion of Yubel and a Z00 fiend, can be fused by returning materials
from hand, field or grave to deck, has Yubel protections, and when the opponent uses an effect
can tribute herself to make the effect your opponent destroys one Yubel in their hand,
deck or field.
Notably this was insane: Yubel now had hand trap insulation, being one of their most major flaws,
that not only deletes the hand trap's effect but actively pushes your own plays forward,
easily summonable with any number of Yubel lines, recurs back your pieces for the grind game,
and notably is not actually once per turn, meaning it can be used as extension too.
This one card would unironically rocket Yubel up in playability,
but only results would tell if it would be enough to combat the Snake Eye onslaught.

### The Infinite Forbidden

- Release date: July 19th, 2024.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Fiendsmith, Millennium, Gimmick Puppet.
- Impact: Rapier 2.0.

The Infinite Forbidden was a third core set, blah blah blah.
Okay, let's just cut to the chase.

`Fiendsmith Engraver` can discard itself to search a Fiendsmith spell trap,
can send an equip card and another card to grave once per turn,
and if in grave can shuffle a light fiend from grave into deck to revive itself.
`Fiendsmith's Lacrima` is a fusion of two light fiends, drops opponents by 600 attack,
summons or recurs a banished or graved light fiend on fusion summon,
and at sent to the grave shuffles a light fiend back into deck to burn the opponent for 1200.
`Fiendsmith's Desirae` takes Engraver and two light fiends, can negate cards on the board up to the
link rating of equipped monsters to itself, and if sent to grave can shuffle back a light fiend
from grave to send a card from field to grave.
`Fiendsmith Kyrie` is a light fiend locked link one that can tribute itself to summon a Fiendsmith
from deck, and when sent to grave can equip itself to a non-link light fiend on field as an equip
spell that gives 600 attack.
`Fiendsmith's Sequence` is a link two requiring at least one light fiend, can fusion summon a
fiend by shuffling materials from grave into deck, and can equip itself from field or grave to a
non-link light fiend to give that monster targeting protection.
`Fiendsmith's Tract` searches a light fiend then discards a card, and can be banished from grave
to fusion summon a Fiendsmith.
`Fiendsmith's Sanct` summons a light fiend token on activation and can be reset from the grave if
a Fiendsmith is destroyed by an opponent's card effect.
`Fiendsmith in Paradise` sends all cards to grave except a level seven or higher fiend, and can
banish itself from grave when the opponent special summons to dump a Fiendsmith from deck or extra
to grave.

So initial thoughts here is that this core is kind of insane.
Going Engraver, for Tract, for `Fabled Lurrie`, gives access to `Fiendsmith's Requiem`,
which in turn gives you easy access for another Engraver, the ability to summon back the first
Engraver with its own effect, and there's material for a rank six right there.
You could use this to access `D/D/D Wave High King Caesar` prior to initiating any combo in deck,
providing an omni-negate insulation point, or you can even access `Beatrice, Lady of the Eternal`,
which as covered earlier can access a wide range of options, now but most likely we'll be
accessing a Rollback line.
The other option is to continue forward from there into Sequence to access Desirae with the
materials up at that point, which, with a Sequence, would have the two point negate,
though this option is heavily susceptible to interruption.
The other side of the line is that any way to access Requiem could also be a full Fiendsmith combo
with any additional body, by summoning Requiem, using it to get to Engraver, linking off the
Engraver and extra body for Sequence, firing it to summon the Lacrima, reviving the Engraver, and
making a rank six from there.
Because of this, leading up to release, many decks tinkered with what generic light fiends one
could use to access Requiem, including but not limited to `Armades, Keeper of Boundaries`,
"Exaton" (unresolved), "Nightmare Griffin" (unresolved) and even "Mellow Melody the Brassin"
(unresolved).
However, we got a much simpler answer through the OCG imports here.
`Moon of the Closed Heaven` is a generic link two whose effect should basically not be printed on
it, because at the end of the day she's a generic link two light fiend, meaning that now any way
into three monsters is a full Fiendsmith line for a rank six.
On top of all of this, Beatrice being able to dump `Snake-Eye Ash`, then link her and the Sequence
off for Promethean Princess, means that you can now actually perform a full Snake Eye line off of
any three monsters, which could be a starter or just make Caesar if you already have a Snake Eye
starter, which is a very easy ask.
All of this is to say that no matter what deck you were looking at for the format, one of the first
things added in was a Fiendsmith engine of three Engraver, one Tract and one Lurrie, alongside the
extra deck pieces.
And the fact that this set released the day before the North American WCQ is almost criminal,
and we have barely scratched the surface here on what these can do.

`Millennium Revelation` can summon `The Unstoppable Exodia Incarnate`
by revealing all five pieces of Exodia from hand, deck or field, then shuffles all monsters you
control except level 10 and higher Exodia monsters and Millennium monsters, shuffling itself back
to deck after usage.
`The Unstoppable Exodia Incarnate` is a fusion of the five Forbidden One monsters, can't be
destroyed by opponent's card effects, gains attack equal to your life in battle, can negate a spell
trap once per turn, and can set an Exodia or Obliterate spell trap from deck in each end phase,
also forcing the controller to lose 1000 life each of their standby phases.
All of the higher level Millennium share an effect to activate themselves from hand as continuous
spells, then can pay 2000 or reveal a Millennium monster in hand to summon themselves from there,
with `Sengenjin` being immune to monster effect destruction and searching a Millennium monster when
summoned with its effect, `Golem that Guards the Millennium Treasures` preventing the negation of
`Millennium Ankh` and searching a `Wedju Temple` when summoned with its effect, and
`Shield of the Millennium Dynasty` being immune to spell trap destruction and searching an Ankh
when summoned with its effect.
`Maiden of the Millennium Moon` and `Fiend Reflection of the Millennium` have the Illusion gimmick:
they can make themselves continuous spells that summon when the opponent takes an action, and each
gives an effect on summon this way, with Maiden giving higher level Illusions and spellcasters
targeting protection, and Fiend Reflection gaining life equal to half the attack of a monster on
board.
`Wedju Temple` can activate a monster from hand as a continuous spell to set up a Millennium from
deck, is one two, and gives all Millenniums the Crystal Beast fallback effect.
`Obliterate!!! Blaze` gives a level 10 or higher Exodia the ability to pay half your life points to
nuke the spell trap zones, then equips the five pieces of Exodia from hand or deck to it as equip
spells, boosting by 2000 for each and giving piercing damage.
`Obliterate!!!`, if you control a level 10 or higher Exodia, nukes the opponent's board and can be
banished from grave to search an Exodia piece or shuffle up to five back from grave or banishment.
So a far more interesting take on Exodia than we've seen in a long time.
Millennium takes a bunch of the old vanillas from the McDonald's promo days and retrains them into
a machine entirely focused around the Unstoppable Exodia, but the issue lies in that, as far as
win cons go, he's not even that great of a win con, though does pack an incredibly powerful punch.
More notably for the short term though would be that of Sengenjin searching Golem, Golem searching
Temple, Temple setting Shield, and Shield summoning itself to give you three monsters onto the
field and a monster from hand in the spell trap zone, all for the cost of 6000 life points without
using the normal summon, which made it a perfect fit to be splashed into decks looking for Requiem
access.
That's right, it's actually just more Fiendsmith.
Though this engine was not the most popular, it would absolutely see play, namely for the fact that
Snake Eyes actually got even more benefit from it, with Temple putting the Snake Eyes in the spell
trap zone providing even more synergy.

`Gimmick Puppet Little Soldiers` dumps a different leveled Gimmick Puppet on summon copying its
level, and can banish itself from grave to boost two Gimmick Puppets on board by four levels.
`Gimmick Puppet Rouge Doll` can reveal a Gimmick Puppet from extra to summon itself and a puppet
from deck with the same level as the revealed monster's rank, and if sent to grave adds herself
back to hand.
`Gimmick Puppet Cattle Scream` can summon itself from hand or grave by detaching an Xyz material
from your board and gives its Xyz a once per turn revival from the opponent's grave to their board.
`Gimmick Puppet Fantasix Machinix` is a rank eight that can detach to search a Rank-Up-Magic spell,
gives an extra machine normal, and can summon itself from grave to either board when you special
summon a Gimmick Puppet Xyz, recurring a Rank-Up-Magic when you do.
`CXyz Gimmick Puppet Fanatix Machinix` is a C Xyz of Fantasix Machinix that searches a puppet trap
on summon, can detach to summon a monster from either grave to the opponent's board, and when a
monster is summoned to the opponent's board pops it to burn them for half of its original attack.
`Mansion of the Dreadful Dolls` searches a Gimmick Puppet on activation, protects Gimmick Puppets
from battle destruction, and gives them immunity to the opponent's monsters' effects except for
Xyz, and can detach an Xyz material from your board to summon a Gimmick Puppet from your grave to
the opponent's board.
`Service Puppet Play` steals monsters up to the number of Gimmick Puppet Xyz you have, and can
banish itself from grave while you control a Gimmick Puppet Xyz to summon an Xyz in grave to either
field.
So Gimmick Puppets have for a while now been attributed to degenerate play patterns, namely
thanks to that of the nightmare lock that Branded is still hung up on, but this time the
degeneracy comes from being given a one card FTK, thanks to the field spell getting you to Little
Soldiers and providing protection from any monster effect hand trap.
Gimmick Puppets now have the ability to loop `Number C40: Gimmick Puppet of Dark Strings` thanks to
searching and recycling `Rank-Up-Magic Argent Chaos Force`, the trigger its pop and burn three
times over in a turn, to burn for over 8000 damage.
While extremely consistent, the primary issue the deck faces is that `Infinite Impermanence`
completely steals your lunch money, leaving you for dead to one of the most common hand traps in
the format.
Still, Gimmick Puppet was a solid rogue option from this and would see play as such.

`Astellar of the White Forest` can send a spell trap from hand or field to grave to summon a light
spellcaster tuner from deck, and if a spell trap is sent for a monster effect cost while in grave,
can revive herself.
`Elzette of the White Forest` can send a spell trap to summon herself from hand, then search a
White Forest monster, and if a light spellcaster tuner is summoned on the opponent's turn while in
grave, she can recur herself.
`Silvy of the White Forest` searches a White Forest spell trap on summon and can bounce a White
Forest synchro on board and in grave to summon itself from grave with its effects negated.
`Rucia of the White Forest` (heard "Ria") can summon herself from hand if you control a White
Forest,
can pitch a spell trap to draw a card, and has the same revive effect as Silvy.
`Silvera, Wolf Tamer of the White Forest` is a level six synchro tuner that flips all opponent's
monsters face down on summon and boosts Illusions and spellcaster synchros by 500 and gives them
double piercing.
`Rciela, Sinister Soul of the White Forest` is a level six synchro tuner that pitches a spell trap
on summon to search a White Forest card or light spellcaster and boosts Illusions and spellcaster
synchros by 500 and gives them immunity to the opponent's card effect destructions.
`Diabell, Queen of the White Forest` is a level eight synchro that, if made with a tuner synchro,
recurs a spell trap, and can pitch a spell trap when the opponent activates anything to summon a
level seven or lower synchro tuner from extra, grave or banishment.
All of the White Forest spell traps reset themselves off pitch for a monster effect cost, with
`Tales of the White Forest` searching a White Forest monster if you control an Illusion or
spellcaster, `Beware the White Forest` popping an opponent's card if you control a level six or
higher Illusion or spellcaster, and `Woes of the White Forest` bouncing an Illusion or spellcaster
you control to summon another from grave or banishment and also summoning a White Forest monster
from hand or deck, then synchro summoning a White Forest synchro.
An interesting initial wave for an archetype for sure, with a solid board starter in Astellar.
White Forest, as it stood, wasn't quite there yet as a standalone deck to be a consistent face in
the meta, though still could be both seen in a rogue setting as well as potentially as a splash in
a deck that has abundant access to level twos and spell traps.

`Tenpai Dragon Genroku` summons itself as a tuner if searched and increases its level by one, and
it can tribute itself to summon a Tenpai Dragon from deck, locking you to dragons if you do.
"Doradora" (unresolved) searches a level four or lower fire dragon on normal and can excavate the
top of the deck, pitching it if it's a fire dragon and boosting itself by 1000 or placing it on the
bottom of the deck.
These two serve as more one card play starters for Tenpai, with Doradora getting access to Genroku,
who'll summon himself, or to `Tenpai Dragon Chundra`, who can use it as a dragon for its summon

`Mementotlan Shleepy` can summon itself from hand if your monster has been destroyed by a card
effect that turn,
fusion summons a Memento on summon, and if destroyed dumps a Memento card from deck.
`Mementomictlan Tecuhtlica - Creation King` is a fusion of three Mementos, can banish itself from
grave to search a Memento, dumps three Memento cards from deck or extra on fusion summon, and can
quick effect pop Mementos you control and cards the opponent controls.
Shleepy gives another access point for the fusions and Creation King provides a new
worthwhile end boss, but the issue lies in that anything you could be doing with this archetype
you could be doing with Fiendsmith better.
Or at least that's what I would say if there wasn't a TCG exclusive that came down at the last
minute.
`Aerial Eater` is a fusion of two fiends with the same attribute, can dump a fiend on
fusion summon, and if in grave can banish two level six or higher fiends with the same attribute
to resummon itself.
While Fiendsmith can easily pivot its way through this in their normal combo line and the Lacrima
point, most decks wouldn't really care for it with a fiend dump, except for specifically Yubel and

Caesar without having to commit the rank six to Beatrice for the dump instead.
The other TCG exclusive fusion would be `Necroquip Princess`, a fusion of a monster equipped with a

grave, and when a monster is sent from hand to grave as cost can either draw a card or equip one
of those to herself as an equip spell that gives 500 attack.
Necroquip was a far more interesting piece for the Fiendsmiths, namely in that it could be summoned
without the need for Sequence or Tract fusions, able to send an Engraver with Requiem equipped and
the Requiem itself as material, since its requirement specifies a fiend monster card rather than a
fiend monster, meaning you can achieve a rank six with a Fiendsmith engine off any two cards now,
thanks to Moon for Requiem, for Engraver, for Necroquip, revive the Engraver with its effect, and
all without burning the Lacrima or Sequence effects, meaning you still have them in reserve.
This wouldn't be an immediate staple for the deck, namely because it wasn't really able to be
labbed out prior to the North American WCQ, being a TCG premiere,
but over time would be a go-to for the extra
deck lineup of Fiendsmith.

`Drytron Nu II` can summon itself if you control a Drytron
card and searches a Drytron monster when special summoned, locking you to machine rituals that turn.
`Drytron Meteonis DA Draconids` gives other Drytron effect destruction immunity, can banish Drytron
from grave to negate a monster effect with less or equal attack, to banish Drytron twice per turn,

it a ritual summon.
So Drytron here got a one card starter thanks to `Drytron Nova` summoning Nu II, which can now
actually trigger immediately to get you your standard Drytron line going with a starter
and tribute fodder piece,
though notably would mean that Drytron needed to change its core over from the ritual fairy
direction it had been going in for years at this point, due to the machine ritual lock.
But the door had now been opened for innovation.

`Centur-Ion Atrii` can be considered a tuner or non-tuner for a synchro summon, draws a card on
summon if you have both Emblema and Centurion cards in grave, and has the Centurion trap resummon
effect.
`Emblema Salvation` banishes a Centurion from spell trap zone to banish an opponent's card, and if

zone.
Atrii would actually be fairly important for Centurion in that it was finally a second tuner for the

"Silohatte Rabbit" (`Silhouhatte Rabbit`) is a generic link two that has the Illusion effect, sets a
continuous trap monster from deck on link summon, and when a card from the spell trap zone is
summoned as a monster pops a spell trap on field.
`Silhouhatte Trick` summons itself as a monster on activation, negates the effects of monsters up to
the number of Illusions you control on special summon, and has the standard Illusion effect.
While an interesting combo of cards that could be generically played in anything, Silhouhatte
Rabbit would be seen more as a tech option for some decks at the time being, used either for its
intended target of Trick or for something like "Azeroth" (unresolved) which trades the imperm effect
of Trick for a summon negation.

### Rage of the Abyss

- Release date: October 11th, 2024.
- Set type: core set.
- Major strategies: Azamina, Mermail, Fiendsmith.
- Impact: if Maxx "C" was so great (heard "if Maxi was so great"), the last core set of the year.

Rage of the Abyss was the last core set of the year, and as such, with all of our major meta
developments coming from core sets thus far, a lot was riding on this one to help shake up the
seemingly stale meta that the lack of new releases had caused.

`The Hallowed Azamina` pitches Sinful Spoils cards to fusion summon an Azamina monster with a level
equal or lower to the number pitched times four without its materials, and if in grave can shuffle
back in an Azamina monster from field or grave to shuffle itself back into deck.
`Azamina Ilia Silvia` is a level six taking an Illusion and a light spellcaster, doubles as
Azamina damage other than itself, can tribute itself to negate any effect, and when destroyed
searches a Sinful Spoils trap.
`Azamina Mu Rcielago` is a level six taking an Illusion and a light spellcaster, drops opponents by
500 attack and defense for each Azamina you control, searches an Azamina or Sinful Spoils card on
fusion summon, and when destroyed searches a Sinful Spoils spell.
`Azamina Sol Erysichthon` is a level six taking an Illusion and a fiend, sends a card on field to
grave on fusion summon, and recurs an Azamina or Sinful Spoils card in the standby phase.
`Azamina Moa Regina` is a level six taking an Illusion and a level six or higher fiend, can revive
an Illusion other than another copy of itself, and pops up to two cards on field when an Azamina or
Sinful Spoils card effect is used.
`Azamina Debtors` (heard "deor", unresolved name) pitches a Sinful Spoils to fusion summon an
Azamina monster with the level equal to or lower than the number pitched times four without its
materials, and as a quick play spell it can be banished from grave to revive an Azamina.
`Deception of the Sinful Spoils` can tribute a monster from hand or field to search an Azamina card,
steals 1500 life from the opponent if a monster is sent to the grave while you control an Azamina,
and can reset itself from grave in the end phase if sent that turn from field while face up.
`Azamina Determination` fusion summons an Azamina by shuffling back Sinful Spoils from grave or
banishment for every four levels, and can banish itself from grave to reset a Sinful Spoils spell
trap from grave, locking it from use that turn.
Immediately these would be recognized as more Snake Eyes, which by proxy it was, being a part of
the same lore series.
Deception on its own can get you to Hallowed, to Mu Rcielago,
to `Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`,
which in turn is the full Snake Eye combo, but it also makes the `Diabellstar the Black Witch`
starting point better by giving her a way to maneuver into Ilia Silvia and search a Snake Eye Ash
without burning the normal, then do the Snake Eye combo under an omni-negate.
This was an incredible revitalization point for Snake Eyes,
who were in a pretty rough spot meta-wise
after the Beatrice and Lacrima bans, so any boost of their viability would be appreciated.

`Abyssrhine, the Atlantean Spirit` is always considered a Mermail as well, can tribute itself from
hand with another Mermail or Atlantean to either search or summon a level seven fish, sea serpent
or aqua monster from deck, for which we'll refer to as the tri-type for this section, and can
banish itself from grave on the opponent's turn and discard a card to draw one.
`Mermail Shadow Squad` is always considered an Atlantean as well, can discard a card to change your
water monsters to level seven, and if sent to grave to use a water monster's effect summons a level
four or lower Atlantean or Mermail from deck.
`Poseidra Abyss, the Atlantean Dragon Lord` is a three material rank seven that can also Xyz summon
over an Atlantean or Mermail Xyz, could detach two materials to dump a water from hand or deck to
bounce up to three opponent's cards to hand, and if sent to grave after being Xyz summoned can
discard a card to summon three level three or lower tri-types from hand or grave.
`Mermail King - Neptabyss` is a link three taking at least one tri-type, locks targeting water
monsters at points to with card effects, searches or equips to itself an Abyss equip spell if a
water monster is sent to grave for cost, and if destroyed by the opponent searches an Atlantean or
Mermail monster.
`Abyss-sting Triaina` summons an Atlantean or Mermail from hand or grave equipping itself to it,
can block destruction of the monster by sending itself to grave, and can banish itself from grave
to shuffle back up to three tri-types from grave or banishment.
Quick note: there's also now an `Eria the Water Channeler` to match the previous one.
Ditching all pretenses, Atlantean and Mermail are now the same archetype, as they always have been
played together.
This would be a solid modernization of the classic Zexal era strategy of Mermail Abyss:
Abyssrhine gets you any of your big Mermail bosses quickly while also triggering an Atlantean
effect and gives you easy ways to trigger them on the opponent's turn with her grave effect.
Shadow Squad makes accessing the rank seven pool easy and has a deck cheat out when you inevitably
pay for a water effect with him.
Poseidra is easy to access thanks to `Mermail Abyssgaios`, who can bounce a board and then
`Soul Charge` back a bunch of bodies when you inevitably link him off for King, and King gives
targeting protection and easy access to the Abyss equip line, getting access to Triaina for the
recursion.
This would also bring `Deep Sea Minstrel` back into talks as a way to trap dust the opponent and
trigger your Atlantean effects, which was always considered a desirable effect regardless of the
format.
Well, clearly not a top tier threat, as most of these legacy waves aren't, Mermail would clearly
be positioned as a potential rogue deck for the new format, which is another notch into the
ever growing pool of rogue archetypes.

`Lacrima the Crimson Tears` is always considered a Fiendsmith, dumps a Fiendsmith card from deck on
summon, and can shuffle itself back from grave on the opponent's turn to revive a Fiendsmith.
`Fiendsmith's Agnumday` is a link three taking at least one light fiend that could revive a
non-link light fiend on quick effect and equip itself to that monster, giving it 600 attack for each
link rating equipped to it and piercing damage.
Agnumday exists. Lacrima, though, is an extremely interesting point, as she proved to be another one
card starter into the Fiendsmith lines, namely in dumping an Engraver on summon,
linking into Requiem
to summon another Engraver from deck, reviving the dumped Engraver and making
`D/D/D Wave High King Caesar` just in time to block Nibiru, or to access Sequence, or, in terms of
Closed Heaven lines, let your Requiem hit Lacrima instead to dump the Engraver and set it up to
come back and get you to Sequence off of just Closed Heaven.
But in a lot of ways we already had that through Necroquip lines using the Engraver grave effect.
The main thing she does is act as a normal summon that is the full Fiendsmith line, or gives you a
Sequence line with an additional light fiend in grave, which in turn means it's easier to access
Desirae.

`Heart of the Blue-Eyes` is always treated as a Millennium card, can't swap control, can discard
itself to search `Millennium Ankh`, and if you've used Millennium Ankh this duel and it's in grave,
can send a monster the opponent summons that is level eight or higher or has 3000 or more attack
to grave to summon itself back once per duel, giving Millennium another way to Ankh and more ways to
actually interrupt thanks to its grave effect.
This gives the deck better consistency that's not nearly as reliant on Exodia, so the pure variant
might actually see some play now.
"Ton Airon" (a Memento card, heard "momento ton airon", unresolved) on summon makes level nine and
higher monsters able to attack directly that turn, if destroyed by card effect recurs a Memento from
grave or banishment, and can revive itself if a Memento in hand or field is destroyed.
This gives Memento a body that comes back each turn quite easily,
but beyond that is a touch brick-y.
Still, it's a nice piece of recursion for Memento and would see play as a one-off because of that.

`Centur-Ion Chimerea` can turn an opponent's chained effect into: your opponent places one
Centurion monster from their face up field or grave into their spell trap zone as a continuous trap,
and has the standard Centurion trap summon effect.
`Centur-Ion Primera Primus` is a level eight generic synchro that can't be destroyed by battle, can
summon from anywhere but the extra deck, searches an Emblema card on summon and pops a card on each
side of the field, and revives itself when the Centurion leaves the field by the opponent's card
effect.
Chimerea wasn't nearly good enough to include, despite being effectively in negate form,
but Primera Primus, being a level eight stepping stone that can search your Emblemas
and come back as
an annoying indestructible by battle bungus, makes it a great inclusion for Centurion,
especially since it was generically makeable, making the synchro engines like Superheavy Samurai a
much easier sell for the deck now that Emblemas were findable with more methods.

`Fire King Courtier Ulcanix` pops a fire on field or hand on summon to search a Fire Beast tri-type
from the deck, making its level match the search, and if destroyed floats into
`Fire King High Avatar Garunix` from deck, a card that the OCG got very shortly after the Fire King
structure deck release.
"Okex" (unresolved) gives Fire King another starter that can pop a random fire in hand to search
`Legendary Fire King Ponix`, who can then trigger and start your lines while also being usable later
in the turn to pop for any of your various Fire King effects to float into a level eight for Xyz or
link plays.
She can also search for `Sacred Fire King Garunix` or Kirin to make herself a level eight and enable
Xyz plays earlier in the turn, which in turn can convert into a negate via
`Number 90: Galaxy-Eyes Photon Lord` or `Number S38: Titanic Galaxy`, with Photon Lord giving you
the hand trap insulation.
Okex would be an extremely powerful option for Snake Eyes in any scenario where you have a one card
starter that doesn't need the normal, which would raise the popularity of the Fire King variant of
Snake Eyes yet again.

`Dominus Impulse` can be activated from hand if your opponent controls a card, negates a special
summon effect and destroys the card too if you have a trap in grave, but locks you out of light,
earth and wind effects for the remainder of the game after activation.
An absolutely powerful hand trap, but that drawback immediately locks it from usage in a handful of
decks, but notably didn't affect the current best decks in the format revolving around darks and
fires.
Lastly, and absolutely most importantly, `Mulcharmy Fuwalos` has the same effect as the previous
Purulia but for monsters summoned from the deck or extra deck.
This was the true Maxx "C" successor, being far more applicable than Purulia's hand version, as
almost every deck in the game will summon cards out of the deck or extra at least once, if not
multiple times, in a turn, which in turn means that effectively it can be Maxx "C" under the right
conditions, and as such became a staple three of hand trap for the format moving forward.

### Crossover Breakers

- Release date: December 6th, 2024.
- Set type: deck building set.
- Major strategies: Ryzeal, Maliss, Ryu-Ge.
- Impact: a breath of fresh air to the format.

Crossover Breakers was the first deck building set we've had in over a year at this stage, with the
last one being Valiant Smashers back in November of last year.
As such, the three archetypes contained within had the expectation on them that they'd be solid
strategies but need a wave or two to get going.
Not the case here: as it turns out, all three of the strategies here would be playable on release,
so let's take a dive into each individually before we get too ahead of ourselves.

`Sword Ryzeal` can summon itself from hand if you have a Ryzeal on field or in grave and searches a
light pyro on summon.
`Node Ryzeal` can summon itself from hand if you have an Xyz on field or in grave and can send a
card from hand or field to grave to revive a Ryzeal with its effects negated.
`Ice Ryzeal` can summon itself from hand by pitching a card from hand or field to grave and on
normal or special summons a Ryzeal from deck.
`Ext Ryzeal` can summon itself from hand by dumping an Xyz from extra on summon and searches a fire
thunder monster if you only control rank and level fours.
`Palm Ryzeal` can be summoned from hand by shuffling back a Ryzeal from hand or grave and if it
battles a monster can dump a level four from hand or deck to boost its attack by that dumped
monster's.
`Ryzeal Duo Drive` is a multi-attach rank four that gains 100 for each material, can attach a
Ryzeal from grave as material on summon, and can detach two to search two different Ryzeal cards.
`Ryzeal Detonator` is a multi-attach Ryzeal locked rank four that can detach a material when the
opponent uses a card or effect to pop a card on field, can attach a monster from grave as material
on summon, and can detach to protect an Xyz from destruction.
`Ryzeal Plugin` summons a Ryzeal or Xyz from grave or banishment then attaches a Ryzeal card from
deck to a rank four.
`Ryzeal Cross` can bottom two Ryzeal cards in grave to draw a card and can detach from a Ryzeal Xyz
to negate an opponent's monster effect at the point of resolution.
`Ryzeal Plasma Hole` pops cards up to the number of Ryzeal Xyz you control, then attaches a Ryzeal
card from grave to a rank four, and can be banished from grave to quick Xyz summon using a Ryzeal.
It's been a while since we've had a much more straightforward deck with the power of the meta, but
here we are.
Ryzeal on the surface is a far simpler deck than any Snake Eyes, Fiendsmith, Yubel or Azamina,
but still has the power to compete with the top strategies, thanks in large part to their
simplicity, with every monster in the deck being an extender, so it doesn't take much to get going,
standardly ending on at minimum a Duo Drive, a Detonator and Cross up, which can use the materials
under Duo Drive to negate a monster effect at resolution.
Detonator also has a lot of malleability against specifically this meta, as it makes it difficult
to establish a board when anything that activates or uses an effect can cause a snipe out.
Notably this stops any continuous or field spells in their tracks, so Snake Eyes and Yubels lose
power spells from their strategies, it makes it nearly impossible to Snake Eye combo since you can't
keep key pieces on the board, and it will always come down with at least three materials since it
reattaches a monster from grave to it as material.
Ext can also cause some fun interaction with its summon effect, as you can send any Xyz to grave
on summon, meaning that you can send something like Aggregator (`Mereologic Aggregator`) for its
summon to act as an imperm, then reattach it to Detonator to use its effect later as a secondary
effect for Detonator.
Ice is obviously the most desirable starting point, since it's a tour guide for the archetype
effectively, but notably is also a level four pyro, meaning that it's accessible with `Bonfire`,
once again making the spell one of the most relevant rogues (heard "Rodas") we've had in years.
It says a lot about an archetype this simple on a surface level holding so much potential, so we'll
put a pin in this conversation until we can talk about the YCS after this release.

All of the Maliss main deck monsters resummon themselves at a cost of 300 life once per turn when
banished.
`Maliss <P> White Rabbit` blocks damage from Maliss links that point to it and sets a Maliss trap
from deck with a different name than those in grave on summon.
`Maliss <P> Chessy Cat` banishes any monster destroyed in battle with a Maliss link that points to
it and can banish a Maliss card from hand to draw two.
`Maliss <P> Dormouse` gives Maliss links pointing to it effect destruction immunity and can banish
a Maliss monster from deck to boost Malisses by 600 for the turn.
Each of the Maliss links are link 3s requiring a Maliss that can also summon themselves when
banished by paying 900 once per turn, with an added effect when you do.
`Maliss <Q> Red Ransom` banishes a Cyberse from deck on its banish summon, swaps the attack and
defense of an opponent's effect monsters while pointing to a monster, and searches a Maliss spell on
summon from anywhere.
`Maliss <Q> White Binder` draws a card on its banished summon, can banish up to three cards from the
graves on summon from anywhere, and can set a Maliss trap from deck or grave once per turn.
`Maliss <Q> Hearts Crypter` doubles its attack on a banish summon
and can quick effect shuffle back a
banished Maliss card to banish a card on field, with it being non-negatable when pointing to a
monster.
`Maliss in Underground` banishes a Maliss card from hand, deck or grave on activation, boosts Maliss
links by 3000 if you have three different Maliss traps banished, and locks the opponent to only
attacking Maliss links.
All the Maliss traps can be activated the turn they're set by banishing a Maliss on field:
`Maliss <C> MTP-07` searching a Maliss monster then banishing a card on field if you control a
Maliss
link, `Maliss <C> GWC-06` summoning a Maliss from grave or banishment then gaining life equal to its
attack if you control a Maliss link, and `Maliss <C> TB-11` summoning a Maliss from deck or a Maliss
link from extra if your opponent has three or more cards on board,
locking that monster's attacks and
effects for the turn.
Maliss was a very interesting Cyberse stack in that it operated on a different axis than most others
but ultimately is wanting to build towards the same goal of
`Firewall Dragon Darkfluid - Neo Tempest Terahertz`,
thanks to Dormouse banishing White Rabbit out of the deck, White Rabbit coming back and
searching TB-11, linking off the two into `Haggard Lizardose` (God, the TCG exclusives coming up in
the weirdest places), or into S:P if you're basic: banishing the Dormouse from grave and bringing
her back to link up into Red Ransom, searching the field spell, banishing Red Ransom for TB-11 to
grab more material, resummoning Red Ransom and banishing Dormouse from deck, which then will also
resummon itself.
This alone leaves you with material for lots of different branching combos, and Maliss, with access
still open to the generic Cyberse options like `Link Decoder`, `Splash Mage` and
`Decode Talker Heatsoul`, but also giving you easy access to the rest of Maliss thanks to the field
spell (`Maliss in the Mirror`) being in hand ready to activate and banish any of the other links you
might pivot through.
Probably one of the biggest draws to the deck is its desire to play with its cards being banished,
meaning that Dimension Shifter has once again found a deck that it can be comfortable in, on a
similar level to Floowandereeze, which leaves Maliss in a really interesting position opposing
Ryzeal solidly as another meta contender for this new format.

`Kyoro Ryu-Ge Kaiva` can spin itself back to hand to search a `Ryu-Ge Realm - Dino Domains`, can
summon itself from hand if two or more cards were destroyed that turn, and can place a Dino Domains
from board on bottom of deck to pop cards on the field up to the number of different types on the
field.
`Kairo Ryu-Ge Emva` can discard itself to search `Ryu-Ge Realm - Sea Spires`, can summon itself from

can place a Sea Spires from board on bottom of deck to banish the opponent's hand then make them
draw
the same number of cards.
`Genro Ryu-Ge Hakva` can banish itself from hand to search a `Ryu-Ge Realm - Wyrm Winds`, can summon
itself if a monster is banished while it's already banished and there are now at least two other
banished monsters, and can place a Wyrm Winds from board on bottom of deck to add a Ryu-Ge card to
hand from deck, grave or banishment.
So `Sosei Ryu-Ge Mistva` (heard "mvah") is a ritual pendulum who in scale can search a non-pendulum
Ryu-Ge card then pop itself, and can summon itself when a monster is destroyed while in face up
extra by tributing a level 10 Ryu-Ge, then pops up to two cards you control to place that many
Ryu-Ge continuous spells from deck face up onto the board.
`Ryu-Ge Rising` searches a Ryu-Ge monster that can add a Ryu-Ge pendulum from hand to face up extra
deck, and can banish itself and another Ryu-Ge monster from grave while you control a Ryu-Ge
pendulum to summon a Ryu-Ge with the same type as the banished monster from deck.
`Ryu-Ge War Zone` searches one, dumps one and banishes one of dinosaur, sea serpent or wyrm from
deck on activation, locking you to those plus dragons for the turn, and if you have a Mistva in your
face up extra can summon three Ryu-Ge with different types from deck, grave and banishment,
one each.
`Ryu-Ge Realm - Dino Domains` boosts a Ryu-Ge by 300 and gives Ryu-Ge pendulums and level 10 plus
dinos the effect of bottom a Ryu-Ge continuous spell on board to negate a monster's effect with less
attack.
`Ryu-Ge Realm - Sea Spires` gives Ryu-Ge battle destruction immunity and gives Ryu-Ge pendulums and
level 10 plus sea serpents the effect to bottom a Ryu-Ge continuous spell to bounce a card on field
on a quick effect.
`Ryu-Ge Realm - Wyrm Winds` is macro (heard "macro",
Dimension Shifter style) from the board for both
players on the opponent's turn and gives Ryu-Ge pendulums and level 10 plus wyrms the effect of
bottom of Ryu-Ge continuous spell on board to drop a monster's attack to zero on a quick effect.
And `Ryu-Ge Rivalry` summons a Ryu-Ge from hand, deck or grave, bouncing it in the end phase, and
could be banished from grave to bottom a Ryu-Ge continuous spell from board, grave or banishment to
draw a card.
Ryu-Ge has a bit of the Veilance (heard "veilance", unresolved) effect to me, where my eyes glaze
over every time I start to sit down and read these effects, but after three or four attempts the
strategy seems to be more of a splasher for other decks in the tri-type than it does for its own
standalone game plan.
There are some things you can do involving setting up the three level 10 as a one card combo from
either Rising or Mistva, using two to set up a `Varudras, the Final Bringer of the End Times`, since
it's a wyrm and plays under the lock, and leaving up either Kaiva to use Dino Domains' monster
negate or Emva to use the Sea Spires' targeted bounce, then being able to convert that remaining
level 10 into a Mistva when Varudras negates and pops, which in turn can convert your War Zone and
use Varudras into any two continuous spells out of the deck.
But that's not particularly impressive when you consider that almost every deck right now can do
something similar.
However, War Zone being able to search anything in the tri-type, provided you can get one card of
each of the remaining two, does mean that it's able to completely ignore the Ryu-Ge cards on search
and set up weirder lines, since you can search War Zone with Mistva, you can use War Zone to search
for any singular card in the tri-type to then dump and banish the corresponding Ryu-Ge from the
other two tri-types, then use War Zone's second effect to bring out the two that were dumped or
banished alongside the third still in deck to access Varudras before utilizing whatever you
searched with War Zone.
At that stage it almost operates like a searcher that insulates other strategies from harm before
the normal, meaning that any deck that uses the tri-type can actually benefit from an engine of
these cards quite effectively.
Immediately, strategies that come to mind for this are Dinosaurs, who are always looking for more
cards to add to the pile, Swordsoul, who now have access to an omni-gate again after the loss of
Baronne, or simply using the three materials to summon out an easy `Archnemeses Protos`, which is
still a pain in the ass to this day.
As for Dinosaurs specifically though, War Zone pulls double duty by searching either a dinosaur for
your combos or for `True King Lithosagym, the Disaster` (heard "lithos sagum"), who's a wyrm that
fits under the lock, and thanks to doing standard Dinosaur shenanigans with popping the babies you
can already trigger the Mistva on your own turn, which in turn can convert more babies into
interactions through the continuous spell lines.
All in all, this is potentially a powerful build-around engine for other decks, but as for what it
can do solo, the options are still pretty limited for now.

#### YCS Anaheim

YCS Anaheim would be held the same weekend, and instantly Ryzeal and Maliss would have an impact,
completely usurping the previous meta and marking themselves as the new decks to beat.
Trifunoski (name as heard, "triff") would take the event on Ryzeal, fulfilling a called shot at the
start of the year to win a YCS by year's end, showcasing how versatile the new cards actually were,
because the Ryzeal don't lock you until you use their summon from hand effects, starting with Ice
for Ext to search Sword allows you to make "Exaton" (unresolved) before the lock, which is a light
fiend for accessing Requiem and, by extension, the entire Fiendsmith engine before proceeding with
Ryzeal plays.
Thanks to this, Ryzeal has the ability to self-insulate its own combo by making a Caesar before
committing the Sword summon, which leaves the option open to get absolutely crazy with your combos.
Beyond that, Maliss would also have a strong showing for its debut, being only a core of about 16
cards, meaning that the remaining deck could be filled with hand traps and generic tools for
enhancing the deck's game plan like the Kashtira pieces.
I'd also be remiss if I neglected mentioning Dimension Shifter, being by far and away the best hand
trap in the deck and arguably the format, as the entire strategy would almost prefer to play under a
constant macro effect thanks to their resummon effects, to help solidify the passing of the torch in
a sense for the meta.

#### December 8th ban list

The ban list would be updated on day two of the event, December 8th, being announced live at YCS
Anaheim directly following the finals.
Newly banned were `Gimmick Puppet Nightmare`, "Jin the Spiritualist" (unresolved)
and `Original Sinful Spoils - Snake-Eye`.
Newly limited were `Phantom of Yubel`, `Tenpai Dragon Chundra`, `Sangen Kaimen` and
`Zoodiac Barrage`, returning from the banned section.
Newly semi-limited were `Cyber Jar`, `Danger!? Jackalope?`, `Danger!? Tsuchinoko?`, "Ava"
(unresolved) and `Performapal Monkeyboard`, all returning from limited.
Lastly, unlimited were `Blaster, Dragon Ruler of Infernos`, `Dinowrestler Pankratops`,
`Speedroid Terrortop`, "E Tiger" (unresolved), `Redox, Dragon Ruler of Boulders`, "Airlifter"
(unresolved), `Tempest, Dragon Ruler of Storms`, `Thunder Dragon Colossus`,
`Tidal, Dragon Ruler of Waterfalls` and `Opening of the Spirit Gates`.

This ban list was odd.
Obviously the biggest change here was the banning of Original Sinful Spoils, which many players
had been wanting for half a year at this stage, but honestly it seems a little off base at this
point in time.
While yes, Snake Eyes and other decks utilizing OSS had been coming back up in the meta since the
release of Rage of the Abyss, it seems like the deck had actually been put a bit on the back burner
with the release of Crossover, feeling a little bit like kicking them while they're down.
The banning of Jin and Nightmare, on the other hand, are directly aimed at Branded at this stage:
anytime Branded was topping, it was on the back of a special summon blocker like these two.
Yubel and Tenpai would also catch hits here, with Phantom going to one hampering Yubel's
repeatability heavily and Tenpai going from six copies of their best starter down to two.
As for the lifts, nothing too major came off the list, with the most notable change here being the
Dragon Rulers, one of the most divisive archetypes in history, now being completely unrestricted
again, but even then they do nothing to the current meta.
